3. **Who pays the settlement's services when volunteers mint the money that buys them?** The time bank promises a 30-percent cut in shared-service costs by replacing bureaucratic employees with credit-paid volunteers; the credit itself is created by the act (issuance = verification of completion), so the services the credits redeem are consumed without any external revenue — the record has an accounting theory in which supply funds demand perfectly. Under what constraint does the bank's issuance stay honest: a cap on credits in circulation, a redemption-side revenue (the fee rails), or the assumption that volunteers always outnumber claimants? *(Why: the flaw text — "municipal budget to water one little tree" — is answered by minting, not by a budget; the requirement's fiscal soundness depends on this unstated constraint. Blocking: yes for adopting the time record as written.)*
4. **Is a saved kilowatt-hour a produced one?** The gamification record's tokens are issued against consumption that did not occur; the monetary standard's backing is energy produced or stored. If restraint tokens eventually redeem against real services, what physically guarantees the services? Either the thrift credit is a discounted bill (self-funding — the money the settlement did not spend pays for the reward) or it is a claim on general revenue. Which model the owner intends decides whether this record inherits the monetary folder's "money without energy is impossible" sentence or escapes it as a rebate instrument. *(Why: the two readings differ on whether the "up to 30 percent" target is inflation-safe at all. Blocking: no for the mechanism; yes for its metrics.)*
