# Custody of Making Tools

## Abstract
A society that houses its residents in twenty-square-meter suites cannot let each of those suites hoard a drill, and a society that does not house them so has no claim on the drill either. This document holds one question — the instruments and workshops of making: private, shared, or a service? — and two recorded answers that abolish private tool ownership at different granularities: [Tool bank and modular FabLab machinery sharing network in the blocks (Tool-as-a-Service FabMesh)](custody-of-making-tools.md#tool-bank-and-modular-fablab-machinery-sharing-network-in-the-blocks-tool-as-a-service-fabmesh) shares the item — a smart lending shelf and digital-fabrication workshop on the minus-5 floor of each building cluster, tools issued on citizen identity and checked for calibration by RFID and machine-vision at return, cutting per-capita household tool purchase cost by 95 percent — while [Dynamic sharing network of maker workshops and modular maker-space projects](custody-of-making-tools.md#dynamic-sharing-network-of-maker-workshops-and-modular-maker-space-projects) shares the hour — equipped basement workshop space booked by residents, cutting individual tool purchases by up to 80 percent. One answer is a library, the other a studio; each presupposes what the other supplies, and the disagreement they record is not whether ownership of tools is waste but what the unit of sharing is — the returned instrument or the reserved machine-hour — and with it, which custody chain (calibration or supervision) the society must staff.

## Introduction

### Motivation
Kept apart, the two records read as redundant answers to a housing-furniture problem: [Tool bank and modular FabLab machinery sharing network in the blocks (Tool-as-a-Service FabMesh)](custody-of-making-tools.md#tool-bank-and-modular-fablab-machinery-sharing-network-in-the-blocks-tool-as-a-service-fabmesh) claims a 95-percent fall in per-capita tool purchase cost, and [Dynamic sharing network of maker workshops and modular maker-space projects](custody-of-making-tools.md#dynamic-sharing-network-of-maker-workshops-and-modular-maker-space-projects) claims an 80-percent fall in individual purchases — a reader meeting both suspects double counting, because the numbers look like rival forecasts of one event. Read together they are not rivals of that kind: one counts money per household (a lending service priced like any other), the other counts units of hardware (a space shared free of the purchase motive), and the gap between them is the record of an unmade decision — whether the society intends to operate tools as a priced service or maker hours as commons infrastructure. The same entanglement runs downward: both records place their installation in basement floors whose existence is a linked idea of the block typology, audited elsewhere, and both settle through a wallet and identity the [Integrated financial interface, digital wallet and real-time settlement without queues or checkout counters](payment-rails-and-service-charging.md#integrated-financial-interface-digital-wallet-and-real-time-settlement-without-queues-or-checkout-counters) record proposes. Separated, each half of the custody chain — calibration at the shelf, supervision at the bench — survives only by accident.

## Explanation

### The problem the answers share
The instruments and workshops of making — tools, machines, benches: private, shared, or a service? A household tool used less than twenty minutes a year is, by both records' arithmetic, a purchase of idle steel; a workshop machine too expensive for any household is by the same arithmetic a machine that must be shared to be used at all. The question is at what unit sharing happens, who guarantees the instrument between uses, and what replaces the buyer's private incentive to keep tools working. The question itself favors none of the answers below.

### Approaches

#### Tool bank and modular FabLab machinery sharing network in the blocks (Tool-as-a-Service FabMesh)
**Mechanism:** Cancel the purchase and storage of low-use household tools — drills, grinders, three-dimensional printers, wrenches. Every building cluster keeps, on its minus-5 floor, a digital-fabrication workshop (FabLab) and a smart lending shelf for tools. The resident receives a tool on citizen identity; after return, the health and calibration of the equipment is checked on the spot by RFID tags and machine-vision cameras.
**Metrics:** 95 percent reduction of per-capita household tool purchase cost; tool utilization efficiency above 80 percent; the freeing of the closet in the 20-square-meter suite.
**Prerequisites:** Self-service smart shelves; RFID scanner gates; interactive training on safe operation of the machines.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Millions of toolboxes bought by single households, used — at most — less than 20 minutes a year, wasting industrial resources.
**Tags:** shared tooling, digital maker workshop, FabLab, removal of tool ownership, sharing economy
**Scale:** Micro — building (minus-5 floor) and mid — building clusters
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | The record's shelf and workshop occupy a minus-5 floor — a space the conventional settlement does not have, and which the block typology's deep basement (an idea of other records, linked in the second answer's synergy lines, never assumed here) supplies; without it the tool bank shrinks to a lend-shop on a leftover ground-floor corner, without the RFID calibration gate or the interactive safety-training room the prerequisites demand. In the existing fabric the 95-percent figure is also unreachable for a second reason: the tool hoard is not yet a cost the household counts, because the cluttered storage the record frees (the 20-square-meter suite's closet) does not exist — the saving has no ground to land on.
**Cognitive anchor:** The old bazaar workshop where the tools belonged to the master's bench, not to the apprentice's home — the trade carried its instruments in common, and the skill traveled with the hand that used them.
**Narrative hook:** "Give the toolbox back to the workshop and keep only the skill — no home in the block needs to own a drill again."
**Syn (synergies):** [20-square-meter kitchen-less dwelling suites with multi-state transformable furniture](../buildings/dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#20-square-meter-kitchen-less-dwelling-suites-with-multi-state-transformable-furniture) (the compact 20-square-meter suite with no surplus space); [Integrated financial interface, digital wallet and real-time settlement without queues or checkout counters](payment-rails-and-service-charging.md#integrated-financial-interface-digital-wallet-and-real-time-settlement-without-queues-or-checkout-counters) — the wallet and the Sabz.City platform
**Source:** Supplementary idea of the system architect (the tool-as-a-service economy)

#### Dynamic sharing network of maker workshops and modular maker-space projects
**Mechanism:** Allocate workshop space equipped with advanced tools — three-dimensional printers, laser cutters, carpentry and electronics equipment — on the basement floors, shared among the residents of the blocks; the space lets citizens meet their repair needs and their creative production on a sharing-economy footing, instead of buying single-ownership tools and accumulating devices at home.
**Metrics:** Reduction of individual household tool purchases by up to 80 percent; growth of manual skills and micro home-work employment in the neighborhood setting.
**Prerequisites:** An online booking platform scheduling the workshop's equipment; a safety protocol for work with industrial tools.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Cumulative single-owner purchases of expensive tools that gather dust in storerooms 99 percent of the time.
**Tags:** sharing economy, maker workshop, Maker-Space, circular economy, local production
**Scale:** Mid — neighborhood (basement floors)
**Fit & substrate:** [Adaptable to existing fabric (Retrofit/Brownfield)] | Of the two answers this is the one that can be installed on the existing fabric — a booked workshop floor needs basement space of any vintage, not a planned minus-5 level — but the record's own prerequisites say otherwise in practice: without the booking platform and the industrial-tool safety protocol, a shared workshop reproduces inside commons walls exactly the flaw it indicts (equipment idle most of the day, now also unsupervised), and without calibration checks — the instrument the *first* answer holds — the 3D printers and laser cutters degrade on strangers' terms. Retrofit fit is therefore real but conditional: the answer borrows its safety from a record that is not itself adopted here.
**Cognitive anchor:** The creativity and innovation workshops of startups.
**Narrative hook:** "Why buy an expensive drill you use once a year? The neighborhood's equipped workshop is always under your feet."
**Syn (synergies):** [Multi-signature custody protocol for the physical portals of sensitive spaces with duress revocation and no automatic repair access](../security-and-resilience/securing-sensitive-spaces.md#multi-signature-custody-protocol-for-the-physical-portals-of-sensitive-spaces-with-duress-revocation-and-no-automatic-repair-access) — the multi-story basements; [Integrated financial interface, digital wallet and real-time settlement without queues or checkout counters](payment-rails-and-service-charging.md#integrated-financial-interface-digital-wallet-and-real-time-settlement-without-queues-or-checkout-counters) (settlement with the urban token); [The network of waterways and in-settlement boat-navigation channels with secondary phytoremediation](../water-and-matter/water-bodies-as-common-infrastructure.md#the-network-of-waterways-and-in-settlement-boat-navigation-channels-with-secondary-phytoremediation) — skills training

### How the approaches collide and compose
**[Tool bank and modular FabLab machinery sharing network in the blocks (Tool-as-a-Service FabMesh)](custody-of-making-tools.md#tool-bank-and-modular-fablab-machinery-sharing-network-in-the-blocks-tool-as-a-service-fabmesh) and [Dynamic sharing network of maker workshops and modular maker-space projects](custody-of-making-tools.md#dynamic-sharing-network-of-maker-workshops-and-modular-maker-space-projects) measure the same abolition of ownership in two currencies — and the currencies do not convert.** The FabMesh record's 95 percent is a reduction of per-capita purchase *cost* under a priced lending service; the maker-space record's "up to 80 percent" is a reduction in units of *purchases* under a booked commons space; the one residual the other counts as its whole subject. A tool library (the first record's shelf) leaves the expensive machines — printers, laser cutters, both named twice — to be booked as hours, and a booked workshop (the second record's floor) leaves the small tools at home because nobody carried them down. So neither number forecasts the other's world: 95 percent assumes the machine-hour is priced as a service, 80 percent assumes it is not. Adjudication consequence: if both records stand, the document needs one meter that spans both — the first record's utilization efficiency (tool use above 80 percent) is the only metric in either that could serve, because it prices no unit — or a ruling on which currency the verdict audits.

**The two answers share one substrate and one settlement rail, and both are linked ideas, not premises.** The FabMesh installs on the minus-5 floor; the maker-space on "the basement floors" — the deep multi-story basement the second record's own synergy line reaches for in [Multi-signature custody protocol for the physical portals of sensitive spaces with duress revocation and no automatic repair access](../security-and-resilience/securing-sensitive-spaces.md#multi-signature-custody-protocol-for-the-physical-portals-of-sensitive-spaces-with-duress-revocation-and-no-automatic-repair-access), a custody protocol this document does not own. Both settle through [Integrated financial interface, digital wallet and real-time settlement without queues or checkout counters](payment-rails-and-service-charging.md#integrated-financial-interface-digital-wallet-and-real-time-settlement-without-queues-or-checkout-counters), and the first issues tools on a citizen identity. A verdict that adopts the lending network without the wallet, or the workshop without the basement and its multi-signature custody rule, adopts a record whose listed conditions it has not adopted — which is precisely the dependency direction the document set fixes: consumer records declare what they need; the rails, the basement and the identity decide whether they can supply it.

**The sharpest collision closes on one decision with two answers: is the unit of sharing the tool or the workshop hour?** The first answer — tool — reads the requirement as custody of items: a resident may leave the shelf with anything, so the society owes calibration at return, RFID identity at issue, and the injury liability of an unsupervised drill; the maker-space then hosts only what no one may take (printers, cutters, and the bench they sit on), and the 95-percent cost claim is the binding metric. The second answer — hour — reads the requirement as access to capability: nobody removes any machine from the floor, the booking platform *is* the custody chain, and the shelf becomes the workshop's concierge desk rather than a separate service; the 80-percent purchase claim and the skill-and-employment metric are then binding. The evidence that would settle direction: the interaction between the two records' prerequisites — a smart shelf with RFID gates (first record) is either staffed by the workshop's supervision (second record) or by none, and the safety records each answer names (interactive training versus industrial-tool protocol) are two documents for one bench. Until the owner rules, the two records compose only under one roof, and that roof is unbilled.
