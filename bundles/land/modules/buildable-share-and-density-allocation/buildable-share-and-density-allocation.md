# Buildable Share and Density Allocation

## Abstract
Every meter a settlement builds onto the ground is a meter of living soil it gives up, and every meter it builds upward is value whose new owner the society must name. This document holds one question — what fraction of a plot may be built on, and how are density and height allocated, under what rule, and to whose benefit? — and holds five recorded answers that are not interchangeable: an absolute 20% coverage lock ([Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule)), a guarantee of 25 square meters of open land per resident ([Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident)), the full repeal of height and floor-area-ratio caps conditional on those two locks ([Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation)), and the same 20/80 arithmetic turned into an orchard owner's reason to keep trees alive ([Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard)), and an enforcement machinery that binds no land at all: ex-post recognition — no pre-permits, satellite-verified territory claims, and suspension or non-recognition of any organization that fails the cap inside its own claimed territory ([Ex-post recognition enforcement: suspension or non-recognition of any organization that fails the coverage cap inside its own claimed territory (Ex-Post Recognition Enforcement)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#ex-post-recognition-enforcement-suspension-or-non-recognition-of-any-organization-that-fails-the-coverage-cap-inside-its-own-claimed-territory-ex-post-recognition-enforcement)). Two of these are guarantees of a single end, one is a deregulation that only survives because the guarantees exist, and one is a transitional instrument addressed to a specific class of owner. The disagreement they record is not about the number 20; it is about who promises what to whom — the society to the soil, the builder to the sky, the tower to the orchard — and no arithmetic among the five settles which guarantee the arbiter must keep when the others are relaxed; the fifth record asks no arithmetic at all, only what happens after the fact to whoever fails whichever number is chosen.

## Introduction

### Motivation
Separated into their own records, [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) and [Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation) look like a contradiction — one document commands a 20% ceiling as absolute and unalterable, the other commands unlimited height as repeal — and a reader who meets only one of them files an objection to half a contract. Read together, they are one bargain: the ground is locked precisely so the sky can be freed. The same entanglement runs through the quota ([Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident)) and the orchard deal ([Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard)): a coverage cap with no per-resident floor is satisfiable by paved plazas, and a green quota with no coverage cap has nothing to count as open. Keeping the first four answers in one place is not an editorial convenience; the dispute they record is between guarantees, not between answers and the question, and no single record contains the dispute.

## Explanation

### The problem the answers share
What fraction of a plot may be built on, and how are density and height allocated — under what rule, and to whose benefit? Coverage, density and height are one land decision seen from three angles: how much ground disappears under structure, how many people a given area carries, and who captures the value created above the plot. Each answer below is a proposal for settling this same question; the question itself favors none of them.

Sharpened, the shared problem is not "how much to build" but which variable each answer agrees to fix forever. [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) fixes a ratio (structure to land); [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident) fixes a quota (land to people). The two are reciprocals of each other and they bind different ways: the ratio is blind to headcount — a settlement of any population can stand on 20% coverage — while the quota is blind to footprint — a tower that breaks the per-resident floor breaks it at 20% coverage or at 2%. [Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation) removes the third conventional constraint, height itself, and wagers that the first two, being reciprocals, will bracket the system between them. [Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard) then tests whether an answer that binds every owner can also persuade the one owner whose asset is living trees rather than raw ground. Whoever adjudicates this requirement is therefore choosing not a number but which guarantee survives when the others are relaxed: fix the ratio and buy a headcount rule, fix the quota and buy a footprint rule, fix neither and the settlement is priced parcel by parcel in a commission — the flaw every answer names.

### Approaches

#### Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)
**Mechanism:** Fix a hard, absolute and unalterable ceiling: no more than 20 percent of the ground area may be occupied by any fixed building structure, and the cap applies uniformly across the entire physical territory of the society in question — village, town or large settlement alike. The remaining 80 percent of the ground may under no circumstances be taken by heavy infrastructure; it is reserved exclusively for open land, living soil, agriculture, gardening and orchards, public parks, or spontaneous natural habitat (forest). The rule's aim is not only ecological: this single sentence stands in place of the hundreds of pages of onerous, vague, rent-generating and self-contradictory regulations of master plans and detailed plans, and of the Article 5 and Article 100 commissions that administer them — dissolving, at the root, the sprawling bureaucracy of municipal administration.
**Metrics:** Maximum coverage by fixed building structures: 20% of total plot area (footprint <= 20%); minimum free, permeable ground: 80% of the land. The benchmark is the conventional fabric: nominally 25% of it goes to asphalted streets, 25% to public uses (which themselves carry at least 10% pure structural coverage), and the remaining 50% is left to owners — yet because plots are built out to 60% coverage, more than 85% of the ground is in practice rendered impermeable, sealed concrete.
**Prerequisites:** Consolidation of fragmented micro-plots into large parcels (preferably above 5 hectares); devolving the responsibility for providing all services onto the buildings themselves.
**Conventional-model flaw:** The conventional municipal model of subdivision and density grants 60 to 100 percent coverage in central areas and zero percent in the gardens — turning the settlement into a hot, impermeable, heat-trapping concrete slab while pushing the gardens toward destruction.
**Tags:** physical form, land use, building coverage, soil protection, microclimate, spatial justice
**Scale:** Macro — plain and settlement / mid — neighborhood
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | Imposing this rule on the fine-grained 200-to-500-square-meter plots of the conventional settlement, without megaplot consolidation, would yield vertical matchboxes on a 40-square-meter footprint, freeze construction, explode Article 100 violations and make housing horrifically expensive. The idea is compatible only with land above 5 hectares; in the existing fabric it becomes workable only in a reverse-renewal phase, after demand has drained away.
**Cognitive anchor:** The feel of living in "the old summer villa-gardens of Shemiran or the Qasr al-Dasht orchards of Shiraz" — a structure that breathes surrounded by ancient trees, leaving 80 percent of the ground open to nature.
**Narrative hook:** "One house among the trees, never trees among houses — the old villa-garden already kept a fifth of the ground for the building; we just write it down."

#### Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)
**Mechanism:** Guarantee, firmly, at least 25 square meters of dedicated, contiguous open and green space for every non-transient (permanent) resident of the built structures. The obligation is the complement of the 20% coverage ceiling: it blocks overloading a site with population and erecting dense towers with no environmental-quota backing. If a builder intends to increase the number of residents, they must enlarge the surrounding land area in proportion to this same coefficient, so that the balance among oxygen, soil breathing and population always stays at a stable level.
**Metrics:** Minimum open and green space per non-transient resident: 25 square meters — congruent with the 20-to-25-square-meter standard ceiling set by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP), which in this model is upgraded to a full 25 square meters of net open space because the conventional car streets are removed.
**Prerequisites:** A digital residency-registration platform and data-driven monitoring of each complex's biocapacity, so that the headcount always matches the area of the grounds.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Master plans set nominal, fictitious per-capita quotas that are overridden in practice by density sales in municipal commissions — ending in a severe poverty of real green space actually accessible to citizens.
**Tags:** environmental per-capita quota, population density, ecological resilience, mental health, UN standard
**Scale:** Mid — neighborhood and macro — settlement
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | In conventional settlements with densities beyond 400 people per hectare and saturated street networks, obliging municipalities to deliver 25 meters of net open space per capita — with no acquisition budget — ends in false statistics (counting street corner wedges and road refuge strips as green space) and in corrupt sham trading of quota allocations, destroying the credibility of environmental standards outright.
**Cognitive anchor:** A carefree walk on "the green campus of the University of Tehran" or in "Shiraz's Eram Garden" — where every citizen sees dozens of square meters of living soil and continuous vegetation underfoot.
**Narrative hook:** "Twenty-five square meters of real ground for every resident — measured in footsteps on soil, not in hectares on a plan sheet."
**Syn (synergies):** [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) — 20% coverage cap; [Optimal scaling of the spatial grain (minimum 5-hectare domain and a 2,000-to-50-thousand-person biological module)](settlement-grain-and-scaling.md#optimal-scaling-of-the-spatial-grain-minimum-5-hectare-domain-and-a-2000-to-50-thousand-person-biological-module) — 5-hectare grain; [Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard) — orchard preservation; on-site sewage treatment recycled back into the green space

#### Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation
**Mechanism:** Repeal outright the conventional height limits (such as a 50-meter ceiling) and the conventional floor-area-ratio caps (such as 500%). In the green-society model the builder may develop vertically to any height that engineering knowledge, material strength and economic viability allow — a 100-story residential tower, for instance. The release is safe because preemptive ecological locks stand in front of it — the "20% coverage cap", the "25-meter open-space floor per resident" and the "mandatory self-sufficiency in effluent treatment and service provision" — which together rule out every form of destructive density and let the optimal physical form emerge naturally rather than being decreed.
**Metrics:** Permitted height limit: unlimited (against 50 meters in the conventional detailed plan); permitted density limit: no rigid numeric ceiling (against the conventional 500%); design sample: a 100-story residential tower standing on a 25-story service base, for 50,000 people.
**Prerequisites:** Advanced structural knowledge of high-rise construction; service and mechanical shafts for central ventilation; deep load-bearing foundations.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Corrupt density-selling and unprincipled height ceilings imposed by municipalities, which shrink buildings into wide, short, blocked, short-lived volumes and destroy the natural land around the settlement horizontally, as sprawl.
**Tags:** unlimited density, free height, high-rise construction, hard deregulation, land efficiency
**Scale:** Micro — building / macro — settlement
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | Releasing height in the conventional settlement without the 20% coverage lock and the deep reactors would repeat the density-selling disaster of Tehran's District 22 — towers glued to one another, blocking the plain's wind flow and overflowing the sewage network. Free height is legitimate only atop 80 percent open ground.
**Cognitive anchor:** The startling prospect of "the slender modern towers of Singapore and Vancouver" — supertall structures that, instead of stealing the sky, rise up toward the light out of the middle of a dense forest park. The Iranian memory that licenses this prospect is the view every Teherani once owned from a rooftop: low slabs of masonry, and the Alborz standing behind all of them, nobody holding a permit to steal the mountains. Free height is not a skyline grab; it is the bargain in which towers take the air so the mountains — and the trees, and the ground — keep their line of sight.
**Narrative hook:** "Build to whatever height engineering allows — that is the payment for never taking a second meter of ground."
**Syn (synergies):** [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) — 20% coverage cap; [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident) — 25-meter quota; [Optimal scaling of the spatial grain (minimum 5-hectare domain and a 2,000-to-50-thousand-person biological module)](settlement-grain-and-scaling.md#optimal-scaling-of-the-spatial-grain-minimum-5-hectare-domain-and-a-2000-to-50-thousand-person-biological-module) — 5-hectare grain; [Computed free distance between buildings in the aerial view, for wind-draught flow and radiation capture](airflow-through-fabric.md#computed-free-distance-between-buildings-in-the-aerial-view-for-wind-draught-flow-and-radiation-capture) — open corridors between buildings

#### Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)
**Mechanism:** End the destructive, anti-ecological paradox of conventional development plans, which fix orchard coverage at zero percent. In the conventional model the orchard owner is forced to bear heavy water, pesticide and labor costs with no financial return, so the economics actively reward deliberately drying out the trees, burning them, or letting them sicken — until the land is reclassified as barren wasteland and becomes buildable for housing! The green model instead allows the owner to raise a low-footprint tower in height on 20 percent of the orchard's ground, on the condition that the remaining 80 percent is kept alive, monitored, and equipped with sustainable irrigation in perpetuity. The tower's financial value is thereby bound to the life and freshness of that living 80 percent of orchard, and the conflict of interest is removed completely.
**Metrics:** Coverage on orchard land: at most 20%; floor for guaranteed living orchard: at least 80%; target outcome: zero case-files of deliberate orchard destruction and fire in the Shiraz plain.
**Prerequisites:** Issuance of a digital identity document for each tree, on a blockchain / urban-system platform, and continuous monitoring of the vegetation canopy through satellite imagery.
**Conventional-model flaw:** The dry bureaucratic thinking of the detailed plans: locking orchards at 0% while granting construction permits to the adjacent parcels has manufactured the economic incentive to destroy the breathing lungs of the settlement.
**Tags:** orchard economics, conflict-of-interest removal, Qasr al-Dasht gardens, ecological resilience, wind corridors
**Scale:** Macro — regional and mid — neighborhood
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional instrument] | The most salvaging instrument available for the Qasr al-Dasht gardens of Shiraz or the Shahriar gardens of Tehran. Its deadly risk arises when it is implemented without a satellite monitoring system: speculators would build their tower on the 20 percent, then set fire to the rest of the orchard to clear it for later phases, destroying it.
**Cognitive anchor:** The grandeur of "the historic palace-gardens of Shemiran and the Afif-Abad orchards of Shiraz" — living above a canopy of ancient trees without a single branch having been cut to build the house.
**Narrative hook:** "The tower now defends the orchard — every year the trees live, the tower is worth more; every fire costs the owner his address."
**Syn (synergies):** [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) — the 20/80 rule; [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident) — 25-meter green-space quota; MBR treatment of the tower's greywater to sustainably supply that same orchard's irrigation; stabilization of Shiraz's cool breeze

#### Ex-post recognition enforcement: suspension or non-recognition of any organization that fails the coverage cap inside its own claimed territory (Ex-Post Recognition Enforcement)
**Mechanism:** Enforce the coverage law with no pre-permit at all — «نظارت پسینی نه پیشینی», ex-post supervision rather than ex-ante: the enforcement guarantee of the society's written laws is never located in the issuance of permits. An organization's claim to a geographic territory is accepted only against submitted data on its built structures; the claimed coverage is then re-checked from satellite maps at fixed intervals, such as monthly. The guarantee for the 20% cap is the organization itself: «تعلیق یا عدم پذیرش سازمانی که در قلمروی جغرافیایی مورد ادعای خود، حداکثر سطح اشغال را رعایت نکرده باشد» — suspension, or non-recognition, of any organization that has not observed the maximum coverage share within the geographic territory of its own claim. No inspectorate is created and no permission is granted in advance; the society simply refuses, after the fact, to accept the offending organization's territorial claim or continued standing.
**Metrics:** Coverage ceiling verified per claimed territory: 20% (with the paired floor of 25 square meters of open and green space per non-transient resident); verification cycle: satellite comparison of claimed structures at fixed intervals (e.g., monthly); pre-permits issued: zero; sanction: suspension or non-recognition of the offending organization.
**Prerequisites:** A registry that recognizes organizations and their claimed geographic territories (the constitution's territory-as-legal-person geography); submission of built-structure data as the condition of accepting any territory claim; satellite imagery at coverage-readable resolution.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Pre-permit supervision: the take-and-give bureaucracy of master plans and their commissions — hundreds of pages of onerous, vague, rent-generating, self-contradictory regulation whose enforcement collapses wherever the authority cannot watch, and whose permits the owner's law explicitly refuses to use as its guarantee.
**Tags:** ex-post enforcement, satellite verification, territory recognition, suspension as sanction, permit abolition
**Scale:** Macro — institutional
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional] | The mechanism presupposes an order of "organizations with claimed territories"; where the existing fabric grants titles to micro-plot owners rather than claimable organizations, there is no organization to suspend, and the cap falls back onto the licensing apparatus this answer abolishes. It is force-fit-resistant precisely where plot fragmentation survives.
**Cognitive anchor:** The traffic camera — nobody asks permission to drive; the photograph arrives afterward, and the picture is the whole of the enforcement.
**Narrative hook:** "Build first and be believed later — the territory you claim is yours only as long as the satellite keeps finding your buildings inside twenty percent of it."
**Syn (synergies):** [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) — the cap this answer enforces; [Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard) — the same satellite watch, extended from trees to all structures; [The constitutional sanctions suite: suspension of offending organs and non-enforcement of offending transactions (Sanctions Suite)](../governance/government-organs.md#the-constitutional-sanctions-suite-suspension-of-offending-organs-and-non-enforcement-of-offending-transactions-sanctions-suite) — non-recognition as the society's universal ضمانت اجرا; [A private municipality as overseer, regulator and non-provider (Private Municipal Regulator)](../governance/government-organs.md#a-private-municipality-as-overseer-regulator-and-non-provider-private-municipal-regulator) — the monitoring-only regulator this enforcement frees

### How the approaches collide and compose
**[Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule)'s absolute 20% and [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident)'s 25 m²/head are one promise in two currencies — and they bind different things.** The coverage cap constrains footprint regardless of population; the per-resident quota constrains population regardless of footprint. When density rises, it is the quota that bites: on the megaplot [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) prefers, 5 hectares of land yield 40,000 m² of open ground at 80%, which at 25 m² per permanent resident supports 1,600 residents and no more — the tower may stand on 20% of the plot and still cannot house a single additional person without purchasing land, which is exactly the mechanism [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident)'s mechanism text demands. The reverse check is sharper: [Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation)'s worked sample of a tower settlement for 50,000 people implies at least 1,250,000 m² of guaranteed open ground (50,000 × 25), and since the 20% lock forces the open share to stay at least four times the footprint, the parcel behind that sample must exceed roughly 150 hectares. The sample satisfies the two locks only because it is a settlement, not a building. Adjudication consequence: [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident) is the population ceiling, [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) the footprint ceiling, and neither is redundant — a cap without the quota lets a settlement of towers pile population onto "open" ground that is really plaza; a quota without the cap lets the open ground be re-sealed under pilotis, podiums and parking until the 80% exists only on paper.

**[Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation)'s release is licensed by [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule)'s lock, not opposed to it — but the license is narrower than the release text claims.** The deregulators' case is not that height does not matter but that height's *ground* dangers — sealed soil, destroyed gardens — have all been priced out in advance: once coverage is absolute at 20%, no amount of vertical ambition can consume another meter of soil, because the ground the tower makes bearable is the same ground the tower is barred from taking. The release mechanism leans further than its locks can carry, however: it names *service load* among the dangers the ecological locks price out, and neither recorded lock prices it. The 20% cap governs footprint; the 25 m² quota governs headcount per open land; nothing in either bounds what 50,000 people concentrated at one mast discharge, draw, and move through the base of the tower — the release's third stated precondition, "mandatory self-sufficiency in effluent treatment and service provision," is invoked as a lock and recorded nowhere in this document. Look the other way and the pairing dissolves into the two failures the records already name: deregulation without the cap is District 22 (towers glued together, wind blocked, sewers overflowing — [Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation)'s own Fit critique), while the cap without any release strands the value the lock protects and teaches the owner to build wide — the horizontal destruction of the periphery that [Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation)'s flaw text indicts. Height is therefore not a rival answer to the 20/80 rule; it is the dividend the 20/80 rule pays to whoever agrees to keep the 80% — and the question the next section refuses to beg is whether the dividend also owes a third lock that nobody here has written down.

**The sharpest unresolved collision in the ledger is not cap against quota but release against its own unnamed condition, and it closes on one decision with two answers.** Is the height release adjudicable only once the flow lock is recorded as a number — a peak effluent, water, energy and vertical-movement load per parcel that the tower must not exceed whatever its footprint does — or do the two ground locks plus the building's own engineering (deep reactors, service shafts, deep foundations, all already in the prerequisites) license the release as written? The first answer argues from the document's own precedent: the release's Fit indicts District 22 precisely because towers were freed without a service lock, and "self-sufficiency" with no ceiling to verify against is the same unenforced promise that made conventional density sales corruptible — an ecological lock that cannot be audited is a permit, not a lock. The second answer argues from cost curves: if on-site treatment genuinely holds at tower density, then engineering *is* the lock, and a new numeric ceiling would re-create the very commission bureaucracy the 20/80 rule exists to dissolve. The evidence that would settle the direction is the effluent-per-capita-versus-height data the micro-MBR record's scale claims can supply, checked against the release's 100-story, 50,000-person sample; until it exists, the release is a conditional whose condition does not live in this document, and any combined verdict must say which document will host the third lock rather than assume the towers will simply hold their own waste.
