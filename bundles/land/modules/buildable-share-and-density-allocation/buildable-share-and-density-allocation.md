# Buildable Share and Density Allocation

## Abstract
Every meter a settlement builds onto the ground is a meter of living soil it gives up, and every meter it builds upward is value whose new owner the society must name. This document holds one question — what fraction of a plot may be built on, and how are density and height allocated, under what rule, and to whose benefit? — and holds five recorded answers that are not interchangeable: an absolute 20% coverage lock ([Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule)), a guarantee of 25 square meters of open land per resident ([Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident)), the full repeal of height and floor-area-ratio caps conditional on those two locks ([Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation)), and the same 20/80 arithmetic turned into an orchard owner's reason to keep trees alive ([Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard)), and an enforcement machinery that binds no land at all: ex-post recognition — no pre-permits, satellite-verified territory claims, and suspension or non-recognition of any organization that fails the cap inside its own claimed territory ([Ex-post recognition enforcement: suspension or non-recognition of any organization that fails the coverage cap inside its own claimed territory (Ex-Post Recognition Enforcement)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#ex-post-recognition-enforcement-suspension-or-non-recognition-of-any-organization-that-fails-the-coverage-cap-inside-its-own-claimed-territory-ex-post-recognition-enforcement)). Two of these are guarantees of a single end, one is a deregulation that only survives because the guarantees exist, and one is a transitional instrument addressed to a specific class of owner. The disagreement they record is not about the number 20; it is about who promises what to whom — the society to the soil, the builder to the sky, the tower to the orchard. The 20% cap and the 25 m² quota are complementary locks, not rivals: the cap limits building footprint; the quota limits population by counting the whole site as open, including the ground floor under buildings, so one hectare holds at most 400 permanent residents. The general 80% is open space, not a tree-planting mandate — a rocky mountain plot may carry almost no canopy and still satisfy it. The orchard record is a separate bargain for orchard land (living, irrigated canopy as collateral), not an extension of the general law. The 20% figure is a ceiling on a developed, joined site, not a duty to put structure on 20% of all land. The cap binds organisations that join the society's governing organisation, and joining requires the intended built structure already in place — existing micro-plot fabric is not annexed as-is. The 20% is a Geniuses.Group proposal for this society's law, amendable like other laws, not an unalterable clause; another governing group may choose other numbers. Height release does not wait on a third numeric effluent ceiling: the owning organisation of the building provides services and carries the liability; the governing organisation does not provide them, and self-sufficiency is not a mandate to sell every service to the resident. Open ground of a joined settlement is wall-less and commons-like for movement; entering a building still requires identity; an organisation may monitor approach to its open ground and apply its rules. Permanent residence is not a few days and is not a dwelling-size test (a tube dwelling can be permanent); borderline abuse is for enforcement, fairness and the society's ethics, not a tight statutory headcount. What remains open is the document's own voice.

## Introduction

### Motivation
Separated into their own records, [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) and [Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation) look like a contradiction — one document commands a 20% ceiling as absolute and unalterable, the other commands unlimited height as repeal — and a reader who meets only one of them files an objection to half a contract. Read together, they are one bargain: the ground is locked precisely so the sky can be freed. The same entanglement runs through the quota ([Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident)) and the orchard deal ([Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard)): a coverage cap with no per-resident floor is satisfiable by paved plazas, and a green quota with no coverage cap has nothing to count as open. Keeping the first four answers in one place is not an editorial convenience; the dispute they record is between guarantees, not between answers and the question, and no single record contains the dispute.

## Explanation

### The problem the answers share
What fraction of a plot may be built on, and how are density and height allocated — under what rule, and to whose benefit? Coverage, density and height are one land decision seen from three angles: how much ground disappears under structure, how many people a given area carries, and who captures the value created above the plot. Each answer below is a proposal for settling this same question; the question itself favors none of them.

Sharpened, the shared problem is not "how much to build" but which variable each answer agrees to fix forever. [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) fixes a ratio (structure to land); [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident) fixes a quota (land to people). The two are reciprocals of each other and they bind different ways: the ratio is blind to headcount — a settlement of any population can stand on 20% coverage — while the quota is blind to footprint — a tower that breaks the per-resident floor breaks it at 20% coverage or at 2%. [Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation) removes the third conventional constraint, height itself, and wagers that the first two, being reciprocals, will bracket the system between them. [Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard) then tests whether an answer that binds every owner can also persuade the one owner whose asset is living trees rather than raw ground. Whoever adjudicates this requirement is therefore choosing not a number but which guarantee survives when the others are relaxed: fix the ratio and buy a headcount rule, fix the quota and buy a footprint rule, fix neither and the settlement is priced parcel by parcel in a commission — the flaw every answer names.

### Approaches

#### Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)
**Mechanism:** Fix a hard ceiling: no more than 20 percent of the ground area may be occupied by any fixed building structure. The ceiling is a society law like other laws, not an unamendable lock — another governing organisation may write a different number. The cap binds every organisation that holds membership in the society's governing organisation; membership requires the intended built structure already in place and registered. A fine-grained building that joins without that rebuild brings its defects with it and is not admitted as-is. The remaining 80 percent of the ground may under no circumstances be taken by heavy infrastructure; it is reserved exclusively for open land — living soil, agriculture, gardening and orchards, public parks, spontaneous habitat, or unplanted ground such as rock — not a duty to install a forest. The 20% figure is a ceiling on a developed, joined site, not a quota to occupy a fifth of all land. The rule's aim is not only ecological: this single sentence stands in place of the hundreds of pages of onerous, vague, rent-generating and self-contradictory regulations of master plans and detailed plans, and of the Article 5 and Article 100 commissions that administer them — dissolving, at the root, the sprawling bureaucracy of municipal administration.
**Metrics:** Maximum coverage by fixed building structures: 20% of total plot area (footprint <= 20%); minimum free, permeable ground: 80% of the land. The benchmark is the conventional fabric: nominally 25% of it goes to asphalted streets, 25% to public uses (which themselves carry at least 10% pure structural coverage), and the remaining 50% is left to owners — yet because plots are built out to 60% coverage, more than 85% of the ground is in practice rendered impermeable, sealed concrete.
**Prerequisites:** Consolidation of fragmented micro-plots into large parcels (preferably above 5 hectares); devolving the responsibility for providing all services onto the buildings themselves.
**Conventional-model flaw:** The conventional municipal model of subdivision and density grants 60 to 100 percent coverage in central areas and zero percent in the gardens — turning the settlement into a hot, impermeable, heat-trapping concrete slab while pushing the gardens toward destruction.
**Tags:** physical form, land use, building coverage, soil protection, microclimate, spatial justice
**Scale:** Macro — plain and settlement / mid — neighborhood
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional] | The cap is a joining condition, not a blanket overlay on existing micro-plot fabric. Imposing it on 200-to-500-square-meter plots without rebuild or consolidation would yield vertical matchboxes on a 40-square-meter footprint, freeze construction, explode Article 100 violations and make housing horrifically expensive. An existing building enters the governing organisation only after it is built to the intended structure and registered; reverse renewal may create that substrate, but it does not annex the old plots as members.
**Cognitive anchor:** The feel of living in "the old summer villa-gardens of Shemiran or the Qasr al-Dasht orchards of Shiraz" — a structure that breathes surrounded by ancient trees, leaving 80 percent of the ground open to nature.
**Narrative hook:** "One house among the trees, never trees among houses — the old villa-garden already kept a fifth of the ground for the building; we just write it down."

#### Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)
**Mechanism:** Guarantee, firmly, at least 25 square meters of dedicated, contiguous open space for every non-transient (permanent) resident of the built structures. Permanent is not a few days and is not a test of dwelling size — a tube dwelling can be permanent residence. Borderline abuse is for the enforcement mechanism, fairness, and the society's ethics, not a tight statutory nights-per-year rule. Open space is not a tree-planting mandate: rock, sparse cover, and living soil all count; a mountain plot may carry almost no canopy. Because the ground floor under buildings is itself open, the quota counts the whole plot, not only the uncovered 80 percent: one hectare holds at most 400 permanent residents (10,000 / 25). The 20% coverage ceiling independently limits building footprint; without the quota a one-hectare site could stack any number of people on that fifth. If a builder intends to increase the number of residents, they must enlarge the surrounding land area in proportion to this same coefficient, so that the balance among oxygen, soil breathing and population always stays at a stable level.
**Metrics:** Minimum open and green space per non-transient resident: 25 square meters — congruent with the 20-to-25-square-meter standard ceiling set by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP), which in this model is upgraded to a full 25 square meters of net open space because the conventional car streets are removed.
**Prerequisites:** A digital residency-registration platform and data-driven monitoring of each complex's biocapacity, so that the headcount always matches the area of the grounds.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Master plans set nominal, fictitious per-capita quotas that are overridden in practice by density sales in municipal commissions — ending in a severe poverty of real green space actually accessible to citizens.
**Tags:** environmental per-capita quota, population density, ecological resilience, mental health, UN standard
**Scale:** Mid — neighborhood and macro — settlement
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | In conventional settlements with densities beyond 400 people per hectare and saturated street networks, obliging municipalities to deliver 25 meters of net open space per capita — with no acquisition budget — ends in false statistics (counting street corner wedges and road refuge strips as green space) and in corrupt sham trading of quota allocations, destroying the credibility of environmental standards outright.
**Cognitive anchor:** A carefree walk on "the green campus of the University of Tehran" or in "Shiraz's Eram Garden" — where every citizen sees dozens of square meters of living soil and continuous vegetation underfoot.
**Narrative hook:** "Twenty-five square meters of real ground for every resident — measured in footsteps on soil, not in hectares on a plan sheet."

#### Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation
**Mechanism:** Repeal outright the conventional height limits (such as a 50-meter ceiling) and the conventional floor-area-ratio caps (such as 500%). In the green-society model the builder may develop vertically to any height that engineering knowledge, material strength and economic viability allow — a 100-story residential tower, for instance. The release is safe because two ground locks stand in front of it — the "20% coverage cap" and the "25-meter open-space floor per resident" — and because the owning organisation of the building, not the society's governing organisation, must provide services (including effluent) and carries the liability when it fails. Self-sufficiency here is that assignment of duty; it is not a mandate to sell every service to the resident, and it is not a third numeric ceiling. Together they rule out every form of destructive density and let the optimal physical form emerge naturally rather than being decreed.
**Metrics:** Permitted height limit: unlimited (against 50 meters in the conventional detailed plan); permitted density limit: no rigid numeric ceiling (against the conventional 500%); design sample: a 100-story residential tower standing on a 25-story service base, for 50,000 people.
**Prerequisites:** Advanced structural knowledge of high-rise construction; service and mechanical shafts for central ventilation; deep load-bearing foundations.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Corrupt density-selling and unprincipled height ceilings imposed by municipalities, which shrink buildings into wide, short, blocked, short-lived volumes and destroy the natural land around the settlement horizontally, as sprawl.
**Tags:** unlimited density, free height, high-rise construction, hard deregulation, land efficiency
**Scale:** Micro — building / macro — settlement
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | Releasing height in the conventional settlement without the 20% coverage lock and the deep reactors would repeat the density-selling disaster of Tehran's District 22 — towers glued to one another, blocking the plain's wind flow and overflowing the sewage network. Free height is legitimate only atop 80 percent open ground.
**Cognitive anchor:** The startling prospect of "the slender modern towers of Singapore and Vancouver" — supertall structures that, instead of stealing the sky, rise up toward the light out of the middle of a dense forest park. The Iranian memory that licenses this prospect is the view every Teherani once owned from a rooftop: low slabs of masonry, and the Alborz standing behind all of them, nobody holding a permit to steal the mountains. Free height is not a skyline grab; it is the bargain in which towers take the air so the mountains — and the trees, and the ground — keep their line of sight.
**Narrative hook:** "Build to whatever height engineering allows — that is the payment for never taking a second meter of ground."

#### Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)
**Mechanism:** End the destructive, anti-ecological paradox of conventional development plans, which fix orchard coverage at zero percent. In the conventional model the orchard owner is forced to bear heavy water, pesticide and labor costs with no financial return, so the economics actively reward deliberately drying out the trees, burning them, or letting them sicken — until the land is reclassified as barren wasteland and becomes buildable for housing! The green model instead allows the owner to raise a low-footprint tower in height on 20 percent of the orchard's ground, on the condition that the remaining 80 percent is kept alive, monitored, and equipped with sustainable irrigation in perpetuity. The tower's financial value is thereby bound to the life and freshness of that living 80 percent of orchard, and the conflict of interest is removed completely.
**Metrics:** Coverage on orchard land: at most 20%; floor for guaranteed living orchard: at least 80%; target outcome: zero case-files of deliberate orchard destruction and fire in the Shiraz plain.
**Prerequisites:** Issuance of a digital identity document for each tree, on a blockchain / urban-system platform, and continuous monitoring of the vegetation canopy through satellite imagery.
**Conventional-model flaw:** The dry bureaucratic thinking of the detailed plans: locking orchards at 0% while granting construction permits to the adjacent parcels has manufactured the economic incentive to destroy the breathing lungs of the settlement.
**Tags:** orchard economics, conflict-of-interest removal, Qasr al-Dasht gardens, ecological resilience, wind corridors
**Scale:** Macro — regional and mid — neighborhood
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional instrument] | The most salvaging instrument available for the Qasr al-Dasht gardens of Shiraz or the Shahriar gardens of Tehran. Its deadly risk arises when it is implemented without a satellite monitoring system: speculators would build their tower on the 20 percent, then set fire to the rest of the orchard to clear it for later phases, destroying it.
**Cognitive anchor:** The grandeur of "the historic palace-gardens of Shemiran and the Afif-Abad orchards of Shiraz" — living above a canopy of ancient trees without a single branch having been cut to build the house.
**Narrative hook:** "The tower now defends the orchard — every year the trees live, the tower is worth more; every fire costs the owner his address."

#### Ex-post recognition enforcement: suspension or non-recognition of any organization that fails the coverage cap inside its own claimed territory (Ex-Post Recognition Enforcement)
**Mechanism:** Enforce the coverage law with no pre-permit at all — «نظارت پسینی نه پیشینی», ex-post supervision rather than ex-ante: the enforcement guarantee of the society's written laws is never located in the issuance of permits. An organization's claim to a geographic territory is accepted only against submitted data on its built structures; the claimed coverage is then re-checked from satellite maps at fixed intervals, such as monthly. The guarantee for the 20% cap is the organization itself: «تعلیق یا عدم پذیرش سازمانی که در قلمروی جغرافیایی مورد ادعای خود، حداکثر سطح اشغال را رعایت نکرده باشد» — suspension, or non-recognition, of any organization that has not observed the maximum coverage share within the geographic territory of its own claim. No inspectorate is created and no permission is granted in advance; the society simply refuses, after the fact, to accept the offending organization's territorial claim or continued standing.
**Metrics:** Coverage ceiling verified per claimed territory: 20% (with the paired floor of 25 square meters of open and green space per non-transient resident); verification cycle: satellite comparison of claimed structures at fixed intervals (e.g., monthly); pre-permits issued: zero; sanction: suspension or non-recognition of the offending organization.
**Prerequisites:** A registry that recognizes organizations and their claimed geographic territories (the constitution's territory-as-legal-person geography); submission of built-structure data as the condition of accepting any territory claim; satellite imagery at coverage-readable resolution.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Pre-permit supervision: the take-and-give bureaucracy of master plans and their commissions — hundreds of pages of onerous, vague, rent-generating, self-contradictory regulation whose enforcement collapses wherever the authority cannot watch, and whose permits the owner's law explicitly refuses to use as its guarantee.
**Tags:** ex-post enforcement, satellite verification, territory recognition, suspension as sanction, permit abolition
**Scale:** Macro — institutional
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional] | The mechanism presupposes an order of "organizations with claimed territories"; where the existing fabric grants titles to micro-plot owners rather than claimable organizations, there is no organization to suspend, and the cap falls back onto the licensing apparatus this answer abolishes. It is force-fit-resistant precisely where plot fragmentation survives.
**Cognitive anchor:** The traffic camera — nobody asks permission to drive; the photograph arrives afterward, and the picture is the whole of the enforcement.
**Narrative hook:** "Build first and be believed later — the territory you claim is yours only as long as the satellite keeps finding your buildings inside twenty percent of it."

### How the approaches collide and compose
**[Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule)'s absolute 20% and [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident)'s 25 m²/head are complementary locks on different quantities.** The coverage cap constrains footprint regardless of population; the per-resident quota constrains population regardless of footprint, and it counts the whole site because the ground under buildings is itself open. On one hectare the quota therefore admits 400 permanent residents (10,000 / 25), not 320 from an 80-percent-only count; the cap still forbids more than 2,000 m² of building footprint on that same hectare. The general 80% is open space, not planted canopy: a rocky plot may satisfy it with almost no trees. The 20% is a ceiling on a developed, joined site, not a duty to put structure on 20% of the planet. Without the quota, the 20% lock would let a tower stack any number of people on that fifth. On five hectares the quota admits 2,000 residents, not the 1,600 the collision previously derived from counting only the uncovered 80%. The reverse check: a 50,000-person sample needs 1,250,000 m² of site (125 hectares) under whole-site counting; the older 150-hectare figure assumed open ground was only 80% of the parcel. The cap without the quota lets a settlement of towers pile population onto "open" ground that is really plaza; the quota without the cap lets the open ground be re-sealed under podiums until the 80% exists only on paper. The cap's territorial sentence is membership-gated: it binds organisations that join the governing organisation with the intended built structure already in place, not existing micro-plots annexed as-is. The word "unalterable" in the migrated proposal is not current doctrine: this is a Geniuses.Group law-proposal, amendable like other laws.

**The joined settlement's open ground is commons-like for movement, not a right to enter buildings.** Wall-free ground of the town or village developed this way is walked without plot walls. Entering any building still requires identity. Approach to a complex's open ground may be monitored by that organisation; its rules apply; a guard — human, robot, camera — may demand identity. Security scales with settlement size. Counted orchard canopy on that ground is therefore not a secret private park, and not an ungated building.

**[Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation)'s release is licensed by [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule)'s lock, not opposed to it.** The deregulators' case is not that height does not matter but that height's *ground* dangers — sealed soil, destroyed gardens — have all been priced out in advance: once coverage is absolute at 20%, no amount of vertical ambition can consume another meter of soil. The third phrase in the release — "mandatory self-sufficiency in effluent treatment and service provision" — is not a numeric peak-load lock and not a captive-sale mandate. It is the assignment of duty: the owning organisation of the building provides the services and is liable for the harm (an absorption well that pollutes groundwater is that organisation's case to answer); the governing organisation of the society provides no such service. Transparent answers the owning organisation must give make cheap dumping a future liability, which is the deterrent. Height is the dividend the 20/80 rule pays to whoever agrees to keep the 80%. Look the other way and the pairing dissolves into the two failures the records already name: deregulation without the cap is District 22; the cap without any release strands the value the lock protects and teaches the owner to build wide.

**[Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard)'s reused 20/80: two decisions, not one principle.** Same number, different logic. [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule)'s rule is a prohibition addressed to everyone — the 80% is use-neutral open space, and a rocky mountain plot with almost no trees still satisfies it. [Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard)'s rule is a purchase addressed to orchard land — the 80% is a specific, monitored, irrigated, productive canopy, and the tower's value is collateralized on that canopy staying alive. The general law does not import the orchard's planting and irrigation instruments. Counted open space, including orchard ground inside a joined settlement, sits on the wall-less commons of that settlement: free movement at ground, identity required at the building door, monitoring of approach allowed.

**As rules they collide; as a timeline they compose.** Only [Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard) can operate on the fabric that exists today — it pays current orchard owners on their current plots, while [Hard 20% cap on the buildable coverage of the developed domain (the golden 20/80 rule)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#hard-20-cap-on-the-buildable-coverage-of-the-developed-domain-the-golden-2080-rule) and [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident) wait on prerequisites (megaplot consolidation above 5 hectares, a residency-registration platform) that are themselves programs of years, and [Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation) should be adjudicated only once the two locks actually bind someone. Sequenced that way, [Redefining the orchards' spatial justice (replacing the ban with the formula of 20% built in height and 80% living orchard)](buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#redefining-the-orchards-spatial-justice-replacing-the-ban-with-the-formula-of-20-built-in-height-and-80-living-orchard) is the instrument that keeps the future settlement's 80% alive during the wait, and the trio arrives into orchards that were preserved rather than burned — the exact transfer the four Fit critiques, read as a set, imply and no single record states.
