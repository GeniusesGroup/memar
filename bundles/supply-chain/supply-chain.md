# Supply chain
A [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain) consists of the group of people, vendors, materials, and activities involved in the creation, production, and distribution of a product. Supply means the activity of supplying or providing something.

## Supply chain and value chain are distinct systems

A [value chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_chain) looks at all the steps involved in bringing a product from the ideation phase to a sellable item, and it seeks ways to bolster the value to the customer.

The supply chain and the value chain are two distinct open systems that influence each other. Neither is a subset of the other, and the relation between them is not set inclusion. Each carries activities the other does not: the supply chain carries the physical flow of goods — extraction of raw materials, production of parts, assembly, transport, and warehousing — while the value chain also carries design, research and development, marketing, sales, and after-sales service. A term borrowed from mathematics is not used to describe a relation outside mathematics; *subset* used this way repeats the error that lets *energy* stand for claims physics does not make.

## Traceability

[Traceability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traceability) is the capability and ability to trace something as it moves through a process. In product development, it refers to the ability to track and trace requirements to artifacts, test runs, and anything else in the product lifecycle.
