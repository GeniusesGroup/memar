# Khayyam Rules (`bundles/khayyam/rules/`)

## What this folder is
The general Memar concept of Rule — ownership, `rules/` beside `protocol/`, linters, and rule engines — is defined in [`docs/rule.md`](../../../docs/rule.md). This folder is **one catalog** of Rules for Khayyam work in this repository.

These rules are introduced by Geniuses.Group for work in Khayyam; they are not part of the Khayyam language, and another organization may define and enforce its own rule set for the same language.

Khayyam's rules: conditions this repository's toolchain enforces or its sources are held to, each with its subject and with whether it gates compilation, reports governance, or rewrites source. **What this folder holds is work on Khayyam-shaped sources in this repository, and nothing else: of the 25 Rules indexed below, one is the language's own, nine are readings or choices this repository's toolchain makes, and fifteen are this repository's conventions.** A Rule about anything else — a Rule about text another tool left in a Khayyam file, for instance — is filed by the module that owns that subject, and the table below is not where it waits. A rule's normative claim — what the language means — stays in the document that owns its subject under [`docs/khayyam/`](../../../docs/khayyam/); this folder holds the executable form and cites that claim. The members a rule declares are the rule model's, and the model has not fixed them: the record is the [toolchain handoff → *Rule model: one authored rule, two request paths, and a compile-time gate*](../khayyam.handoff.md), which enumerates identity, subject, condition, consumes, produces, enforcement and scope and states that every one of them is provisional, its **Rule** and **rule engine** protocol work being unfinished. Until that work settles them, this index claims none of them: what a rule document states is the four things [below](#what-a-rule-document-states), and a member the model has not fixed is not among them. Nothing here is final.

Each rule has its own folder, holding a main document named after the rule, a handoff beside it carrying that rule's open questions, and this pointer README. The folder listing below is the index; the documents are not restated here. The one sibling catalog in this repository is [`targets/go/rules/`](../targets/go/rules/README.md), which holds the conditions on what the Go → Khayyam port left in these sources.

Observer and mutator abstractions (`Observer_X`, `Mutator_X`) are governed by the [ADT observer and mutator rule](../../../modules/computer/adt/rules/observer-mutator/observer-mutator.md), not Khayyam grammar.

## This set is a floor, not a ceiling
The rules stated here are the ones this repository holds itself to. The set is explicitly open to extension, in both directions:

- **A rule this repository has not thought of is not thereby wrong.** Bring it — an issue or a pull request is the way in, and Memar is receptive to good rules.
- **A rule proposed here and not accepted is not thereby wrong either.** Acceptance answers whether the rule fits *this* repository, not whether it is valid. An unaccepted rule may still be the right rule for the project that proposed it.
- **You may keep that rule in your own organization's repository and enforce it in your own code.** That is a supported outcome, not a workaround: the same subject, a different owner, a different rule set. What belongs here is the rules worth sharing — those whose subject is the language itself, and whose absence would change what Khayyam programs may mean for everyone.

## Why it works this way
This is the point of Khayyam and of the architecture around it: nothing is simply right or wrong, and a claim is answered where it belongs rather than forced down to the lowest abstraction. A rule that is right for a codebase is not thereby a claim about every codebase, and a rule that belongs to a project is not thereby a defect in the language.

The base documents under [`docs/`](../../../docs/) do the other half of that work: they state what is certain about the language, and they do not carry prohibitions. A condition whose handling may differ between one tool and the next is stated by the tool that applies it, which is why a rule that used to sit in a language document — the comment forms, the `sc` placement limit, the shape of an import path — is a folder here instead, and why the language document's own statement of the matter is a positive principle rather than a list of things the grammar refuses.

## Membership criterion
A rule belongs here when all of the following hold:
- its subject is Khayyam itself — the language or a contract Khayyam owns;
- it is executable as a condition over analysis input, not a statement of intent;
- it cites the document that states its normative claim;
- it states its enforcement class (gates compilation, reports governance, or rewrites source) and its scope (a unit or a repository).

A rule about your own project's conventions does not belong here — it belongs to your project, and saying so is a legitimate answer, not a rejection. A rule about how this repository's *documentation* is written does not belong here either: its subject is the documentation, not Khayyam, and it is carried as a decision in the [toolchain handoff](../khayyam.handoff.md) instead.

Where a rule whose subject is not the language does belong is fixed by [rule.md → Which Module a Rule Belongs To](../../../docs/rule.md#which-module-a-rule-belongs-to): in the module that owns its subject, which for this repository means a module of its own rather than a row in this index. The standing cases are [`modules/computer/adt/rules/`](../../../modules/computer/adt/rules/README.md), which took the observer and mutator rule out of this catalog, [`modules/process/test/rules/`](../../../modules/process/test/rules/README.md) and [`modules/process/control-flow/rules/`](../../../modules/process/control-flow/rules/README.md), which hold the conditions their own protocols' subjects carry, and [`targets/go/rules/`](../targets/go/rules/README.md), which took the three Rules about the Go port's residue. This catalog is the Khayyam module's rules, not the repository's rule store: a rule about the language joins it, and a rule about anything else is not thereby improved by joining it.

## What a rule document states
Each rule's document says four things: what the rule is, why it exists, whether an organization may keep it or drop it, and what it does not claim. The last of these is load-bearing rather than a formality — it is what keeps a rule from reading as a language guarantee, and it is where a second toolchain's freedom to differ is written down.

## Which rules here are the language's own
The owner's question of 2026-10-03 was whether all rules relating to Khayyam belong in the language's rules folder, and the answer given was a criterion rather than a move in either direction ([`docs/rule.changelog.md`](../../../docs/rule.changelog.md):73-80). [Rule → Which Module a Rule Belongs To](../../../docs/rule.md#which-module-a-rule-belongs-to) states it as one sentence with two halves, and this section applies it rule by rule so that a reader can see which half decided each verdict.

**The test, and the half that decides.** A Rule belongs to a language exactly when no program satisfies every Rule of that language's catalog and still violates this one — that is, when turning the condition off would change what a program means rather than how well a codebase is kept. The second half is the one that decides, and every verdict below is that half applied. Read literally, the first half says the opposite for at least one rule here: [`other-language-keyword`](./other-language-keyword/other-language-keyword.md) argues *from* that phrasing that it is **not** the language's, because a program that violates it always violates [`name-not-declared`](./name-not-declared/name-not-declared.md) or [`unbound-type-name`](./unbound-type-name/unbound-type-name.md) too, so no program satisfies the catalog and violates this one alone — which the sentence's own form calls belonging to the language. Applied literally it would also fail [`scope-visibility`](./scope-visibility/scope-visibility.md), the one rule whose subject is plainly the language's. The disagreement is recorded here rather than settled; the sentence in `rule.md` needs one half of it rewritten.

**Four Rules have left this catalog, and none of them was the language's.** [`disregarded-error`](../../../modules/process/error/rules/disregarded-error/disregarded-error.md) went to [`modules/process/error/rules/`](../../../modules/process/error/rules/README.md) — a folder that already existed and was empty, named by the owner as a candidate and recorded as such at the time ([`docs/rule.changelog.md`](../../../docs/rule.changelog.md):74 and :84). The three Go-residue Rules went to [`targets/go/rules/`](../targets/go/rules/README.md), which was created for them on 2026-10-04 and now holds [`go-clue-residue`](../targets/go/rules/go-clue-residue/go-clue-residue.md), [`commented-generic-bindings`](../targets/go/rules/commented-generic-bindings/commented-generic-bindings.md) and [`result-parameter-names`](../targets/go/rules/result-parameter-names/result-parameter-names.md); the reasoning is in the Go target's [research record](../targets/go/target.research.001.md). Three rules are new to this catalog, each on the owner's request: [`scope-visibility`](./scope-visibility/scope-visibility.md), [`other-language-keyword`](./other-language-keyword/other-language-keyword.md), and [`declaration-rename`](./declaration-rename/declaration-rename.md), the last of which has moved its *mechanism* twice while the Rule itself stayed here — out of `targets/go/` because the mechanism's subject is Khayyam and the Go target's does not outlive the port, then briefly out of this module on the claim that a name's meaning is no language's, and back on the owner's ruling of 2026-10-04 that the requirement is Khayyam's and no other language's requirement is to be developed for it at all: the mechanism is [`declaration-rename.ts`](../services/rename/declaration-rename.ts) beside the binding at [`services/rename/`](../services/rename/khayyam-binding.ts), in this module ([`declaration-rename.md`](./declaration-rename/declaration-rename.md)).

## What the audit found about this folder
- **One of 25 Rules is the language's own** on the test above: `scope-visibility`. The other 24 answer to a tool or to this repository's conventions — 9 tool readings or choices and 15 repository conventions. No Rule here has the Go port's residue as its subject; that class left this folder on 2026-10-04 and is held by [`targets/go/rules/`](../targets/go/rules/README.md).
- **24 of 25 also fail this index's own first membership bullet**, "its subject is Khayyam itself — the language or a contract Khayyam owns". The index's prose at [Why it works this way](#why-it-works-this-way) already explains why several tool Rules are here anyway, which is the tension stated plainly: the bullet and the prose describe two different folders.
- **Against the earlier measurement**, [`docs/rule.changelog.md`](../../../docs/rule.changelog.md):80 counted 13 of 26 as having a subject this repository chose. That class is 15 of 25 now, the tool class is 9, and the residue class is 0 — so this folder grew by one net Rule while the share that is not the language's fell by three.
- **On whether this folder grows without limit:** 25 folders, of which 22 predate 2026-10-03 and 3 arrived on that day. Nothing here is temporary by its own account any more, and the mechanism of one Rule was moved out of the Go target for want of a subject to match it, then out of this module for want of a subject narrower than a language ([`declaration-rename.md`](./declaration-rename/declaration-rename.md):89). The part of this folder that would grow to hundreds is the naming and layout half, and that half is repository convention rather than language, so its growth is a governance question and not a claim about what a Khayyam program may mean.
- **On what the mechanisms behind these Rules are.** **Everything this project has developed as a script is provisional and is eventually to become real code in modules.** That includes the Python tooling under [`bundles/khayyam/targets/go/`](../targets/go/) that applies the four residue Rules above, and it is the standing reason a mechanism was moved twice before it came to rest in this module. The same note is carried where a reader of [`.agents/scripts/README.md`](../../../.agents/scripts/README.md) meets it.
