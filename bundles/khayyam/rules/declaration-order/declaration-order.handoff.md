# Khayyam Rule — Declaration Order Handoff
Open state for [declaration-order.md](./declaration-order.md), the rule that a method is declared after the type it belongs to. See the repository's [documentation-handoff.md](../../../../docs/documentation-handoff.md) for what a handoff is. The measurements below were taken over the `.kh` files under `modules/` on 2026-10-04, counting a `tp {name} mt (self {owner})` declaration whose `{owner}` is declared in the same file.

## Open Questions

### Does a composition block stand with its own declaration, or below its owner's methods?
- State: not settled, and the sources do not agree, which is why the rule states nothing about it. 81 owners under `modules/` declare a composition block and at least one method on themselves; in 74 the block stands first and the methods follow it (`modules/process/event/event.kh`, `modules/process/event/protocol/event.kh`, `modules/process/race/protocol/event-target-race-event.kh`), and in 7 a method stands above the block (`modules/math/boolean/protocol/boolean.kh`: four methods above `tp Boolean ab {`; `modules/codec/string/protocol/character.kh`, `modules/codec/string/protocol/string.kh`, `modules/computer/buffer/protocol/buffer.kh`, `modules/memory/address/protocol/address.kh`). The 7 are the shape the ecosystems' own contracts have, where an interface's methods are read as the interface's body and its composition list as a header over them.
- Next: a ruling on whether the composition block is part of the declaration the reader meets first or a summary of the methods below it. The first answer makes the block the anchor this rule orders against; the second makes the methods the anchor and makes "a method after its type" a rule about `tp X cp|ab` with no block.
- Question for the owner: is the composition block the head of a type or its summary, and does a method belong above it or below it?

### The eleven methods the sources put above their owner — are they defects or the sources' shape?
- State: 11 of the 745 methods whose owner their own file declares stand above it: `modules/codec/string/protocol/character.kh:8`; `modules/codec/string/protocol/string.kh:16` and `:18`; `modules/computer/buffer/protocol/buffer.kh:22`; `bundles/khayyam/targets/js/bootstrap/state.kh:32`; `modules/math/boolean/protocol/boolean.kh:16`, `:18`, `:20`, `:22`; `modules/memory/address/protocol/address.kh:8` and `:10`. All 11 are the same three things — an accessor and a `tp X ab` or `tp X cp` composition block that composes the accessor — and none is a method whose owner's name the reader could not infer. The last of them is in a target's bootstrap source rather than in a protocol folder.
- Next: whether they are reordered. Six of the seven files above are protocol folders, so reordering is a change to a module's protocol surface and not only to formatting; the seventh is under `bundles/khayyam/targets/`, which the toolchain handoff records as to be deleted.
- Question for the owner: does this rule come with a pass that reorders the eleven, and is a bootstrap target's source in scope for a rule about how a reader meets a file?

### Does the clause reach a method attached to a method?
- State: unstated by the rule and by the sources. [Method Structure](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#method-structure) holds that a method may be attached to *any* type, "including an abstraction (`ab`) or even another method (`mt`) — wherever a receiver is genuinely warranted", and that a method implements an abstraction by declaring methods whose owner is that method. No `.kh` file under `modules/` declares a method on a method, so there is no instance either way and the clause's subject is stated as `(self {owner})` without naming a kind.
- Next: either the rule says the clause covers every owner the language admits, or it says it covers the types and leaves the method-owner case to whoever writes one.
- Question for the owner: does "the type it belongs to" mean every owner `self` may name, or only `cp` and `ab`?

### Should anything report a violation?
- State: no. [Abstraction → Abstraction Realization](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#abstraction-realization-implicit-satisfaction) judges satisfaction by method name and receiver, never by position, so `analyze()` cannot report this and the [file layout rule](../file-layout/file-layout.md) has the same open question for its own contracts-first clause. This rule's enforcement class is governance, which is where the file layout rule's sits.
- Next: a ruling on whether a linter should report the order, which would make the 11 above into 11 diagnostics rather than a measurement, and would give the rule a mechanism the way `tidy --rule method-separation` gives the [method separation rule](../method-separation/method-separation.md) one. Nothing in this module depends on the answer.
- Question for the owner: is a report wanted, or is the rule's whole enforcement being a reader who finds it in this index?

## Anticipated Work
- **The 745 methods are the rule's evidence, and re-measuring them is cheap.** A pass that reports each `mt` whose owner is declared below it, and each owner brought in by `in` separately from each owner the file declares, is what would turn the numbers in the rule document into something a reader can reproduce after the next change; the measurement behind this handoff was a scan over the sources and was not kept.
- **A tool that folds declarations would make this rule moot in the way the file layout rule's does not.** Companion tooling already presents structure first, and a view that showed every owner above its methods would satisfy the rule without any source changing. Whether the rule then still says anything is the question the [file layout rule](../file-layout/file-layout.md) raises for its own clause and carries as open.
