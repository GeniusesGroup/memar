# Rename Management Handoff
Open work for [`rename-management.md`](./rename-management.md), which classifies the declaration-rename mechanism's claims as language-level, per-target, or neither. See the repository's [documentation-handoff.md](../../../../docs/documentation-handoff.md) for what a handoff is.

## Open Questions

### Does the language-independent claim have an owner, or is it this rule's own?
- State: [`rename-management.md`](./rename-management.md) records the engine - sources, plan, explicit write, refuse-on-newly-broken, report - as **neither** language-level nor protocol-level, because no document under [`docs/modules/`](../../../../docs/modules/) states a rename and the language cannot discharge one. It follows [`binding.md:162`](../../../../modules/computer/binding/binding.md), which takes the same reading for a question with no umbrella. So the mechanism's language-independent half has a Rule and a module but no protocol.
- Next: the owner's answer, which is one of three and each costs something different. A protocol document gives the claim an owner and makes another language's realization a matter of citing it, at the price of a framework-level definition the owner must introduce. Leaving it as the rule's own condition costs nothing and leaves the engine's portability unstated, which is where it started. A middle position - the engine stated in this module as a realization-side contract - is what [`rename-management.md`](./rename-management.md) currently is, and it is weaker than a protocol because nothing outside this module may cite it as one.
- Question for the owner: does enumerating sources, turning ranges into a reviewable plan, writing only under an explicit request, refusing a write that newly breaks a file, and reporting everything found and left belong in `docs/modules/` as a contract any language's toolchain can realize, or do they stay this Rule's own condition?
- Decided 2026-10-05: protocol document. The Next above becomes the work.

### What should `core/` publish, and at what granularity?
- State: **resolved 2026-10-05.** `core/` publishes both answers now. [`core/src/references.ts`](../../core/src/references.ts) is the one placement walk — every form the reader produces a type for, including the `vr` and `in` forms the editor's own copy deliberately skipped — and [`core/src/frontend.ts`](../../core/src/frontend.ts) publishes `visibleNames`, the resolver's own visible set, extracted from the body of `analyzeImports`. The binding's second walk and `walk_mismatches` are gone; `lsp/src/reference-places.ts` is deleted and [`lsp/src/unbound-names.ts`](../../lsp/src/unbound-names.ts) filters the published walk instead. What was recorded here as the cause of the `weak.kh:16` row was not a disagreement about references at all: that file's line 21 is `tp mt ReferenceAlive …`, the subtype-before-name form the reader refuses as `keyword-as-identifier`, so recovery left line 16 as the last declaration and `declarationSlices` ran its slice to the end of the token stream. The row was a slice bug wearing a walk's name.
- Next: two things the publication exposed and did not fix. `declarationKind` answers `"tp"` for a `vr-in` ([`core/src/sr.ts:132`](../../core/src/sr.ts)), so an included `vr-in` is refused `kind-mismatch` although its kind is right. And `visibleNames` returns a refusal for five of the repository's units — four under `modules/memory/record/services/` and `modules/process/control-flow/protocol/if.kh` — and `Found.faults` carries a required `line` that a non-parse refusal has none of, so a file whose visible set did not finish is not named in the report and its references are reported `unbound`.
- Question for the owner: whether `declarationKind` should answer a `vr-in` as `vr`, and where a resolver's non-parse refusal is reported given that `Found.faults` is shaped for parse faults.

### Does `Binding` stay?
- State: with the table in hand, four of its seven members are per-target, and `walk_mismatches` — the fifth — is gone. The registry, `--language`, and the three refusals exist for a second language, which the owner ruled on 2026-10-04 is not to be developed for.
- Next: the narrowing itself, which [`Revision`](../../../../docs/modules/computer/revision.md) now authorises by saying `Binding` keeps the binding-resolution contract and that residue modes, second-language members and prose classes stay per-target and never enter a shared interface. The consequence, stated plainly: `--language` leaves the command, `mode` in the report becomes a single value, and the three refusals reduce to one.
- Question for the owner: none outstanding for the registry's removal. What the owner has not ruled is where the residue mode goes once its members leave the shared contract — that question is the port's, and it is open in [`declaration-rename.handoff.md`](../../rules/declaration-rename/declaration-rename.handoff.md).

## Decisions (2026-10-05, owner)
- **Code under Content with a twin split.** Computer code is Content for identity, addressing, and versioning of its text; binding truth stays with the language analyzer (the `analyze()` write-gate stays). Neither full absorption nor separation. Consequence: occurrence-identity work goes to the Content-identity mechanism; binding questions stay with `core/` and the reader.
- **Occurrence identity is ContentUUID+offset.** `Place{path,line}` remains as one locator projection (a `path` locator beside others), not the identity. Blocked on the Organization content-identity-across-edit mechanism (open detail there); no independent identity scheme is to be invented in this module.
- **The engine gets a protocol owner.** The sources→plan→explicit-write→refuse→report engine belongs in `docs/modules/` as a contract any language toolchain can realize. The `binding.md:162` reading is superseded for this claim. Next: draft the protocol document with the model before the code, per the Modeling Practice gate.
- **Unblock order is top-down.** Protocol-owner ruling first (this decision), then `core/` publication (shared walk, `walk_mismatches` deleted outright), then Organization concurrency semantics. Work on a lower layer that a higher ruling would invalidate stays paused.
- **Re-homing map completed 2026-10-05.** Every rename claim assigned a home: abstract enumerate/plan/gate/report → Revision; token/place computations, `referencedTypes`, `analyzeImports`, acceptance → core/Lexer/Compiler; residue extent, sha-seal, `second`/`foreignRegions`/`residue`/`proseClass`, Go-lexical corners → per-target Go port; `tp`/`vr`/`in` ontology, scope, collisions, argv → the rule stays. Guardrails: no token computation, no residue fact, and no structural rule moves. Detail is reconstructible from the cited sources; this bullet is the record that the map exists and where each family landed.

## Anticipated Work

- **The residue mode's home follows from the table rather than from preference.** `foreignRegions`, `residue` and `second` are per-target facts about this repository's Go port, so the mode belongs to the port's rule rather than to a shared interface. The owner's ruling on whether `--residue` becomes a `tidy` rule of the Go target is open in [`declaration-rename.handoff.md`](../../rules/declaration-rename/declaration-rename.handoff.md), and this table is the argument for it rather than a substitute for the ruling.
- **The coincidence test cannot reach a second language.** Stated in [`rename-management.md`](./rename-management.md) and load-bearing for every claim in it: the instrument separates a language from its own targets. Work that needs to say what another language would do needs an instrument this repository does not have, which is the strongest argument for the first question above.
