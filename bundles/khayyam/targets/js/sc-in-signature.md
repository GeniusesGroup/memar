# Scope in a Signature
## Abstract
The owner has ruled that an Abstraction has no need of `sc`, and that a code scope is in essence a kind of implementation — not an abstraction and not a protocol. The rule this confirms was already stated at [`docs/khayyam/abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) under *What You Cannot Do*; the confirmation settles the rule and gives a reason the documents do not yet carry, and it has consequences nobody has worked through. This document works them through and drafts the minimum text each would need. The findings: a scope may be declared only inside a method body and an abstraction holds no method body, so in this repository's toolchain no abstraction can declare or drive a scope — the only open half is the *receiving* side, an abstraction-owned method a caller uses to drive one. The kinds of type that could hold a scope in a type-level argument are an abstraction, a capsule, a method, and the `sc` category written directly; the documents permit the first two, admit the third as a position with no declared holder, and do not address the fourth or say which owner may write it. The Control Flow protocol's two sentences are **not** in conflict: it requires an observable dependency boundary and permits "named scopes, explicit closures, or another mechanism", and its replaceability claim is about a library an organization mandates through its Linter, not about type substitutability. The sources neither contradict the ruling nor needs to change: not one `.kh` file under `modules/` declares a method with a scope in a type-level argument, and the one declaration in the tree that does — a test fixture, not a `.kh` file — already names a capsule, which is the shape the ruling sanctions. Ten drafts follow, each marked as a language, protocol, or toolchain decision and saying whether a drafter may phrase it or the owner must choose first; one of the ten is the finding that a protocol sentence needs no text at all.

## Introduction
### Motivation
Three modelling sessions wrote themselves into a corner on this subject, and the corner was a single unexamined sentence. [`modules/process/control-flow/`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/docs/answers.md) has no declaration it can write, because the type that would own a conditional pair is a category the language will not admit to a signature. [`bundles/khayyam/targets/js/language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md) drafted an intervention to open that corner (I-C) and a second to say how the position is spelled (I-E), and between them they wrote down what admitting a Scope would cost and what refusing it costs. The owner has now refused it. That refusal is a ruling, and a ruling is not an answer: it settles one question and opens the next three, which is why the finding belongs in a document rather than in a reply.

The subject of this document is narrower than "flow control" and wider than one sentence. It is the type-level argument position that a method's influencing group opens for a scope, and everything that position touches: which owner may write it, what may fill it, what the Control Flow protocol claims about the library that uses it, and what the sources already assume about it.

### Methodology

Two cautions a reader of the module documents should carry into these findings. First, the citations those documents make into [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) are stale: the *Scope* section is cited at `:128`, `:131`, and `:132` where the text now stands at `:127`, `:130`, and `:131`, and *Type Principles Realized* at `:209` and `:211` where it stands at `:206` and `:208`. Second, [`modules/process/control-flow/answers.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/docs/answers.md) at `:61` and `:179` and [`language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md) at `:344` quote "The type of a scope argument is unstated in the documents, so the model declares one" as sitting under **Ambiguities Resolved** in [`bundles/khayyam/targets/js/target.handoff.md`](./target.handoff.md) at `:50`. The conflict [`answers.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/docs/answers.md) reports at `:179` therefore rests on a quotation its cited file does not contain.

## Explanation
### What the ruling closes, and what it leaves open
The rule the owner confirmed is at [`docs/khayyam/abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) under *What You Cannot Do*, whose second bullet reads that a capsule type may not be an argument or return type in an abstraction's method signature, "only other abstractions are allowed, ensuring the specification remains implementation-agnostic". The same document states the rule a second time at `:67`, in *Defining an Abstraction*, as "only abstractions may appear in an abstraction-owned method signature".

Neither sentence names a scope, and neither needed to: both are exhaustive, so a scope was already outside what they allow. What the confirmation adds is a reason that is not the one the bullet gives. The bullet's reason is implementation-agnosticism; the owner's is that a code scope *is* an implementation — `docs/type.md` under *Scope — Type as Semantic Boundary* at `:182` counts a Scope as "a Type that delimits a semantic boundary", `:184` counts a local algorithmic block among its realizations, and `:191` puts the four categories on one level with "none is nested inside another as a subtype". A Scope is a boundary a particular program draws; an Abstraction is what a program requires regardless of how it is built. Those are different kinds of thing, and no amount of `cp`-versus-`ab` precision makes them one.

**The declaring side is already settled, and not by the ruling.** [`docs/khayyam/abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) under *What You Cannot Do* at `:100` refuses a method body inside an abstraction definition, and [`bundles/khayyam/rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.md`](../../rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.md) at `:12` states that a `sc` declaration appears only inside a method body. A scope therefore has no home inside an abstraction, and a method that could drive one has nowhere to declare it. That conjunction is a language fact and a toolchain rule respectively — the placement rule says so itself at `:21`, and [`docs/type.md`](../../../../docs/type.md) at `:186` places placement with the realization — so the conclusion holds for this repository's toolchain rather than for every Khayyam, and is stated that way below.

What is left open is the **receiving** side: an abstraction-owned method that a caller uses to drive a scope. Nothing can be declared inside such a method, and the position its influencing group opens may now hold nothing an abstraction may name. That one gap is what the first three drafts below close.

### Who may take a scope as a type-level argument
The position is admitted twice and specified nowhere. [`docs/khayyam/method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) under *Type-level arguments for `sc` and `mt`* at `:71` states that a method's influencing group may receive a `vr`, an `sc`, or an `mt`, and that "An `sc` is a code scope: a library method that drives a scope receives that scope here". [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) under *Scope* at `:131` restates it from the grammar's side: an argument position may be satisfied by a `vr` of the declared type or, for `sc` and `mt`, by the type itself passed as a type-level argument.

Which types could hold the scope in that position, and what the documents say about each:

1. **An abstraction.** Permitted today only in an abstraction-owned signature — [`abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) at `:108-109` states that which of an abstraction or a capsule a signature names "follows from who owns the signature", and that an abstraction-owned signature "may name abstractions only". Under the ruling this is closed for a scope, because a Scope is not an Abstraction. An abstraction *typed variable* remains admissible in an abstraction-owned position, which is the route that keeps a contract a contract — see the third option below.
2. **A capsule.** Permitted today in a capsule-owned signature: [`abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) at `:109` states that "A signature owned by a capsule may name a capsule, including the capsule itself." This is the shape the tree's only measurement writes.
3. **A variable of an abstraction type.** Permitted today: [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) at `:71` says a `vr` "is an instance of a capsule or an abstraction", and an abstraction-typed `vr` may sit in an abstraction-owned position. Under [`docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md) at `:95` a branch would then be a named capsule implementing a named abstraction — "There is no closure or lambda syntax in the grammar; any callback-shaped requirement is expressed as a named capsule implementing the relevant abstraction" — which is a capsule per branch, declared by name.
4. **The `sc` category written directly** — `(branch sc)`. **Not addressed anywhere.** [`khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) at `:131` says the position may be satisfied "by the type itself", without saying by which owner, and `abstraction.md` at `:108-109` states only what an abstraction-owned and a capsule-owned signature may name. Whether a capsule-owned signature may write the category is the unstated case; whether an abstraction-owned one may is what the ruling closes.
5. **A method.** Admitted as a *position* at [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) at `:71`, with no declared holder anywhere: [`language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md) at `:27` records the measurement that the grammar admits a method in an argument position and "no unit can declare the type that holds one". The ruling says nothing about `mt` in a signature, so this case is untouched by it and left untouched here — see D2 and D3.
6. **A signature whose owner is a method or a scope.** **Not addressed.** [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) at `:29` and [`docs/type.md`](../../../../docs/type.md) at `:207` admit a method on any type, and [`abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) at `:108-109` then gives the admissible types for exactly two owners. A method-owned signature is therefore neither permitted nor refused by any sentence, and the sources contain none.

**What a concrete capsule costs.** Naming a capsule is the only option that both survives the ruling and is stated today, and it is nominal where the language's substitutability is structural. [`abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) at `:47` says "Any capsule that provides those methods can be used wherever a `Reader` is expected", and at `:137` states satisfaction as a rule over signatures; a position that names a capsule forfeits both, because a capsule is satisfied by nothing but itself. Three further costs follow, and each is stated by a document rather than argued here:

- **The name becomes this repository's.** [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) at `:62` makes a name collision an architecture error with no alias syntax to resolve it, so one library's `Scope` capsule and any second library's are one concept with two declarations, and the fix is a root-cause redesign rather than a rename at the import site.
- **No document states what relates the capsule to a scope.** The only Capsule-to-Abstraction relation the model carries is `satisfies` at [`docs/type.md`](../../../../docs/type.md) at `:195`, and the relations table at `:193-198` has no Scope row at all. A capsule-typed scope parameter is therefore a name with no stated relation to the `sc` the grammar produces; the tie is this toolchain's convention — [`target.md`](./target.md) at `:52` says a scope is emitted as a function declared inside the method that holds it, and at `:73` that a linked module receives "a scope as the function the compiler emitted for it".
- **The protocol's replaceability stops applying to this realization.** [`modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.md) at `:130` states that "organizations can configure their Linter to block raw intrinsic jump methods and mandate their own approved control-flow libraries, without changing the protocol". A capsule fixed in this repository is not a library an organization may mandate, and [`modules/process/control-flow/answers.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/docs/answers.md) at `:215` says so in those words.

### The two Control Flow statements
The question as it reaches this document is whether a conditional pair's members must name a scope and whether replaceability survive the ruling together. They do not conflict, for a reason that is worth stating before any option is laid out: **the protocol does not say the members must name a scope.**

[`modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.md) at `:37` says a realization "**may** expose a generic conditional abstraction that receives an explicit branch scope". At `:59` it says "`ELSE` always takes an explicit reference back to the same condition value it pairs with; the construct carries its dependencies in its own arguments rather than relying on position or surrounding text. A realization **may** express that rule through named scopes, explicit closures, or another mechanism with the same observable dependency boundary." What is required is the dependency boundary, not a parameter of a given type, and the protocol names three ways to reach it. At `:150` it registers the conditional-pair contract as a topic "to be developed here" and holds none of it.

The "must" is a reading, and it is a reading two documents share: [`modules/process/control-flow/answers.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/docs/answers.md) at `:157` ("The pair's members must name a scope") and [`modules/process/control-flow/answers.handoff.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/docs/answers.handoff.md) at `:14` (the same sentence as a reason). The words each quotes are in the protocol; the modality is theirs. [`language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md) is cited for this claim at `:58` and `:255` of this document and holds the other half of it rather than the same half: its I-C at `:90` says that "the type that would own a conditional pair cannot name the scope its members receive" — the gap the "must" reading would close, stated from the language's side. The three do not agree on a claim, and this document records that rather than merging them.

**The replaceability claim is also not what it is usually read as.** [`control-flow.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.md) at `:130` is about a *library* an organization may mandate through its own Linter. It is not a claim that an implementation may be replaced by another of a different type, and it does not rest on substitutability of a position's type. The language's substitutability claims live elsewhere — [`abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) at `:47` and `:137`, and the covariant-return rule at [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) under *Abstraction* at `:104` — and the ruling reaches those only in the way this document records under D5.

So the two statements are compatible as written, and neither has to give. What the ruling does cost is the realizability of one sentence — `:37`'s "generic conditional abstraction that receives an explicit branch scope" has no Khayyam realization once the abstraction's own member may not name a scope — and the options are these. None of them is chosen here.

**A. The scope reaches the pair through a type its signature admits, and the contract stays an abstraction.** The abstraction-owned member receives the branch as a variable of a named abstraction type, satisfied by a named capsule per branch ([`encapsulation.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md) at `:95`), and the capsule-owned member drives it. *Forecloses:* nothing in the protocol's text; `:37` read as the abstraction's own member receiving the scope. *Costs:* a named capsule per branch site, and [`khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) at `:129` and `:130` — the `sc` primitive as the branch-grouping unit and the compiler's lowering over it — no longer describe this form, so the Scope section and this protocol's *Library-Defined Control Flow* would each need a sentence saying which form uses which.

**B. The pair is owned by a concrete capsule.** This is the shape the tree's only measurement writes ([`emit.test.ts`](./tests/emit.test.ts) at `:18-21`). *Forecloses:* the abstraction as the pair's owner, and with it `:130`'s replaceability for this repository's library, exactly as [`answers.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/docs/answers.md) at `:215` states. *Costs:* the same; the `:59` dependency boundary is unaffected either way, because a capsule-owned member may name a capsule.

**C. The protocol is amended to say the pair is expressed over whatever type the realization's signatures admit.** *Forecloses:* nothing, and settles nothing — it moves the choice to the realization, which is where [`control-flow.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.md) at `:32` already puts it ("Across realizations, the common denominator is the branch-grouping primitive, not how a particular library or compiler drives it"). *Costs:* a protocol sentence that says less than the topic at `:150` was going to say, which may be the honest minimum.

**D. The grammar admits the category.** `(branch sc)` in an abstraction-owned signature, which is what [`language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md) drafted at I-C and I-E. *Forecloses:* the ruling itself. It is listed because it was on the table and refused, and because [`control-flow.handoff.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.handoff.md) at `:37` still offers it as one of three options to the owner.

### Whose methods the Method section speaks for
[`docs/khayyam/method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) under *Type-level arguments for `sc` and `mt`* at `:70-71` reads, in full: "A method's influencing group may receive a `vr`, an `sc`, or an `mt`. The compiler distinguishes the three from the callee's signature and performs the behavior that position requires. A `vr` is an instance of a capsule or an abstraction. An `sc` is a code scope: a library method that drives a scope receives that scope here. An `mt` is a method. Bare-type and closure-style `mt` passes this repository discourages are stated in [type as argument]."

Yes — the section needs to say whose methods may, and the position is not type-independent. Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **The section's claim is now false as written for one owner.** It says "a method's influencing group", which quantifies over every method. Under the ruling that is no longer true of an abstraction-owned method receiving an `sc`, so the sentence has to carry the owner or narrow its claim. This is the whole of the edit.
2. **The documents already make ownership the deciding fact, elsewhere.** [`abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) at `:108-109` says which types a signature may name "follows from who owns the signature" and then enumerates two owners. A section that stated the position without the owner would be the only place in the set where a signature's admissible types did not depend on its owner, which is the inconsistency [Content Rule](../../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#content-rule-no-fabricated-or-redundant-provenance) exists to prevent.
3. **No other document may say it for this one.** [`bundles/khayyam/rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md`](../../rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md) at `:15` says type-level arguments "that the signature explicitly allows remain valid" and at `:24` that it "does not claim the grammar forbids type-level `sc` or `mt` arguments where the signature requires them" — a governance rule that disclaims the grammar's subject on purpose. And the subject is the language's: [`khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) under *Minimal Legislation: A Principle* at `:177` keeps in the grammar "what a program's meaning requires", and which types a signature may name decides whether a program is well-formed.

One boundary on the edit. The section must not absorb `abstraction.md`'s rule: the rule and its reason stay where they are, and the Method section states which owner may write the position and points at the reason. A Method section that restated the prohibition would be a second authority over it.

### What the repository's own files contain
Two scans over every `.kh` file under `modules/` — one for a method declaration whose influencing group names a type denoting a scope, one for any declaration of a type whose name denotes one — return nothing under `modules/`. That is a statement about this repository's tree and about nothing else, and **it is withdrawn as evidence on 2026-10-03**: the code under `modules/**` is the residue of an attempt to develop these ideas in another language that did not succeed, and the owner has ruled it neither evidence nor a premise. A body of source that has never been written in this language's final syntax would return the same result for every construct, which is why the absence of a shape cannot show that nothing depends on it — that observation is [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.research.001.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.research.001.md)'s and this document adopts it rather than restating it. ### What else the ruling touches
1. **I-C and I-E of this folder's own measurements are now drafts of refused positions.** [`language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md) at `:89-112` drafts the "yes" branch in two sentences with its reasoning, and at `:97` gives replacement text for `abstraction.md`'s bullet; at `:133-158` it drafts how a scope parameter is spelled, and at `:144` would replace [`khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) at `:131`. Under the ruling the first is a position the owner refused and the second is a spelling that survives only where the owner admits it. Its Abstract at `:12` and its ordering paragraph at `:42` are both rewritten since; the ordering no longer rests on that branch standing.
2. **The category is stated once in the model's relations and never.** [`docs/type.md`](../../../../docs/type.md) at `:198` records "commonly used within | Scope → Method | current Khayyam usage pattern", and the table at `:193-198` carries no row in which a Scope participates in anything else. A capsule-typed scope parameter introduces a relation the model does not have.
3. **The base-level Scope section ties flow constructs to `sc` without saying who drives one.** [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) at `:129` says a code scope is inert until "a library-provided method drives it", and at `:130` that a compiler lowers `sc`-driven branches to internal jumps. Both stay; neither says whether that method may be one an abstraction names, which is now the question a reader has.
4. **Three models that reason from `abstraction.md:101` are confirmed rather than broken.** [`modules/computer/binding/binding.md`](../../../../modules/computer/binding/binding.md) at `:51` and `:240` and [`modules/process/construction/construction.md`](../../../../modules/process/construction/construction.md) at `:154` each reason that a construction contract cannot be an Abstraction, or — in the binding model's own terms — that no shared contract may name a binding's crossing where the crossing is a place, because of that rule. The ruling removes an alternative they had to hold open; it changes no sentence of theirs. Recorded so a later reader does not treat them as casualties.

### The drafts
Ten drafts, in the order a reviewer should take them. Each names the document, the section, the text in that document's own voice, why that text rather than a weaker or stronger one, and what it releases. Six are a drafter's to phrase; four need the owner to choose first, and say so.

#### D1 — What an abstraction's signature may name, and why
**Document and anchor.** [`docs/khayyam/abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md), section **What You Cannot Do** — the second bullet.

**Proposed text.**

```
- You **cannot** use a capsule type, or a scope, as an argument or return type in an abstraction's method signature — only other abstractions are allowed. The specification stays implementation-agnostic by naming what it requires rather than how it is built: an abstraction names concepts, and a code scope is a boundary one program draws, which [Type → Scope — Type as Semantic Boundary](../../../../docs/type.md#scope--type-as-semantic-boundary) counts at several levels — a namespace, a package's visibility, a local algorithmic block — none of which settles what an implementer is. What it may not name is a capsule, because a capsule is one realization, and a scope, because a scope is that realization's own boundary rather than a requirement anything else must meet.
```

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** The rule is unchanged; two things are added and both are the ruling's own. The first is the word *scope*, so that a reader who knows `sc` knows the case was considered rather than missed — the rule was already exhaustive and nobody had to read it that way before, because nobody had asked. The second is the reason, which is not the reason the bullet gave: implementation-agnosticism explains the capsule and does not reach a boundary, so the sentence now derives the second refusal the way it derives the first. A weaker text — the rule unchanged, reason unchanged — leaves the ruling's own reasoning in the conversation where it was spoken, and [Documentation → Written surface](../../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#written-surface) keeps a decision's reason with the rule it explains. A stronger one would refuse an abstraction-typed *variable* of scopes as well, which the owner did not rule on and which option A of the second question above depends on.

**What it would unblock.** The receiving half of the gap: with the rule carrying the scope by name, [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md)'s section can state which owner may write the position without restating a prohibition, and the two documents stop being readable as though one of them had an omission the other had noticed.

**What it would change elsewhere.** [`abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) at `:67` restates the rule in *Defining an Abstraction* and would gain the same word, or point here; [`language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md) at `:97` drafts this bullet already and would be withdrawn rather than applied.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language**. A drafter may phrase it: the claim is the owner's and the sentence is the drafting.

#### D2 — Whose method may write an `sc` position
**Document and anchor.** [`docs/khayyam/method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md), section **Type-level arguments for `sc` and `mt`** — the paragraph at `:71` extended.

**Proposed text, for the `sc` half only.**

```
A method's influencing group may receive a `vr`, an `sc`, or an `mt`, and which of the three a position admits follows from the type that owns the signature. The compiler distinguishes the three from the callee's signature and performs the behavior that position requires. A `vr` is an instance of a capsule or an abstraction. An `sc` is a code scope: a library method that drives a scope receives that scope here ([Khayyam → Scope](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope)); a method whose owner is a capsule or a method may write that position, and one whose owner is an abstraction may not — a code scope is a boundary a program draws rather than a requirement, and [Abstraction in Khayyam → What You Cannot Do](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do) says what an abstraction's signature may name and why. An `mt` is a method. Bare-type and closure-style `mt` passes this repository discourages are stated in [type as argument](../../rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md).
```

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** It makes one claim — the owner decides the position — and points at the document that holds the rule rather than repeating it, because a Method section that restated the prohibition would be a second authority over a rule [`abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) already owns. It deliberately says "a method whose owner is a capsule or a method", because [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) at `:29` admits a method on any type and `abstraction.md` at `:108-109` enumerates only two owners — the third is left named rather than left out, because the sources contain no method-owned signature with a scope and a reader needs to know whether one is refused or merely unaddressed. A stronger text would forbid an `sc` in *any* signature, which is a second ruling the owner did not make: the ruling is about an abstraction's having no need of `sc`, and a capsule naming its own mechanism is the ordinary case it leaves alone.

**What it would unblock.** Every signature in [`modules/process/control-flow/`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/), whose answers document calls the critical path at `:214`; the sources' only witness becoming a declaration rather than a comment ([`listeners.kh`](../../../../modules/computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh) at `:3` and `:6`); and the fourth option in the second question above — a branch arriving as an abstraction-typed variable — becoming writable rather than merely arguable.

**What it would change elsewhere.** [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) at `:131` says the same position is satisfied "by the type itself passed as a type-level argument", which after this text is true only where this text says — D3's second clause. [`bundles/khayyam/rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md`](../../rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md) at `:15` and `:24` keep their claim either way.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language**. A drafter may phrase the `sc` half. The `mt` half is not drafted here and is the owner's — see D3.

#### D3 — The Scope section's own statement of the position, and the two it does not yet settle
**Document and anchor.** [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md), section **Scope** — the fifth bullet at `:131` replaced, and a clause added to the inertness bullet at `:129`.

**Proposed text, for the bullet at `:131`.**

```
- An argument position may be satisfied by a `vr` of the declared type or, where the signature's owner admits it, by the scope itself passed as a type-level argument. See [Method in Khayyam → Type-level arguments for `sc` and `mt`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#type-level-arguments-for-sc-and-mt).
```

**Proposed text, for the clause added to `:129`.**

```
A code scope is inert until a method that holds a body drives it: a scope is declared inside one, and [Abstraction in Khayyam → What You Cannot Do](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do) refuses a method body inside an abstraction, so no abstraction declares or drives a scope. See [Control Flow](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.md) for how flow constructs are built as libraries within this mechanism.
```

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** Both are single-clause edits, and each removes an ambiguity a reader now has. The first replaces a sentence that says the position is satisfied without saying by whom; "where the signature's owner admits it" defers the whole list to the Method section rather than duplicating it, which is the direction [Content Rule](../../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#content-rule-no-fabricated-or-redundant-provenance) requires. The second says who may drive a scope and rests it on two sentences that already exist — one language rule and one that this toolchain's placement rule makes true — so it asserts nothing new. It says *no abstraction declares or drives a scope*, which is stronger than the ruling and is what follows: a scope's declaration site is already closed by the two sentences it cites. A weaker clause would leave "a library-provided method" unexamined, which is the phrase the ruling makes worth examining.

**What it would unblock.** [`language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md)'s I-E at `:136`, which names this bullet as the sentence that "currently describes the other mechanism"; and the option-A fork in the second question above, which needs the declaring side and the receiving side both stated before a library can be written.

**What it would change elsewhere.** [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) at `:71` says the same position in the same words and the two must be drafted together or they contradict. [`bundles/khayyam/rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.md`](../../rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.md) at `:15` rests on the sentence this clause edits and stays correct; at `:21` it disclaims the language's placement and this clause does not restore it, because the clause rests on the abstraction rule and not on the rule's placement.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language**. The bullet's first sentence is a drafter's. Two things are **not** drafted and are the owner's: whether a capsule-owned signature may write the `sc` category directly — `(branch sc)`, which [`language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md) drafted at `:141` and `:144` and which no document addresses for any owner — and whether an abstraction-owned signature may name an `mt` position, which the ruling does not reach.

#### D4 — Whether the model gives a scope a category relation
**Document and anchor.** [`docs/type.md`](../../../../docs/type.md), section **Scope — Type as Semantic Boundary** — one sentence added after the relations table at `:193-198`.

**Proposed text.**

```
A scope named in a signature stands for the boundary its realization draws, not for a relation this model names between categories: where a signature writes a declared type rather than the category, that type is the realization's own and the model holds no claim about what relates the two. A realization's placement choices are its own, and which level it declares a scope at is stated in the language that declares it.
```

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** It states that the model declines to relate the categories rather than relating them, because the two candidate texts differ by a ruling rather than by a phrasing. A stronger text would add a `Scope → Capsule` row to the table at `:193-198` — and that row would say that a code scope *is* one realization of a capsule, which is a claim about the category that no document supports and that the toolchain's own tie contradicts: [`target.md`](./target.md) at `:52` and `:73` supplies the tie as an emission convention, not as a language relation. A weaker text would say nothing and leave the base layer unable to answer a reader who has seen `tp Scope cp {` and wants to know what the two names have to do with each other. This document does not choose between the two, because the weaker text is not the neutral one — it is a decision that the model has no such relation.

**What it would unblock.** [`modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.handoff.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.handoff.md) at `:37`'s "whether the library restates the grammar's category or names a contract over it" becomes answerable in either direction, and [`modules/process/construction/construction.md`](../../../../modules/process/construction/construction.md) at `:154`, which already reasons from the same boundary, gains the base-level warrant for it.

**What it would change elsewhere.** [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) at `:174` states that the grammar "does express the modeling concepts defined at the base layer — `tp` for Type, `sc` for Scope — because those definitions are general and language-independent"; a sentence saying the model holds no relation would have to sit beside that without contradicting it, which is why the proposed text defers to the language for placement.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language**, and the **owner's**: whether the model gains a relation is a claim about the category, and the choice above is not a phrasing.

#### D5 — The conditional-pair topic the protocol registers and does not hold

#### D10 — What the branch reaches, when the pair's owner is an abstraction
**Document and anchor.** This is not a draft of text. It is the fork the four drafts above leave open, named here so it is not mistaken for drafting work: if the pair is owned by an abstraction, a branch body reaches its members as a variable of a named abstraction type, and [`docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md) at `:95` requires that variable to be a named capsule implementing it — a named capsule per branch site, with its own declaration site, its own field list, and its own entry and exit. If the pair is owned by a capsule, the branch stays an `sc` in the shape [`emit.test.ts`](./tests/emit.test.ts) already writes, and what is lost is the replaceability [`control-flow.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.md) at `:130` states an organization has. **Language, protocol, or toolchain.** Both, and squarely the **owner's**: it decides whether a branch body is a boundary or a value in every document that mentions either.

### Implications
Adopting D1 through D3 as drafted would make the three Khayyam documents agree about who may write a scope in a signature, and would leave `modules/process/control-flow/` with a signature it can write for the first time — a capsule-owned member receiving a scope it names as a capsule, which is the shape its only measurement already assumes. It would not by itself give the module a replaceable library: that is D5's and D10's business, and until one of them is chosen the module's owner question at [`control-flow.handoff.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/control-flow.handoff.md) `:29-33` stays open with a narrower field.

The needs in the sources no change to become consistent with the ruling, which is the cheapest consequence in this document: nothing under `modules/` declares the shape at all, and the one declaration that does names a capsule. What it needs is for two artifacts to stop describing that declaration as a workaround — [`answers.md`](../../../../modules/process/control-flow/docs/answers.md) at `:159` and [`language-gaps.md`](./language-gaps.md) at `:90` — and for the two comment files whose signatures the sources do carry to be read as evidence of what an author reached for rather than as declarations.

The expensive consequence is at the base layer. A Scope that a signature may not name is a category the type model relates to nothing, and D4's choice is between stating that and introducing a relation no document supports. Every signature written as `tp Scope cp {` inherits whichever answer the owner gives, which is the reason D4 is not a drafting question.
