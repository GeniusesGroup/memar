# In-Fabric Thermal Buffer

## Abstract
Warmth that has to be moved is cheapest when the building's own body moves it: a latent-heat layer — in the floor the seedlings stand on, in the plaster the family leans against — swallows the noon and hands the heat to the night at a steady 21 to 24 degrees, paying no electricity, no drilling and no dedicated vessel. This document holds one question — is the thermal buffer in the envelope itself (phase-change materials, PCM) or in separate equipment? — and records both of its answers on the envelope side: the composite floor of the closed farms ([Integrated thermal floor covering with phase-change materials (PCM Thermal-Storage Floor Slab)](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#integrated-thermal-floor-covering-with-phase-change-materials-pcm-thermal-storage-floor-slab)) and the phase-change wall of the suites ([Phase-change thermal batteries (PCM) in the passive envelopes of buildings](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#phase-change-thermal-batteries-pcm-in-the-passive-envelopes-of-buildings)). The question's other half — the equipment answer, the water and soil stores — is recorded in another document, and that asymmetry is not an editorial accident to be tidied away: the two records here overlap the equipment answers there on exactly the peak kilowatt-hours each claims to shave, and the adjudication cannot decide "fabric or equipment" until the overlap is assigned. The verdict will be logged in this document's changelog.

## Introduction

### Motivation
Read apart, the two records of this page look like one technology listed twice — phase-change material, once in a floor, once in a wall — and an objection to putting chemistry inside a structural layer (the cycle-life question both records carry as a prerequisite) is filed at once against envelope comfort and against farm horticulture. Read together they show two different promises wearing the same material: the floor is a substitute for a concrete pour, serving roots; the wall is a 50-percent cut of a peak, serving residents; and the band they stabilize, 21 to 24 degrees, is identical in both — a coincidence strong enough to be the document's best argument and weak enough to be a drafting echo nobody has checked. The page also holds a question with a missing half: it asks whether the buffer belongs in the fabric or in separate equipment, and records no equipment answer at all — the stores that would fill that side of the question live in the neighbouring document — so keeping the two records scattered would not merely separate rivals, it would leave the dispute invisible and let each side of the wider ledger claim the same saved kilowatt-hour.

## Explanation

### The problem the answers share
Is the thermal buffer in the envelope itself (PCM) or in separate equipment?

Sharpened, the shared problem is where a day's heat surplus should be parked when the building is the meter: in the body that must be built anyway (both records here say yes, at pour or plaster time), or in a vessel sized and sited for the duty (the answer this page records nowhere and `thermal-sources-and-storage.md` carries). Two edges the records leave open. (1) **Attribution.** Both records work by moving heat from day to night, and both are linked, in their own synergy lines, to other night-movers — the night-flush cooling of `passive-cooling-paths.md` and the in-wall HVAC of the buildings placement record — yet the same diurnal curve can be flattened once; the wall's 50-percent peak cut and the floor's zero-electricity night return cannot both be booked against every kilowatt-hour the other also claims. (2) **The 21-to-24 coincidence.** Root-zone biology and human comfort land on the same band by the two records' metrics; whether one PCM formulation family therefore serves farm and suite, or whether the identity is a drafting echo that will misprice the shared supply chain, is not stated anywhere. The question "fabric or equipment?" is therefore really a boundary question: which fraction of the settlement's peak may be dissolved into material, and what equipment must remain to catch what material cannot hold.

### Approaches

#### Integrated thermal floor covering with phase-change materials (PCM Thermal-Storage Floor Slab)
**Mechanism:** Replace the heavy concrete pour of the floors of closed farms and gardening spaces with a multi-layer composite, energy-absorbing floor covering that includes a thermal-insulation layer, a layer of micro-encapsulated phase-change-material (PCM) capsules, and a heat-transfer fluid layer. The covering absorbs the day's surplus heat and melts; in the cold of night, refreezing, it gives the stored heat back without consuming electricity — eliminating thermal shock to the soil and the roots.
**Metrics:** Floor-temperature fluctuation stabilized in the 21 to 24 degrees band; latent heat absorption up to 200 kilojoules per kilogram; a full 100 percent removal of impermeable concrete pours from greenhouse floors.
**Prerequisites:** [Absolute protection of the soil bed: aerial concrete pedestrian lines and non-mortar dry-laid stone paving](../form-and-land/ground-surface-treatment.md#absolute-protection-of-the-soil-bed-aerial-concrete-pedestrian-lines-and-non-mortar-dry-laid-stone-paving) (preserving the substrate soil), [Developing the 500-hectare Atna Farm industrial agriculture complex on closed hydroponic/aquaponic loops](../food/food-production-and-assurance.md#developing-the-500-hectare-atna-farm-industrial-agriculture-complex-on-closed-hydroponicaquaponic-loops) (the hydroponic farms).
**Conventional-model flaw:** Thick concrete slabs poured as greenhouse floors — destroying the soil and, while creating no latent exergy store, raising the artificial heating and cooling load.
**Tags:** phase-change materials, PCM, thermal floor covering, closed farms, thermal inertia, eliminating the floor pour
**Scale:** Macro — structural (closed farms and greenhouses)
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | The covering is an assembly that replaces the pour, so it can only be won at construction time; chipping out a greenhouse's existing slab to install it costs the production cycle, and an overlay dropped onto an existing floor without a fresh insulation course melts against the subgrade — bleeding the store into the ground it was meant to spare. The record's own promise, a night return "without consuming electricity," silently presumes the insulation layer only a new build lays.
**Cognitive anchor:** The village *sardāb* — the earthen-floored cold cellar that held autumn's fruit sweet through the deep of winter without burning anything, where the ground itself was the fridge and the only machine was the door. [Integrated thermal floor covering with phase-change materials (PCM Thermal-Storage Floor Slab)](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#integrated-thermal-floor-covering-with-phase-change-materials-pcm-thermal-storage-floor-slab) turns the *sardāb*'s floor into an active party: the same mass that once swallowed the night's cool now swallows the noon's heat as well, and pays it back before the seedlings shiver.
**Narrative hook:** "A floor that eats noon and feeds the night — the old cellar's trick, now working for tomatoes, with no kilowatt-hour burned."
**Syn (synergies):** [Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks](directed-energy-transfer.md#fifth-generation-district-heatingcooling-loop-5gdhc--thermal-bus-for-binding-chillers-to-heat-sinks) — the 5GDHC loop; [Real-time exergy balance and monitoring at subsystem level (Real-Time Exergy Ledger)](../information-systems/observability-of-flows.md#real-time-exergy-balance-and-monitoring-at-subsystem-level-real-time-exergy-ledger) — energy accounting
**Source:** Atna Farm justification plan (p. 11)

#### Phase-change thermal batteries (PCM) in the passive envelopes of buildings
**Mechanism:** Adding micro-encapsulated phase-change material (PCM) to the inner layer of the concrete and plaster of the blocks' exterior walls; these materials absorb the day's excess heat by melting at a defined temperature and, refreezing at nightfall, return the stored heat into the interior — minimizing temperature swings inside the suite. In the working sense this documentation set defines, the walls are thermal batteries: energy taken in the sun's cheap hours, returned in the expensive dark ([what "battery" means here](thermal-sources-and-storage.md#terminology-what-battery-means-here)).
**Metrics:** The inner wall surface temperature held in the comfort band (21 to 24 degrees); a 50 percent cut in the buildings' peak cooling and heating load.
**Prerequisites:** Standard PCM building-composite formulations; confirmed chemical stability across the life cycle.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Thin, undefended walls of the conventional building — hot as a kiln in summer, cold as ice in winter — demanding power-hungry mechanical systems.
**Tags:** thermal battery, phase-change materials (PCM), passive architecture, thermal inertia, energy storage
**Scale:** Micro — building (the suites' envelopes)
**Fit & substrate:** [Adaptable to existing fabric (Retrofit/Brownfield)] | The capsules go into the inner plaster and concrete layers — thin wet-trade work that can ride a renovation of the inside face without touching the street, the one asset of this pair that the existing fabric can buy room by room. The force-fit failure is in the layer order, not the chemistry: PCM on the inner face of an uninsulated wall receives the same heat flux a moment later and returns it into the suite when the room no longer asks for it — the envelope battery pays only where the insulation line and a night heat-rejection path (the night-flush its own synergy names) exist to let it refreeze by morning; without them, the wall stores the problem instead of the heat.
**Cognitive anchor:** The regulating suits and spacecraft of advanced thermal-management technology.
**Narrative hook:** "Your home's walls are alive — they swallow the heat by day and give it back at night, so you always stay at a pleasant temperature with no cooler and no heater."
**Syn (synergies):** [Seasonally reversible in-wall HVAC (In-Wall Seasonal Reversible HVAC)](../buildings/placement-and-routing-of-service-flows.md#seasonally-reversible-in-wall-hvac-in-wall-seasonal-reversible-hvac) (the in-wall air handler); [Passive night-flush ventilation cooling (Night-Flush) and neutralization of the radiation trap in climates with diurnal swings above 15 degrees](passive-cooling-paths.md#passive-night-flush-ventilation-cooling-night-flush-and-neutralization-of-the-radiation-trap-in-climates-with-diurnal-swings-above-15-degrees) (passive night ventilation); [Water-storing biosolar roof with evaporative photovoltaic cooling by plant cover (Blue-Green Biosolar Roofs)](../buildings/multifunctional-surfaces.md#water-storing-biosolar-roof-with-evaporative-photovoltaic-cooling-by-plant-cover-blue-green-biosolar-roofs) (the cool roof)

### How the approaches collide and compose
**The two records stabilize the same band for different tenants, and the settlement's ledger cannot pay both in full.** [Integrated thermal floor covering with phase-change materials (PCM Thermal-Storage Floor Slab)](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#integrated-thermal-floor-covering-with-phase-change-materials-pcm-thermal-storage-floor-slab) holds a greenhouse floor at 21 to 24 degrees for roots, returning night heat "without consuming electricity"; [Phase-change thermal batteries (PCM) in the passive envelopes of buildings](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#phase-change-thermal-batteries-pcm-in-the-passive-envelopes-of-buildings) holds the same 21 to 24 degrees in the inner wall surface for people, and prices its result as a 50 percent cut in the buildings' peak load. The band identity composes beautifully on the material side — one PCM family, one supplier chain, one qualification regime for farm and suite alike — and collides immediately on the arithmetic side, because the diurnal curve can only be flattened once per kilowatt-hour: [Phase-change thermal batteries (PCM) in the passive envelopes of buildings](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#phase-change-thermal-batteries-pcm-in-the-passive-envelopes-of-buildings)'s synergy lines enlist the night-flush cooling record and the in-wall reversible HVAC, both of which also move the day's heat into the night, and the carrier document's own peak-shifting rule ([Energy stabilization by carrier separation and time-shifting of consumption (Energy Time-Shifting)](energy-carriers-and-grid-posture.md#energy-stabilization-by-carrier-separation-and-time-shifting-of-consumption-energy-time-shifting), elsewhere) claims the same hours for the grid schedule. One question, two answers. **Envelope credit**: the wall and floor cuts are booked independently — the 50 percent stands as a property of the fabricated layer, measured with the mechanical systems off — which lets each record keep its metric but forces the settlement's energy balance to deduct the overlap somewhere, probably from the night-flush record's claim rather than this page's. **System credit**: the cuts are shares of one diurnal service, apportioned by the real-time exergy ledger [Integrated thermal floor covering with phase-change materials (PCM Thermal-Storage Floor Slab)](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#integrated-thermal-floor-covering-with-phase-change-materials-pcm-thermal-storage-floor-slab)'s own synergies name — which is honest about physics and rewrites every percentage on this page into a contribution rather than a total. Neither answer is stated in either record; the adjudication that approves the pair without choosing will have double-booked the same heat twice, and the double-booking will surface first in [Energy stabilization by carrier separation and time-shifting of consumption (Energy Time-Shifting)](energy-carriers-and-grid-posture.md#energy-stabilization-by-carrier-separation-and-time-shifting-of-consumption-energy-time-shifting)'s 90-percent self-sufficiency arithmetic, three documents away.

**The page's question has an answer recorded only outside itself.** "Buffer in the fabric (PCM) or in separate equipment?" is, on this page, answered twice for the fabric and zero times for the equipment — yet the equipment side is not empty ground: the water and soil stores of `thermal-sources-and-storage.md`, the ice sink included, are exactly the separate-equipment answer, and they claim overlapping duties (the elevated reservoir and the time-shift both price the same peak window). The collision that follows is about substitution: if envelope PCM dissolves a 50-percent peak, equipment sized for the old peak is overspecified — smaller stores, cheaper; if the equipment stores are counted first, the envelope layer is buying a peak the basement already absorbs — and the two investments force-fit each other into stranded cost. The composition, read charitably, is a ladder the records gesture at and none states: the fabric catches the fast daily swing at zero operating cost, the equipment catches what is left after the fabric has taken its share, and the night-flush moves both to the cool hours. What makes the reading dangerous is [Integrated thermal floor covering with phase-change materials (PCM Thermal-Storage Floor Slab)](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#integrated-thermal-floor-covering-with-phase-change-materials-pcm-thermal-storage-floor-slab)'s own prerequisite structure: the floor serves the 500-hectare closed-farm program, the wall serves every suite, and the settlement that adopts both has moved a third of its buffering into layers that cannot be metered, tuned or drained the way a tank can. One question, two answers, owner's to settle: does the envelope keep a **fixed share** of the buffer by right of being built anyway (equipment sized to the remainder), or does the envelope earn its share **competitively** each season — with the equipment answer fully specified first, so a wall that fails its cycle-life prerequisite fails quietly into a network that already covers it? The records cannot answer; the equipment side is in another document, and the direction-of-dependency rule forbids this page from deciding its capacity.

**Sequenced, the pair is a build-order rule rather than a policy choice.** The floor is a greenfield decision — it is cast or lost at pour time, and every greenhouse built without it re-pours the substrate later; the wall is a fabric decision — plasterable into the conventional settlement one renovation at a time, and uniquely among this folder's storage moves it needs no excavation, no shaft, no aquifer. So even before the fabric-versus-equipment question is ruled, the two records already bind the timeline: specify [Integrated thermal floor covering with phase-change materials (PCM Thermal-Storage Floor Slab)](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#integrated-thermal-floor-covering-with-phase-change-materials-pcm-thermal-storage-floor-slab) as a construction-time standard wherever new farms are poured, and let [Phase-change thermal batteries (PCM) in the passive envelopes of buildings](in-fabric-thermal-buffer.md#phase-change-thermal-batteries-pcm-in-the-passive-envelopes-of-buildings) ride renovation cycles, while the unrecorded equipment answer keeps its capacity sized to whatever the fabric has not yet taken.
