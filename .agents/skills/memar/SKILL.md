# Memar
This file holds only what is the agent's own: how an agent's runtime — its context window, its sub-agents, its tools — carries out work the Memar way. Memar's knowledge is in its documents, and how its tools are used is declared by `memar` and the tools it leads to; neither is repeated here. This file is not complete: what it does not say may still be in Memar, so look it up. Where this file disagrees with a document or with `memar`, the document or `memar` wins, and the disagreement is reported.

## Scope
Memar is the framework the work is conducted within ([Framework](/docs/framework.md)), not a library to consult. The work is usually the person's own project — not necessarily software — and [GeniusesGroup/memar](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar) is its reference; do not assume the project is Memar. "Write this in Khayyam" engages this skill without the word Memar.

## Reaching Memar Server
Memar is reached by one of two routes.

- **The `memar` command.** `memar ai get-task-init-context` prints every command with its call shape and a line on what it is for, and an agent app that registered Memar's MCP server lists the same commands as its tools; a chat that opens no task and needs a command runs `memar commands` once. A command's own `--help`, or its tool's description, which is the same text, says its reasons and words: take each named thing's interface from it each time it is used, not from memory. Find and read content the ways it offers, in preference to resolving links by hand or reading whole documents. Resolve a link in this file that begins with `/` the way it offers, never into the folder this copy sits in nor into the project being worked on.
- Before deleting, moving, restoring, or committing files, read or ask `--help` of [`modules-plugable/computer/git/services/_host_git_cli.py`](/modules-plugable/computer/git/services/_host_git_cli.py) and the filesystem services under [`modules-plugable/computer/filesystem/services/`](/modules-plugable/computer/filesystem/services/); for putting text in a file, use a file-write tool or `memar content write`/`edit` (never shell text handling).
- **Where `memar` is absent**, read those same files' opening comments and `--help` ([GitHub: paths under GeniusesGroup/memar](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar)). If they cannot supply what the work needs, stop and say so.

## Consult the governing document before each act
- Each step reads, in this context and not as a delegate's summary, the passages that govern its act and its subject. What an act requires is declared once, by kind, in the lists of [Normative References](/modules/knowledge/content/rules/normative_references/normative_references.md), which owns that claim, and `memar` prints them: open a task with its act's kind (`memar content get-act-context --help` names the kinds), take it up with `memar ai get-task-init-context --task-id ID --without-skill` (without the flag where the runtime has not loaded this file), and read every part it prints before acting. A question answered in chat opens no task: run `memar content get-act-context --kind answer` and read every part first.
- The trigger is the step, not the task. When the act changes — asking the human (`answer`), critiquing (`critique`), delegating, creating, queuing or handing over a task, opening a chat, or asking for a review (`plan`), or another kind than the plan's — run `memar content get-act-context --kind K --after K0 --task-id ID` (K0 each kind already read, ID the task) and read what it prints first; after a compaction, without `--after`. Thinking runs in every step, and no step names it as its act: every kind prints its passages.
- A subject is not a kind: its governing documents are found by searching, with `memar content search` for where a term occurs and `memar content find` for the sections most like the subject (informative only: it ranks by word similarity and binds nobody), for the subject's term and each other term the step uses, `docs/` hits first; read the governing document it surfaces at the section that governs the step, and follow its links where the act depends on them. A subject that enters the work midway is consulted when it enters. A step on a Khayyam construct reads this way [Khayyam](/docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#abstract)'s Abstract, [Separation of Syntax and Governance](/docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#separation-of-syntax-and-governance-a-principle), and [Minimal Legislation](/docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#minimal-legislation-a-principle).
- Read each as [Reading guidance](/docs/README.md#reading-guidance) says, and act from the section that governs the act — not from memory, from training, or from this file; the act's own document says what has to come before it. Where it links a Practice for the act, do the act as that Practice says. A proposal that departs from a passage cites it.
- Where the search finds nothing, proceed as [Cognition Practice → Norm Application Points](/docs/cognition.practice.md#norm-application-points) says for a lookup that matches nothing.

## Task and plan
- Before any other work, open a task through Memar ([Reaching Memar Server](#reaching-memar-server)) with the kind of its act ([Consult the governing document](#consult-the-governing-document-before-each-act)) — or, when your brief hands you one, take that one up and open none. Before opening one, read what `memar task ensure` says (its `--help`, or the `task_ensure` tool's description): it decides when a task needs none and what its plan holds.
- Judge a step by what its development costs over the whole work, rework it prevents included, not by the tokens of its own chat alone.
- A runtime's to-do list or plan mode is not the task's plan: it ends with the context; the plan outlives it.
- Intake binds before work ([Cognition Practice → Norm Application Points](/docs/cognition.practice.md#norm-application-points)) and ends in the written plan, made before reading the material the work is about. Decide each step in it local or delegated by the test under [Delegation](#delegation); a step the plan did not foresee takes the same test before it starts.
- An agent that judges its own reasoning too weak for the work handed to it writes, in the task's report, that and why, sets it Blocked, and asks for another agent to take the task up. Writing a plan and its critique is judgment; a small model runs only mechanical steps — search, count, moves — after the plan is written and reviewed.

## Context window and compaction
- After any compaction or summary of the context — announced or not; a context that opens with a summary has been compacted — re-read this file and the task's plan, and run `memar content get-act-context` for the current act's kind without `--after`: a summary drops rules and decisions that still bind. What was read before a compaction and not since is unverified reading ([What Implementation Asymmetry Obliges](/docs/cognition.md#what-implementation-asymmetry-obliges)).
- The chat's context is a budget as well as each step's: tool output, reports, and the conversation accumulate across steps that each passed the test. Ask a command for the least output that answers — a test run's summary line, a diff's stat.
- Once the context is past about half the window (reported or estimated), take no further step locally: hand what remains of this task to a new chat on a task linked to this one, or delegate it where [Delegation](#delegation) allows a sub-agent.
- A prompt cache reuses only an unchanged prefix, and it lasts about an hour. So the skill, the context its kind printed, and the plan stay unchanged within a chat; a new chat, or a changed prefix, pays the whole context again. Keep a task's work in its own chat rather than extending a long one.

## Handing work to another agent
- A chat opened for a task whose result this chat does not need is left alone once it is open: read nothing from it, poll it for nothing, and do not follow it up or ask it for news. Each look spends this chat's context for nothing; the owner answers its questions there, and a task that needs its outcome reads its report.
- Before you hand work to another agent — a sub-agent, a new chat, a queued task — or wait on one, read what `memar task ensure` says (its `--help`, or the `task_ensure` tool's description where Memar's MCP server is registered) and act as it says: it decides where the work runs, and each verb it names says the rest in its own. The act is then `plan` ([Consult the governing document](#consult-the-governing-document-before-each-act)).

## Delegation
- Delegate a step, with its acceptance check, when what it reads would take a large share of the context and the task needs only its conclusion — measured in bytes, not in files — or when it reruns a script until it comes out clean; keep only the report. Measure before reading; about 20 KB of output or of a single file, and about a quarter of the window for a step's whole reading, are the working marks, not laws. How large a task is, and when tasks are split or merged, is set by [Sizing a Task and Recording Follow-Ups](/modules/knowledge/documentation/handoff/handoff.practice.md#sizing-a-task-and-recording-follow-ups); read it before splitting, merging, or queuing work. The sections this file links and the passages a report cites, read at the cited lines, do not count; nor does what `memar ai get-task-init-context` and `memar content get-act-context` print, whatever its size: it is read whole, every part, in this context. A step that crosses the line midway is delegated from there.
- Sub-agents run one at a time, each to its end, unless the owner asks otherwise: several at once spend the owner's usage limit together and are all cut off midway, leaving half-made edits; and a sub-agent starts no sub-agents of its own.
- A reviewer is a newly started sub-agent that has not seen the work — never the agent that made it, nor a sub-agent resumed from the work.
- Editing a file exempts no step: an edit that carries out a decision already taken — code and its tests, a document change whose content is settled — is delegated by the same test, and only an edit that is itself the decision stays local.
- Keep local the target's structure — built from reports, not from a survey — the decisions over findings, and the merge. Judgment exercised while producing a finding is delegable with its criteria, so calling a step judgment never keeps it local.
- Do not delegate work whose intermediate context is the deliverable, or work that needs the task's decision between its steps; where such work exceeds the size test, split it at those decisions and delegate the parts between.
- A tangent the task will not otherwise need is not a step to delegate; it is handled per [Mid-task tangents](/modules/knowledge/documentation/handoff/handoff.practice.md#mid-task-tangents).

## Asking the human
- Ask as [Cognition Practice → Norm Application Points](/docs/cognition.practice.md#norm-application-points) says — what to settle first, and the shape of the question; an act beyond the instance is confirmed first ([Confirm before large-scope changes](/docs/cognition.md#confirm-before-large-scope-changes)).
- Before an agent brings the owner a question — a line under a report's `Decisions needed`, its own written there first — it hands it to a newly started agent on the strongest model, as `memar ai get-task-questions-answer-brief` says, and brings the owner only what that agent leaves to the owner.
- A question only the owner can answer, found during a task, is written as an entry in the handoff of the document it concerns ([Questions Found During a Task](/modules/knowledge/documentation/handoff/handoff.practice.md#questions-found-during-a-task)); the conversation and the report hold at most a pointer to it, and recording a question never needs a task of its own. A question about this skill or Memar's scripts is the exception that section names: it is queued at once as a task that studies and decides it.
- When the runtime's sandbox or permissions refuse a command, report the refusal or ask for the permission; do not reach the same act by another route.
- A sub-agent has no human to ask: what it would ask, it ends on as blocked or approval-required, with the question stated, and the task that delegated it asks.

## Closing
- Before ending work on a task — finished or not — write its report, with its Status, and set its plan's Status to agree, as `memar task --help` says: a task left Active after its check passed reads to every later task as work still owed.
- Each pass, local or delegated, has a finite boundary, the same acceptance check, and a named end, the Status its report states: Complete, Blocked, or Active ([Status](/modules/knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#status)). Errors and exhausted budgets are never reported as Complete ([Acceptance Criteria](/docs/agency.md#acceptance-criteria)).
- Before staging a commit, ask `_host_git_cli.py commit --help` (and the root `--help`), then run `memar content check` on the files the task changed **before** the commit, and commit only when it reports no problems.
- Close by type per [Handoff Practice](/modules/knowledge/documentation/handoff/handoff.practice.md), each durable result in the home [Results routing](/modules/knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#results-routing) gives it.
