# Process Audit 002 — The Problem Behind a Request
This file is an **audit** of this project's own documents, not a Research. The four disciplines it examined were raised from published notes read outside the repository, but the method was a coverage survey of Memar's own sections against them, and [What Qualifies as an Inquiry Worth This Facet](./documentation-research.md#what-qualifies-as-an-inquiry-worth-this-facet) asks for a method this project's vocabulary does not already supply as well as for material from outside it. It is kept under its own name because the changelog entries that carried its boundary decisions cite it ([Process Changelog](./process.changelog.md), [Modeling Changelog](./modeling.changelog.md)), and its Open Questions live in the base handoffs.

## Question and Goals
- **Question 1:** Does Memar already own the discipline of treating a request as a report of a problem rather than as the problem itself, and if so where? — **Answered**
- **Question 2:** Does Memar own the discipline of a causal analysis that keeps competing candidate causes, marks which statements are observations and which are claims about them, and reports an intervention point rather than only a generating cause? — **Answered**
- **Question 3:** Does Memar own anything about retiring a constraint whose original problem nobody can reconstruct? — **Answered**
- **Question 4:** Does Memar own anything about deciding that a problem is solved — a desired state written before the solution, signals distinguished from the thing itself, a stated limit on what may get worse on the way, and a finish line drawn before results are seen? — **Answered**
- **Question 5 (added mid-inquiry):** When one of these disciplines belongs to Memar, which facet holds it — a concept document, a practice companion, or neither? — **Answered**
- **Goal 1:** establish what Memar's live documents already own, so that each gap is a change to something existing rather than an addition beside it — **Met**
- **Goal 2:** keep each discipline at the layer its own content belongs to, and refuse the change that would make a concept document prescribe how a process is carried out — **Met**
- **Goal 3:** leave every question that belongs to the owner visible rather than answered on his behalf — **Met**
- **Scope:** what Memar's `docs/` owns about understanding a problem, analyzing a defect's causes, retiring a constraint, and judging a problem solved. Excludes a general curriculum on problem solving, any change to how a development is planned or staffed, and any evaluation of the informal material the questions were raised from.

## Methodology
**Questions 1–4.** A survey of Memar's live `docs/` on 2026-10-04, reading the sections that bear on each discipline — [Process → Mechanism-First Reasoning](./process.md#mechanism-first-reasoning), *Intent*, *Expectations and Checks*, and *Defect Resolution as an Inquiry*; [Modeling → Initial Discovery Questions](./modeling.md#initial-discovery-questions) and *Assumptions Are Modeling Targets*; [Modeling Practice](./modeling.practice.md) in full; [Cognition → Discourse Norms](./cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model) and [Knowledge](./knowledge.md) — followed by regex searches of the whole doc set for each notion a discipline would need (`root cause`, `five whys`, `hypothesis`, `intervention`, `desired state`, `guard condition`, `baseline`, `acceptance criterion`, `legacy`, `reversib`, `metric`, `indicator`, `problem statement`). Prior art was not consulted for these four questions: each is answered from Memar's own model, and what Memar does not say is a fact about Memar that no external source can settle.

**Sources.** The four disciplines were raised from a set of informal published notes read outside the repository. Per [Documentation → Citations → Source standing](./documentation.md#citations) they are recorded here as the origin of the questions and cited nowhere: no wording, example, or claim from them supports any finding below, and every finding was checked against Memar's own documents instead. A published book of record on problem solving (Pólya, *How to Solve It*) was read to test coverage, since its four-phase structure — understand the problem, devise a plan, carry it out, look back — is the nearest formal statement of the same ground; it is named as prior art and carries no finding, because what the phases expose here is which of them Memar states and which it does not.

**Known blind spots.** The four disciplines arrive as a set, and this inquiry tested them as one set; whether any of the four is owed a home independently of the others was not asked, since the owner raised them together. Whether Memar should own problem-solving as a subject at all — and therefore whether a document should exist that no discipline of ours names — was answered from Memar's stated scope rather than argued from the size of the gap, and an owner who reads the scope differently can reopen it. The informal notes were read in full but are not assessed on their own claims: this record judges nothing they say, only what Memar independently holds.

## Findings
### Answering Question 1: the concept is owned twice, and the missing piece was already deliberately placed in Practice
**The principle Memar states.** [Process → Mechanism-First Reasoning](./process.md#mechanism-first-reasoning) names the shape directly: "A recurring design error is to encounter a technical mechanism and search for a problem that appears to justify it", and [Process → Common Modeling Errors](./process.md#common-modeling-errors) adds "Treating a familiar pattern as a requirement." [Process → Intent](./process.md#intent) states that "Intent should not be reduced to a user request." Memar therefore already holds that what a requester proposes is not the statement of what is wrong.

**The practice Memar states.** [Modeling → Assumptions Are Modeling Targets](./modeling.md#assumptions-are-modeling-targets) holds that "statements from stakeholders should be treated as hypotheses rather than facts" and that "the responsibility of the modeler is not to record assumptions. — The responsibility of the modeler is to test them," and [Modeling Practice → Starting a New Requirement](./modeling.practice.md#starting-a-new-requirement) carries the counterpart: "The wording supplied by a requirement, stakeholder, or existing model is evidence to investigate, not an authoritative model boundary."

**The gap was scoped out on purpose, and the scoping holds.** [Modeling](./modeling.md) twice declines to carry the entry-point questions, stating that "The concrete entry-point questions a modeler asks before proposing any node are execution practice rather than architectural definition, and are not restated here." That decision is correct under the boundary the owner set while reviewing this inquiry: a concept document states what something is, and a session's opening questions are how a session is carried out. The consequence of the decision was that the questions which existed nowhere in Memar — what improves if this is done, and whose problem it is — had no home, since the existing seven are all about graph shape.

**What changed.** The three step-back questions and the two-layer answer were added to [Modeling Practice](./modeling.practice.md#stepping-back-from-the-requirement), where the entry-point questions already are and where they are conduct rather than concept (Space Bunny Alpha — claimed). No concept document was changed for this question (Omid Hekayati — decided).

### Answering Question 2: the epistemic half was owned and under-specified; the structural half is not Memar's to prescribe
**What Memar already holds.** [Process → Defect Resolution as an Inquiry](./process.md#defect-resolution-as-an-inquiry) states the inquiry's expectations, requires "observations that discriminate among candidate causes", and names the characteristic failure: "confirmation, the discrepancy-noticing that only confirms the first candidate cause." [Cognition](./cognition.md) asks what else explains the same phenomena, and [Protocol](./protocol.md) requires hypotheses to be testable and falsifiable. Memar's position is not missing; it is stated in the vocabulary of awareness and never made into a requirement the inquiry's evidence has to satisfy.

**The defect was that one candidate was structurally permitted.** The topic asks the inquiry to return "the generating cause identified, or the inquiry closed without one", and a single-cause answer satisfies it as written. A tidy linear account therefore passes every test Memar states, while [Expectations and Checks](./process.md#expectations-and-checks) — which can only catch a departure from stated intent — has nothing to return a verdict on, because a cause supported only by being consistent with the observations available was never checked against the model (Space Bunny Alpha — argued).

**What changed.** The topic now states two properties of the inquiry's evidence: candidate causes are held in the plural, and every candidate names the observation that would refute it. Both follow from positions the document already held — the confirmation failure and the requirement that evidence discriminate — so this is a specification of what the topic meant, not a new discipline imported into it (Space Bunny Alpha — argued).

**What was deliberately not added.** Three things were left out, each because the owner corrected the direction of the inquiry and each still open (Omid Hekayati — decided; recorded under Open Questions): the decomposition of one incident into separately asked questions — why it occurred, why it reached a participant, why it was not detected earlier, why it lasted — because a concept document states what an inquiry is and not the procedure a team follows while running one; the causal tree as the shape of the record, for the same reason; and the intervention point as a decision the inquiry returns, because whether a single generating cause is the right outcome of an inquiry is a question about the topic's own definition rather than an application of it.

### Answering Question 3: nothing owns it, and that is a fact worth having rather than a gap to fill
**No Memar document governs retiring a constraint.** The nearest statements are three, and none is a discipline: [Agency → Historical Continuity](./agency.md) treats prior conceptual vocabulary as knowledge that must not be erased, [Knowledge](./knowledge.md#definition) makes "Why was this architecture chosen?" a canonical knowledge question, and [Modularity](./modularity.md) judges a Module's removal by its consequence rather than by the problem its existence solved. Asking the question is Memar's habit; obliging anyone to answer it before deleting anything is not stated anywhere.

**The three fates are already distinguishable in what Memar holds, so the missing part is the obligation.** Whether the original problem still exists, whether the constraint remains the right answer to it, and whether a different constraint now answers it better are three positions a reader can already take; a document nowhere requires a retiring party to take any of them, which is why a ten-year-old rule with no known origin can be removed by someone who never asked (Space Bunny Alpha — argued).

**The disposition.** Rule is the concept a constraint belongs to — [Rule](./rule.md) defines a Rule as "an authored, replaceable claim" and a constraint as one kind of Rule — so a rule, a mandatory step, a compatibility branch, and a legacy guard are one shape, and the shape's home is where a retiring party would look. But Memar has no practice companion for retiring one, and the boundary that put entry-point questions in Modeling Practice rather than in Modeling applies here with more force: a session's opening questions and a change's closing questions are both conduct. The question therefore went to [Rule Handoff](./rule.handoff.md) as an open question rather than into a document that does not exist yet (Space Bunny Alpha — claimed).

### Answering Question 4: the ordering discipline is fully owned; the rest is unowned, and none of it is Process's to say
**What Memar holds, and holds well.** [Process → Expectations and Checks](./process.md#expectations-and-checks) is the strongest of the four: expectations "precede every particular enactment — an instance can be checked against them precisely because they were stated first", and a statement written after the enactment "is able to confirm what was built yet unable to catch a departure from what was intended." [Test → The Expectation Is Written Before the Work](./protocols/process/test.md) and [Test Practice](./protocols/process/test.practice.md) carry the operational form. Memar has the half of this discipline that is hardest to retrofit onto a team that has not adopted it.

**What Memar does not hold, stated as absences found by search.** No document defines a desired state; the phrase appears once, undefined, in [Agency](./agency.md). The words guard condition, baseline, and finish line appear in no document. "Root cause" appears four times across the repository, always as a singular upstream assertion and never as a discipline, and [Type](./type.md) goes so far as to state that "the root cause usually lies upstream", which is the tidiest-story form the discipline exists to catch. The nearest thing to a guard is [System → The Inseparability of Capability and Constraint](./system.md#the-inseparability-of-capability-and-constraint), which is an architectural claim rather than a success criterion, and the nearest thing to an anti-gaming rule is [Cognition Practice](./cognition.practice.md): "A dimension is never promoted into a threshold because it is measurable" — a rule about which number to trust, with nothing about declaring the number before the work or about what must not get worse while it improves (Space Bunny Alpha — claimed).

**Why none of it was applied.** Every part of this discipline is a statement about how work is judged rather than about what a process is, and the two documents that would have to change are a concept document and a protocol specification. The finish-line rule in particular is a requirement on the order of enactment, which is [Process](./process.md)'s subject — but the *content* of what is declared is a property of the work, not of the process, and stating it in Process would make the document prescribe a practice for every development rather than define the concept it owns. It went to [Process Handoff](./process.handoff.md#expectations-and-checks) unanswered (Omid Hekayati — decided).

### Answering Question 5: the disciplines split along the facet line Memar already draws, and none of the four became a new document
**The split.** Two of the four are conduct and belong in a Practice companion; two are epistemic conditions on work Memar already recognizes, and the one gap among those is a practice nobody owns yet. No discipline needed a new concept document, and none needed a new concept: [Terminology](./terminology.md) defines no `Problem`, and the definitions the four depend on — Intent, Check, expectation, Rule, constraint — are all already defined.

**The correction that changed the direction of this inquiry.** My first proposal put the step-back questions, the causal decomposition, and the finish-line rule into [Process](./process.md), reasoning that a document which owns Intent and the inquiry is the natural home for the disciplines that qualify them. That proposal was wrong on the owner's boundary and would have been wrong on the document's own terms: `process.md` states what a process is, and "how a development should be carried out" is not that. The correction moved two of the three into [Modeling Practice](./modeling.practice.md) and left the third as an open question (Omid Hekayati — decided; Space Bunny Alpha — argued). The finding survives the correction intact: Memar was never missing a document on this subject, and the two changes worth making were both small.

**What the whole set says about Memar's shape.** Each of the four arrived as a technique someone found useful, and in every case Memar already held the position the technique serves — mechanism after process, intent not reduced to a request, hypotheses tested as hypotheses, expectations before enactment, evidence that discriminates. What it lacked in three of the four cases was the place where conduct belongs, and in the fourth a condition already implied by a rule it had written down. That is a framework behaving as a framework, and it is the reason no new document was proposed (Space Bunny Alpha — argued).

## Open Questions
- **Should an inquiry's causes be organized as a tree across separately asked questions** — why the defect occurred, why it reached a participant, why it was not detected earlier, why it lasted as long as it did — rather than as the candidates of one question? The topic now requires plural candidates and a refuting observation each; it does not require the candidates to be organized, and it still returns a single generating cause.
- **Should the point at which a change would keep a class of these from arising be a decision the inquiry returns in its own right,** or an interpretation layered on its finding by whoever reads it? The second answer is the current one, stated nowhere.
- **Should a check's expectations carry a baseline and a declared limit** — what must not get worse on the way to the outcome — so that a result is read against something declared before the work rather than against whichever number each reader prefers? And is that second half a concept Memar should name, or an application of [System → The Inseparability of Capability and Constraint](./system.md#the-inseparability-of-capability-and-constraint) that needs no name?
- **Does retiring a Rule, a mandatory step, or a compatibility branch need a stated procedure,** and if so whose practice companion is it? [Rule](./rule.md) owns the concept; no Memar document owns the act of retiring one.
- **Is `terminology.md` complete without an entry for the problem a process serves,** given that four independent disciplines all needed the word and none of them found it? The entry's absence was recorded as a finding rather than supplied here, since defining it is the owner's.
- **Should Memar state anywhere that its scope includes problem solving as a subject,** or does [README → Where Memar Stops](./README.md) settle it by omission? A framework's limits are checkable only where it names them.

## Notes
This inquiry is the second against [Process](./process.md), and the two overlap on method rather than subject: Research 001 asked what a development decomposes into, this one asked what a request is. Both ended in the same place — the concept was already owned, and what was missing was a home for conduct or a condition implied by a rule already written. The pattern is worth recording because it is the failure mode a framework is most likely to have: reading a technique as a missing document.

The informal material that prompted the four questions was read in full and is named nowhere in this record. That is the rule this inquiry also produced, applied to itself: [Documentation → Citations](./documentation.md#citations) now requires both a rights test and a standing test before a source is recorded, and the questions these notes raised are the strongest case for why — a source of no standing can be the right thing to have read and still the wrong thing to have written down.
