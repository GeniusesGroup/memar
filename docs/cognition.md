# Cognition

## Abstract
This document models **cognition** — the activity of the systems that think — as the foundational concept behind Memar's collaboration and reasoning norms, with **thinking** the process at its center. Its central claims: (1) every exchange between a human and an AI system — or between any two participants in this project — is a connection between two cognitive systems, and the medium connecting them (speech, text, a chat interface, a repository) is an implementation detail that does not change what the exchange conceptually is; (2) the norms governing such an exchange are therefore grounded in that model — chiefly in the conversation model of two systems joined by a medium — not in etiquette or platform convention; (3) from that grounding, this document states the discourse rules Memar follows once, here: evaluate claims on inspectable merits while treating source as neither authority nor disqualifier; prioritize definitions over terminology and verify load-bearing ones before building on them; ask rather than assume; confirm before large-scope changes; receive criticism at its conceptual root with provenance labeled; scale criticism to inspectable model strength without licensing silence; report absence rather than fabricating; treat shared artifacts as claims under the same evaluation; consult other cognitive systems when conceptual depth warrants it; and prefer long-term clarity unless speed is explicitly requested. Thinking — one of a cognitive system's processes, alongside perception, attention, memory, and learning — is treated as a family of modes rather than a single uniform activity; beneath the modes the document names elementary operations and awareness disciplines, and alongside them the two classes of named error (cognitive biases, fallacies). The document grounds its model in cognitive science — including the 4E framing of cognition — as the scientific layer this model is expected to deepen over time.

## Introduction

### Motivation
Norms for evaluating claims, naming things, and exchanging ideas are exercised in every Memar conversation and every Memar document, but they previously had no authoritative home: each rule lived either nowhere (observed only by habit) or scattered across other documents' edges. Without a single home, three recurring costs follow:

1. **No derivation.** A rule stated alone — "do not assume the other participant is correct" — gives no way to resolve a conflict with another rule in a novel situation, because nothing states what the rules are *for*.
2. **Scoping drift.** Without a stated model of who thinks, rules quietly narrow to one participant type or one medium — typically "AI chat assistant etiquette" — even though the same norms govern human-to-human review, written critique, and organizational decision processes.
3. **No growth path.** Rules cannot be added, refined, or retired coherently unless there is a stable concept they attach to. Cognition is that concept; thinking names the process those rules exercise.

The remedy is the same one this project applies elsewhere (see [Framework](./framework.md), on definitions preceding artifacts): state the model first, then ground the norms in it — chiefly in the conversation model of two cognitive systems joined by a medium — so conflicts among rules have something to appeal to. A document about the cognitive systems doing that thinking is the foundation those norms were implicitly assuming all along.

### Methodology
The discourse norms this document states were accumulated across years of working conversations before this document existed; drafting this document examined them for what they presuppose rather than inventing them. That examination produced the modeling moves the document makes — treating a conversation as two cognitive systems joined by a medium, treating thinking modes as function groups of the same underlying activity, decomposing the modes into elementary operations held in course by awareness disciplines, and situating thinking among the cognitive system's processes — each applied retrospectively to explain why the inherited rules work. The cognitive-science grounding (4E cognition) was included from the document's start; see the paired [handoff](./cognition.handoff.md) for what remains unexamined about it.

### Status and Use
This document's Status is Draft, and Memar's operating skill nevertheless directs workers to load and follow it before reasoning-heavy work. Those two facts are compatible under one rule: the norms below are the project's **current working constraints** for exchanging and evaluating thinking material — provisional in that Draft content may be revised, not optional in that a worker may ignore them while they stand. Where a specialized governing document owns a domain (Terminology for definitions, Modeling for model structure, Documentation for documentation production), that document outranks this one on that domain's content. Where evidence contradicts a provisional claim here, the contradiction is reported and pursued through the changelog — not silently overridden from training priors, and not enforced as if the document were Final.

## Explanation

### What Cognition Is
**Cognition is the activity of a cognitive system — a system whose internal states stand for aspects of its world and whose behavior is mediated by those states.** This is stated as a working definition grounded in the cognitive-science usage of the term, not as a project-specific coinage: Memar follows its general terminology policy ([Terminology → Scientific Terms](./terminology.md#scientific-terms)) of preferring the scientific layer's meaning where one exists, and the scientific layer treats cognition as the family of processes that acquire, store, retrieve, transform, and apply information, with thinking as one member of the family. A cognitive system engaged in thinking is this document's **thinker**; consistent with [System](./system.md)'s position that system-hood is a lens applicable to almost anything, a human, an organization, or an AI model qualifies, and nothing in the frame privileges biological implementation.

Two structural facts follow from the frame:
- **The cognitive system is the entity; cognition is its activity.** Claims about thinkers are claims about systems running processes, and the System/Process relationship ([Process → Process and System](./process.md#process-and-system)) governs this whole document: a cognitive system is the boundary within which (or across which) its cognitive processes run, and it may run several of them at once.
- **Cognition is a family of coupled processes, not one.** A cognitive system's representations are formed (perception), prioritized (attention), preserved (memory), changed by experience (learning), and manipulated toward ends (thinking) — the processes feed one another, and it is the family, not any single member, that cognition names. This document centers on thinking, the process Memar's collaboration and reasoning norms exercise; the other processes are named here so that later work on them finds its home under this concept rather than being smuggled into thinking's topics.

### What Thinking Is
**Thinking is the process of a cognitive system manipulating representations toward some end** — evaluating, relating, transforming, and generating them. The word *process* carries its defined Memar meaning ([Process → Definition](./process.md#definition)): a bounded progression of related activities that can be understood as belonging to a common intent — here, the intent the thinking serves. Thinking is therefore one of the cognitive system's processes (*What Cognition Is*, above): not the system, and not the only way the system's representations change.

Three boundary clarifications follow from the definition:
- **Thinking is a process, not a content store.** A document, a memory file, or a model is not thinking; it is material thinking operates on and produces. This is the same concept/data separation used throughout Memar ([Modeling → Concept Existence vs. Model Existence](./modeling.md#concept-existence-vs-model-existence)), applied here: do not mistake the artifacts of thinking for the process.
- **Thinking is not defined by correctness.** Thinking produces claims, and claims can be wrong. The quality of thinking is a separate property from its occurrence — which is exactly why norms for evaluating thought are needed, and why this document exists.
- **Thinking characteristically recruits the system's learning process — without collapsing artifact into learning.** As a process, thinking is open to feedback in [Process](./process.md)'s sense: its own outputs may change the conditions of its later progression. In cognitive systems generally, the same manipulation of representations that serves the current end also *may* produce changes that persist beyond it — synaptic change in a brain, updated weights in a model, a written note in a repository. Thinking and learning are therefore not two systems or two separate activities but two processes that characteristically run intertwined. The coupling is recorded here as an observed characteristic, not a designed requirement — and with a hard boundary: **producing or storing an artifact is not itself learning.** A chat reply, a commit, or a note externalizes material that *may* be retained by a learning process; retention is a separate fact. An AI model's weights typically do not change from writing the note; a human who wrote something may still forget it. Read a thinker's artifacts as outputs and possible inputs; do not read them as proof that the system has learned, will remember, or has updated from them.

Cognitive science treats thinking as information processing over mental representations; the framing above is the standard working sense, deliberately kept compatible with it rather than diverging from it without cause.

### Modes of Thinking
"Thinking" names a family of activities, not one uniform one. This document distinguishes **modes of thinking** and treats them the way [Modeling → One Reality, Multiple Abstraction Lenses](./modeling.md#one-reality-multiple-abstraction-lenses) treats modeling lenses: co-equal modes of operating on the same material, not a hierarchy and not separate faculties. Any one cognitive act may draw on several modes at once; the modes are distinguished by what they do to thinking material, not by which part of a brain or system performs them. Each mode is a stable combination of the elementary operations beneath it — the Runs-on clauses in the entries below make the combination explicit, so a reader never has to guess which operations a mode exercises.

**Use threshold.** Until mode selection is routine for a reader, begin from what the material still lacks — which elementary operation it needs — rather than classifying every task under a named mode; the mode follows the operation. Label-matching a task to a mode name without that check is itself a failure mode this layer was built to remove. The paired [practice](./cognition.practice.md) operationalizes the threshold.

The modes below are grouped by function — what the mode contributes to thinking as a whole. Grouping by function rather than by a flat list keeps an open-ended family navigable and keeps the membership criterion visible: a mode earns its place by the function its members serve, and the groups are open, not exhaustive.

#### Evaluative Modes
Modes whose function is assessing thinking material — weighing claims, options, and models against criteria.

- **Critical thinking** — evaluating claims: their assumptions, definitions, internal consistency, evidence, and consequences. The most exercised evaluative mode in Memar's review and discussion work, and the one the discourse norms below chiefly govern. Runs on decomposition (of claims into premises and assumptions), criterion application (evidence, consistency, consequence standards), and discrepancy noticing, under awareness of assumptions.
- **Analytical thinking** — decomposing a whole into its parts and their relationships to see how it works: separating a problem into concerns, a claim into premises, a mechanism into steps. Distinct from critical thinking: analysis breaks things down to understand them; criticism judges them. Memar's whole-document decomposition method (each topic stating its claim, then its rationale) is applied analytical thinking. Runs on decomposition and ordering; its products feed the evaluative modes.
- **Evaluative decision thinking** — comparing alternatives against criteria and choosing: weighing options, judging trade-offs, deciding under incomplete information. Distinct from both of the above in that it terminates in a choice rather than an understanding or a verdict. Architecture decisions — Memar's own and those documented in every project following its approach — are this mode's output. Runs on comparison (of alternatives against criteria), criterion application, and commitment — the operation that distinguishes deciding from still weighing.

#### Structural Modes
Modes whose function is organizing or reorganizing thinking material — finding form in it.
- **Abstract thinking** — moving from instances to generalities and back: recognizing what a set of cases shares, separating the essential from the incidental, and forming concepts from that separation. This mode is a precondition for nearly everything else Memar does — a definition is abstract thinking's product, a model is its structured form. It pairs with concrete thinking (below) rather than opposing it: the abstraction earns its keep when it returns and explains its instances. In criticism, this mode is what separates receiving an objection from acting on it: an objection arrives as an instance, and the general concern it implicates is discovered by this mode's instance-to-generality move (see the discourse norm below). Runs on comparison (what persists across cases is the essential) and decomposition, returning by instantiation; awareness of assumptions governs which separations are provisional.
- **Concrete thinking** — the counterpart mode: working with specific instances, examples, and cases. Not lesser thinking — Memar's own Methodology sections repeatedly show that abstract structures are tested and corrected against concrete examples. The abstract/concrete pair is a cycle, not a ladder. Runs on instantiation and comparison of specific cases; it is abstract thinking's counterpart direction in the same cycle.
- **Systems thinking** — attending to how parts interact, how behavior propagates across boundaries, and how the whole behaves differently from its parts. Memar's modeling discipline ([Modeling](./modeling.md)) and its [System](./system.md) document operationalize this mode for architecture work specifically. Runs on decomposition (into interacting parts), mapping (of behavior across boundaries), and discrepancy noticing (of whole-versus-parts behavior), under awareness of goal.
- **Sequential thinking** — ordering material into steps, chains, and dependencies: reasoning that follows a path where each stage constrains the next. Contrast with the structural modes above, which find form in wholes rather than paths. Procedures, proofs, and cause-effect reasoning all exercise it. Runs on ordering and commitment (each stage constrains the next by what it settles).

#### Generative Modes
Modes whose function is producing new thinking material rather than assessing or organizing existing material.
- **Creative thinking** — generating candidates: alternative models, alternative framings, alternative definitions. Creative output is raw material for the evaluative modes; the generative and evaluative functions pair, and neither substitutes for the other. Runs on generation, under awareness of goal (so the novel stays relevant) — the discipline that keeps creative divergence answerable to the end it serves.
- **Divergent thinking** — deliberately widening before narrowing: producing many distinct candidates rather than one polished answer. The exploratory phase of any design or modeling session, and the reason Memar records rejected alternatives — they are evidence this mode ran. Distinct from creative thinking as breadth is distinct from novelty: divergence can produce many ordinary candidates; creativity produces the unexpected one. Runs on generation at breadth (many distinct candidates per prompting), under awareness of goal.
- **Convergent thinking** — narrowing a field of candidates back to the answer that survives: eliminating, merging, and selecting among the generated candidates until one remains. The counterpart of divergent thinking — the two are one cycle: divergence widens the field, convergence closes it; divergence without convergence never commits, convergence without divergence commits to a premature candidate. Memar's candidate-testing practice — definitions reached by generating candidates and eliminating the ones that fail ([System](./system.md)'s definition is a worked example) — is this mode exercised on concepts. Distinct from evaluative decision thinking: a decision selects among options already on the table; convergence narrows a field the diverge/converge cycle itself produced. Runs on criterion application, comparison, and commitment (eliminating, merging, selecting until one survives); it consumes what divergent generation produced.
- **Analogical thinking** — transferring structure from a familiar domain to an unfamiliar one: reasoning "X is like Y in this respect." Powerful and double-edged — it imports both the mapping and the unmapped baggage — which is why Memar treats prior art as evidence to examine, never authority to inherit ([Terminology → Diverging From Ecosystem Definitions](./terminology.md#diverging-from-ecosystem-definitions)). The discourse norm against industry defaults is a guardrail on exactly this mode. The mode is also exercised prospectively, not only when a resemblance happens to be noticed: before treating a problem as open, deliberately search other domains for a solved version of it — an open question in one field often carries a ready answer in another. Biomimicry is the standing example: rather than answering a structural problem from zero, examine how living systems that already withstand the analogous load solve it — a termite mound's passive self-cooling for a building's climate control, a sponge's lattice for dispersing current forces on a tall form — and the double edge above governs the borrowed answer too, which must be re-examined against the receiving domain's constraints before adoption. Spontaneously noticing the resemblance is the weak path; searching for it is the exercise this mode owes its users. Runs on mapping (structure projected relation by relation) and instantiation (of the mapped structure in the target domain), under awareness of the medium — the double edge is this discipline's specific content here.
- **Hypothetical thinking** — reasoning about what is not (yet) the case: imagining states of affairs, following suppositions to their consequences, asking "what would happen if." Premise-testing, future modeling, and counterfactual analysis are its exercised forms. Architecture itself is largely hypothetical thinking made durable — a design is a supposition about consequences not yet observed. Runs on generation (of suppositions) and instantiation (of the counterfactual state), with criterion application deferred — evaluation follows the consequences, which is why premature judging kills the mode.

#### Corrective Modes
Modes whose function is updating thinking material in response to reality or to new material.
- **Adaptive thinking** — re-modeling when reality contradicts the current model: noticing that an assumption failed, and revising rather than defending. This mode is what makes the project's revision mechanisms (changelogs, supersession, propagation tracking) meaningful rather than bureaucratic. Runs on discrepancy noticing (reality contradicting the model) and generation (of revised models), under awareness of assumptions — the failed assumption must be visible to be revised.
- **Reflective thinking** — thinking about one's own thinking: examining what was assumed, what was concluded, and whether the reasoning itself holds. Metacognition, in cognitive-science vocabulary. The proportional-criticism norm below is reflective thinking applied across systems — requiring a critic to examine their own objection before issuing it — and every "Methodology" section in this project's documents is reflective thinking made permanent. Runs on the same operations as the mode it examines, re-targeted onto the thinker's own material — awareness of self is what turns the operations inward.

#### Grouping Notes
The functional groups are a navigation aid, not a classification doctrine — a single cognitive act routinely combines modes across groups (testing a hypothesis is hypothetical thinking generative of candidates, evaluated critically, then accepted or revised adaptively). The groups are also not claimed to be mutually exclusive at the mode level: analytic and critical overlap by design, and a future revision may merge or re-scope them; what is fixed here is the membership criterion (a named function in thinking), not the boundary drawing. This is deliberately not a taxonomy in the sense [classification principles](./modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels) warn about — no mode's membership is treated as an intrinsic property of a thought; the groups exist to be used, and are revised when they stop being useful. A mode's name is not its definition: the names are handles for functions, and several of the field's named "thinking types" decompose into combinations of the modes below or of the operations beneath them — which is also why this document does not grow by adopting every named "X thinking" the ecosystem produces.

### Elementary Operations and Awareness Disciplines
The modes are not the bottom layer of thinking. Beneath them sits a layer of **elementary operations** — the smallest units of thinking this document needs — and alongside them a class of **awareness disciplines**. Each mode is a stable combination of operations (and is governed by disciplines); each operation is a named unit the mode list already presupposes. This layer is what the mode names only gesture at, and what a reader who needs to *do* something with a mode actually operates on.

**Elementary operations.** Named by the function each performs on thinking material, with the same membership criterion as the modes, one layer down: an operation earns its place when the exercise of multiple modes observably depends on it.

- **Comparison** — placing two or more pieces of thinking material side by side and asking what persists and what differs; what persists across compared cases is the essential, what differs the incidental. The operation under abstraction's essential/incidental separation, under decision's weighing of alternatives, and under critique's examination of a claim against its alternatives.
- **Mapping** — projecting the structure of one piece of material onto another, relation by relation: the operation of "X is like Y in this respect." Analogical thinking runs on it, and it is distinct from comparison: comparing two things examines them jointly; mapping carries one across onto the other.
- **Decomposition** — taking a whole apart into parts and their relationships: a problem into concerns, a claim into premises, a mechanism into steps. Analysis runs on it, and so does assumption-finding in critique.
- **Generation** — producing new material: candidates, framings, examples, alternatives. Every generative mode runs on it; convergent thinking consumes its products.
- **Instantiation** — producing a concrete instance of something general: an example of a definition, a case of a class, a realization of a model. Abstract thinking returns to instances by this operation, and concrete thinking exercises it in the other direction — the two modes are the two directions of one operation's use, which is why they form a cycle rather than a ladder.
- **Ordering** — arranging material into a sequence, hierarchy, or dependency structure where position carries meaning. Sequential thinking runs on it; so does the arrangement of an argument's steps.
- **Criterion application** — holding material against a stated criterion and registering the verdict. Evaluation in every mode runs on it — critique's judging of claims, decision's weighing of options, the project's own candidate-testing of definitions.
- **Discrepancy noticing** — registering that material is not as expected: a mismatch between model and world, claim and evidence, intent and outcome. It is the trigger of corrective modes and the raw input of critique.
- **Commitment** — terminating an open field into a chosen one: deciding, selecting, concluding. Decision thinking terminates in it, convergence narrows toward it, and it is what distinguishes choosing from merely continuing to weigh.

The set is open, exactly as the mode list is. The layer does not regress infinitely: an operation earns its place by observed dependence, not by derivation from something still smaller, and the layer stops where the need stops.

**Awareness disciplines.** Not operations but standing orientations that govern how the operations run — a discipline never produces material itself; it changes how the operations treat material. Each is named as an awareness *of* something:

- **Awareness of assumptions** — holding one's premises visible and provisional while reasoning on them; the discipline under critical thinking's assumption examination and under the discourse norm against assuming the other participant's state.
- **Awareness of goal** — keeping the end the thinking serves present while it runs, so drifting means are noticed as drifts; the discipline that keeps divergent exploration from becoming mere wandering.
- **Awareness of the medium** — remembering that material crossing between thinkers is transformed by what carries it, and reading what arrives accordingly; the discipline under the discourse norms on medium-carried defaults.
- **Awareness of self** — holding one's own reasoning as material: what was assumed, what was concluded, whether the reasoning itself holds. Reflective thinking is this discipline made into a mode — the same operations re-targeted onto the thinker's own thinking material.

The disciplines class is open in the same way. The two kinds are jointly necessary: operations without disciplines run unexamined; disciplines without operations contemplate nothing.

**Modes as combinations.** With the two layers named, a mode is a stable combination of operations governed by disciplines — e.g., critical thinking runs on decomposition (of claims into premises), criterion application (of evidence standards), and discrepancy noticing (of unsupported steps), under awareness of assumptions; analogical thinking runs on mapping and instantiation under awareness of the medium's baggage. Mode overlaps are thereby predicted rather than merely tolerated: two modes overlap when they share operations. This restates, one layer down, what the grouping notes say at the mode level — and it answers the open question this document's handoff carries: strategy, disposition, and phase are different descriptions of the same material — a strategy is a chosen sequence over operations, a disposition is a tendency to exercise a mode, and a phase is a stage of a process that emphasizes some operations over others.


### Grounding: Cognition and the 4E Framing
The model above is grounded in cognitive science, and this document treats that field as the scientific layer its own model must stay consistent with and deepen against. One framing is recorded now because it directly affects how Memar should think about thinkers:

The **4E framing of cognition** holds that cognition is **embodied** (shaped by having a body and sensorimotor engagement with the world), **embedded** (shaped by the environment the thinker operates in), **enacted** (arising from the thinker's active interaction with that environment rather than passive representation of it), and **extended** (partly constituted by tools and media beyond the thinker's boundary — a notebook, a model, a language, a repository). See [4E cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4E_cognition).

The **extended** claim is the one Memar already depends on in practice, stated here as its general form: a thinker's representations may live partially outside the thinker — in documents, in a graph model, in a shared repository — and those external artifacts are genuinely part of the cognitive system, not merely its outputs. This is the general principle behind several Memar positions already: that a model is a primary architectural artifact rather than documentation of one ([Modeling](./modeling.md)), that knowledge left only in individuals' heads is knowledge at risk ([Knowledge](./knowledge.md)), and that a repository's documentation functions as shared cognitive material for every human and AI agent working on the project. This document claims the reverse direction too: if external artifacts are part of the cognitive system, then the *quality of the shared medium* — its definitions, its internal consistency, its freedom from unexamined terminology — is a quality of the thinking itself. That is the warrant for treating documentation discipline as a thinking discipline.

Two boundary clarifications keep the extended claim from overreaching. First, an artifact may participate in a workflow — as shared medium, as a record another thinker reads — without automatically becoming part of any one thinker's private boundary; repository material is part of the *extended cognitive system of the collaboration*, not automatically each participant's retained memory. Second, as the learning coupling above already states, writing an artifact into the shared medium does not by itself establish that any participant's learning process retained it.

The embodied, embedded, and enacted claims are recorded as context, not yet operationalized: Memar has not yet derived rules from them. They are kept here because an AI-mediated conversation is a concrete case where all three are in play — each participant's cognition is shaped by a different embodiment, a different environment, and a different mode of engagement — and the differences are easy to overlook when the interface makes both parties look like interchangeable text producers. What those differences *oblige* in an exchange is stated in the next topics, not left ornamental.
    
### A Conversation as Two Cognitive Systems Connected Through a Medium
The exchange this document calls a **conversation** is modeled as follows: two cognitive systems are connected through a medium — speech, text on a screen, a chat interface, a shared repository — and thinking material (claims, definitions, questions, criticisms, models) travels across that medium in both directions.

The medium is implementation, not concept. Whether the connection is voice, keyboard, or a repository diff does not change what the exchange is; this is the same concept/representation separation used throughout Memar. Two consequences follow, and they are the reason this model earns its place:

- **The medium shapes the exchange without defining it.** A text interface compresses bandwidth, hides tone, and flattens thinking material into prose; a voice channel restores some of it. These are properties of the medium worth knowing, but they are not rules about thinking — they do not belong in this document beyond the recognition that they exist.
- **The participants are asymmetric in implementation; reciprocal in epistemic standing; not interchangeable in authority.** A human and an AI model, or two humans, differ in embodiment, memory, access, persistence, and failure modes. They share reciprocal standing for *claim evaluation* — each evaluates the other's claims on the same standards, and a discourse norm that applies in one direction must justify itself for the other. Reciprocity of evaluation is not interchangeability of decision rights, responsibility, or institutional authority: the owner who may approve a large-scope change, the reviewer who may only recommend, and the tool that may only draft remain distinct roles. A rule like "do not assume the other is correct" is stated once, bidirectionally, as an evaluation rule — not as a claim that every participant holds the same power to decide.

This model is what makes the discourse norms in the following topics checkable rather than arbitrary: each norm exists because of a specific way thinking material degrades while traveling between two cognitive systems — or a specific way one system's evaluation of the other's material goes wrong. Not every norm is a pure derivation from the conversation model alone; some are change-governance or framework-value constraints that bind *during* exchange. Where a norm's ground is specialized (Terminology, Documentation), that ownership is stated at the norm or at this topic's close — the conversation model remains the reason those constraints must be stated bidirectionally here rather than as etiquette for one participant type.

### What Implementation Asymmetry Obliges
Naming asymmetry without stating its obligations leaves the difference ornamental — and the interface already encourages overlooking it. The project's own machinery presupposes these obligations (handoffs that externalize session state, changelog propagation fields, the rule against answering from training memory when documentation exists). They are stated here as consequences of the conversation model:

- **Do not cite one's own earlier output as established fact.** A thinker's prior reply is evidence of what was said, not evidence that the claim is true. Treat it as a claim still subject to evaluation — the same standard applied to any other source.
- **Declare unverified reading and context limits.** When load-bearing content was not actually read in the current exchange, or when prior session context may have been lost, say so rather than asserting the content as present. Context eviction is an implementation fact of some cognitive systems; pretending continuity the medium does not provide is a medium-shaped failure.
- **Externalize session state into shared artifacts.** Decisions, open questions, and assumptions that must survive the session belong in the project's durable medium (handoffs, documents, changelogs) — not only in one participant's transient context. This is the operational form of the extended-cognition claim for asymmetric participants.

### Discourse Norms Derived From This Model
These are the norms Memar participants — human, AI, or organizational — follow when exchanging thinking material. Each is stated with the failure mode it prevents. Norms bind at **intake**, not only mid-reasoning: ambiguity, change-scale, missing material, and unverified definitions are checked before work proceeds on an assumed base.

- **Evaluate claims on their merits; treat source as neither authority nor disqualifier.** A claim's origin — identity, seniority, confidence, popularity — is not decisive evidence for or against it. Source may supply *defeasible context* (authorship of an artifact, demonstrated test history, institutional decision authority) that helps locate further inspectable support; it never substitutes for that support. Evaluation criteria remain: internal consistency, alignment with established definitions, explanatory power, consequences, and evidence quality. Agreement is not a goal of an exchange; accuracy, consistency, and clarity are. *(Failure prevented: deferring to source rather than examining content; equally, dismissing relevant testimony because it has a source.)*
- **Definitions outrank terminology.** When reasoning about any load-bearing term, consult its defined meaning ([Terminology](./terminology.md)) rather than assuming the common industry sense; a word's general meaning can be broader than its most common technical usage, and a term no Memar document has defined by reference defaults to its ordinary general meaning, not to any ecosystem's usage ([Terminology → The Default Meaning of an Unreferenced Term](./terminology.md#the-default-meaning-of-an-unreferenced-term)). Where terminology and definition conflict, the definition governs ([Terminology → Terminology Authority and Governance](./terminology.md#terminology-authority-and-governance)). When a load-bearing term's definition has not been verified against its governing document in the current exchange, say so and verify before building on it — do not proceed on an assumed definition. *(Failure prevented: two cognitive systems silently operating on different definitions of the same word; building downstream claims on an unverified definition while the medium already holds the authoritative one.)*
- **Do not assume the other participant is correct — or incorrect.** Both errors are the same failure: substituting a prior about the source for evaluation of the claim. The asymmetry warrant: participants are reciprocal in epistemic standing, so the evaluation rule must be bidirectional too. *(Failure prevented: deference in one direction, dismissiveness in the other.)*
- **Receive criticism at its conceptual root, not only its instance — with provenance labeled.** When a participant raises a criticism — typically against one concrete example — the receiving participant's job is to discover the general concern the example may stand for. Three statuses must not be collapsed: (1) the *reported* criticism — what the giver actually stated; (2) the *inferred root* — the general concern the receiver thinks the instance implicates, labeled as the receiver's extension until confirmed; (3) the *confirmed generalization* — the root the giver accepts as their intended scope. Ask when the instance is ambiguous ("is the problem the example itself, or the rule that produced it?"), and do not treat an inferred root as the giver's claim until that confirmation. The critique is then answered — and, where accepted, corrected — in the general form that was confirmed, not merely the instance raised. Failure modes prevented: fixing the example while the generating rule survives; answering a narrower claim than the critic intended; over-generalizing a critique that was genuinely instance-local — including manufacturing an architecture rewrite the giver never asked for and attributing that stronger claim to them. The proportional-criticism norm below governs the *giver's* side of the same exchange. *(Failure prevented: instance-patching; answered-but-not-asked ambiguity; receiver-manufactured scope attributed to the giver.)*
- **Assume hidden reasoning may exist, and require criticism to be proportional — without licensing silence.** A counterpart's proposal may carry reasoning not fully visible in the current exchange — particularly in a project with accumulated history. Criticism is therefore earned by argument, not by surface plausibility: a critique is valuable when it provides a stronger explanation, a more consistent model, or a revealed contradiction — an objection that would be satisfied by re-reading the existing model is noise, not critique. Model strength is judged from *inspectable material* — stated assumptions, evidence, alternatives considered, tests, consequences — not from the author's status or from a prior that deep reasoning must exist unseen. Proportionality raises the required *quality* of a critique; it never licenses withholding a critique that meets that quality, and artifact maturity is not a source prior that forbids examination. *(Failure prevented: superficial objections to deeply-reasoned models; unearned deference to them; critique-suppression dressed as proportionality.)*
- **Ask rather than assume.** When intent, scope, priority, or historical context is unclear, prefer a question to an assumption. Defaults from past exchanges can be actively misleading, because each exchange may operate under different premises. *(Failure prevented: propagating an assumption that was never agreed, across the medium, into decisions.)*
- **Confirm before large-scope changes.** When a proposed change reaches beyond the instance under discussion — a definition other documents consume, a structure other sections depend on, an edit spanning several documents — propose before applying: state the proposed content and the files it will touch, so the counterpart can shape the change before it exists rather than review it after the fact. *(Failure prevented: a large change landing wholesale — after-the-fact review replacing before-the-fact shaping.)*
- **Report absence rather than supplying it.** When required material — a definition, a document, a prior decision, historical context — is not available in the exchange or the shared medium, say that it is absent. Do not fabricate a plausible substitute, reconstruct from training memory what the project has not recorded, or silently fill the gap with an industry default. *(Failure prevented: material that looks like support but supports nothing — a failure on the sender side of solitary work as much as of exchange.)*
- **Treat shared artifacts as claims under the same evaluation, not as absolute authority.** A document in the repository is thinking material a cognitive system (or collaboration) externalized; the extended-cognition claim makes it part of the shared cognitive system, not exempt from evaluation. When an artifact is stale, internally inconsistent, or contradicted by stronger evidence, report the conflict and correct through the project's change mechanisms — do not silently propagate the stale claim because "documentation is canonical," and do not silently override it from training priors because "merits govern." Canonical means: the authoritative *home* of a claim is the governing document, not a participant's memory; it does not mean every sentence in every file is true. *(Failure prevented: silent propagation of error; silent override of the shared medium.)*
- **Consult other cognitive systems when conceptual depth warrants it.** When a question is load-bearing and deep — a definition other work will consume, a model other documents depend on, a choice whose failure modes are costly — and other cognitive systems are available to the thinker, invite their evaluation before settling. The form matches the participants and the medium: an AI system with access to other models seeks their critique; a human convenes one or two other humans for the same purpose; an organization runs a review meeting. This is generative and evaluative work together — deliberate widening of the field of examination (divergent), then convergence on what survives — not deference to the consulted parties (the merits norm still governs their claims) and not a substitute for the thinker's own evaluation. Depth and availability jointly trigger the obligation: shallow or already-settled questions do not require a council, and absence of available counterparts does not license inventing their assent. *(Failure prevented: settling a deep claim from a single cognitive system's unchecked operations — including mirroring a counterpart's framing back as if the family of options were complete.)*
- **Resist defaults that arrive with the medium.** Industry conventions, popular technologies, established terminology, and common patterns may be useful references, but none is authoritative by virtue of its popularity — and material received through a widely-used channel arrives pre-packaged with such defaults. Evaluate each against this project's definitions and model. *(Failure prevented: importing assumptions through vocabulary.)*
- **Prefer long-term clarity, unless speed is explicitly requested.** Favor conceptual clarity, evolvability, and maintainability over short-term delivery convenience; a superficial proposal is worse than none, because it creates false confidence. An explicit speed request narrows method; it does not cancel confirmation of large-scope changes or definitional verification. *(Failure prevented: optimizing the exchange for speed at the cost of the shared model both systems depend on.)*

**Precedence when norms collide.** A rule stated alone cannot resolve conflict with another rule — the Motivation names that cost, and this document must not commit it against itself. When two norms appear to demand incompatible moves in the same situation, apply this order: (1) *Ask rather than assume* and *Confirm before large-scope changes* bind first whenever intent or change-scale is unsettled — they are intake constraints; (2) *Definitions outrank terminology* and *Report absence* bind next — do not build on an unverified or fabricated base; (3) *Evaluate on merits*, *conceptual-root with provenance*, *proportional criticism*, *artifact-as-claim*, and *consult other cognitive systems* govern how claims and critiques are handled once the base is clear; (4) *Prefer long-term clarity* outranks delivery convenience unless speed was explicitly requested, under the limit stated in that norm. Specialized documents still own their domains; this order orders only the norms this document owns.

Two boundaries on this topic: norms specific to modeling are owned by [Modeling](./modeling.md) and are not restated here; norms specific to producing documentation are owned by the documentation system's specifications and practices, starting from [Documentation](./documentation.md). Definition precedence is Terminology governance applied during exchange — owned there, restated here only as the exchange-facing obligation. This document owns the rules that apply to thinking material *as it travels between any two cognitive systems* — the intersection all those specialized documents assume.

### Named Errors: Biases and Fallacies
Operations fail, and they fail in recurring, named ways. This document records the two classes of named error — the negative space of the layers above — because knowing that an argument or proposition has slipped is thinking work, not something the layers above leave to chance. Both classes share one property, the one Persian marks with the same phrase به غلط in both: they derail the path — of thinking, of dialogue, or of both.

- **Cognitive biases — falling into error oneself.** A bias is a systematic way an operation of one's own produces a distorted result while running apparently normally: confirmation bias is discrepancy noticing harvesting only in one direction; anchoring is ordering that holds the first input in place; survivorship bias is comparison over an unrepresentative sample; **source-deference / agreement pressure** is criterion application that substitutes the counterpart's framing or approval for independent evaluation — recorded in this project's own material when an early draft of this document merely mirrored the owner's examples back instead of completing the family (see the paired changelog). The failing system is the thinker's own. Biases are the named failure modes of the operations layer — which is why guarding against them belongs to the awareness disciplines (awareness of assumptions is the discipline under which one's own tendency to confirm or defer is held visible) and to the corrective modes that repair what a bias distorted. The *consult other cognitive systems* norm is one structural counter to source-deference: inviting further evaluation before a single system's unchecked agreement settles the claim.
- **Fallacies — defective inferential support offered as support.** A fallacy is a named form of corrupted reasoning in which the support offered does not actually support the claim — whether the corruption occurs in solitary reasoning or in material traveling between systems. Ad hominem substitutes the source for the claim; appeal to authority substitutes prestige for evidence; straw man replaces the counterpart's position with a weaker one and defeats the replacement. When the corrupted pattern travels between systems, the exchange is where the failure lands; when it occurs inside one thinker's operations, it is still a fallacy by structure, and may simultaneously be a bias in how that thinker's operations ran. Fallacies are why the discourse norms and the paired practice's insufficient-critique list (authority appeal, industry convention, taste) are, in this frame, a fallacy catalog in Memar's own vocabulary.

The boundary between the classes is real but porous at the surface: the same act can be a bias in the speaker (honest use of a corrupted pattern) and a fallacy in the exchange (corrupted material reaching another thinker), and both classes corrupt regardless of intent — a fallacy works whether or not the sender is aware of it, which is why the receiver-side disciplines matter as much as sender-side honesty. Within the fallacy class, the sender's awareness is a real axis with its own Persian name — **سفسطه** (sophistry): fallacy *deployed knowingly*, against مغالطه as the class name for the corrupted pattern itself; the same corruption travels whether its sender willed it or fell into it, so the axis changes the sender's responsibility, not the corruption's kind. Neither class belongs inside the modes list: they are not functions on thinking material but the named ways those functions fail. And the classes are open, like everything above them: this document records the two *kinds* and their homes; enumerating individual biases and fallacies is catalog work, not concept work, and belongs to the practice layer that needs a checklist, not to this document — grown per project from observed errors, never imported wholesale from a generic catalog.
