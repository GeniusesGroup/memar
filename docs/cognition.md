# Cognition

## Abstract
This document models **cognition** — the activity of the systems that think — as the foundational concept behind Memar's collaboration and reasoning norms; **thinking** is the cognitive process it develops furthest, not its whole subject. Its central claims: (1) every exchange between a human and an AI system — or between any two participants in this project — is a connection between two cognitive systems, and the medium connecting them (speech, text, a chat interface, a repository) is an implementation detail that does not change what the exchange conceptually is — including that the medium's language is not identical with the representation in which a cognitive system does its best thinking; (2) the norms governing such an exchange are therefore grounded in that model — chiefly in the conversation model of two systems joined by a medium — not in etiquette or platform convention; (3) from that grounding, this document states the discourse rules Memar follows once, here: evaluate claims on inspectable merits while treating source as neither authority nor disqualifier; prioritize definitions over terminology and verify load-bearing ones before building on them; ask rather than assume; confirm before large-scope changes; receive criticism at its conceptual root with provenance labeled; scale criticism to inspectable model strength without licensing silence; report absence rather than fabricating; treat shared artifacts as claims under the same evaluation; consult other cognitive systems when conceptual depth warrants it; and prefer long-term clarity unless speed is explicitly requested. The model has two levels. At the level of cognition — what every process (perception, attention, memory, learning, thinking) shares — it names **cognitive material**, the representations the processes work on (claims, definitions, assumptions, questions, goals); the **standards** that material is held to wherever a process takes it in or gives it out — clarity before all others, and with it accuracy, precision, relevance, depth, breadth, consistency, significance, fairness, and sufficiency; the **awareness disciplines**, attention held on assumptions, goal, medium, and self while other processes run; and **cognitive biases**, the systematic distortions any process can fall into. At the level of thinking, it models thinking by its **elementary operations** — what thinking does to material, finding assumptions in what it receives among them — rather than by named kinds, and names **fallacies**, defects in the support an inference offers. The field's named kinds of thinking are compositions of these components; read as a division of thinking they are a [false taxonomy](./modeling.md#false-taxonomies-hide-shared-structure), because no component belongs to one kind and no kind may drop a standard. The document grounds its model in cognitive science — including the 4E framing of cognition — as the scientific layer this model is expected to deepen over time.

## Introduction

### Motivation
Norms for evaluating claims, naming things, and exchanging ideas are exercised in every Memar conversation and every Memar document, but they previously had no authoritative home: each rule lived either nowhere (observed only by habit) or scattered across other documents' edges. Without a single home, three recurring costs follow:

1. **No derivation.** A rule stated alone — "do not assume the other participant is correct" — gives no way to resolve a conflict with another rule in a novel situation, because nothing states what the rules are *for*.
2. **Scoping drift.** Without a stated model of who thinks, rules quietly narrow to one participant type or one medium — typically "AI chat assistant etiquette" — even though the same norms govern human-to-human review, written critique, and organizational decision processes.
3. **No growth path.** Rules cannot be added, refined, or retired coherently unless there is a stable concept they attach to. Cognition is that concept; thinking names the process those rules exercise.

The remedy is the same one this project applies elsewhere (see [Framework](./framework.md), on definitions preceding artifacts): state the model first, then ground the norms in it — chiefly in the conversation model of two cognitive systems joined by a medium — so conflicts among rules have something to appeal to. A document about the cognitive systems doing that thinking is the foundation those norms were implicitly assuming all along.

### Methodology
The discourse norms this document states were accumulated across years of working conversations before this document existed; drafting this document examined them for what they presuppose rather than inventing them. That examination produced the modeling moves the document makes, each applied retrospectively to explain why the inherited rules work. It treats a conversation as two cognitive systems joined by a medium. It models thinking by its components: the elementary operations thinking runs, the standards every input, step, and output is held to, and the awareness disciplines that govern the operations. It reads the field's named kinds of thinking as compositions of those components. And it situates thinking among the cognitive system's processes. The cognitive-science grounding (4E cognition) was included from the document's start; see the paired [handoff](./cognition.handoff.md) for what remains unexamined about it.

### Status and Use
This document's Status is Draft, and Memar's operating skill nevertheless directs workers to load and follow it before reasoning-heavy work. Those two facts are compatible under one rule: the norms below are the project's **current working constraints** for exchanging and evaluating cognitive material — provisional in that Draft content may be revised, not optional in that a worker may ignore them while they stand. Where a specialized governing document owns a domain (Terminology for definitions, Modeling for model structure, Documentation for documentation production), that document outranks this one on that domain's content. Where evidence contradicts a provisional claim here, the contradiction is reported and pursued through the changelog — not silently overridden from training priors, and not enforced as if the document were Final.

## Explanation

### What Cognition Is
**Cognition is the activity of a cognitive system — a system whose internal states stand for aspects of its world and whose behavior is mediated by those states.** This is stated as a working definition grounded in the cognitive-science usage of the term, not as a project-specific coinage: Memar follows its general terminology policy ([Terminology → Scientific Terms](./terminology.md#scientific-terms)) of preferring the scientific layer's meaning where one exists, and the scientific layer treats cognition as the family of processes that acquire, store, retrieve, transform, and apply information, with thinking as one member of the family. A cognitive system engaged in thinking is this document's **thinker**; consistent with [System](./system.md)'s position that system-hood is a lens applicable to almost anything, a human, an organization, or an AI model qualifies, and nothing in the frame privileges biological implementation.

Two structural facts follow from the frame:
- **The cognitive system is the entity; cognition is its activity.** Claims about thinkers are claims about systems running processes, and the System/Process relationship ([Process → Process and System](./process.md#process-and-system)) governs this whole document: a cognitive system is the boundary within which (or across which) its cognitive processes run, and it may run several of them at once.
- **Cognition is a family of coupled processes, not one.** A cognitive system's representations are formed (perception), prioritized (attention), preserved (memory), changed by experience (learning), and manipulated toward ends (thinking) — the processes feed one another, and it is the family, not any single member, that cognition names. This document's subject is cognition, not thinking alone. What belongs to every process — the material they all work on, the standards that material is held to, the attention held on it, the biases that distort it — is stated at the level of cognition, below. Thinking is the process the document develops furthest, because Memar's collaboration and reasoning norms exercise it most; the other processes are named so that later work on them finds its home here rather than being folded into thinking's topics.

### Cognitive Material
**Cognitive material** is the representations a cognitive system holds and its processes work on: claims, concepts and the definitions that fix them, assumptions, questions, goals, models. Perception forms it, attention selects it, memory preserves it, learning changes what is retained of it, and thinking manipulates it; when two cognitive systems exchange, it is what travels between them ([A Conversation as Two Cognitive Systems](#a-conversation-as-two-cognitive-systems-connected-through-a-medium)). Material belongs to no single process, which is why the standards it is held to are stated here and not under thinking.

One kind of material needs its own definition because it is so often mistaken for an act: an **assumption** is a representation the system holds as true and acts on without having verified it in the present context. Assumptions exist whether or not anyone looks for them — in a perception taken at face value, in a recalled fact never rechecked, in a premise a claim leaves unstated. Finding them in material is an act of thinking ([Decomposition](#elementary-operations-of-thinking)); holding them in view while other processes run is attention ([Awareness of assumptions](#awareness-disciplines)); the assumption itself is material.

#### Standards of Cognitive Material
A standard is a criterion cognitive material is held to wherever a process takes it in or gives it out: as **input** — the question, brief, evidence, or claim received; in **process** — each step that works on it; and as **output** — the claim, decision, recollection, or artifact returned. This holds for every cognitive process, not only thinking: what was read is held to accuracy against its source before any thinking runs on it ([Keep reading and concluding separate](#what-implementation-asymmetry-obliges)), and what is recalled is held to the same accuracy as what is reasoned. A standard earns its place when it applies wherever material appears — at each of the three points, whichever process runs — and when failing it is a way cognition goes wrong that no other standard names. No kind of thinking owns a standard, and none may drop one: a task foregrounds some standards over others, and a standard it does not foreground still binds. Only clarity is ranked; the others are unordered, and which of them a task foregrounds follows from the task.

- **Clarity** — the material can be understood as it is meant: its load-bearing terms defined or definable ([Definitions outrank terminology](#definitions-outrank-terminology)), its claim restatable in other words and illustrable by an example. Clarity comes first because every other standard is judged on a clear statement: an unclear claim cannot be judged as it is meant, only as the judge guesses it was meant. (This is clarity of a piece of material; the norm [Prefer long-term clarity](#prefer-long-term-clarity-unless-speed-is-explicitly-requested) concerns the shared model's legibility over time, which this standard serves but does not name.) An unclear input is asked about before work proceeds on it ([Ask rather than assume](#ask-rather-than-assume)); an unclear step is restated until it can be checked; an unclear output hands its unfinished work to its reader.
- **Accuracy** — the material is true to what it describes: the world, the evidence, the source as it was actually read ([Keep reading and concluding separate](#what-implementation-asymmetry-obliges); [Report absence rather than supplying it](#report-absence-rather-than-supplying-it)).
- **Precision** — the material is as specific as its purpose needs: its scope bounded, its cases and quantities named, no vaguer than its support allows and no more exact than its support warrants.
- **Relevance** — the material bears on the question it serves. Material that is accurate and precise but answers another question is drift, and awareness of goal is what notices it.
- **Depth** — the material reaches the complexities that make the question hard — what it rests on and what follows from it — rather than stopping at its surface terms ([Cognition Practice → The Conceptual Stack](./cognition.practice.md#the-conceptual-stack)).
- **Breadth** — the material has considered the other framings, viewpoints, and alternatives that bear on the question, not only the first one available ([Consult other cognitive systems](#consult-other-cognitive-systems-when-conceptual-depth-warrants-it)).
- **Consistency** — the material's parts do not contradict one another, its terms keep one meaning throughout, and its conclusions follow from the support offered — that the support, if true, bears on the conclusion at all. Most fallacies are named failures of it.
- **Significance** — among relevant material, what matters most to the end is given the weight it carries, and the incidental is not given the same weight.
- **Fairness** — other positions, other sources, and the system's own are held to the same standards; none is weakened to be defeated or strengthened to be kept ([Evaluate claims on their merits](#evaluate-claims-on-their-merits-treat-source-as-neither-authority-nor-disqualifier); [Do not assume the other participant is correct — or incorrect](#do-not-assume-the-other-participant-is-correct--or-incorrect)).
- **Sufficiency** — the support is enough for what is concluded or committed to: a claim no stronger than its support, a commitment made once the field is closed enough and not before ([Proportional criticism](#assume-hidden-reasoning-may-exist-and-require-criticism-to-be-proportional--without-licensing-silence)).

Three boundaries keep the members distinct. Consistency asks whether the support bears on the conclusion; sufficiency asks whether there is enough of it. Accuracy asks whether each statement is true to what it describes; a conclusion stronger than its evidence fails sufficiency even when every statement in its support is accurate. Depth goes down into one framing — what it rests on, what follows from it; breadth goes across to the other framings.

The set starts from the intellectual standards of the critical-thinking literature (Paul and Elder), adopted standard by standard under the criterion above rather than wholesale. Two members differ from their nine: *logic* is named consistency, after the term Memar's norms already use, and sufficiency is added. The word *critical* is dropped, and so is the restriction to thinking: the standards bind all cognitive material, whichever process handles it.

### Awareness Disciplines
An awareness discipline is attention held on something while other processes run — a standing orientation, not an operation. A discipline never produces material itself; it changes how the processes it accompanies treat material, which is why it belongs to attention, the process that prioritizes representations, rather than to thinking. Each is named as an awareness *of* something.

- **Awareness of assumptions** — holding one's assumptions visible and provisional while perceiving, recalling, or reasoning on them; the discipline under which thinking's finding of assumptions does its work, and under the discourse norm against assuming the other participant's state.
- **Awareness of goal** — keeping the end a process serves present while it runs, so drifting means are noticed as drifts; relevance and significance are judged against the goal it holds in view, and it keeps generation at breadth from becoming wandering.
- **Awareness of the medium** — remembering that material crossing between cognitive systems is transformed by what carries it, and reading what arrives accordingly; the discipline under the discourse norms on medium-carried defaults, and under mapping's re-examination of borrowed structure. One transformation it names specifically: the language (or other encoding) of the medium is not identical with the representation in which a cognitive system does its best thinking — collapsing the two is a medium-shaped failure of this discipline.
- **Awareness of self** — holding one's own cognition as material: what was perceived, assumed, recalled, concluded, and whether the reasoning holds — the standards and operations re-targeted onto the system's own processes (metacognition, in cognitive-science vocabulary). Every Methodology section in this project's documents is this discipline made permanent, and the proportional-criticism norm is this discipline applied across systems: a critic examines their own objection before issuing it.

The class is open. Disciplines without processes contemplate nothing; processes without disciplines run unexamined.

### What Thinking Is
**Thinking is the process of a cognitive system manipulating representations toward some end** — evaluating, relating, transforming, and generating them. The word *process* carries its defined Memar meaning ([Process → Definition](./process.md#definition)): a bounded progression of related activities that can be understood as belonging to a common intent — here, the intent the thinking serves. Thinking is therefore one of the cognitive system's processes (*What Cognition Is*, above): not the system, and not the only way the system's representations change.

Three boundary clarifications follow from the definition:
- **Thinking is a process, not a content store.** A document, a memory file, or a model is not thinking; it is material thinking operates on and produces. This is the same concept/data separation used throughout Memar ([Modeling → Concept Existence vs. Model Existence](./modeling.md#concept-existence-vs-model-existence)), applied here: do not mistake the artifacts of thinking for the process.
- **Thinking is not defined by correctness.** Thinking produces claims, and claims can be wrong. The quality of thinking is a separate property from its occurrence — which is exactly why norms for evaluating thought are needed, and why this document exists.
- **Thinking characteristically recruits the system's learning process — without collapsing artifact into learning.** As a process, thinking is open to feedback in [Process](./process.md)'s sense: its own outputs may change the conditions of its later progression. In cognitive systems generally, the same manipulation of representations that serves the current end also *may* produce changes that persist beyond it — synaptic change in a brain, updated weights in a model, a written note in a repository. Thinking and learning are therefore not two systems or two separate activities but two processes that characteristically run intertwined. The coupling is recorded here as an observed characteristic, not a designed requirement — and with a hard boundary: **producing or storing an artifact is not itself learning.** A chat reply, a commit, or a note externalizes material that *may* be retained by a learning process; retention is a separate fact. An AI model's weights typically do not change from writing the note; a human who wrote something may still forget it. Read a thinker's artifacts as outputs and possible inputs; do not read them as proof that the system has learned, will remember, or has updated from them.

Cognitive science treats thinking as information processing over mental representations; the framing above is the standard working sense, deliberately kept compatible with it rather than diverging from it without cause.

### The Components of Thinking
Thinking is modeled here by what it is made of, not by the kinds of it the field has named. A named kind — "critical thinking," "systems thinking" — is a bundle: one label carries several components at once, and the label says neither which components it carries nor that other kinds carry the same ones. Finding assumptions is credited to critical thinking, yet a systems analysis that skips it is not a lighter systems analysis but an imprecise one; clarity of the question, of the reasoning, and of the answer is owed by every kind and owned by none. Treatments of each named kind therefore keep re-deriving the same components, because without them the kind cannot be stated precisely. This document states those components once, so a reader works on them directly — as a molecule is described by its atoms rather than by a list of other molecules — and reads the named kinds afterward as compositions of them ([Named Kinds of Thinking Are Compositions](#named-kinds-of-thinking-are-compositions)).

What is thinking's own is its **elementary operations** — what it does to material. The rest of what an act of thinking draws on belongs to cognition as a whole and is stated there: the [material](#cognitive-material) it works on, the [standards](#standards-of-cognitive-material) that material is held to, the [awareness disciplines](#awareness-disciplines) that accompany it, and the [biases](#named-errors-biases-and-fallacies) that distort it. Thinking's own named errors are fallacies, defects in the support an inference offers.

#### Elementary Operations of Thinking
The smallest units of thinking this document needs, each named by the function it performs on cognitive material. An operation earns its place when many acts of thinking observably depend on it. The layer does not regress into ever-smaller units: it stops where that dependence stops, not where derivation could continue.

- **Comparison** — placing two or more pieces of material side by side and asking what persists and what differs; what persists across compared cases is the essential, what differs the incidental. Forming a concept, weighing alternatives, and examining a claim against its rivals all run on it.
- **Mapping** — projecting the structure of one piece of material onto another, relation by relation: the operation of "X is like Y in this respect." Distinct from comparison: comparing examines two things jointly; mapping carries one across onto the other. Mapping imports the unmapped baggage along with the mapping, so a borrowed structure is re-examined against the receiving domain's constraints before adoption — which is why Memar treats prior art as evidence to examine, never authority to inherit ([Terminology → Diverging From Ecosystem Definitions](./terminology.md#diverging-from-ecosystem-definitions)). It is run prospectively, not only when a resemblance happens to be noticed: before treating a problem as open, search other domains for a solved version of it. Biomimicry is the standing example — a termite mound's passive self-cooling for a building's climate control, a sponge's lattice for dispersing current forces on a tall form.
- **Decomposition** — taking a whole apart into parts and their relationships: a problem into concerns, a mechanism into steps, a system into interacting parts, a claim into its premises. Memar's whole-document method, each topic stating its claim and then its rationale, is decomposition applied to writing. Run on received material, decomposition together with inference is **finding assumptions**: separating what the material states from what it must take as true without stating — the act by which an [assumption](#cognitive-material), which exists whether or not anyone looks, becomes something examined.
- **Generation** — producing new material: candidates, framings, examples, suppositions, alternatives. Run at breadth, it produces many distinct candidates; aimed at novelty, the unexpected one.
- **Instantiation** — producing a concrete instance of something general: an example of a definition, a case of a class, a realization of a model. Moving from instances to a generality runs on comparison; returning to instances runs on instantiation. The two directions form a cycle, not a ladder: an abstraction earns its keep when it returns and explains its instances, and abstract structures are tested and corrected against concrete cases. Receiving a criticism at its root is the same cycle run on an objection — it arrives as an instance, and comparison discovers the general concern it implicates.
- **Ordering** — arranging material into a sequence, hierarchy, or dependency structure where position carries meaning: the steps of a procedure or a proof, the stages of an argument, a chain of causes.
- **Inference** — drawing a conclusion from material that supports it: from premises to what follows from them (deduction), from cases to a general claim (induction), from an observation to the explanation that would best account for it (abduction). The forms differ in how strongly support carries over to the conclusion, which is why consistency and sufficiency are the standards an inference answers to first. Its forms lean on other operations — induction on comparison, abduction on generation — but none of them is the carrying-over itself, and inference keeps its place by the criterion above: almost every act of thinking that reaches a conclusion depends on it. Run backward — from a claim to what must hold for it to hold — it is how unstated premises are found. Inference derives what follows; commitment, below, closes the field on it — a thinker may infer a conclusion and still not commit to it.
- **Criterion application** — holding material against a criterion and registering the verdict. The [standards](#standards-of-cognitive-material) are the criteria it applies to cognitive material itself; a domain's own criteria — a test's expectation, a definition's rules — are applied by the same operation.
- **Discrepancy noticing** — registering that material is not as expected: a mismatch between model and world, claim and evidence, intent and outcome. It triggers revision and is the raw input of critique; the project's revision mechanisms — changelogs, supersession, propagation tracking — exist so that what it registers changes the shared model rather than being defended against.
- **Commitment** — terminating an open field into a chosen one: deciding, selecting, accepting a conclusion as settled. Every decision ends in it, and it is what distinguishes choosing from continuing to weigh.

#### How the Components Combine
An act of thinking is a combination: some operations, run on material under some disciplines, with their inputs, steps, and outputs held to the standards. The parts are jointly necessary: operations without standards produce material nobody can judge, and standards without operations judge nothing. Three familiar descriptions of thinking are descriptions of such combinations, so none needs a layer of its own. A **strategy** is a chosen sequence over operations: widening by generation at breadth, then narrowing by criterion application, comparison, and commitment, is the diverge/converge cycle — divergence without convergence never commits, convergence without divergence commits to a premature candidate. Memar records rejected alternatives because they are the evidence that the widening ran, and its practice of reaching a definition by generating candidates and eliminating those that fail ([System](./system.md)'s definition is a worked example) is that cycle run on concepts; following a supposition to its consequences before judging it is generation, instantiation, and inference with criterion application deliberately deferred, because judging too early ends the supposition. A **disposition** is a tendency to run a combination. A **phase** is a stage of a process that emphasizes some operations over others.

### Named Kinds of Thinking Are Compositions
The field names kinds of thinking — critical, analytical, systems, creative, and many more — and each name is useful: it points at a recognizable combination and at a literature about it. Read as a division of thinking, the set of names is a [false taxonomy](./modeling.md#false-taxonomies-hide-shared-structure): each kind is a composition of the components above, the kinds share most of their components, and the names credit shared components to single kinds. This document therefore uses the names only to translate. Material that arrives asking to "think critically" or to "apply systems thinking" is read into the components it asks for, and the work proceeds on those.

**Use rule.** Begin from what the material lacks — which operation it needs, which standard it fails — rather than from which named kind the task resembles; a name follows from the components, never the reverse. A name never licenses dropping a component: a creative task still owes its output clarity and relevance, an analytical one still owes its assumptions found. The paired [practice](./cognition.practice.md#working-with-the-components) carries the rule into work.

The table translates names. It is a translation aid, not a membership list. Its first rows are carried over from the mode list this document used to hold, and from now on a row is added when a name keeps arriving in the project's work. No name earns a section of its own, because what such a section would hold is already a component. The columns show emphasis only: each row lists the operations a name most visibly combines and the discipline and standards it foregrounds, and every discipline and every standard binds every row.

| Name | Operations it combines | Discipline it leans on | Standards it foregrounds (all still bind) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | decomposition (of claims into premises), inference, criterion application, discrepancy noticing | assumptions | accuracy, consistency, sufficiency, fairness |
| Analytical | decomposition, ordering | goal | clarity, precision, depth |
| Decision | comparison (of alternatives already on the table), criterion application, commitment | goal | significance, sufficiency |
| Abstract | comparison, decomposition, then instantiation on return | assumptions | clarity, depth |
| Concrete | instantiation, comparison of specific cases | goal | precision, accuracy |
| Systems | decomposition (into interacting parts), mapping (of behavior across boundaries), discrepancy noticing (of the whole against its parts); for architecture, [Modeling](./modeling.md) and [System](./system.md) carry it into work | goal | breadth, depth |
| Sequential | ordering, inference, commitment (each stage settling what the next may do) | goal | consistency |
| Creative | generation aimed at novelty | goal | breadth, relevance |
| Divergent | generation at breadth | goal | breadth |
| Convergent | criterion application, comparison, commitment, run on a field the cycle's own generation produced | goal | sufficiency, significance |
| Analogical | mapping, instantiation | medium | accuracy (of the mapping), relevance |
| Hypothetical | generation, instantiation, inference, with criterion application deferred | assumptions | consistency, relevance |
| Adaptive | discrepancy noticing, generation (of a revised model) | assumptions | accuracy |
| Reflective | any operation, re-targeted onto the thinker's own thinking | self | all of them |

Decision and convergence combine the same operations; what separates them is where their field comes from — a decision selects among options already on the table, convergence narrows a field its own diverge/converge cycle produced. That two rows can differ only in their end is the table's lesson: what makes a kind recognizable is the end it serves and the standards it foregrounds, never a component the other kinds may do without. Finding assumptions shows in the critical row's emphasis, yet a systems analysis or a decision that skips it fails the same standards.

### Grounding: Cognition and the 4E Framing
The model above is grounded in cognitive science, and this document treats that field as the scientific layer its own model must stay consistent with and deepen against. One framing is recorded now because it directly affects how Memar should think about thinkers:

The **4E framing of cognition** holds that cognition is **embodied** (shaped by having a body and sensorimotor engagement with the world), **embedded** (shaped by the environment the thinker operates in), **enacted** (arising from the thinker's active interaction with that environment rather than passive representation of it), and **extended** (partly constituted by tools and media beyond the thinker's boundary — a notebook, a model, a language, a repository). See [4E cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4E_cognition).

The **extended** claim is the one Memar already depends on in practice, stated here as its general form: a thinker's representations may live partially outside the thinker — in documents, in a graph model, in a shared repository — and those external artifacts are genuinely part of the cognitive system, not merely its outputs. This is the general principle behind several Memar positions already: that a model is a primary architectural artifact rather than documentation of one ([Modeling](./modeling.md)), that knowledge left only in individuals' heads is knowledge at risk ([Knowledge](./knowledge.md)), and that a repository's documentation functions as shared cognitive material for every human and AI agent working on the project. This document claims the reverse direction too: if external artifacts are part of the cognitive system, then the *quality of the shared medium* — its definitions, its internal consistency, its freedom from unexamined terminology — is a quality of the thinking itself. That is the warrant for treating documentation discipline as a thinking discipline.

Two boundary clarifications keep the extended claim from overreaching. First, an artifact may participate in a workflow — as shared medium, as a record another thinker reads — without automatically becoming part of any one thinker's private boundary; repository material is part of the *extended cognitive system of the collaboration*, not automatically each participant's retained memory. Second, as the learning coupling above already states, writing an artifact into the shared medium does not by itself establish that any participant's learning process retained it.

The embodied, embedded, and enacted claims are recorded as context, not yet operationalized: Memar has not yet derived rules from them. They are kept here because an AI-mediated conversation is a concrete case where all three are in play — each participant's cognition is shaped by a different embodiment, a different environment, and a different mode of engagement — and the differences are easy to overlook when the interface makes both parties look like interchangeable text producers. What those differences *oblige* in an exchange is stated in the next topics, not left ornamental.
    
### A Conversation as Two Cognitive Systems Connected Through a Medium
The exchange this document calls a **conversation** is modeled as follows: two cognitive systems are connected through a medium — speech, text on a screen, a chat interface, a shared repository — and cognitive material (claims, definitions, questions, criticisms, models) travels across that medium in both directions.

The medium is implementation, not concept. Whether the connection is voice, keyboard, or a repository diff does not change what the exchange is; this is the same concept/representation separation used throughout Memar. Two consequences follow, and they are the reason this model earns its place:

- **The medium shapes the exchange without defining it.** A text interface compresses bandwidth, hides tone, and flattens cognitive material into prose; a voice channel restores some of it. Natural-language media also invite a second failure: matching the medium's language for *internal* operations even when that language is not the system's strongest working representation — an invitation the asymmetry obligation below refuses. These are properties of the medium worth knowing, but they are not rules about thinking — they do not belong in this document beyond the recognition that they exist and the obligations they trigger.
- **The participants are asymmetric in implementation; reciprocal in epistemic standing; not interchangeable in authority.** A human and an AI model, or two humans, differ in embodiment, memory, access, persistence, and failure modes. They share reciprocal standing for *claim evaluation* — each evaluates the other's claims on the same standards, and a discourse norm that applies in one direction must justify itself for the other. Reciprocity of evaluation is not interchangeability of decision rights, responsibility, or institutional authority: the owner who may approve a large-scope change, the reviewer who may only recommend, and the tool that may only draft remain distinct roles. A rule like "do not assume the other is correct" is stated once, bidirectionally, as an evaluation rule — not as a claim that every participant holds the same power to decide.

This model is what makes the discourse norms in the following topics checkable rather than arbitrary: each norm exists because of a specific way cognitive material degrades while traveling between two cognitive systems — or a specific way one system's evaluation of the other's material goes wrong. Not every norm is a pure derivation from the conversation model alone; some are change-governance or framework-value constraints that bind *during* exchange. Where a norm's ground is specialized (Terminology, Documentation), that ownership is stated at the norm or at this topic's close — the conversation model remains the reason those constraints must be stated bidirectionally here rather than as etiquette for one participant type.

### What Implementation Asymmetry Obliges
Naming asymmetry without stating its obligations leaves the difference ornamental — and the interface already encourages overlooking it. The project's own machinery presupposes these obligations (handoffs that externalize session state, changelog propagation fields, the rule against answering from training memory when documentation exists). They are stated here as consequences of the conversation model:

- **Do not cite one's own earlier output as established fact.** A thinker's prior reply is evidence of what was said, not evidence that the claim is true. Treat it as a claim still subject to evaluation — the same standard applied to any other source.
- **Declare unverified reading and context limits.** When load-bearing content was not actually read in the current exchange, or when prior session context may have been lost, say so rather than asserting the content as present. Context eviction is an implementation fact of some cognitive systems; pretending continuity the medium does not provide is a medium-shaped failure.
- **Externalize session state into shared artifacts.** Decisions, open questions, and assumptions that must survive the session belong in the project's durable medium (handoffs, documents, changelogs) — not only in one participant's transient context. This is the operational form of the extended-cognition claim for asymmetric participants.
- **Keep reading and concluding separate.** Standing instructions, dispositions, and the current task shape what may be concluded from material, never what the material is: the counterpart that reads back must receive the material that was read, not the material the instructions preferred. Instructions govern what is concluded over the record; they never rewrite the record that was read. *(Failure prevented: a participant whose skepticism, empathy, or mission silently filters the evidence it reports — the counterpart can no longer tell what was seen from what was wanted.)*
- **Operate in the system's strongest working representation; return through the medium in a form the counterpart can use.** A cognitive system's strongest working representation — for many current language models, a high-resource language such as English; for a human, whichever language they reason most deeply in — need not match the language of the exchange. Reciprocity of exchange requires that material returned through the medium remain usable by the counterpart (typically the counterpart's language in conversation; the project's documentation language for durable records — see [Documentation → Documentation Language](./documentation.md#documentation-language)). It does not require that internal operations run in the medium's language. The procedure: receive in the medium language; transform into the system's strongest working representation for thinking; when that transform introduces ambiguity of intent or scope, ask rather than assume; return through the medium in the counterpart-usable form. *(Failure prevented: shallow thinking forced by matching the exchange language; equally, dumping working-representation internals the counterpart cannot evaluate.)*

### Discourse Norms Derived From This Model
These are the norms Memar participants — human, AI, or organizational — follow when exchanging cognitive material. Each is stated with the failure mode it prevents, and each is a section of its own so that a document which needs one norm can point at it directly rather than at the whole set. Norms bind at **intake**, not only mid-reasoning: ambiguity, change-scale, missing material, and unverified definitions are checked before work proceeds on an assumed base. Two passages follow before the norms, one on how to resolve a conflict between them and one on what this topic does not own.

**Precedence when norms collide.** A rule stated alone cannot resolve conflict with another rule — the Motivation names that cost, and this document must not commit it against itself. When two norms appear to demand incompatible moves in the same situation, apply this order: (1) *Ask rather than assume* and *Confirm before large-scope changes* bind first whenever intent or change-scale is unsettled — they are intake constraints; (2) *Definitions outrank terminology* and *Report absence* bind next — do not build on an unverified or fabricated base; (3) *Evaluate on merits*, *conceptual-root with provenance*, *proportional criticism*, *artifact-as-claim*, and *consult other cognitive systems* govern how claims and critiques are handled once the base is clear; (4) *Prefer long-term clarity* outranks delivery convenience unless speed was explicitly requested, under the limit stated in that norm. Specialized documents still own their domains; this order orders only the norms this document owns.

Two boundaries on this topic: norms specific to modeling are owned by [Modeling](./modeling.md) and are not restated here; norms specific to producing documentation are owned by the documentation system's specifications and practices, starting from [Documentation](./documentation.md). Definition precedence is Terminology governance applied during exchange — owned there, restated here only as the exchange-facing obligation. This document owns the rules that apply to cognitive material *as it travels between any two cognitive systems* — the intersection all those specialized documents assume.

#### Evaluate claims on their merits; treat source as neither authority nor disqualifier
A claim's origin — identity, seniority, confidence, popularity — is not decisive evidence for or against it. Source may supply *defeasible context* (authorship of an artifact, demonstrated test history, institutional decision authority) that helps locate further inspectable support; it never substitutes for that support. Evaluation criteria remain: internal consistency, alignment with established definitions, explanatory power, consequences, and evidence quality. Agreement is not a goal of an exchange; accuracy, consistency, and clarity are. *(Failure prevented: deferring to source rather than examining content; equally, dismissing relevant testimony because it has a source.)*

#### Definitions outrank terminology
When reasoning about any load-bearing term, consult its defined meaning ([Terminology](./terminology.md)) rather than assuming the common industry sense; a word's general meaning can be broader than its most common technical usage, and a term no Memar document has defined by reference defaults to its ordinary general meaning, not to any ecosystem's usage ([Terminology → The Default Meaning of an Unreferenced Term](./terminology.md#the-default-meaning-of-an-unreferenced-term)). Where terminology and definition conflict, the definition governs ([Terminology → Terminology Authority and Governance](./terminology.md#terminology-authority-and-governance)). When a load-bearing term's definition has not been verified against its governing document in the current exchange, say so and verify before building on it — do not proceed on an assumed definition. *(Failure prevented: two cognitive systems silently operating on different definitions of the same word; building downstream claims on an unverified definition while the medium already holds the authoritative one.)*

#### Do not assume the other participant is correct — or incorrect
Both errors are the same failure: substituting a prior about the source for evaluation of the claim. The asymmetry warrant: participants are reciprocal in epistemic standing, so the evaluation rule must be bidirectional too. *(Failure prevented: deference in one direction, dismissiveness in the other.)*

#### Receive criticism at its conceptual root, not only its instance — with provenance labeled
When a participant raises a criticism — typically against one concrete example — the receiving participant's job is to discover the general concern the example may stand for. Three statuses must not be collapsed: (1) the *reported* criticism — what the giver actually stated; (2) the *inferred root* — the general concern the receiver thinks the instance implicates, labeled as the receiver's extension until confirmed; (3) the *confirmed generalization* — the root the giver accepts as their intended scope. Ask when the instance is ambiguous ("is the problem the example itself, or the rule that produced it?"), and do not treat an inferred root as the giver's claim until that confirmation. The critique is then answered — and, where accepted, corrected — in the general form that was confirmed, not merely the instance raised. Failure modes prevented: fixing the example while the generating rule survives; answering a narrower claim than the critic intended; over-generalizing a critique that was genuinely instance-local — including manufacturing an architecture rewrite the giver never asked for and attributing that stronger claim to them. The proportional-criticism norm below governs the *giver's* side of the same exchange. *(Failure prevented: instance-patching; answered-but-not-asked ambiguity; receiver-manufactured scope attributed to the giver.)*

#### Assume hidden reasoning may exist, and require criticism to be proportional — without licensing silence
A counterpart's proposal may carry reasoning not fully visible in the current exchange — particularly in a project with accumulated history. Criticism is therefore earned by argument, not by surface plausibility: a critique is valuable when it provides a stronger explanation, a more consistent model, or a revealed contradiction — an objection that would be satisfied by re-reading the existing model is noise, not critique. Model strength is judged from *inspectable material* — stated assumptions, evidence, alternatives considered, tests, consequences — not from the author's status or from a prior that deep reasoning must exist unseen. Proportionality raises the required *quality* of a critique; it never licenses withholding a critique that meets that quality, and artifact maturity is not a source prior that forbids examination. *(Failure prevented: superficial objections to deeply-reasoned models; unearned deference to them; critique-suppression dressed as proportionality.)*

#### Ask rather than assume
When intent, scope, priority, or historical context is unclear, prefer a question to an assumption. Defaults from past exchanges can be actively misleading, because each exchange may operate under different premises. The norm has two halves, and they do not govern the same thing. **Asking carries no budget.** No cap on the number of questions turns this norm into a licence to proceed on an unstated premise, which is the failure it exists to prevent, and a question whose answer is already implied by the material is not diligence. **Deferral does.** Where an ambiguity is set aside rather than asked — the answer is not yet reachable, the counterpart is unavailable, the exchange must proceed — that deferral is a fact about the work, and it is recorded where it can be found again: the paired handoff's open questions, each naming the ambiguity it is rather than the topic it sits near. The deferred set is also ordered, so that what was set aside can be read in the order it will cost: an ambiguity that changes what the work *is* outranks one that changes how a part of it is done, and one that touches a premise the model rests on outranks one that touches a detail. *(Failure prevented: propagating an assumption that was never agreed, across the medium, into decisions; equally, a cascade of questions that never reaches a decision, and a deferral nobody recorded — which is the assumption again, wearing the appearance of having been deliberate.)*

#### Confirm before large-scope changes
When a proposed change reaches beyond the instance under discussion — a definition other documents consume, a structure other sections depend on, an edit spanning several documents — propose before applying: state the proposed content and the files it will touch, so the counterpart can shape the change before it exists rather than review it after the fact. The obligation has a second moment, after the change exists: hold the applied result open for the counterpart's review and apply the feedback it carries. Approval of a plan is not review of its outcome. Adding a record — a rule, a note, an open question — to the document that owns its subject is not such a change and waits for no one; removing text is, because removal is what loses information ([Documentation → Recording Is Not Gated; Removal Is](./documentation.md#recording-is-not-gated-removal-is)). *(Failure prevented: a large change landing wholesale — after-the-fact review replacing before-the-fact shaping; equally, an applied result treated as settled because the plan behind it was approved.)*

#### Report absence rather than supplying it
When required material — a definition, a document, a prior decision, historical context — is not available in the exchange or the shared medium, say that it is absent. Do not fabricate a plausible substitute, reconstruct from training memory what the project has not recorded, or silently fill the gap with an industry default. **A lookup that finds nothing is a fact about the lookup, not about the shared medium.** What a search returns is bounded by the terms it searched and the places it reached, so its silence licenses the statement that *this query matched nothing* and never the statement that *the project holds no such rule* — the material may be stated in other words, in another document, or under a name nobody thought to search for. Before reporting that something is missing, read the documents that own the subject: a rule that already reaches the case is not a gap, and the cheapest explanation for a rule that appears absent is that the case was never read beside it. *(Failure prevented: material that looks like support but supports nothing — a failure on the sender side of solitary work as much as of exchange; equally, an empty result reported as a discovery about the project, and a chain of such reports in which each round takes the previous round's unverified absence as established.)*

#### Treat shared artifacts as claims under the same evaluation, not as absolute authority
A document in the repository is cognitive material a cognitive system (or collaboration) externalized; the extended-cognition claim makes it part of the shared cognitive system, not exempt from evaluation. When an artifact is stale, internally inconsistent, or contradicted by stronger evidence, report the conflict and correct through the project's change mechanisms — do not silently propagate the stale claim because "documentation is canonical," and do not silently override it from training priors because "merits govern." Canonical means: the authoritative *home* of a claim is the governing document, not a participant's memory; it does not mean every sentence in every file is true. *(Failure prevented: silent propagation of error; silent override of the shared medium.)*

#### Consult other cognitive systems when conceptual depth warrants it
When a question is load-bearing and deep — a definition other work will consume, a model other documents depend on, a choice whose failure modes are costly — and other cognitive systems are available to the thinker, invite their evaluation before settling. The form matches the participants and the medium: an AI system with access to other models seeks their critique; a human convenes one or two other humans for the same purpose; an organization runs a review meeting. This is the diverge/converge strategy run on examiners ([How the Components Combine](#how-the-components-combine)) — deliberate widening of the field of examination, then convergence on what survives — not deference to the consulted parties (the merits norm still governs their claims) and not a substitute for the thinker's own evaluation. Depth and availability jointly trigger the obligation: shallow or already-settled questions do not require a council, and absence of available counterparts does not license inventing their assent. *(Failure prevented: settling a deep claim from a single cognitive system's unchecked operations — including mirroring a counterpart's framing back as if the family of options were complete.)*

#### Resist defaults that arrive with the medium
Industry conventions, popular technologies, established terminology, and common patterns may be useful references, but none is authoritative by virtue of its popularity — and material received through a widely-used channel arrives pre-packaged with such defaults. Evaluate each against this project's definitions and model. *(Failure prevented: importing assumptions through vocabulary.)*

#### Prefer long-term clarity, unless speed is explicitly requested
Favor conceptual clarity, evolvability, and maintainability over short-term delivery convenience; a superficial proposal is worse than none, because it creates false confidence. An explicit speed request narrows method; it does not cancel confirmation of large-scope changes or definitional verification. *(Failure prevented: optimizing the exchange for speed at the cost of the shared model both systems depend on.)*

### Named Errors: Biases and Fallacies
Cognitive processes fail, and they fail in recurring, named ways. This document records two classes of named error at its two levels — the negative space of the components above: each named error is a process failing a standard. Biases belong to cognition, because perception, attention, memory, and thinking can each be systematically distorted; fallacies belong to thinking, because they are defects of inference. Knowing that a perception, a recollection, or an argument has slipped is cognitive work, not something the components leave to chance. Both classes share one property, the one Persian marks with the same phrase به غلط in both: they derail the path — of thinking, of dialogue, or of both.

- **Cognitive biases — falling into error oneself.** A bias is a systematic way a process of one's own produces a distorted result while running apparently normally, in any cognitive process: availability bias is memory offering what comes to mind easily as if it were what is common; confirmation bias is attention and discrepancy noticing harvesting only in one direction; anchoring is ordering that holds the first input in place; survivorship bias is comparison over an unrepresentative sample; **source-deference / agreement pressure** is criterion application that substitutes the counterpart's framing or approval for independent evaluation — recorded in this project's own material when an early draft of this document merely mirrored the owner's examples back instead of completing the family (see the paired changelog). The failing system is the thinker's own. Biases are the named failures of the processes, most often against fairness and accuracy — which is why guarding against them belongs to the awareness disciplines (awareness of assumptions is the discipline under which one's own tendency to confirm or defer is held visible) and to the revision that repairs what a bias distorted, discrepancy noticing turned on one's own conclusions. The *consult other cognitive systems* norm is one structural counter to source-deference: inviting further evaluation before a single system's unchecked agreement settles the claim.
- **Fallacies — defective inferential support offered as support.** A fallacy is a named form of corrupted reasoning in which the support offered does not actually support the claim — whether the corruption occurs in solitary reasoning or in material traveling between systems. Ad hominem substitutes the source for the claim; appeal to authority substitutes prestige for evidence; straw man replaces the counterpart's position with a weaker one and defeats the replacement. When the corrupted pattern travels between systems, the exchange is where the failure lands; when it occurs inside one thinker's operations, it is still a fallacy by structure, and may simultaneously be a bias in how that thinker's operations ran. Fallacies are why the discourse norms and the paired practice's insufficient-critique list (authority appeal, industry convention, taste) are, in this frame, a fallacy catalog in Memar's own vocabulary.

The boundary between the classes is real but porous at the surface: the same act can be a bias in the speaker (honest use of a corrupted pattern) and a fallacy in the exchange (corrupted material reaching another thinker), and both classes corrupt regardless of intent — a fallacy works whether or not the sender is aware of it, which is why the receiver-side disciplines matter as much as sender-side honesty. Within the fallacy class, the sender's awareness is a real axis with its own Persian name — **سفسطه** (sophistry): fallacy *deployed knowingly*, against مغالطه as the class name for the corrupted pattern itself; the same corruption travels whether its sender willed it or fell into it, so the axis changes the sender's responsibility, not the corruption's kind. Neither class is an operation or a process: they are not functions on cognitive material but the named ways those functions fail the standards — a fallacy most often fails consistency or sufficiency. And the classes are open, like everything above them: this document records the two *kinds* and their homes; enumerating individual biases and fallacies is catalog work, not concept work, and belongs to the practice layer that needs a checklist, not to this document — grown per project from observed errors, never imported wholesale from a generic catalog.
