# Domain-Driven Design

## Abstract
This document states Memar's position on **Domain-Driven Design**. The phrase is ordinary compositional English — *domain*, *driven*, *design* — and Memar's documents already speak those words at their general meaning; the acronym that circulates over them is a Business Term whose accumulated senses Memar does not inherit. The document accepts the umbrella's existence, records what under that name is sound for Memar's work, what is problematic, and where each piece actually lives in Memar's own documents. What it gathers are commitments Memar already holds — that design is driven by the subject matter rather than by tools, technologies, presentations, industry taxonomies, or mechanisms; that the model is the primary architectural artifact; that terminology is an architectural dependency; that abstractions are justified by responsibility rather than by data alone; and that boundaries stay conceptual until a realization is justified — each owned by [Modeling](../modeling.md), [Terminology](../terminology.md), [Modularity](../modularity.md), [Process](../process.md), or [Protocol](../protocol.md) and not restated here. Worked examples are deliberately absent.

## Introduction

### Motivation
Left without a Memar position, the phrase "Domain-Driven Design" does not stay empty. It arrives with the medium — a brief, a job description, a model's training prior — already filled by whichever formulation circulates most widely, and the reader treats that fill as if it were Memar's. Wide circulation is not authority ([Cognition → Discourse Norms](../cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)), and the mechanism is not anyone's bad faith: a Business Term accumulates senses because adoption, not precision, is what shaped it ([Terminology → Business Terms](../terminology.md#business-terms)). An unexamined default is not neutrality; it is delegation — the same failure [filesystem.md](./filesystem.md) names for another inherited surface.

Two further costs follow. Agents asked to work "the DDD way" answer from literature memory rather than from Memar's documents. And canopy terms travel in Memar prose without a place that is responsible for saying which of them Memar has defined, which it refuses, and which it has simply not named yet.

### Methodology
The document consumes [Modeling](../modeling.md), [Terminology](../terminology.md), [Modularity](../modularity.md), [Process](../process.md), and [Protocol](../protocol.md) — including Terminology's existing classification of the acronym as a Business Term, which this document consumes rather than revises — and adds no modeling, terminology, or modularity definition of its own. What it defines is confined to the protocol layer: the reading of the name, the criterion for what the name gathers, the conformance rule when the name is invoked, and the refusals that follow. Citation runs down to those base documents, per this folder's citation rule. Ecosystem formulations of Domain-Driven Design are examined as prior art to critique — what travels well under the three words, what does not — never as authority for Memar's rules. A canopy term is related only where a Memar document already defines a home; where none exists, the absence is recorded rather than filled. Worked examples are excluded so the reasons remain the load-bearing content.

## Explanation

### Memar's Stance on the Name
**Domain-Driven Design** names a direction: design driven by the domain. Memar's stance is the one [filesystem.md → Memar's Stance on the Filesystem Protocol Surface](./filesystem.md#memars-stance-on-the-filesystem-protocol-surface) takes toward another widely held name: acknowledge the surface, refuse the unexamined default, and state Memar's own criteria for what under that name is kept, refused, or still undefined.

The acronym and the phrase are not the same object; that distinction is stated next. Under Memar, invoking the name means the combination and the conformance rule below — Memar's reading of design driven by the domain — not a license to import an external taxonomy as primitives. An external catalog may be useful as critique material; it does not redefine the words.

This is why the document lives in [`protocols/`](./README.md). It does not answer "what is domain-driven design?" for the ecosystem — no Memar document governs how the wider world uses three ordinary words, and [Terminology → Terminology Authority and Governance](../terminology.md#terminology-authority-and-governance) is explicit that Memar's governance does not extend to ordinary English usage of words no document has defined. It answers the question this folder exists for: what are Memar's rules when development is conducted under that name — which commitments bind, which reductions are refused, and where each piece is actually owned. Like [TDD](./tdd.md), the governed subject is a process (development); the filesystem parallel is a stance method, not an identical membership rationale — filesystem names an externally specified interface Memar implements, while this document names a circulated design vocabulary.

The stance carries an obligation, not only a permission. When the phrase arrives on a project — in a brief, a job description, an inherited codebase, or a model's suggestion — the reader's task is to replace the label with the checkable content: which of the commitments below is actually being asked for, and which competing driver is actually determining the design. The mapping below serves that substitution. Reading it, a canopy term either resolves to the Memar topic that owns the question, resolves to a refusal with a stated reason, or resolves to a recorded absence in which the ordinary meaning of the words governs and no ecosystem special sense attaches. What is not available is carrying the term forward with its ecosystem sense intact because the phrase was on the page.

### The Acronym and the Phrase Are Not the Same Object
Memar's base layer has already classified the acronym. [Terminology → Business Terms](../terminology.md#business-terms) lists **DDD** among the terms optimized for adoption rather than for conceptual precision — beside Agile, DevOps, and OOP — and its caution applies unchanged: a Business Term "frequently accumulates multiple meanings over time and should be treated with caution when used as a foundation for learning, modeling, or architectural reasoning." Nothing in this document softens that classification, and this document is not a promotion of the acronym out of that layer.

What the classification governs is the **acronym as a circulated label** — the accumulated body of schools, catalogs, certifications, and contested readings that travel under three letters. It does not govern the three ordinary words. [Terminology → The Default Meaning of an Unreferenced Term](../terminology.md#the-default-meaning-of-an-unreferenced-term) settles that directly: a word appearing in Memar's documents without a reference to a Memar Definition carries its general meaning "and nothing more," and that default explicitly excludes "an ecosystem's redefinition, an industry's accumulated mythology, a framework's marketing sense." The worked boundary case given there is *monolithic* — the ordinary sense governs, and the narratives accumulated around "monolithic architecture" are not part of the word's meaning in Memar. *Domain*, *driven*, and *design* stand in exactly that position.

The two facts therefore compose rather than conflict: **the phrase is usable at its general meaning; the acronym's accumulation is not inherited by using it.** A reader who writes "this system was designed domain-first" is speaking ordinary English that Memar's own documents already speak. A reader who writes "we follow DDD" has said something a Business Term's multiple accumulated senses make unreliable — the "illusion of shared understanding" [Business Terms](../terminology.md#business-terms) names — and this document is what that reader must be pointed at to recover a checkable meaning.

The closest structural sibling is [Terminology → OOP Warning](../terminology.md#oop-warning): a label whose meaning fragmented across books, vendors, and communities until disagreement about whether a design "is" or "is not" OOP became a disagreement about vocabulary rather than about the design. Terminology's remedy there is to study the underlying concepts directly at their own layer, and only then evaluate what an ecosystem made of them. This document applies the same remedy to this label: the concepts under the canopy are studied in [Modeling](../modeling.md), [Terminology](../terminology.md), and [Modularity](../modularity.md), and the canopy is evaluated against them — never the reverse.

### What *Driven* Means Here
The three words make one claim, and it is about causation: which force is permitted to determine the design. Memar already fixes that ordering. [Terminology → Concept-First Thinking](../terminology.md#concept-first-thinking) states it as a sequence — Problem → Concept → Scientific Principle → Technology → Tool → Product — and requires each layer to be understood before the next, so that technologies do not define the problem. [Tool-First Thinking](../terminology.md#tool-first-thinking) names the reversal as an anti-pattern: a designer meets the tool first, absorbs its assumptions as properties of the concept, and then lets those assumptions constrain how the problem itself is stated.

Read against that ordering, **domain-driven design is Concept-First Thinking applied to design: the subject matter determines the design, and every downstream layer is derived from it rather than permitted to define it.** This is the reading Memar means when the phrase is used as an umbrella name, and it is why the phrase is worth keeping — the ordering is a real constraint, not a slogan.

The claim is sharpest in its negative space, and Memar has already named each competing driver:

- **Tool-driven** — the design derived from the mechanism that will realize it ([Terminology → Tool-First Thinking](../terminology.md#tool-first-thinking)).
- **Technology-driven** — adaptability sought in the stack rather than in the model ([Modeling → Adaptability Emerges from Modeling, Not Technology](../modeling.md#adaptability-emerges-from-modeling-not-technology)).
- **Presentation-driven** — concepts discovered from screens, forms, and views ([Modeling → Concept Discovery Must Not Be Driven by Presentation](../modeling.md#concept-discovery-must-not-be-driven-by-presentation)).
- **Taxonomy-driven** — decomposition inherited from an industry's product packaging ([Modularity → Foundational Concepts Should Remain Few](../modularity.md#foundational-concepts-should-remain-few)).
- **Mechanism-driven** — the process shaped by a lock, a transaction, or a saga assumed before the process was modeled ([Process → Process Before Mechanism](../process.md#process-before-mechanism), [Mechanisms Are Not Obligations](../process.md#mechanisms-are-not-obligations)).

Each of these is a live default with an ecosystem behind it, which is why the ordering needs a name at all. A design is domain-driven in Memar's sense when none of the five is the actual determinant — a claim about the design that can be checked against the model, not a claim about which vocabulary the team uses.

### What This Protocol Governs
This protocol governs the development process only when a participant invokes **Domain-Driven Design** as the reason for a design decision. Its subject is not the domain process being modeled, and it does not define a new kind of model, Module, or Protocol. It governs the direction by which a development justifies its commitments: the domain model supplies the concerns, relationships, processes, and applicable contracts that implementation must realize.

A development conforms to this protocol when an implementation commitment can be explained from that current model, or is returned to modeling as a candidate that may revise it. A presentation artifact, an implementation mechanism, or an ecosystem pattern may expose a question worth examining; none establishes a model boundary merely by arriving first. Where it conflicts with, or cannot be explained by, the model, the participant either revises the model on its own grounds or rejects the commitment. The model is not silently reshaped to preserve the borrowed mechanism.

This is not a linear phase order. Modeling and observation remain a cycle: implementation and its consequences may reveal that the model needs revision. The constraint is only that the revision is made explicit and judged at the model's own level, rather than inferred backward from the mechanism.

### What the Name Gathers in Memar
A commitment belongs under this name when it constrains **which force determines the design** in the sense just stated — when dropping it would let one of the competing drivers back in. That criterion, not a count, fixes the membership: the list may grow when another Memar topic is found to carry the same constraint, and a member leaves if its topic is shown to constrain something else. Four qualify today, each already defined elsewhere and none restated here:

1. The model is discovered and validated before implementation and remains the source of truth ([Modeling → The Model as the Primary Artifact](../modeling.md#the-model-as-the-primary-artifact), [Modeling Before Implementation](../modeling.md#modeling-before-implementation)).
2. The words naming a domain are an architectural dependency rather than a communication detail ([Terminology → Terminology Shapes Mental Models](../terminology.md#terminology-shapes-mental-models)).
3. Abstractions are justified by independent responsibility, behavioral boundary, or lifecycle, never by the existence of data alone ([Modeling → Concept Existence vs. Model Existence](../modeling.md#concept-existence-vs-model-existence)).
4. The model's abstractions, relationships, and boundaries remain conceptual until a specific realization is justified; where a modeled process needs declarative rules, the applicable Protocol states that contract ([Modeling → Modeling Produces Conceptual Abstractions](../modeling.md#modeling-produces-conceptual-abstractions-not-implementation-structures), [Protocol → What is a Protocol?](../protocol.md#what-is-a-protocol)).

Each of the four blocks a specific driver: the first blocks implementation from defining the model, the second blocks vocabulary from arriving unexamined, the third blocks the shape of stored data from standing in for a responsibility, the fourth blocks a realization structure from being mistaken for a boundary. That is what makes them members rather than a list of things Memar happens to believe. Keeping the name is how the constraint reaches practitioners who already carry the phrase; the name confers no authority to settle questions the base documents answer.

### What Is Problematic under the Canopy
Critique of common reductions that travel under the acronym — not of the three ordinary words:

- **Aggregate-root-as-owner.** [Modeling → Domain Decomposition over Aggregate-Root Modeling](../modeling.md#domain-decomposition-over-aggregate-root-modeling) rejects beginning from a canonical owning entity that contains its sub-concerns as fields. Aggregation is a consequence of modeling, not a primitive; aggregators are context-specific, plural, named after the use case they serve, and commonly form at the composition layer.
- **Identity of data as the criterion for what earns an abstraction.** Circulated tactical vocabulary often sorts model elements by whether their instances have a distinguishable identity across time. Memar sorts by something else: [Modeling → Concept Existence vs. Model Existence](../modeling.md#concept-existence-vs-model-existence) holds that an abstraction is justified by an independent responsibility, behavioral boundary, or lifecycle — data decomposition is a consequence of responsibility decomposition, not its objective. Where the criteria conflict, Memar's governs; the failure the data criterion invites is artificial decomposition — multiple names for the same concern promoted into independent architectural elements.
- **Multiple models of one reality treated as a design goal.** Circulated strategic vocabulary often encourages each context to hold its own model of an overlapping concern and to translate at the seams. Memar's constraints on that are already stated. [Modeling → One Reality, Multiple Abstraction Lenses](../modeling.md#one-reality-multiple-abstraction-lenses) holds that differing views are observations of one reality rather than entities in the model, and that parallel structures whose only difference is the lens usually mean one concern has been counted several times. [Modeling → One Authoritative Location per Concern's Data](../modeling.md#one-authoritative-location-per-concerns-data) adds that a concern's authoritative data has exactly one location — a boundary realized twice is two boundaries — and a copy with no defined re-derivation path is a second, diverging original. Memar does not forbid separately authored models — organizational and deployment reality produces them — but it refuses to treat the multiplication as an achievement. Each additional model of the same concern owes a stated authority and a stated re-derivation path.
- **Stakeholder vocabulary adopted as the model's vocabulary.** Shared language with subject-matter experts matters, and Memar agrees that naming is architectural ([Terminology → Terminology Shapes Mental Models](../terminology.md#terminology-shapes-mental-models)). Agreement on the importance of language is not agreement on which language wins. Stakeholder vocabulary is an input to be examined, not a terminus: [Terminology Layers](../terminology.md#terminology-layers) ranks terms by the methodology that established them, [Business Terms](../terminology.md#business-terms) places commercial labels and industry narratives at the least precise layer, and [Concept-First Thinking](../terminology.md#concept-first-thinking) requires movement toward the most fundamental layer available before structure is committed. Adopting an organization's working words unexamined can be Tool-First Thinking arriving through vocabulary.
- **Criticality or labels treated as intrinsic kinds.** Treating a domain as intrinsically "core," or a stereotype (entity, value, service) as what a thing *is*, fails [Modularity → What Earns Foundational Status](../modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status), [Foundational Concepts Should Remain Few](../modularity.md#foundational-concepts-should-remain-few), and [Modeling → Classification Emerges From Rules and Relations](../modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels): a classification is not an inherent property of a node; it emerges when the node satisfies stated rules that let it participate in a role.
- **Mechanism catalogs as obligations** — repositories, factories, aggregates-as-construct, layered-architecture packaging. These are realization choices under [Process → Process Before Mechanism](../process.md#process-before-mechanism) and [Mechanisms Are Not Obligations](../process.md#mechanisms-are-not-obligations): available where a model calls for them, never constitutive of designing from the domain.

**Terms with no Memar home.** Patterns absent from Memar's documents are not thereby Memar commitments. Their names retain only their ordinary meaning unless a Memar Definition is referenced; whether a particular pattern is justified is decided from the current model and the documents that own its concerns.

- **Domain** (in narrower circulated senses — subdomain, business slice, org-aligned partition). Memar defines no `Domain` concept; the general meaning governs per [Scope](#scope) below.
- **Bounded context.** No Memar Definition. Conceptual Boundaries, Module boundaries, and Protocol contracts are related concerns, none of which *is* this term. Absence of a definition is not absence of a position: what Memar holds about multiple models of one reality is stated under [What Is Problematic under the Canopy](#what-is-problematic-under-the-canopy).
- **Domain service.** No home. Behavior with an independent responsibility is a Module or a Process.
- **Repository, factory.** No home as patterns. [One Authoritative Location per Concern's Data](../modeling.md#one-authoritative-location-per-concerns-data) constrains where authoritative data lives without prescribing an access construct.
- **Context mapping and its relationship patterns** (shared kernel, conformist, anti-corruption layer). No home. Cross-boundary translation is treated per-protocol, not as a catalog.
- **Strategic vs. tactical design.** No home. Memar's concept / protocol / implementation layering is not a replacement taxonomy for that split.

### Scope
The generality follows from the words rather than from a sibling analogy. Memar defines no concept named *Domain* — the word appears unreferenced throughout Memar's documents — so [Terminology → The Default Meaning of an Unreferenced Term](../terminology.md#the-default-meaning-of-an-unreferenced-term) governs it: *domain* carries its general meaning, a sphere of activity or subject matter, and nothing more. Narrower circulated senses — a business subdomain, a slice of an organization's product map — are ecosystem redefinitions that the general meaning excludes.

Consequently the name is read for the development of systems generally, and none of the four commitments presupposes code: a building, an organization, or a society page developed the Memar way has a subject matter whose model precedes its realization, whose terminology shapes its model, and whose decomposition follows responsibility. *Design*, likewise, is not narrowed to a phase — [System → Architecture](../system.md#architecture) and [Modeling → Modeling, Protocol, and Architecture](../modeling.md#modeling-protocol-and-architecture) already treat designing as spanning discovery through implementation rather than occupying a slot before it.

Where the developed system is software, the same commitments bind. Language and stack mechanisms that help express a domain model remain mechanisms under Process — they do not redefine the combination the name gathers.

### Relationship to Other Concepts
**Modeling.** The principal home. The division of labor: Modeling owns what a model is, how it is discovered, and what justifies an abstraction; this document owns only the claim that the model is what design answers to — and the refusals that follow when something else answers instead. Every substantive ruling cited here, the aggregate-root refusal included, is Modeling's, on Modeling's grounds. A reading of the umbrella that contradicts Modeling is a wrong reading of the umbrella, not a competing position.

**Terminology.** Classifies the acronym as a Business Term, supplies Concept-First / Tool-First thinking that define *driven*, and supplies the default-meaning rule that keeps the three ordinary words from inheriting ecosystem mythology.

**Modularity.** Boundary and core/optional questions; foundational-status tests that refuse taxonomy-driven decomposition.

**Process.** The development process this protocol governs when the name is invoked, and the process-before-mechanism constraint that keeps mechanism catalogs from becoming obligations.

**Protocol.** Where modeled processes that need declarative rules receive their contracts. This document is itself such a contract for development conducted under the name.

**TDD.** Sibling case: an ecosystem-circulated name under which Memar states its own combination and critiques reductions. TDD governs the ordering of expectations and checks; Domain-Driven Design names the subject-matter commitments those expectations are written about.
