1. **Which instrument carries the strategic water buffer — the surface lake or the sealed deep shaft?** The two "hydraulic buffer" records are not synonyms: [The multi-purpose artificial lake](water-bodies-as-common-infrastructure.md#the-multi-purpose-artificial-lake-iran-map-geometry-as-thermal-sink-and-hydraulic-buffer) buffers by area (open surface, gives heat, humidity and birds, loses water to evaporation and fears eutrophication); [The dual-use 60-meter ultra-deep pool](water-bodies-as-common-infrastructure.md#the-dual-use-60-meter-ultra-deep-pool-as-strategic-hydraulic-and-diving-buffer-dual-use-deep-dive-well-and-water-buffer) buffers by depth (sealed column, gives pump-free fire pressure and biological stability, touches no sky). One question, two answers: reserve-that-works (the surface volume earns its land by five functions, per the lake's own argument) vs. reserve-that-holds (a head no pump can fail and no heat exchanger spends, per the shaft's). *(Why: they carry the same name and opposite physics; an owner approving "the hydraulic buffer" has approved neither. Blocking: yes for the buffer doctrine, for the supply ledger's reserve question, and for any fire-pressure guarantee.)*
2. **Does the ground plane or the water plane own the cooling credit?** The lake record's function 2 (thermal sink) and the waterways record's evaporative-cooling reference both spend surface water to cool the plain, and the supply ledger's atmospheric harvester spends the same band's heat the opposite way. The lake is a heat source in winter and a sink in summer; the deep shaft must stay near-constant to keep its water potable-stable. Which ledger sets the temperature band the water bodies must hold, and does the deep shaft's "no chlorination, continuous biological filtration" premise survive a thermal-sink role it is not given? *(Why: the four records assume one water temperature and are asked to serve three. Blocking: yes for the lake's combined-function claim; no for the canal/overflow transit clauses.)*
