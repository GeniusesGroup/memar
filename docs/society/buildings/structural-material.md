# Structural Material System

## Abstract
What the frame is made of and what the formwork is for turn out to be one decision, because here the recorded answers make the formwork part of the building. Two answers stand in this ledger. In the first, the ten basement levels are poured in impervious reinforced concrete and everything above grade is erected in mass engineered timber — CLT decks on glulam columns — buying a 60-to-75 percent cut in embodied carbon, a 50 percent lighter dead load and a 3-to-5-day floor cycle ([Hybrid structure: impervious reinforced concrete below grade, mass engineered timber above (Hybrid Concrete-Mass Timber)](structural-material.md#hybrid-structure-impervious-reinforced-concrete-below-grade-mass-engineered-timber-above-hybrid-concrete-mass-timber)). In the second, the frame stays concrete, but the timber panels that shaped the pour are never struck: they remain in place as permanent formwork, thermal insulation and finished facade, locking atmospheric CO2 in the building body and reducing plastering and finishing costs to zero ([Permanent mass-timber formwork left in place as thermal insulation and finished facade (Permanent Mass Timber Shuttering & Carbon Lock)](structural-material.md#permanent-mass-timber-formwork-left-in-place-as-thermal-insulation-and-finished-facade-permanent-mass-timber-shuttering--carbon-lock)). The two cannot share a single floor: one specifies wood as the deck, the other specifies a two-way hollow concrete slab as its prerequisite. They agree only on the enemy — the struck-away shutter and the plaster crew that follows it — and they disagree on where the carbon sink lives: in the load-carrying mass itself, or in the skin that carries nothing.

## Introduction

### Motivation
The two records describe the same event — a concrete pour, a floor plate, a decision about what stays visible — from opposite conclusions. Filed separately, a reviewer of the hybrid timber record never meets the sentence that contradicts it ("two-way hollow concrete slabs" as a prerequisite of the permanent-formwork record), and a reader who dislikes exposed timber in a seismic zone dismisses both records at once, taking an objection to one frame for an objection to the material question itself. The dispute also carries a quantitative edge that only one page can show: one record claims 50 percent less dead load, the other keeps the concrete mass and claims to bury a CO2 quantity "equivalent to the total cement consumed" — a lock whose plausibility depends on how much timber a given pour actually skins, which neither record computes. Keeping the answers adjacent is what turns two unobjectionable-sounding sheets into one decision with two admissible answers.

## Explanation

### The problem the answers share
What material should the structural frame be, and is the formwork itself the final finish? The question has three axes the answers place themselves on: what carries load above grade (timber section or concrete frame), what the surface treatment is (nothing, because the structure is the finish — or plaster and paint, as convention decrees), and where the carbon benefit is booked (in the load-bearing mass or in a permanent skin). Neither answer is the question; the requirement stands whichever way the frame is settled.

### Approaches

#### Hybrid structure: impervious reinforced concrete below grade, mass engineered timber above (Hybrid Concrete-Mass Timber)
**Mechanism:** The 10 basement levels are executed in impervious reinforced concrete, for their resistance to earth and water pressure, while the 10 residential floors above ground are built of mass engineered timber — CLT deck slabs and Glulam columns. The wood acts as an atmospheric carbon reservoir and drastically lightens the structure's weight; this raises seismic flexibility against earthquakes and multiplies the speed of industrial modular installation.
**Metrics:** Embodied carbon reduction of 60 to 75 percent against a concrete skeleton; 50 percent reduction of the building's dead load; construction speed of 3 to 5 days per floor.
**Prerequisites:** Reliable supply of standard timber from renewable, regenerating forests; [Thermoactive deep load-bearing foundations (Energy Piles & Diaphragm Heat Exchangers)](../energy/thermal-sources-and-storage.md#thermoactive-deep-load-bearing-foundations-energy-piles--diaphragm-heat-exchangers).
**Conventional-model flaw:** Reckless consumption of cement and steel on the upper floors, with astronomical greenhouse-gas emissions and a heavy dead load that raises seismic destruction risk.
**Tags:** engineered timber, CLT, glulam, negative carbon, modular structure, dead-load reduction
**Scale:** Micro — building (floors 1 through 10 above grade)
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | The 3-to-5-day floor is a crane-and-flatbed cycle: panels arrive just-in-time onto a clear, dry site with room to land them, and the timber must stay dry through the whole structural phase — conditions an in-fill lot inside the existing fabric, working down to its own foundations between occupied walls, cannot offer. Forced onto a standing building, the answer demands stripping the existing frame and re-erecting a lighter one the existing foundations were never designed against, and the ten-basement premise beneath it does not exist there to begin with; the import exposure of the prerequisite (no domestic mass-timber certified supply is named) compounds the risk: a greenfield method stranded by a customs queue is neither greenfield nor fast.
**Cognitive anchor:** The wooden verandas and shingled roofs of "the Gilan and Lahijan timber houses" — buildings that sway with the quake instead of resisting it, raised and repaired by the family that lives in them.
**Narrative hook:** Gilan has built houses that flex with the earthquake for generations — we are writing that lesson into the tenth floor.
**Syn (synergies):** [Releasing the height and floor-area-ratio caps in favour of ecological self-regulation](../form-and-land/buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#releasing-the-height-and-floor-area-ratio-caps-in-favour-of-ecological-self-regulation) — optimal high-rise construction; [20-square-meter kitchen-less dwelling suites with multi-state transformable furniture](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#20-square-meter-kitchen-less-dwelling-suites-with-multi-state-transformable-furniture) (prefabricated modular suites); mental-health uplift through biophilic texture
**Source:** supplementary idea of the systems architect — negative-carbon body engineering

#### Permanent mass-timber formwork left in place as thermal insulation and finished facade (Permanent Mass Timber Shuttering & Carbon Lock)
**Mechanism:** Processed timber panels serve as the formwork for pouring ceilings and columns in such a way that the panels are never struck after the concrete cures: they remain as a permanent part of the structure, as thermal insulation, and as the final architectural finish. Besides increasing structural resistance and permanently burying atmospheric CO2 inside the building body, the method zeroes the costs of plastering and finishing work.
**Metrics:** Permanent CO2 lock equivalent to the total cement consumed in the building body; service life beyond 100 years with fire-retardant heat treatment; 100 percent elimination of scrap metal and plastic/oiled-wood shuttering waste.
**Prerequisites:** Fire-resistant treated timber; two-way hollow concrete slabs.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Temporary metal and oiled-wood formwork discarded after a few reuses, and rough concrete surfaces that then demand heavy plaster coatings and chemical paints.
**Tags:** permanent formwork, Mass Timber, carbon sequestration, biophilic aesthetics, elimination of applied finishes
**Scale:** Micro — building (the structural skeleton of buildings)
**Fit & substrate:** [Adaptable to existing fabric (Retrofit/Brownfield)] | The device is a better pour, not a better drawing: any site that casts concrete — including an in-fill block rebuilt inside the existing fabric — can swap struck-away shuttering for stay-in-place timber with no change to its structural scheme. The force-fit failure sits in approval, not in construction: where fire code and party-wall rules will not accept exposed timber as the recognized finish surface, the panels become a hidden extra layer nobody sees, the zero-finishing saving evaporates into a concealed second lining, and the carbon-lock metric shrinks from "the building body" to the bare tonnage of the buried boards.
**Cognitive anchor:** The exposed timber beams laid across the brick of "the old Yazd and Kashan house ceilings" — chub-khaab that was never plastered over and grew harder and handsomer with each decade of lamp smoke.
**Narrative hook:** The old masters left the beams visible and called the ceiling finished — here the formwork retires in place and you buy the ceiling with the concrete.
**Syn (synergies):** [Hybrid structure: impervious reinforced concrete below grade, mass engineered timber above (Hybrid Concrete-Mass Timber)](structural-material.md#hybrid-structure-impervious-reinforced-concrete-below-grade-mass-engineered-timber-above-hybrid-concrete-mass-timber) — the concrete-timber hybrid frame; [Acoustic Micro-Baffle Geometry for the 500-Person Hall in Glass](../form-and-land/sound-environment.md#acoustic-micro-baffle-geometry-for-the-500-person-hall-in-glass) — acoustic geometry improvement
**Links:** [Level-accurate slab pouring with a power trowel (5 mm tolerance): no corrective sloping screed, direct epoxy/parquet finish](placement-and-routing-of-service-flows.md#level-accurate-slab-pouring-with-a-power-trowel-5-mm-tolerance-no-corrective-sloping-screed-direct-epoxyparquet-finish) [complement] Shared philosophy: finishing stages eliminated by improving the quality of the structure/slab itself
**Source:** Yalda justification plan, p. 25

### How the approaches collide and compose
The two answers look composable — timber above for lightness, timber skins on whatever concrete remains — but [Hybrid structure: impervious reinforced concrete below grade, mass engineered timber above (Hybrid Concrete-Mass Timber)](structural-material.md#hybrid-structure-impervious-reinforced-concrete-below-grade-mass-engineered-timber-above-hybrid-concrete-mass-timber) and [Permanent mass-timber formwork left in place as thermal insulation and finished facade (Permanent Mass Timber Shuttering & Carbon Lock)](structural-material.md#permanent-mass-timber-formwork-left-in-place-as-thermal-insulation-and-finished-facade-permanent-mass-timber-shuttering--carbon-lock) contradict each other in their own texts, at the level of a single floor. The permanent-formwork record lists as its prerequisite what the hybrid record abolishes: a two-way hollow concrete slab. A CLT deck poured between glulam beams needs no concrete formwork at all, and a concrete slab skinned in timber keeps precisely the dead load and seismic mass the hybrid record's flaw text indicts as "astronomical". They are therefore not layers of one answer but rival cross-sections of the same floor plate, and any settlement-wide adoption must pick per project — or per block — which frame is standard.

One decision closes the seam, one question with two answers. Is the above-grade frame adjudicated as timber structure with concrete only below grade — the hybrid answer — with the permanent-skin method admitted solely on the concrete elements that survive anyway (cores, ramping, the basement shells)? Or is the frame adjudicated as concrete throughout, with the timber skin as the house method — the shuttering answer — and the CLT promise held only as a future variant once a certified domestic timber supply exists? The first answer argues from the numbers the hybrid record itself carries: a 50 percent lighter dead load is also a smaller seismic force at the foundation, which matters most under exactly the ten-basement deep structure both answers presuppose; and 3-to-5-day floors are installation speed the shuttering method, tied to every cure time, cannot match. The second answer argues from supply and code: the hybrid's own prerequisite (renewable-forest certified timber at structural scale) names no source in Iran, whereas the skin method rides the existing concrete industry, needs fire-treatment only of panels, and books a carbon lock whose claimed size — equivalence with the total cement of the body — is testable arithmetic. What evidence would settle it: the mass ratio of permanent timber to cement per square meter of floor (to check the equivalence claim), the fire classification of an exposed timber face in an escape route, and a landed cost curve for CLT sections against the local concrete-and-plaster bundle. Neither record contains these; the requirement stays open until they do.

They compose in one direction at least, and the records see it: both attack the same conventional waste — struck shuttering in the skip, the plasterer's crew following the pour — and the skin record's own link line pairs it with the level-slab method of the services requirement ([Level-accurate slab pouring with a power trowel (5 mm tolerance): no corrective sloping screed, direct epoxy/parquet finish](placement-and-routing-of-service-flows.md#level-accurate-slab-pouring-with-a-power-trowel-5-mm-tolerance-no-corrective-sloping-screed-direct-epoxyparquet-finish)), the three together forming one stance: the structure leaves the works as the finish. Where they must not be composed is the carbon ledger: embodied-carbon savings credited to timber substitution (the hybrid's 60-to-75 percent) and to carbon locked in skin (the shutter's cement-equivalence claim) measure different carbon flows; a block that used both devices on different elements could otherwise count the tree twice and the cement once.
