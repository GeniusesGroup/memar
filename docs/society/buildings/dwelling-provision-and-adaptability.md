# Dwelling Provision and Adaptability

## Abstract
The private share is the meter the society argues about most, because it is the meter every resident measures their life in: how much sealed space counts as theirs, who may change it, and what still fits the children of the people who move in. This document holds one question — how large is the private share, and how does the dwelling stay arrangeable and generation-fit? — and seven recorded answers that are not interchangeable: a co-living architecture that grants each resident a net 25 square meters of private space in exchange for at least 20 square meters of communal hospitality services per resident ([Co-living architecture with 25 square meters of private and 20 square meters of communal service space per resident](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#co-living-architecture-with-25-square-meters-of-private-and-20-square-meters-of-communal-service-space-per-resident)); a 20-square-meter, kitchen-less suite whose transformable furniture claims the function of a conventional 60-square-meter apartment ([20-square-meter kitchen-less dwelling suites with multi-state transformable furniture](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#20-square-meter-kitchen-less-dwelling-suites-with-multi-state-transformable-furniture)); dynamic non-load-bearing partitioning plus 4-complex service pooling as the adaptation instrument for a changing population pyramid ([Dynamic partitioning of spaces and inter-complex service sharing in the transition era](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#dynamic-partitioning-of-spaces-and-inter-complex-service-sharing-in-the-transition-era)); a neuroscience-led interior program for the native digital generations ([Space ergonomics and housing psychology for native digital generations (Gen Z/Alpha Ergonomics)](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#space-ergonomics-and-housing-psychology-for-native-digital-generations-gen-zalpha-ergonomics)); and the frameless pocket door that reclaims the swing area and removes the handle from hygiene ([Frameless sliding pocket doors with foot actuator and identity transmitter (Frameless Pocket Doors & Foot-Actuated Hygiene)](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#frameless-sliding-pocket-doors-with-foot-actuator-and-identity-transmitter-frameless-pocket-doors--foot-actuated-hygiene)). Two of the original five set rival numbers for the same meter — 25 as a minimum private floor, 20 as the whole private cell — so the document's deepest unresolved dispute is arithmetical before it is philosophical: a suite built to the 20-square-meter answer literally violates the 25-square-meter one, and only a decision about which number is the floor, and which is a permitted variant below it, can make the two answers coexist. Two recovered answers add a vertical section to the same floor plan: at least one floor of every complex reserved for capsule stays for tourists and shift-working service staff ([At least one capsule-residence floor per complex for tourists and shift-working service staff (Capsule-Residence Floor)](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#at-least-one-capsule-residence-floor-per-complex-for-tourists-and-shift-working-service-staff-capsule-residence-floor)), and a stair-first building that allows no escalators anywhere and instead makes the stair low-rise, wide and generous enough to tempt walking ([Stair-first design: no escalators anywhere, low-rise wide generous stairs that tempt walking (Stair-First Movement)](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#stair-first-design-no-escalators-anywhere-low-rise-wide-generous-stairs-that-tempt-walking-stair-first-movement)).

## Introduction

### Motivation
These five records were drafted together in the complex-building and life-complex proposals, yet separated into their own files they become five unrelated interior notes — a quota, a compact unit, a flexibility rule, a psychology brief, a door detail. Brought onto one page, three arguments that exist nowhere in the individual records appear. First, the quota argument: the co-living answer forbids the 20-square-meter suite it otherwise complements, because its own minimum is 25; no arithmetic in either text says which binds. Second, the work-location argument: one answer puts deep work in a built-in desk inside the private cell, the next puts the coworking hall in the communal 20, and the third claims a 40-percent focus gain without naming which box the person sits in. Third, the adaptability argument: the only records that answer "how does the plan survive the demographic pyramid" — reconfigurable partitions and pooled heavy services — are the retrofit-friendly ones, while the smallest and most radical cell is greenfield-only; on the existing fabric, "generation-fit" therefore arrives through flexibility, not through the 20-square-meter promise, and that sequencing is visible only when all five are read at once.

## Explanation

### The problem the answers share
How large should the private share be, and how does the dwelling stay arrangeable and fit for the generations that pass through it? Every approach below spends the same scarce currencies — square meters of sealed private space, square meters of communal service area, cubic meters of construction material, and the occupants' tolerance for change — and each answer trades them differently: give less private space and buy communal capacity, give no fixed functions and buy reconfigurability, give no kitchen and buy a restaurant, give up the hinged door and buy back its swing. The question does not favor any of them. Its verdict also has to survive time: a plan that fits the resident at move-in must fit the same rooms through a changing population pyramid — smaller households, older occupants, working hours that no longer leave — which is what "arrangeable and generation-fit" demands of whoever adjudicates the meters.

### Approaches

#### Co-living architecture with 25 square meters of private and 20 square meters of communal service space per resident
**Mechanism:** Delete the redundant and rarely-used household sections (the dedicated laundry, the dark storerooms, the idle reception halls) and allocate a net 25 square meters of fully independent space for sleep and privacy, in exchange for guaranteeing at least 20 square meters of shared hospitality service area — shared-work halls, sports, games and gathering spaces — for every resident.
**Metrics:** Minimum 25 square meters of sealed private space per person; minimum 20 square meters of sealed public-service space per person; a 35-percent reduction in the cost of building the shelter.
**Prerequisites:** A secure biometric or card access-control system; very high acoustic insulation in the shared walls between sleeping units.
**Conventional-model flaw:** The family isolation of conventional shoe-box apartments, which imprison 60 square meters in expensive equipment and induce a sense of emptiness and estrangement.
**Tags:** shared living, Co-Living, space optimization, sharing economy, social welfare
**Scale:** Micro — building
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional instrument] | The culture of solitary apartment-dwelling in the traditional fabric raises heavy resistance against shared spaces; imposing the pattern by force ends in neighbor tensions and the destruction of the commons.
**Cognitive anchor:** The verve and cheer of life in "the dormitories and shared complexes of the world's top universities" — a snug private room backed by shared facilities no single household could afford.
**Narrative hook:** "The best housing most of us ever had was a dormitory — your own room, a whole world outside the door; twenty-five square meters are yours, twenty more are everyone's."
**Syn (synergies):** [Every building mandated to provide basic services (sports hall, gatherings and shared assembly)](../form-and-land/service-placement-scale.md#every-building-mandated-to-provide-basic-services-sports-hall-gatherings-and-shared-assembly) — the mandatory basic services; [Abolishing the domestic micro-kitchens and establishing a shared centralized restaurant on the first floor](../food/meals-and-shared-cooking.md#abolishing-the-domestic-micro-kitchens-and-establishing-a-shared-centralized-restaurant-on-the-first-floor) — the first-floor centralized restaurant; [Redefining housing ownership as indirect shareholding to guarantee energy quality and building survival](../economy/land-housing-tenure.md#redefining-housing-ownership-as-indirect-shareholding-to-guarantee-energy-quality-and-building-survival) — share-based housing tenure
**Source:** Yalda-Life-Complex-Proposal.docx | Complex-Building-Proposal.docx

#### Dynamic partitioning of spaces and inter-complex service sharing in the transition era
**Mechanism:** Ban load-bearing masonry walls in the service spaces; use modular dividers and fitted (false) ceilings and floors so that the use of a space can be turned over within hours as the population pyramid shifts. During the urban transition, 4 neighboring complexes split the heavy services among themselves — one takes the education hub, another specialized sport, and so on — so the financial burden is shared out.
**Metrics:** 100-percent reconfigurability of the service plans; the education and health service scale distributed across 4 complexes without imposing a car trip inside the settlement.
**Prerequisites:** A long-span structural frame with hollow concrete slabs; a legal protocol for sharing the commons between neighboring complexes.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Rigid municipal rules that make moving a single plasterboard partition depend on a completion certificate, violation commissions and a fine.
**Tags:** dynamic spaces, physical adaptability, neighborhood network, transition era, infrastructure efficiency
**Scale:** Inter-building plane / mid — neighborhood
**Fit & substrate:** [Adaptable to existing fabric (Retrofit/Brownfield)] | Using movable walls inside existing small houses is possible, but traditional brick structures do not carry the loads of moved partitions, and un-engineered execution leads to walls collapsing.
**Cognitive anchor:** The transforming homes of "foresightful science-fiction films" — one gesture and the room becomes a study, a reception or a rest.
**Narrative hook:** "One room, three lives by dinner — and four neighbors share one gym none of them could own alone."
**Syn (synergies):** [Optimal scaling of the spatial grain (minimum 5-hectare domain and a 2,000-to-50-thousand-person biological module)](../form-and-land/settlement-grain-and-scaling.md#optimal-scaling-of-the-spatial-grain-minimum-5-hectare-domain-and-a-2000-to-50-thousand-person-biological-module) — the 5-hectare grain; [Co-living architecture with 25 square meters of private and 20 square meters of communal service space per resident](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#co-living-architecture-with-25-square-meters-of-private-and-20-square-meters-of-communal-service-space-per-resident) — co-living; [Synergistic clustering of 4 adjacent complexes for an economic level of specialized services](../form-and-land/service-placement-scale.md#synergistic-clustering-of-4-adjacent-complexes-for-an-economic-level-of-specialized-services) — the 4-complex local clusters
**Source:** Complex-Building-Proposal.docx | Yalda-Life-Complex-Proposal.docx

#### 20-square-meter kitchen-less dwelling suites with multi-state transformable furniture
**Mechanism:** Redefine the dwelling cell as a dedicated 20-square-meter suite without a kitchen, centered on quality sleep and deep work. The space is fitted with folding transform furniture (a wall bed that becomes an ergonomic sofa, an integrated pull-out desk). Because the need for rest and for work never occurs simultaneously, the functional yield of this 20-square-meter suite equals that of a conventional 60-square-meter apartment, and the cost of building shelter falls sharply.
**Metrics:** Net private area: 20 square meters per person; spatial utilization efficiency: above 90 percent; a 60-percent cut in the construction material consumed per household.
**Prerequisites:** Industrial design of durable mechanical modular furniture; acoustic insulation of the shared walls.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Family capital imprisoned in spacious yet idle apartments — heavy, unusable furniture bought, and a large share of the home converted into a storeroom for it.
**Tags:** compact suite, folding furniture, spatial transformer, square-meter optimization, economical housing
**Scale:** Micro — building (dwelling units)
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | In Iran's traditional housing market, where the large home and the fitted kitchen are the value, the suite meets severe psychological resistance and price depreciation.
**Cognitive anchor:** The cleverness of "Tokyo's minimal suites" — a compact yet open-feeling space whose furniture changes use with one movement.
**Narrative hook:** "Twenty square meters that sleep, work and receive — a room that idles nothing, not even its own bed."
**Syn (synergies):** [Abolishing the domestic micro-kitchens and establishing a shared centralized restaurant on the first floor](../food/meals-and-shared-cooking.md#abolishing-the-domestic-micro-kitchens-and-establishing-a-shared-centralized-restaurant-on-the-first-floor) — the centralized dining restaurant; [Co-living architecture with 25 square meters of private and 20 square meters of communal service space per resident](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#co-living-architecture-with-25-square-meters-of-private-and-20-square-meters-of-communal-service-space-per-resident) (the co-living hotel-like shared spaces); [Seasonally reversible in-wall HVAC (In-Wall Seasonal Reversible HVAC)](placement-and-routing-of-service-flows.md#seasonally-reversible-in-wall-hvac-in-wall-seasonal-reversible-hvac) — the in-wall air handler
**Source:** Complex-Building-Proposal.docx | Yalda-Life-Complex-Proposal.docx

#### Space ergonomics and housing psychology for native digital generations (Gen Z/Alpha Ergonomics)
**Mechanism:** Redesign the architecture of the private suites on the basis of neuroscience and the biological preferences of the generations born into the digital age: abolish idle luxury objects, use calming tactile materials, apply dynamic lighting matched to the body's clock (Circadian Lighting), install super-heavy acoustic insulation for deep focused work, and hold continuous sightlines to the green cover outside so that display fatigue is discharged.
**Metrics:** A 40-percent increase in mental-focus efficiency; a reduction in stress-hormone secretion, based on biometric health data; 100-percent reconfiguration of the space for day and night scenarios.
**Prerequisites:** [Co-living architecture with 25 square meters of private and 20 square meters of communal service space per resident](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#co-living-architecture-with-25-square-meters-of-private-and-20-square-meters-of-communal-service-space-per-resident) / [20-square-meter kitchen-less dwelling suites with multi-state transformable furniture](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#20-square-meter-kitchen-less-dwelling-suites-with-multi-state-transformable-furniture) (compact suites with folding furniture), legacy-ref (unresolved) / [Bio-adaptive control of the indoor climate on operative temperature and body physiology (Bio-Adaptive Control)](../energy/climate-signal-authority.md#bio-adaptive-control-of-the-indoor-climate-on-operative-temperature-and-body-physiology-bio-adaptive-control) (bio-adaptive temperature control).
**Conventional-model flaw:** The insistence on apartments with traditional plans and formal reception rooms that match nothing in the temperament, the communication needs and the working style of the present generation.
**Tags:** environmental psychology, digital generations, spatial flexibility, biophilic light, circadian rhythm
**Scale:** Micro — building (interior architecture)
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional instrument] | Every measure in the mechanism except one is a retrofittable interior treatment: circadian lighting, heavy acoustic insulation, tactile materials and the deletion of idle furniture fit any existing room. The one that defines the promise — the continuous sightline to green cover outside — is unbuyable for any owner whose window opens onto a wall, so in the existing fabric the approach degrades into a well-equipped dark room, which is precisely the flaw text it indicts; in the new settlement the same measures only work if the ground plane they look out on is kept, which makes this record a client of other verdicts rather than an independent one.
**Cognitive anchor:** The quietest room most Iranians remember from a grandmother's house: thick walls that held the street's noise out, and a window onto the courtyard's one tree where homework was done in cool shade. The digital-native's deep work asks for exactly those two things — silence and something living in view — with screens added; the approach is that old room rebuilt as the standard, not the inheritance.
**Narrative hook:** "The best work room you ever had was your grandmother's — thick walls, one green view; we rebuilt it for the generation of the screen."
**Syn (synergies):** [A wellness centre with continuous preventive health monitoring at the complex's service level](../living-and-culture/continuous-health-watch.md#a-wellness-centre-with-continuous-preventive-health-monitoring-at-the-complexs-service-level) — continuous health monitoring; [Daylighting architecture and the optimization of incidence angle](daylight-to-interiors.md#daylighting-architecture-and-the-optimization-of-incidence-angle) — natural daylighting; [Hyper-local distributed employment and co-working spaces adjacent to dwelling (Hyper-Local Co-Working)](../form-and-land/service-placement-scale.md#hyper-local-distributed-employment-and-co-working-spaces-adjacent-to-dwelling-hyper-local-co-working) — the calm shared work environment
**Source:** Yalda-Life-Complex-Proposal.docx | Complex-Building-Proposal.docx

#### Frameless sliding pocket doors with foot actuator and identity transmitter (Frameless Pocket Doors & Foot-Actuated Hygiene)
**Mechanism:** Delete hinges and rigid frames from all interior doors entirely, replacing them with rail-guided sliding doors that retract into the thickness of the drywall partitions (Pocket Doors). The doors open and close without handles, commanded either by distance-detection of the person's identity transmitter or by pressure/light foot sensors at the threshold, so that microbial and contact contamination is not carried through washrooms.
**Metrics:** 1.5 square meters of swing area saved per door; 100-percent elimination of hand contact with contaminated handles; motion energy below 15 watts.
**Prerequisites:** [20-square-meter kitchen-less dwelling suites with multi-state transformable furniture](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#20-square-meter-kitchen-less-dwelling-suites-with-multi-state-transformable-furniture), [A self-sovereign citizen identity with no disciplinary ranking (Sovereign Digital Identity)](../governance/membership-and-identity.md#a-self-sovereign-citizen-identity-with-no-disciplinary-ranking-sovereign-digital-identity) (the self-sovereign identity transmitter).
**Conventional-model flaw:** Hinged conventional doors that waste a quarter of a small room's area in their swing arc, with handles that are hubs for transmitting viruses and bacteria.
**Tags:** biosanitary hygiene, hidden sliding door, foot actuator, RFID transmitter, frame removal
**Scale:** Micro — building (suite interiors)
**Fit & substrate:** [Adaptable to existing fabric (Retrofit/Brownfield)] | The pocket and its rail live in non-load-bearing partitions and a 15-watt supply, so any renovated washroom in the existing fabric can take them — but chased into a traditional brick bearing wall, the pocket cut is exactly the un-engineered demolition the dynamic-partition record warns ends in falling walls, and a door that reads an identity transmitter in a settlement without one is a sensor waiting for a system it does not own.
**Cognitive anchor:** Everyone in Iran knows the arithmetic of a small kitchen with guests: hands full of bread, tea and a sleeping child, elbowing a hinged door open. The automatic entrance at a mall taught the same lesson from the other side — a door that opens before you touch it is felt as welcome, not as gadgetry; the pocket door brings the mall's relief into every washroom and reclaims the floor its swing stole.
**Narrative hook:** "Your hands are full of bread and a sleeping child — the door has already opened."
**Syn (synergies):** [Cancelling retail packaging: food and dairy flow from cold store to meal in large reusable steel vessels](../food/meals-and-shared-cooking.md#cancelling-retail-packaging-food-and-dairy-flow-from-cold-store-to-meal-in-large-reusable-steel-vessels) — the service-carrying partition walls; [Space ergonomics and housing psychology for native digital generations (Gen Z/Alpha Ergonomics)](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#space-ergonomics-and-housing-psychology-for-native-digital-generations-gen-zalpha-ergonomics) — digital-generation ergonomics
**Source:** Yalda justification plan (pp. 25-26) )

#### At least one capsule-residence floor per complex for tourists and shift-working service staff (Capsule-Residence Floor)
**Mechanism:** Reserve at least one floor of every residential complex for the capsule style: the private portion reduced to its smallest state — a cabin at two-by-one-by-one meters or larger dimensions — containing the minimum private space, with the essential services, sanitary facilities among them, held in common on the floor. The floor's first users are guests and the complex's own service staff resting between shifts, so the complex never has to answer a short stay with a long-stay suite or export its workers' rest to a hotel outside the door.
**Metrics:** Capsule floors per complex: at least one; smallest private capsule volume: 2 × 1 × 1 meters (larger dimensions permitted); private sanitary provision on the capsule floor: none — services shared; use pattern: short stays, in some cases extended.
**Prerequisites:** Shared sanitary and service infrastructure per floor; access control that can grant a guest or a shift crew entry to one floor without opening the suites of the rest; [Co-living architecture with 25 square meters of private and 20 square meters of communal service space per resident](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#co-living-architecture-with-25-square-meters-of-private-and-20-square-meters-of-communal-service-space-per-resident) (the communal-services machinery the capsule floor extends downward).
**Conventional-model flaw:** The hotel as a separate building type — a whole structure of corridors, lobbies and daily linen change standing empty half the year two streets away, while the residence next door cannot house the visitor or the night-shift nurse it already employs.
**Tags:** capsule hotel, short-stay housing, shift workers, service floor, minimum private space
**Scale:** Micro — building (one floor)
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | The capsule floor is one slab of a tower it was designed with; inserted into an existing building it loses its premise — the shared sanitary commons, the access-controlled separation from suites, the service staff whose shifts created the demand — and becomes a hostel that the neighboring residents did not consent to.
**Cognitive anchor:** The first bunk of an Iranian youth hostel or the train's sleeping compartment: a private volume barely bigger than the body, clean, lockable, shared bathroom down the corridor — rest without apartment.
**Narrative hook:** "One floor per tower where the private share is a body's length and a night — the guest sleeps here, the night-shift nurse sleeps here, and neither needed a hotel."
**Syn (synergies):** [Abolishing the domestic micro-kitchens and establishing a shared centralized restaurant on the first floor](../food/meals-and-shared-cooking.md#abolishing-the-domestic-micro-kitchens-and-establishing-a-shared-centralized-restaurant-on-the-first-floor) — the shared service plant the capsule floor draws on; [Co-living architecture with 25 square meters of private and 20 square meters of communal service space per resident](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#co-living-architecture-with-25-square-meters-of-private-and-20-square-meters-of-communal-service-space-per-resident) — communal services below the capsule's floor minimum; [Stair-first design: no escalators anywhere, low-rise wide generous stairs that tempt walking (Stair-First Movement)](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#stair-first-design-no-escalators-anywhere-low-rise-wide-generous-stairs-that-tempt-walking-stair-first-movement) — the floor a guest reaches by stair

#### Stair-first design: no escalators anywhere, low-rise wide generous stairs that tempt walking (Stair-First Movement)
**Mechanism:** Design the building's vertical movement from the stair outward, not the machine outward. Escalators are refused as an element of the building; in their place, and in place of the lift's monopoly, the stairs are made easy enough to take — low risers, wide flights, dimensions that read as generous rather than as emergency escapes — so that movement is returned to the person's own legs and the body stays mobile without a gym's appointment. The stair carries the building's design feeling: people should be tempted to take the stair instead of the elevator, and the decoration the building lavishes on its paths belongs on the stair as much as anywhere.
**Metrics:** Escalators installed: zero; stair proportion: reduced riser height with enlarged, generous dimensions; the design test: the stair is the path a resident would choose, not the one left over when the lift breaks.
**Prerequisites:** Floor-to-floor heights and unit layouts set so that the generous stair fits the slab economy; [20-square-meter kitchen-less dwelling suites with multi-state transformable furniture](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#20-square-meter-kitchen-less-dwelling-suites-with-multi-state-transformable-furniture) and the service floors — the vertical loads the stair must carry daily; lift cores retained for goods, mobility-impaired residents and height.
**Conventional-model flaw:** The escalator as generous technology — moving carpet that spends continuous energy to spare a person the effort that kept their body working — and the conventional stair: a dim, narrow escape shaft that punishes anyone who chooses health over convenience.
**Tags:** stair-first design, no escalators, active mobility, energy avoidance, human-powered movement
**Scale:** Micro — building
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional] | Refusing escalators is free in new construction and costs nothing in the existing fabric — an escalator can simply be removed and the stair re-clad — but the low-rise, wide-flight geometry needs the floor heights and slab spans a conventional building already committed elsewhere; in retrofits the mechanism survives only as the negative half: no escalators, generous decoration and lighting on the existing stair.
**Cognitive anchor:** The grand staircase of an old house or bazaar teahouse — wide, shallow, beautiful, where everyone walked because walking was pleasant; the stair as room, not leftover.
**Narrative hook:** "The building spends on its stairs what others spend on escalators — and the residents stop needing to exercise."
**Syn (synergies):** [At least one capsule-residence floor per complex for tourists and shift-working service staff (Capsule-Residence Floor)](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#at-least-one-capsule-residence-floor-per-complex-for-tourists-and-shift-working-service-staff-capsule-residence-floor) — the short-stay floor reached by stair; [A wellness centre with continuous preventive health monitoring at the complex's service level](../living-and-culture/continuous-health-watch.md#a-wellness-centre-with-continuous-preventive-health-monitoring-at-the-complexs-service-level) — daily movement instead of prescribed exercise; [Regenerative-braking elevators with per-user identity-metered energy accounting (Regenerative Elevator Metering)](placement-and-routing-of-service-flows.md#regenerative-braking-elevators-with-per-user-identity-metered-energy-accounting-regenerative-elevator-metering) — the lift whose remaining energy the stair halves
**Source:** Yalda-Life-Complex-Proposal.docx | Complex-Building-Proposal.docx

### How the approaches collide and compose
**Twenty-five or twenty: the private minimum is one question with two numbers, and the smaller answer lives inside the larger record's own synergy lines.** [Co-living architecture with 25 square meters of private and 20 square meters of communal service space per resident](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#co-living-architecture-with-25-square-meters-of-private-and-20-square-meters-of-communal-service-space-per-resident) states "minimum 25 square meters of sealed private space per person" as a metric, and its mechanism reaches the number by deleting idle household sections — laundry, storeroom, reception. [20-square-meter kitchen-less dwelling suites with multi-state transformable furniture](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#20-square-meter-kitchen-less-dwelling-suites-with-multi-state-transformable-furniture) reaches its 20 by deleting a whole standing function — the kitchen — and time-multiplexing the room (rest and work never occur simultaneously), then claims equality with the same 60-square-meter conventional apartment the co-living record's flaw text attacks. The suite's own synergies line links to the co-living record for "the hotel-like shared spaces," so the two answers are designed to compose; as metrics they cannot: a building of 20-square-meter suites breaches the co-living minimum on every door, and a building of 25-square-meter rooms idles five square meters per person by the suite's arithmetic. One question, two answers, one owner ruling: is 25 the floor with 20 a permitted variant bought by deleting a function (at which point the co-living metric becomes an average and needs an averaging rule), or are 25 and 20 rival minima and must one be struck? The evidence that would settle it is the functional-yield claim itself — the assertion that a 20-square-meter time-shared room equals a 60-square-meter apartment — which no recorded study supports, only the furniture's geometry.

**Where does the work sit?** The suite puts deep work inside the private cell on an integrated pull-out desk; the co-living answer counts shared-work halls among the communal 20 it promises; the ergonomic record adds that deep focus — its claimed 40-percent gain — needs super-heavy acoustic insulation, and its synergy lines reach outward to the hyper-local co-working spaces of the service-placement ledger. One question, three recorded answers about the same evening: does the resident shut a 15-watt door on a bedroom desk, walk to the building's work hall, or cross the cluster to the coworking floor? The answers are not free — the acoustic insulation that makes working at home viable is priced into the suite's prerequisites, and the shared halls are priced into the communal quota, so the location of work decides which record carries the square meters and the watts. The owner must name the default work location; the ergonomic record cannot even state its own metric until the box the person sits in is fixed.

**As a timeline the five compose, and the sequence is written in their Fit fields.** [Dynamic partitioning of spaces and inter-complex service sharing in the transition era](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#dynamic-partitioning-of-spaces-and-inter-complex-service-sharing-in-the-transition-era) is the only answer admissible on the existing fabric as written — movable dividers inside inherited units, four complexes pooling heavy services to afford them — and it is also what makes the other minima survivable across time: 100-percent reconfigurability is the sole mechanism in the document that answers "generation-fit" for a household whose size changes, where the two quota records answer it only for a household whose functions change. The suite is greenfield-only by its own critique (the market resists the 20-meter cell); the ergonomic record is [Dual — transitional] but depends on greenfield sightlines to be honest; the pocket door is retrofit-friendly but requires an identity system governance has promised elsewhere. Read in order, the dynamic-partition answer is the one an existing settlement can buy today with no verdict on the quota dispute, which makes it the first instrument and the quota dispute the decision deferred — not solved — into the new town. Meanwhile [Space ergonomics and housing psychology for native digital generations (Gen Z/Alpha Ergonomics)](dwelling-provision-and-adaptability.md#space-ergonomics-and-housing-psychology-for-native-digital-generations-gen-zalpha-ergonomics)'s own prerequisite line still carries a `legacy-ref (unresolved)`, so at least one more dependency of the ergonomic record is not yet attributed to any approach in the ledger.
