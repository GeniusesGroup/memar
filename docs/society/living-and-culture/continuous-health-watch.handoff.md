## Open Questions
1. **Is continuous monitoring one program with three sensors or three programs with one shared body — and where does the data sleep?** The wellness centre lists the complex as its site, the mirror lists the centre as its platform, and the toothbrush lists the self-sovereign identity as its endpoint — two custodians claimed by one stream of samples from one organism. See the collision section's one question, two answers. *(Why: the 60%-fewer-visits metric cannot be attributed to any device until someone states whether the body's data sleeps in a facility, a vault, or both; and the identity page's no-tracking clause either governs all three endpoints or governs only the brush. Blocking: yes for the combined verdict; yes for the mirror record alone, which names no custodian.)*
2. **How much of the everyday body is too much?** The requirement's own question ("how much... should be monitored") is answered by escalation — periodic, daily, twice-daily — and by nothing that sets a floor, an opt-out, or an aggregation rule that counts the three together. *(Why: a citizen may consent to each device and be surveilled by the set; no record defines whether consent is per-endpoint or per-profile. Blocking: no for the requirement's adjudication; yes for implementing any endpoint without a consent architecture.)*
3. **Does the wellness centre's economics contradict its metric?** The centre reduces hospital visits by over 60% while operating as a service the complex provides; its Fit field names the capture mode — referral income under a monopoly — and the page records nothing that separates the centre's revenue from the referrals its prevention produces. Is the centre non-referring, capitated, or audited on its reduction metric? *(Why: the record's success number and its own warning are pointed at each other; a verdict without an incentive rule adjudicates a fiction. Blocking: yes for the wellness record's verdict; no for the endpoints, which depend on it.)*
4. **Is bathroom telemetry lifestyle data — and therefore forbidden by the identity record?** The mirror reads skin lesions, respiration, pulse and temperature in the one room the lifestyle clause of governance/membership-and-identity.md was written to keep dark; disease markers are not beliefs or dress, but they are body data from a private space, and the identity system's promise is that no one tracks personal behavior. Does health data inherit the lifestyle exemption, or does attestation-only identity make the endpoints unlawful to read it centrally? *(Why: this is the membership page's ranking question returning with skin on; one of the two pages must yield, or the exemption must be drafted. Blocking: yes for the mirror record's verdict; yes for the joint identity ruling.)*
