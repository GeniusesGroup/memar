# Data Storage and Processing

## Abstract
One requirement, two architectures that refute different propositions: the society's data can be stored and processed in a single megadatacenter whose waste heat is the product it sells — [100% recovery of megadatacenter thermal exergy (the Middle East datacenter)](data-storage-and-processing.md#100-recovery-of-megadatacenter-thermal-exergy-the-middle-east-datacenter) — or sunk as a sealed data hall into one floor of every 500-person block's ten basements — [Block datacenter: distributed compute and data storage on one floor of every block's ten basements, in opposition to the centralized megadatacenter](data-storage-and-processing.md#block-datacenter-distributed-compute-and-data-storage-on-one-floor-of-every-blocks-ten-basements-in-opposition-to-the-centralized-megadatacenter). The megacenter earns its scale thermodynamically — it recovers above 90% of its heat and holds PUE below 1.15 only at a size no block can host — while the block answers on jurisdictional grounds the megacenter's text never addresses: single points of failure, and citizen data parked outside the community it describes. The verdict between them is open, and the openness is substantive, not editorial: other records in this set take "the datacenter" as a prerequisite — the policy sandbox that rehearses bills on 50000 simulated residents, the campus that uses the centre as a living laboratory — and whichever answer wins relocates what those prerequisites silently assumed.

## Introduction

### Motivation
The first answer was drafted inside the founding design documents as an industrial-symbiosis asset — a plant whose exhaust is someone else's crop heat. The second arrived later, dictated into a working session as an explicit dissent ("opposition to the centralized datacenter") and recorded there so the position would not be lost between meetings. Scattered, they mislead each other in both directions: read alone, the dissent looks like a nitpick against an unchallenged plan, and the plan's heat-recovery arithmetic looks uncontested when its strongest objection is a floor away. Read together, the disagreement is visible as what it is — a dispute over whether a society's compute is a plant (big, hot, shared, geographically singular) or a fixture (small, redundant, local, thermodynamically modest) — and that choice decides where every other record's "datacenter" prerequisite actually points.

## Explanation

### The problem the answers share
At what scale, and where, should data-storage and processing capacity sit? The question is answerable without prejudging either architecture: it fixes neither the number of sites nor their geography, and it leaves open the two things the records bundle — whether waste heat is an argument for concentration or an accident of it, and whether a citizen's data must live inside the community it describes. Sharpened, the shared problem has three axes each answer must state a position on: scale (one megacenter versus one hall per block), jurisdiction (whose precinct holds the records), and substrate (whether the answer needs the deep-basement module or the technology-park plain). The question favors none of the positions on record; it only refuses to let a verdict on one axis pass as a verdict on all three.

### Approaches

#### 100% recovery of megadatacenter thermal exergy (the Middle East datacenter)
**Mechanism:** Establish the largest datacenter site in the Middle East as the driving engine of the green society's software economy. Datacenters are heavy electricity consumers that convert more than 98% of that power directly into waste heat, and the record stakes nothing less than 100 percent recovery of that thermal exergy: applying direct-to-chip liquid cooling of the servers, the outgoing hot water (50 to 65 degrees) is injected, without loss, through industrial heat exchangers into the heating of hotel-apartment zones, hydroponic greenhouses and aquaponic ponds, driving the need for expensive evaporative cooling installations down to nearly zero.
**Metrics:** Server heat-recovery efficiency: above 90%; the datacenter's power-usage effectiveness reduced to PUE < 1.15; free thermal supply for dozens of hectares of controlled-cultivation installations.
**Prerequisites:** Server racks designed around closed water-cooling circuits; complete thermal insulation of the flow paths; co-location adjacent to the heat-demanding sections.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Building datacenters with vast air chillers that spend gigawatts to dump this heat into the atmosphere and waste the resources.
**Tags:** megadatacenter, waste-heat recovery, liquid cooling, industrial symbiosis, digital ecology
**Scale:** Macro — the technology-park site
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | Siting giant datacenters amid the residential fabric of the conventional settlement is impossible, and transmitting their heat through long urban channels is economically unjustified because of exergy loss; the phenomenon requires structural integration in the new settlement.
**Cognitive anchor:** The modern efficiency of "advanced cloud-server liquid-cooling systems" — where the heat of the processors, instead of being wasted, warms the home.
**Narrative hook:** "A power plant wearing a server rack: 98% of what it eats comes back out as heat — the only question is whether it heats greenhouses or sky."

#### Block datacenter: distributed compute and data storage on one floor of every block's ten basements, in opposition to the centralized megadatacenter
**Mechanism:** In opposition to the centralized megadatacenter architecture ([100% recovery of megadatacenter thermal exergy (the Middle East datacenter)](data-storage-and-processing.md#100-recovery-of-megadatacenter-thermal-exergy-the-middle-east-datacenter)), the society's storage and computing capacity is sited distributively at each 500-person block: one of the block's 10 specialized basement floors is assigned to a data hall (the block datacenter), enclosed by 50-centimeter concrete walls behind a heavily secured door. This way no single large-scale point of failure exists, citizens' data stays within the precinct of the same local community, and physical access to the society's computing infrastructure is secured by the block's surface-level perimeter instead of a distant sanctuary. Control of the physical entrances rests with the multi-signature custody protocol ([Multi-signature custody protocol for the physical portals of sensitive spaces with duress revocation and no automatic repair access](../security-and-resilience/securing-sensitive-spaces.md#multi-signature-custody-protocol-for-the-physical-portals-of-sensitive-spaces-with-duress-revocation-and-no-automatic-repair-access)).
**Metrics:** Computing capacity distributed per block instead of geographically concentrated; 50-centimeter retaining concrete wall; elimination of the single point of failure at large scale.
**Prerequisites:** Allocation of one level of the [Vertical zoning of 10 basement levels (Deep Subsurface Clustering) and 35-meter penetration](../form-and-land/which-plane-carries-which-flow.md#vertical-zoning-of-10-basement-levels-deep-subsurface-clustering-and-35-meter-penetration) to the data hall; the block's local power and cooling infrastructure; the access-protocol suite of [Multi-signature custody protocol for the physical portals of sensitive spaces with duress revocation and no automatic repair access](../security-and-resilience/securing-sensitive-spaces.md#multi-signature-custody-protocol-for-the-physical-portals-of-sensitive-spaces-with-duress-revocation-and-no-automatic-repair-access).
**Conventional-model flaw:** The centralized megadatacenter architecture: a single point of failure, the concentration of citizens' data in a precinct outside the community, and total dependence on the continuity of one geographically singular infrastructure.
**Tags:** distributed computing, data infrastructure, passive defence, resilience, specialized basement
**Scale:** Mid — block (one floor of the 10 basement floors)
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | The data hall has no room to exist without the 10-basement module it presupposes: in the existing fabric a block's deepest basement is a car-park slab without the retaining strength, dedicated power or fire compartmentation a 50-centimeter enclosure assumes, and retrofitting the hall there spends the budget on walls whose adversaries — flood, fire, and the parking public — are precisely the ones the module was designed not to have.
**Cognitive anchor:** The secure quarter of the old neighbourhood — the block whose own alley walls were the boundary: what mattered stayed inside, and its staying there did not depend on trusting anyone beyond them.
**Narrative hook:** "Your data sleeps under your neighbours' feet — behind the same concrete that holds up their courtyard."

### How the approaches collide and compose
**The two answers do not contradict each other on the same variable — each one attacks a proposition the other never asserts, which is why neither can simply win.** [100% recovery of megadatacenter thermal exergy (the Middle East datacenter)](data-storage-and-processing.md#100-recovery-of-megadatacenter-thermal-exergy-the-middle-east-datacenter) argues on thermodynamics and economics: heat recovery above 90% and PUE < 1.15 are plant-scale properties, and its stated payoff — free heat for dozens of hectares of controlled cultivation — cannot be earned by a hall that serves one block. [Block datacenter: distributed compute and data storage on one floor of every block's ten basements, in opposition to the centralized megadatacenter](data-storage-and-processing.md#block-datacenter-distributed-compute-and-data-storage-on-one-floor-of-every-blocks-ten-basements-in-opposition-to-the-centralized-megadatacenter) argues on failure geography and jurisdiction: its flaw text indicts the single point of failure and the parking of citizens' data outside their own community — objections the megacenter's mechanism never answers, because its mechanism is about heat, not about custody. And the block's own fields concede the reverse silence: it records no thermal story at all, so the argument that made the megacenter legitimate — exhaust as a product — evaporates at block scale, replaced by an unnamed cooling burden behind the 50-centimeter wall. As a set, the two records also compose silently on one point: the block record's opening link names its counterpart as the architecture it opposes, so the ledger itself treats them as one dispute with two answers, not two unrelated needs.

**One decision with two answers: is this a verdict between architectures, or a recorded workload split nobody has written down?** The first answer says the records are rivals and a society chooses one: if the heat is sold, take the plant — the synergy lines make the Atna farms and the solar station clients and suppliers of the megacenter, a circular industrial economy a per-block hall cannot start; if the passive-defence logic governs, take the blocks — and then the megacenter's heat argument is forfeit, because a society that distributes its compute cannot concentrate its exhaust. The second answer says a society can hold both, because the hall is small (one floor per block) and the plant is hot: bulk computation — the [Policy-simulation sandbox with agent-based computational modelling (Agent-Based Policy Sandbox)](../governance/decision-and-deliberation-process.md#policy-simulation-sandbox-with-agent-based-computational-modelling-agent-based-policy-sandbox)'s 50000-agent runs, whose prerequisites name the megacenter's cloud capacity — at the centre, custody of citizen records under the block's concrete. But that split is itself a new dispute no record owns: the sandbox needs de-identified behavioral data about residents, and the block answer's core promise is that citizen data never leaves the block precinct — so any combined verdict must say which data is bulk-computable and whose consent path crosses architectures, or the two records will have composed a contradiction. Until such a split is recorded, the verdict between [100% recovery of megadatacenter thermal exergy (the Middle East datacenter)](data-storage-and-processing.md#100-recovery-of-megadatacenter-thermal-exergy-the-middle-east-datacenter) and [Block datacenter](data-storage-and-processing.md#block-datacenter-distributed-compute-and-data-storage-on-one-floor-of-every-blocks-ten-basements-in-opposition-to-the-centralized-megadatacenter) stays open, as its source recorded it — and the openness is carried in this document's handoff, not here resolved.
