## Open Questions
1. **What may a reading act on without a person, and on whose clock?** The four records install four hands on one set of levers: the water center automates routing completely, second by second ([Intelligent monitoring and management center for greywater and drinking-water resources](observability-of-flows.md#intelligent-monitoring-and-management-center-for-greywater-and-drinking-water-resources)); the exergy ledger settles accounts on 5-second steps ([Real-time exergy balance and monitoring at subsystem level (Real-Time Exergy Ledger)](observability-of-flows.md#real-time-exergy-balance-and-monitoring-at-subsystem-level-real-time-exergy-ledger)); the twin acts only through its owner across a 50-year horizon ([Digital twin and building lifecycle monitoring (6D/7D BIM Lifecycle)](observability-of-flows.md#digital-twin-and-building-lifecycle-monitoring-6d7d-bim-lifecycle)); the hall acts with a human in under 30 seconds ([Operational monitoring and command core of the four central towers (Central Quad-Towers Monitoring Core)](observability-of-flows.md#operational-monitoring-and-command-core-of-the-four-central-towers-central-quad-towers-monitoring-core)). The water loop routinely outruns the hall's own response budget, and the security ledger's rule that no machine may open the door these instruments live behind is the page's borrowed, unanswered doctrine. One question, two answers: automaticity as the goal, tiered by reversibility and stated nowhere (which leaves the valve's crisis-routing unowned), or a written actuation-authority boundary (physical flows inside the water system may move automatically only where the next reading corrects them, and every reading touching a protected space inherits the custody protocol) — with the hall's role defined as exception and not theatre. *(Why: the title's "who turns the reading into action" is currently answered four times, incoherently; any adoption fixes the stack's politics. Blocking: yes for [Intelligent monitoring and management center for greywater and drinking-water resources](observability-of-flows.md#intelligent-monitoring-and-management-center-for-greywater-and-drinking-water-resources)'s full automation and for [Operational monitoring and command core of the four central towers (Central Quad-Towers Monitoring Core)](observability-of-flows.md#operational-monitoring-and-command-core-of-the-four-central-towers-central-quad-towers-monitoring-core)'s crisis doctrine; no for [Real-time exergy balance and monitoring at subsystem level (Real-Time Exergy Ledger)](observability-of-flows.md#real-time-exergy-balance-and-monitoring-at-subsystem-level-real-time-exergy-ledger)'s metering and [Digital twin and building lifecycle monitoring (6D/7D BIM Lifecycle)](observability-of-flows.md#digital-twin-and-building-lifecycle-monitoring-6d7d-bim-lifecycle)'s modeling on their own.)*
2. **Who audits the instruments?** Every completeness figure on the page is an instrument claim — 0.1 percent error, 100 percent components, 5-second cadence, 100,000 sensors — and the only verification instrument this page itself has is the hall that reads them, the platform that bills them, and the twin that logs them, all on a custody architecture that (per its page) may not be inspectable. Who holds the accuracy of the meters that define accuracy elsewhere in the batch? *(Why: observability is the settlement's root of trust for every numeric claim in every other page; if its own numbers are unchecked, the whole ledger's arithmetic inherits their error bars. Blocking: yes for adopting any metric on this page as an acceptance criterion; this is the batch's deepest dependency.)*
3. **One sensor census or four metering economies?** [Operational monitoring and command core of the four central towers (Central Quad-Towers Monitoring Core)](observability-of-flows.md#operational-monitoring-and-command-core-of-the-four-central-towers-central-quad-towers-monitoring-core)'s "more than 100,000 sensors" is the settlement's single stated sensor population; [Intelligent monitoring and management center for greywater and drinking-water resources](observability-of-flows.md#intelligent-monitoring-and-management-center-for-greywater-and-drinking-water-resources), [Digital twin and building lifecycle monitoring (6D/7D BIM Lifecycle)](observability-of-flows.md#digital-twin-and-building-lifecycle-monitoring-6d7d-bim-lifecycle) and [Real-time exergy balance and monitoring at subsystem level (Real-Time Exergy Ledger)](observability-of-flows.md#real-time-exergy-balance-and-monitoring-at-subsystem-level-real-time-exergy-ledger) each assume their own probes on the same pipes, fittings and converters. Is the hall's fleet the sum of the stacks (one installation, four readers) or independent (four billing relationships with the same buildings)? *(Why: the difference is real money, real maintenance and real data ownership; the records never reconcile their populations. Blocking: yes for the hall's capacity claim; yes for any per-building metering budget.)*
4. **Whose memory is the twin?** The per-building model is 100 percent of components tracked across 50 years and is priced against a shareholding incentive (its prerequisite: indirect share ownership that makes owners care); the hall aggregates the same physical world in the operational tense, in a room that also houses the platform's own developers. If the building's truth is the twin and the hall's wall is its projection, then the hall depends on owners keeping their models current — which is the conventional failure the twin's flaw text describes, with a share-ownership patch. If instead the hall is authoritative and the twin downstream, the building's 50-year diary belongs to the platform that also runs its billing. Which copy is true, and can the answer be both without a stated precedence rule when they disagree? *(Why: the settlement's self-model is a custody object — the platform ledger's question lands on this page's instruments. Blocking: yes for [Digital twin and building lifecycle monitoring (6D/7D BIM Lifecycle)](observability-of-flows.md#digital-twin-and-building-lifecycle-monitoring-6d7d-bim-lifecycle)/[Operational monitoring and command core of the four central towers (Central Quad-Towers Monitoring Core)](observability-of-flows.md#operational-monitoring-and-command-core-of-the-four-central-towers-central-quad-towers-monitoring-core) joint adoption.)*
