#### What changed
- The collision section gained the default-surface ruling the first pass left hidden inside a harmony claim: the deck record binds every necessary pedestrian ground to dry-laid stone while MICP claims pedestrian paths as crusted soil — two defaults for one floor; the owner decision (universal paving clause vs crust supersedes where maintained) is named with the evidence each side argues from, and opened as handoff Q5 with a blocking flag.
- The deck–MICP pairing was corrected from "one act stated twice" to "one end pursued by two rival means", since both records prescribe the walking surface itself.
- The mist curtain's "touches no soil" claim was corrected: what the curtain precipitates (85–92 percent of swept particulate) is removed from the air, not destroyed, and falls onto the contested ground — feedstock for a maintained crust, silt for a grass lattice, re-loosened dust for stone joints; the siting consequence opened as handoff Q6.
- The MICP Fit field's substrate requirement was corrected: the crust needs exposed, uncemented, sprayable in-situ soil, not "unsalted living sand" — the bacterium arrives by spray, and the binding constraints are the absence of pavement and the compaction of the soil beneath it, which is exactly what the retrofit critique then restates without a false premise.
- Spec audit: frontmatter intact; Abstract carries only this document's own claims; no open questions or reviewer flags in the body.
- Handoff: Q1–Q4 all remain live (none answered by this pass); Q5 and Q6 added; the spraying-regime assumption recorded as Unexamined with the pointer that Q5 converts it into a measured reliability figure; next steps and extensions updated.

#### Deliberation
- The first pass held that the deck record and the crust "are one act stated twice" and that the curtain "touches no soil"; the second pass countered both — one plaza has one floor, and both the paving clause and the crust claim it, while the curtain's captured dust must land somewhere — and the deposition coupling (curtain output as crust feedstock or lattice silt) is the general concern the first pass's surface-disjointness framing had concealed. What tipped the balance was routing every claim through one physical surface: once deck, lattice, crust and curtain output are read on the same square meter, the allocation cannot be presented as free coexistence, so the harmony sentence was narrowed and two owner decisions (Q5, Q6) named instead.
