7. **Does the 45-decibel zone cap count engineered masking? (owner call — one question, two answers)** Second-pass sharpening of the collision section's closing knot: [Active Bioacoustic Scape: wildlife monitoring and masking with flowing water](sound-environment.md#active-bioacoustic-scape-wildlife-monitoring-and-masking-with-flowing-water)'s "noise kept under 45 decibels" and [Hydraulic Pink-Noise Sound Masking for speech privacy in the transparent ground floor](sound-environment.md#hydraulic-pink-noise-sound-masking-for-speech-privacy-in-the-transparent-ground-floor)'s "40 to 45 decibels" collide only under one reading of the ceiling. Answer one: the cap is a **total ambient budget** — every source, designed masking included — and the water curtain is effectively barred from occupied zones, since it would spend the zone's whole allowance. Answer two: the cap is a **nuisance budget** — engineered, intended sound sits outside what the sensors police — and the curtain runs at full level, but the bioacoustic monitoring that makes the hierarchy enforceable is then blind to the loudest intentional sound in its own zone. Evidence for reading one: the monitoring metric is stated as a background sound level, which physically cannot distinguish source from mask; evidence for reading two: the masking record's own level is specified as "matched to ear ergonomics," i.e., designed as part of the norm, not against it. *(Why: the choice decides whether the source-first hierarchy is auditable or decorative, and whether masking's "40 to 45" is a feature or a violation. Blocking: yes — Questions 2 and 3 cannot be judged before it, because both weigh masking against the zone norm.)*
