# Directed Energy Transfer

## Abstract
This document stakes one rule: pumped heat has no right to be dumped, only a right to step down. Its requirement — "Where should pumped energy actually go, and how are thermal sources and thermal uses paired?" — is answered from its own Explanation onward by the temperature cascade: every hotter stream may discharge only into a consumer whose demanded temperature is lower, and no pumping happens without a named destination. The six answers gathered here do not disagree about that thermodynamics; they disagree about governance. Four route the streams by engineering — the shared bus ([Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks](directed-energy-transfer.md#fifth-generation-district-heatingcooling-loop-5gdhc--thermal-bus-for-binding-chillers-to-heat-sinks)), the bakery sink ([Preheating bakeries and processing industries with heat-pump waste heat](directed-energy-transfer.md#preheating-bakeries-and-processing-industries-with-heat-pump-waste-heat)), the paired hubs ([Balancing the thermal capacity of the settlement's heat sinks (Thermal Sinks Capacity Matching); pairing condensers with a thermophilic digester](directed-energy-transfer.md#balancing-the-thermal-capacity-of-the-settlements-heat-sinks-thermal-sinks-capacity-matching-pairing-condensers-with-a-thermophilic-digester)), the exergy ledger ([Integrated exergy balancing across cold stores, datacenters and heat pumps](directed-energy-transfer.md#integrated-exergy-balancing-across-cold-stores-datacenters-and-heat-pumps)) — one routes them by price ([Local peer-to-peer thermal-energy market between 500-person blocks](directed-energy-transfer.md#local-peer-to-peer-thermal-energy-market-between-500-person-blocks)), and one ([Graywater heat recovery ahead of MBR entry](directed-energy-transfer.md#graywater-heat-recovery-ahead-of-mbr-entry)) sits upstream of the network entirely and shows how much heat is saved before the cascade is ever invoked. The rule's two counterpart-clauses — the register that verifies the declared bands the cascade reads from, and the accounting that prices heat lifted uphill — are named here as requirements the page states but does not itself carry: neither exists among the six records, and whether either becomes a requirement of its own is decided at the owner's threshold, not in these texts. Adjudication of these answers will be logged in this document's changelog.

## Introduction

### Motivation
The settlement's heat is already moving — for every 1 kW of comfort a facade buys, roughly 1.35 kW is fired into the alley beside it — and the only thing missing is entitlement: who may send what, to whom, at what temperature, and who checks. These six answers must be weighed in one place precisely because they look like six technologies and are in fact six proposals for the same allocation: keep them scattered, and an objection to the block market ([Local peer-to-peer thermal-energy market between 500-person blocks](directed-energy-transfer.md#local-peer-to-peer-thermal-energy-market-between-500-person-blocks)) is logged as an objection to the thermal bus ([Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks](directed-energy-transfer.md#fifth-generation-district-heatingcooling-loop-5gdhc--thermal-bus-for-binding-chillers-to-heat-sinks)), while the cascade both silently presuppose never comes to a vote. Together, the actual dispute is visible — engineering matching versus price matching, and one standing exception (cooling) that none of them has yet argued.

## Explanation

### The problem the answers share
Our society carries thermal energy in several intermediary fluids held at graded temperature bands — water, steam, air, soil, refrigerants — and every one of them is pumped somewhere. The question all the recorded answers share is where each stream is entitled to go.

The fundamental rule they all serve — the heart of this document — is the temperature cascade: **every hotter stream may discharge only into a consumer whose demanded temperature is lower.** No heat is rejected without its temperature level stepping down, and no pumping happens without a named destination. The lowest rung of every cascade still owes an answer: a plan to step the residual heat down to a far lower temperature — handing the low-exergy remainder to deep sinks, the ground-exchange and radiative-cooling systems.

To make a rule of this kind enforceable, the dependency between this document and every system document must run one way. This network-level document names no consumers; each system document — cooking, wastewater treatment, water supply — declares its own temperature band explicitly toward the network. The worked examples of a cascade appear here only as declarations recorded in the systems' own documents, never as dependencies of this one: cooking demands the 50 to 150 degree band and, at raised pressure, applies a lower working temperature; the wastewater-treatment (MBR) reactors want 50 to 60 degrees; and at the foot of each ladder sits the residual low-exergy handoff to ground-exchange / radiative cooling named above. The systems declare their needs to the network — never the reverse.

Sharpened, the shared problem has three edges that the six records leave open, and every answer settles at most two of them. (1) **Verification.** Each declaration — cooking 50 to 150, MBR 50 to 60, proofing 35 to 40, digester 55 constant — is a claim the network is asked to read as a promise, yet no record names the verifier or the destination of a broken promise; a cascade that cannot audit a band is etiquette, not a rule, and a reactor that declares 50 to 60 and is fed 40 violates nothing in the cascade's letter while its biology quietly fails. (2) **Direction.** The rule as written governs only falling heat, but the settlement's dominant summer load is heat pumped upward; until the document says what work-purchased lift costs the network, the heat pumps stand in the awkward position of serving a rule that does not describe them. (3) **Quantity beside quality.** A declared band is a temperature, not a capacity; [Balancing the thermal capacity of the settlement's heat sinks (Thermal Sinks Capacity Matching); pairing condensers with a thermophilic digester](directed-energy-transfer.md#balancing-the-thermal-capacity-of-the-settlements-heat-sinks-thermal-sinks-capacity-matching-pairing-condensers-with-a-thermophilic-digester) exists precisely because matching bands without matching capacities produced a shortfall (40 to 60 kWh of condenser heat per dwelling per day against 3 to 4 kWh of water-heater absorption), so the registry the rule needs must carry both fields, not the one field the records declare.

### Approaches

#### Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks
**Mechanism:** In warm seasons, chillers and split units fling roughly 1.35 kW of heat directly into the alleys for every 1 kW of cooling they deliver indoors, turning the settlement into an oven. In this system the air-cooled condenser is eliminated altogether: every building joins one closed bidirectional hydraulic loop — a Thermal Bus running its fluid between 40 and 60 degrees — which connects the buildings to one another and to the metropolitan-scale heat sinks. The recovered heat, instead of suffocating the streets, is directed as a free energy input into the treatment-plant reactors, the bakeries, the workshops, and the hot-water systems.
**Metrics:** Fluid temperature band in the summer thermal loop: 40 °C to 60 °C; complete elimination of anthropogenic heat injection into the air of residential neighborhoods; street air temperature at night reduced by up to 5 degrees Celsius.
**Prerequisites:** Buried twin pipe with polyurethane-insulated polyethylene jacketing; variable-speed, constant-pressure pumps; chillers fitted with recoverable water-cooled condensers.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Tens of thousands of split-condenser units bolted onto building facades, firing their heat at the sidewalks and building a severe heat trap over the streets.
**Tags:** district thermal network, 5GDHC, thermal bus, condenser heat recovery, heat sinks
**Scale:** Plain-wide (metropolitan) and inter-building
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional (a transition-era tool)] | Trenching traditional streets for the decentralized twin-pipe water runs paralyzes the settlement and collides with the gas and sewer lines. In the existing fabric this idea is workable exclusively at the scale of enclosed townships and of reverse renewal; in a new settlement it is a fundamental artery of the depths.
**Cognitive anchor:** An engineered re-running of "the historic *mādi* — the small diversion channels of the Zayandeh River running through the alleys of Isfahan" — a hidden network that absorbs the shops' wasted heat and turns it into domestic hot water for the homes.
**Narrative hook:** "Your alley once had a mādi that knew exactly where the water was going — now your building has one that knows where the heat goes."

#### Preheating bakeries and processing industries with heat-pump waste heat
**Mechanism:** Bakeries are continuous consumers of thermal energy at low and medium temperatures: the dough-mixing water needs a mild temperature, and the resting and proofing chambers (Proofing Chambers) demand a stable 35 to 40 degrees at a set humidity. The bakeries connect to the settlement's low-temperature heating network. Heat captured while cooling residential or commercial spaces is fed directly into these proofing chambers and into the bakeries' water preheating, so that the bakery burners' gas use shrinks to the one high thermal level that genuinely needs it: final baking.
**Metrics:** Metropolitan sample count of bakeries: over 2,500 units; proofing/fermentation temperature band: 35 °C to 40 °C; a 30 to 40 percent cut in bakery-burner fuel consumption through feedwater preheating.
**Prerequisites:** Standard indirect heat-exchanger installations on the bakeries' water heaters; thermostatic monitoring of the proofing chambers.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Burning high-pressure, high-value natural gas in atmospheric bakery burners merely to warm cold mains water — in winter and in summer alike.
**Tags:** sustainable bakery, proofing chamber, food industry, thermal efficiency, exergy sink
**Scale:** Neighborhood-middle and basic services
**Fit & substrate:** [Adaptable to existing fabric (Retrofit/Brownfield)] | In traditional bakeries with gas burners, injecting heat-pump heat without reworking the plumbing meets the resistance of the master bakers and outright inefficiency; adapting them requires an engineered rebuild of the baking lines.
**Cognitive anchor:** The nostalgic smell and taste of "the traditional sangak bakeries with their brick-fired tandoors" — the clever capture of the system's hidden heat to bake quality bread without wasting energy.
**Narrative hook:** "Tomorrow's sangak is proofed by last night's borrowed warmth — the bread only asks that the heat holds 35 to 40 degrees, not who paid the gas."

#### Graywater heat recovery ahead of MBR entry
**Mechanism:** The warm graywater from showers and washbasins is routed through plate heat exchangers before it reaches the MBR reactor; the valuable heat of this water is recovered ahead of disposal and used to preheat the building's domestic hot water.
**Metrics:** Recovery of more than 60 percent of the waste heat carried by domestic hot water; a raised overall energy-optimization coefficient for the building.
**Prerequisites:** Fouling-resistant steel heat exchangers; parallel heat-transfer piping.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Hot bath water draining straight into the sewer pit, its valuable thermal exergy thrown away.
**Tags:** wastewater heat recovery, sewer heat exchanger, water preheating, effluent exergy
**Scale:** Block-level MEP micro-scale
**Fit & substrate:** [Adaptable to existing fabric (Retrofit/Brownfield)] | The exchanger itself is a short plumbing run, so of all the moves in this document it is the one that can earn its keep building by building in the existing fabric — but the force-fit failure is precise: conventional stacks carry shower and basin water down the same pipe as the toilet, so a plate exchanger dropped into a shared drain is recovering heat from blackwater (fouling, hygiene, and the recovered exergy is worth less than the cleaning). The retrofit sequence is therefore two-stage — source-separate the graywater at the fixture or the branch, then fit the exchanger — which makes it wet-wall work for whole wet stacks, not a pipe patch. In greenfield it is one clause in the plumbing design: the parallel graywater line laid once, and every shower thereafter feeds both the hot-water preheat and, at its foot, the MBR's declared band with a warmer, cleaner influent.
**Cognitive anchor:** A heat hunter on warm water. The shared memory beneath it is the household's old arithmetic with warm water: the basin of bath water kept back for the courtyard, the last warm rinse carried to the plants — a generation that never called used warmth rubbish, only warmth that had already served once and owed the house one more task.
**Narrative hook:** The hot shower water gives its heat back to you before it ever reaches the treatment plant, so that none of the energy is wasted.
**Syn (synergies):** [Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks](directed-energy-transfer.md#fifth-generation-district-heatingcooling-loop-5gdhc--thermal-bus-for-binding-chillers-to-heat-sinks); [Decentralized effluent treatment by membrane bioreactor (Micro-MBR) at the 500-person building scale](../water-and-matter/treatment-and-return-of-used-waters.md#decentralized-effluent-treatment-by-membrane-bioreactor-micro-mbr-at-the-500-person-building-scale); [Dual-pipe plumbing: separating greywater from blackwater at the source (Dual-Pipe System)](../water-and-matter/treatment-and-return-of-used-waters.md#dual-pipe-plumbing-separating-greywater-from-blackwater-at-the-source-dual-pipe-system)

#### Balancing the thermal capacity of the settlement's heat sinks (Thermal Sinks Capacity Matching); pairing condensers with a thermophilic digester
**Mechanism:** Corrects a theoretical deviation in summer heat recovery: the buildings' cooling load across the day is far larger than the absorption capacity of household hot water — 40 to 60 kWh of condenser heat generated per dwelling unit per day against an absorption capacity of only 3 to 4 kWh for a water heater. In this model the enormous surplus of condenser reject heat is injected, via the thermal bus, into stable heavy-consuming hubs: the thermophilic digesters of large sludge-treatment plants (which demand a constant 55 degrees Celsius) and the plain's industrial preheating lines — definitively preventing heat from spilling into the enclosed street environment.
**Metrics:** Heat-balance matching rate: 100 percent of chiller reject heat absorbed, none discharged to the atmosphere; thermophilic digester working temperature: a constant 55 °C; the 90-percent absorption shortfall of household water heaters covered by metropolitan-scale hubs.
**Prerequisites:** Twin-pipe metropolitan heat main with polyurethane insulation; thermophilic anaerobic digestion reactors with heat-exchange jackets at the treatment plants.
**Conventional-model flaw:** The simplistic notion that household hot water can absorb all the reject heat of the gas coolers — in fact the water heater saturates within the first minutes and 90 percent of the heat keeps turning the settlement into an oven.
**Tags:** applied thermodynamics, thermal load balancing, temperature sinks, treatment-plant thermophilic digester, solving the summer heat trap
**Scale:** Metropolitan and plain-wide
**Fit & substrate:** [Dual — transitional] | The heavy sinks this rule pairs with — sludge digesters at the treatment plants, industrial preheat lines on the plain — already exist in the existing fabric; what does not exist is the main that carries a building's surplus to them. Force-fit therefore bites twice: trenching a metropolitan twin-pipe through saturated streets collides with the gas and sewer lines exactly as [Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks](directed-energy-transfer.md#fifth-generation-district-heatingcooling-loop-5gdhc--thermal-bus-for-binding-chillers-to-heat-sinks)'s own Fit warns, and the pairing rule cannot move a single kilowatt-hour until that pipe is under its hub. What is immediately adoptable in the existing fabric is the rule as paper — pair declared capacities, not just declared bands, and refuse any connection whose hub is not yet registered — which is siting discipline and costs nothing; what moves the heat is the bus, and that arrives only with enclosed townships, reverse-renewal districts, or new ground.
**Cognitive anchor:** The mechanism — surplus given, by a schedule everyone can read, to the steady sink declared to take it — is remembered in the qanat's *sharīʿa*: the old water-division of Iranian settlements, each field's turn fixed by hour and by night so that no field drank before the one above it had taken its stated share, and the largest meadow was given the flow it could actually hold. [Balancing the thermal capacity of the settlement's heat sinks (Thermal Sinks Capacity Matching); pairing condensers with a thermophilic digester](directed-energy-transfer.md#balancing-the-thermal-capacity-of-the-settlements-heat-sinks-thermal-sinks-capacity-matching-pairing-condensers-with-a-thermophilic-digester) is that order rebuilt in heat: the thermophilic digester is the field with the declared capacity, the condenser surplus is the season's flood, and the street the water-division once kept from quarrels is the street that stays cool.
**Narrative hook:** "Your cooler's surplus has nowhere to go and every water-heater at home is full — the qanat's oldest rule still works: the big sink takes its declared turn first."

#### Integrated exergy balancing across cold stores, datacenters and heat pumps
**Mechanism:** Puts the second law of thermodynamics to work to zero out the losses of energy quality (exergy): the reject heat of chillers and datacenters is lifted directly, by heat pumps, to the temperature needed for domestic-water preheating, the bakeries, and the heat-storage pools.
**Metrics:** The energy system's overall efficiency coefficient (COP) raised above 4.5; thermal losses cut to below 5 percent.
**Prerequisites:** Closed hydraulic heat-exchange circuits of the fifth-generation urban network (5GDHC).
**Conventional-model flaw:** Valuable heat of industrial equipment thrown away to the open air by gas-cooler fans, while fresh gas is burned to make hot water.
**Tags:** exergy balance, thermal losses, temperature recovery, hybrid heat pumps, thermodynamic efficiency
**Scale:** Installation micro-scale and neighborhood macro-scale
**Fit & substrate:** [Adaptable to existing fabric (Retrofit/Brownfield)] | The lift is a machinery-room device, not a civil work: an existing cold store or data hall can bolt a heat pump to its own rejection and feed a nearby hot-water load with short runs, touching no street at all — this is the only summer-recovery move here that begins without the bus. The fragile assumption is the promise itself: a COP above 4.5 is bought only at a short lift — a warm source met by a cool sink, not merely low temperatures — so force-fitting the scheme onto an existing building's 60-degree radiator returns or a hot tap-water demand lifts the heat twice and the compressor eats the gain; without the low-temperature discipline of the 5GDHC circuit named in its prerequisites, the exergy arithmetic degrades to the gas-boiler waste it indicts.
**Cognitive anchor:** A pleasant, intelligent coolness. In Iran the word for that coolness is the *bādgīr*: the windcatcher that cooled Yazd's rooms because it was placed, not powered — and [Integrated exergy balancing across cold stores, datacenters and heat pumps](directed-energy-transfer.md#integrated-exergy-balancing-across-cold-stores-datacenters-and-heat-pumps) is the windcatcher's learned inversion, a coolness that stays intelligent only because someone has already planned, band by band, exactly where the expelled heat will be re-used.
**Narrative hook:** The computers and servers are burning up the air; we collect their heat to supply your bath water and your fresh bread!
**Syn (synergies):** [Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks](directed-energy-transfer.md#fifth-generation-district-heatingcooling-loop-5gdhc--thermal-bus-for-binding-chillers-to-heat-sinks) — 5GDHC thermal loop; [100% recovery of megadatacenter thermal exergy (the Middle East datacenter)](../information-systems/data-storage-and-processing.md#100-recovery-of-megadatacenter-thermal-exergy-the-middle-east-datacenter) — datacenter exergy; [Cancelling retail packaging: food and dairy flow from cold store to meal in large reusable steel vessels](../food/meals-and-shared-cooking.md#cancelling-retail-packaging-food-and-dairy-flow-from-cold-store-to-meal-in-large-reusable-steel-vessels) — cooking hub

#### Local peer-to-peer thermal-energy market between 500-person blocks
**Mechanism:** Connects the fifth-generation thermal-loop (5GDHC) network of the 500-person blocks to a peer-to-peer (P2P) exchange platform: when a block runs a heat surplus — heavy datacenter or industrial-kitchen activity, say — the surplus is routed automatically to the neighboring residential block that needs hot water, and a society value token is exchanged between the two.
**Metrics:** The whole village's energy-recovery factor raised above 95 percent; a standing economic incentive for block managers to optimize consumption.
**Prerequisites:** Smart heat meters; an automatic local-blockchain settlement protocol.
**Conventional-model flaw:** Each building paying to expel its surplus heat into the outdoor air with a gas cooler, while its neighbor burns energy for heating.
**Tags:** energy market, peer-to-peer (P2P), thermal loop, exergy balance, neighborhood energy economy
**Scale:** Village-wide macro and inter-block
**Fit & substrate:** [Greenfield only] | A market can only trade what it can meter, and the existing fabric cannot meter most of its heat: shared risers serve whole buildings, individual gas appliances own their waste privately, tenants hold no title to sell, and the 500-person block is itself a planning grain the conventional parcel map does not contain. Force-fit yields a ledger trading kilowatt-hours no pipe ever carried — quota fraud in thermodynamic dress, the same corruption [Mandatory minimum open- and green-space quota (at least 25 square meters per permanent resident)](../form-and-land/buildable-share-and-density-allocation.md#mandatory-minimum-open--and-green-space-quota-at-least-25-square-meters-per-permanent-resident)'s green-space market was predicted to breed — while every real kilowatt-hour still leaves through the old gas cooler. Where the 5GDHC loop and the smart meters are laid with the foundations, the market is the cheapest possible dispatcher: it needs no new physics, only the bus it sits on.
**Cognitive anchor:** The modern energy-exchange market floor. The Iranian memory that makes the abstraction neighborly is the *ḥezb* — the neighborhood's rotating credit circle, in which whoever held surplus paid into the common turn and whoever faced need was paid out, the whole exchange kept honest by a ledger every household could read. [Local peer-to-peer thermal-energy market between 500-person blocks](directed-energy-transfer.md#local-peer-to-peer-thermal-energy-market-between-500-person-blocks)'s value token is that same trust made automatic: heat given in a month of surplus, heat owed in a month of need.
**Narrative hook:** The surplus heat of your tower's kitchen is sold to the next block and earns you an income token; the neighborhood's energy economy is alive!

### How the approaches collide and compose
**The cascade presupposes a registry none of the six provides.** Every answer reads temperatures to do its work — the bus at 40 to 60, the proofing chambers at 35 to 40, the digester at a constant 55, the reactors at 50 to 60 — and the document's own one-way dependency ("the systems declare their needs to the network — never the reverse") leaves those declarations unverified by design. Self-declared bands fail quietly: a reactor that declares 50 to 60 and is fed 40 obeys the cascade — the source is still hotter than the demand it fails — while its biology goes mesophilic and the sludge contract breaks. A violation of this kind is invisible to the rule and visible only to flow-temperature logging at each handoff, so the registry the cascade needs must carry, for every consumer, the declared band, the metered inlet, and the destination of a breach. None of the six records names who keeps it or where violations go; adjudicating any of the six without that institution ratifies a rule that cannot be enforced. And the registry is itself a new requirement, not a clause of this one: its own axes — who operates it, the interval of handoff metering, the destination of a breach — are shared with none of the six thermal allocations, and the same audit of promises would serve water or matter as readily as heat. The one-way dependency doctrine survives its creation unscathed: the registry stores what the system documents declare toward the network, naming no consumers of its own — but that leaves the cascade standing at the threshold of a second requirement filed nowhere, and where the filing belongs is an owner's decision, opened in this document's handoff.

**Cooling is heat pumped uphill, and the cascade only describes the downhill.** The rule's sentence — every hotter stream may discharge only into a consumer whose demanded temperature is lower — is a law about falling heat, but the settlement's summer load is rising heat: a chiller is a cascade that runs backward, paying work to lift captured warmth to a usable band. [Integrated exergy balancing across cold stores, datacenters and heat pumps](directed-energy-transfer.md#integrated-exergy-balancing-across-cold-stores-datacenters-and-heat-pumps)'s hybrid heat pumps are the counter-direction openly; [Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks](directed-energy-transfer.md#fifth-generation-district-heatingcooling-loop-5gdhc--thermal-bus-for-binding-chillers-to-heat-sinks)'s recovered condensers are the same lift taken for granted, since the 40-to-60 bus is hotter than the rooms being cooled; and [Graywater heat recovery ahead of MBR entry](directed-energy-transfer.md#graywater-heat-recovery-ahead-of-mbr-entry) marks the boundary from the other side — its plate exchanger needs no pump precisely because shower water at 35 to 40 degrees can preheat a colder mains supply passively, which is exactly why it recovers ahead of the MBR entry instead of robbing the reactors' declared 50 to 60 band. The composition is a rule the records gesture at but never state: take every passive step down first, lift only the residual, and meter the work so the ledger can tell a cascade from a pump with a receipt. But two tests break that candidate composition open, and both belong to the owner. **The first is the cascade's own letter turned on refrigeration.** The rule says "no pumping happens without a named destination," and a bare air-cooled chiller is exactly that: pumping with no named destination, its whole purpose to dump uphill into the sky. Read strictly, then, the cascade does not merely tolerate the thermal bus as one good option among six — it *forbids* the stand-alone split unit, making the recovered-condenser bus the only lawful cooling there is. That is a far stronger claim than the abstract's careful neutrality admits, and it decides, before any market or pairing is weighed, whether the six answers are rivals or whether five of them are simply compliance devices for one rule that already chose. **The second is the exergy the receipt is supposed to count.** The cascade is a law about falling temperature; a heat pump manufactures falling temperature with purchased work, and at a COP above 4.5 the work is a fifth of the heat moved. So the exergy ledger cannot read a lifted stream as a falling one: the same 50-degree water delivered passively down a cascade and delivered by a 4.5-COP lift carries wildly different entitlement, because the lifted one spent high-grade electricity to arrive. Metered work is therefore not one more column beside the declared band and capacity — it is the *price* the cascade must net against before it can call recovered heat free, and the "take the passive step down first, lift only the residual" rule is a strict economic ranking only if the meter can tell a step from a lift. Neither test is argued in any of the six records; both stand open in this document's handoff.

**[Balancing the thermal capacity of the settlement's heat sinks (Thermal Sinks Capacity Matching); pairing condensers with a thermophilic digester](directed-energy-transfer.md#balancing-the-thermal-capacity-of-the-settlements-heat-sinks-thermal-sinks-capacity-matching-pairing-condensers-with-a-thermophilic-digester) and [Local peer-to-peer thermal-energy market between 500-person blocks](directed-energy-transfer.md#local-peer-to-peer-thermal-energy-market-between-500-person-blocks) are rival governments of one allocation.** Both answer the same question — who receives the surplus condenser heat of a summer afternoon — and they disagree about the arbiter. [Balancing the thermal capacity of the settlement's heat sinks (Thermal Sinks Capacity Matching); pairing condensers with a thermophilic digester](directed-energy-transfer.md#balancing-the-thermal-capacity-of-the-settlements-heat-sinks-thermal-sinks-capacity-matching-pairing-condensers-with-a-thermophilic-digester)'s argument is arithmetic: the 40 to 60 kWh per dwelling-day against 3 to 4 kWh of water-heater absorption mismatch cannot be negotiated, so the hub with the declared, constant, thermophilic appetite takes by right of capacity. [Local peer-to-peer thermal-energy market between 500-person blocks](directed-energy-transfer.md#local-peer-to-peer-thermal-energy-market-between-500-person-blocks)'s argument is a price: no central pairer knows what a block will pay for, and the token finds matches a registry misses. They compose only in priority: hubs hold the base claim, the market trades what hubs leave. They collide the moment a block sells hub-bound surplus to a nearer bidder, because then [Balancing the thermal capacity of the settlement's heat sinks (Thermal Sinks Capacity Matching); pairing condensers with a thermophilic digester](directed-energy-transfer.md#balancing-the-thermal-capacity-of-the-settlements-heat-sinks-thermal-sinks-capacity-matching-pairing-condensers-with-a-thermophilic-digester)'s metric — 100 percent of chiller reject heat absorbed, none discharged to the atmosphere — is no longer guaranteed by engineering but by whether the price cleared, and [Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks](directed-energy-transfer.md#fifth-generation-district-heatingcooling-loop-5gdhc--thermal-bus-for-binding-chillers-to-heat-sinks)'s promise of complete elimination of anthropogenic heat injection inherits the same contingency. Either the registry reserves declared hub capacity off-market, or the absorption figures must be restated as expected, not guaranteed; that choice belongs to the owner, not to the texts.

**As a ladder, the six fit in order — and the order is the enforceable content.** Graywater recovery at the fixture ([Graywater heat recovery ahead of MBR entry](directed-energy-transfer.md#graywater-heat-recovery-ahead-of-mbr-entry)) takes heat before anyone must pump it; bakery proofing ([Preheating bakeries and processing industries with heat-pump waste heat](directed-energy-transfer.md#preheating-bakeries-and-processing-industries-with-heat-pump-waste-heat)) is a neighborhood-scale sink that drinks passively only at the bus's cool return end, since 35 to 40 sits below 40 to 60 — a band-fit the records pair but never reconcile; the bus ([Fifth-generation district heating/cooling loop (5GDHC / Thermal Bus) for binding chillers to heat sinks](directed-energy-transfer.md#fifth-generation-district-heatingcooling-loop-5gdhc--thermal-bus-for-binding-chillers-to-heat-sinks)) carries what remains to the plain; the hubs ([Balancing the thermal capacity of the settlement's heat sinks (Thermal Sinks Capacity Matching); pairing condensers with a thermophilic digester](directed-energy-transfer.md#balancing-the-thermal-capacity-of-the-settlements-heat-sinks-thermal-sinks-capacity-matching-pairing-condensers-with-a-thermophilic-digester)) give the bus a floor under its summer absorption; the exergy balance ([Integrated exergy balancing across cold stores, datacenters and heat pumps](directed-energy-transfer.md#integrated-exergy-balancing-across-cold-stores-datacenters-and-heat-pumps)) prices the lifts the ladder could not avoid; and the market ([Local peer-to-peer thermal-energy market between 500-person blocks](directed-energy-transfer.md#local-peer-to-peer-thermal-energy-market-between-500-person-blocks)) dispatches the residual among metered parties. Read this way the approaches stop being alternatives: each rung is legal only if the rungs below it have taken their declared share first — which is what the cascade, with a registry attached, would actually say.
