## Methodology
Three passes, in this order. First, the previous session's cluster-level manifest was re-verified row by row against the current documents rather than trusted: every path in its target column was stale after the protocol set was reorganized into folders, and several of its citations pointed at passages about a different subject, so each row was re-sourced from the current text. Second, each of the three extracts was read end to end and inventoried claim by claim in the author's own words, without the manifest's clusters in view, so that a claim the earlier clustering had missed would surface on its own. Third, every claim that survived the first two passes was matched against the current documents and, where it did not match, against the handoff that would own it.

The rule the third pass applied, in the order the steps are taken:
1. A claim a governing document already carries, in the documents' own vocabulary, gets no new text. A second statement of it is a copy that will drift.
2. A claim that is correct but that no document has decided does not become body text. It is recorded as an open question in the handoff of the artifact that owns the subject, carrying the owner's reasoning with it, because a handoff is where an undecided question lives and because writing an unratified claim into a governing document would make a conversation look like a decision.
3. A claim that is neither carried nor undecided — a position about one ecosystem, a forwarded argument, personal material, or a claim the documents already reject — is recorded here with the reason it was not carried, and nowhere else.

One limit governs every attribution below. The extracts are Omid-*targeted*, not Omid-only: the extractor emits any block whose inherited sender name matches, a block without a sender name inherits the previous one, dates could not be recovered, and a forwarded quotation inside a block is evidence of transmission, not authorship. Nothing in this record is therefore attributed more strongly than "stated by the owner in those groups", and a claim visible in the sources as someone else's is not treated as his.

## Findings
### The rule this inquiry applied
The three rules above are the whole method. Their consequence is worth stating plainly, because it is the record's main content: **of the twenty-six architectural claim clusters the earlier manifest identified, twelve were already carried by the documents in the documents' own words, one was carried except for a decision that an existing handoff already held, and eleven were absent or carried only in part.** Only those eleven needed to be written anywhere, and what they needed was a home, not a rule. The cluster identifiers below (`A-01` … `A-26`) are this record's labels, inherited from the deleted manifest so that a reader of an earlier session's notes can still match them.

### Claim clusters the documents already carry
Each is stated by the documents at the linked section; none received new text.
- **Model before storage, API, and structure** (`A-01`) — [Modeling → The Model as the Primary Artifact](./modeling.md#the-model-as-the-primary-artifact) and [Modeling → Modeling Produces Conceptual Abstractions, Not Implementation Structures](./modeling.md#modeling-produces-conceptual-abstractions-not-implementation-structures), with [System](./system.md)'s statement that implementations are projections of the model.
- **A Module is a conceptual boundary, not a deployment unit** (`A-02`) — [Modularity → Module](./modularity.md) states that a Module is independent of the mechanism used to deploy it and that deployment is a representation concern.
- **Independent identities for routing, service, operation, and request** (`A-03`) — [sRPC → Independent identities](./protocols/net/sRPC.md#independent-identities) states it as a positive contract, and the service declaration's open half is already an open question in [SDK Handoff → What the declaration artifact is](./protocols/modules/sdk.handoff.md#what-the-declaration-artifact-is).
- **Acquired facts, derived views, and caches differ in authority** (`A-04`) — [Modeling → Acquired Data vs. Discovered Data](./modeling.md#acquired-data-vs-discovered-data), the shortcut-edge rule, and [Memory → The memory/storage dichotomy rejected](./protocols/memory/memory.md#the-memorystorage-dichotomy-rejected)'s shadow-tier rule; when a discovered value may be persisted is already [Modeling Handoff](./modeling.handoff.md)'s question.
- **Error, Log, and Bug as three entities** (`A-08`, partly) — [Error → Boundary discipline](./protocols/process/error.md#boundary-discipline) draws the Error/Log line and names the Bug as the third entity. The panic half of this cluster was already recovered into [Control Flow Handoff](./protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md) on 2026-09-17.
- **Operation semantics, not a universal context object** (`A-09`) — [Process → Data carriage between layers](./process.md#data-carriage-between-layers) already refuses context as a cross-layer data carrier, which is also the position the sources take; the plan's `OperationContext` proposal contradicts the document and was not carried.
- **Channels and buffers are different things, and execution units are pooled rather than spawned per request** — [Concurrency → Channels are signals, not stores](./protocols/computer/concurrency.md#channels-are-signals-not-stores) and [Concurrency → Execution units are pooled, not spawned](./protocols/computer/concurrency.md#execution-units-are-pooled-not-spawned) both state the position the sources argue for, each with the ecosystem evidence attached.
- **Ownership, copy, and lifetime are contract members; allocation claims are measurement claims** (`A-21`, `A-22`) — [Memory → Copy and ownership semantics are contract members](./protocols/memory/memory.md#copy-and-ownership-semantics-are-contract-members) and [Memory → Allocation claims are measurement claims](./protocols/memory/memory.md#allocation-claims-are-measurement-claims). The rule is carried; no benchmark number from the sources was carried with it, which is the correct outcome — the documents record the discipline, not one ecosystem's measurements.
- **The GUI does not speak the network; one routing authority; validation generated once** (`A-14`, `A-15` in part) — [GUI → Position on GUI Architecture](./protocols/gui/gui.md), whose changelog already attributes the claims to the owner.
- **Logical routing and link-layer carriage are different owners** (`A-18`) — [GP → Routing architecture](./protocols/net/giti.md#routing-architecture) and [Chapar → Scope and terminology](./protocols/net/chapar.md).
- **HTTP is a guest surface, and its URL is not the routing model** (`A-19`) — [HTTP → Memar's Stance on the HTTP Protocol Surface](./protocols/net/http.md#memars-stance-on-the-http-protocol-surface) and [HTTP → Four Identities Collapsed into One String](./protocols/net/http.md#four-identities-collapsed-into-one-string) state both the four independent identities and the guest status.
- **Dependency and version management is the repository's own mechanism** — [Dependency and Version Management](./protocols/modules/dependency-management.md) already carries the submodules, relative-path, fork, and optional-semver positions the sources argue for, including the counter-arguments.
- **Encapsulation, closure-as-implicit-capsule, and the danger of discarding concepts wrapped in rejected names** (`A-23`) — [Khayyam → Sovereign Encapsulation](./khayyam/encapsulation.md), [Khayyam → Closures as Implicit Capsule Syntax](./khayyam/encapsulation.md), and [Type → Concepts Outlive Their Labels](./type.md#concepts-outlive-their-labels).
- **Communication channels produce knowledge that must be extracted and preserved, and are not repositories** (`A-25`) — [Knowledge → Communication Channels Produce Knowledge but Are Not Knowledge Repositories](./knowledge.md). This inquiry is that principle being carried out, and the record it produced is its output.

### Claim clusters the documents did not carry
Eleven clusters were absent or carried only in part. Each is now an open question in the handoff of the artifact that owns the subject, stated with the owner's reasoning in the artifact's own vocabulary; the links are to the entries written.
- **Storage capabilities as independent, separately adoptable axes** (`A-05`) — [Memory Handoff → Should storage capabilities be named as independent capabilities, against this document's rejection of named kinds?](./protocols/memory/memory.handoff.md#should-storage-capabilities-be-named-as-independent-capabilities-against-this-documents-rejection-of-named-kinds). The claim is in tension with the Memory document's designed refusal to reify the field into named kinds, which is why the entry is a question against that document rather than a rule beside it.
- **The retention and erasure rule beside history preservation, and where a record's versions live** (`A-06`) — [Memory Handoff → What is the retention and erasure rule for preserved history, and where do a record's versions live?](./protocols/memory/memory.handoff.md#what-is-the-retention-and-erasure-rule-for-preserved-history-and-where-do-a-records-versions-live). The sources hold "never update or delete" together with secret erasure, legal purge, and bounded retention, and argue both ways about node co-location inside one message; nothing in the documents held any of it.
- **An event taxonomy** (`A-07`) — [Process Handoff → Events](./process.handoff.md#events). The documents carry Event, the modeling of state change as events, and the log event; what they do not name is that a durable fact, a contract message between components, and a runtime signal are three different things sharing one word in ordinary use.
- **Where tracing belongs, and whether a Trace concern exists beside Error and Log** (`A-08`) — [Error Handoff → Does Memar need a Trace concern beside Error and Log?](./protocols/process/error.handoff.md#does-memar-need-a-trace-concern-beside-error-and-log). The owner's own position places tracing with logging, so what is undecided is only whether the framework names it.
- **The composition root, and the line against a service locator** (`A-10`) — [Runtime Handoff → Where do shared mechanisms come from — a composition root, or a lookup?](./protocols/computer/runtime.handoff.md#where-do-shared-mechanisms-come-from--a-composition-root-or-a-lookup).
- **Lifecycle per resource family, and whether teardown is idempotent** (`A-11`) — [Memory Handoff → Must teardown be idempotent, and which lifecycle names belong to which resource family?](./protocols/memory/memory.handoff.md#must-teardown-be-idempotent-and-which-lifecycle-names-belong-to-which-resource-family), which the existing "must every `Service` carry `Deinit`?" entry depends on and now cross-references.
- **One identity or several representations of it** (`A-12`) — [Media Type Handoff → One identity or several representations of it?](./protocols/identifier/media-type.handoff.md#one-identity-or-several-representations-of-it). This artifact and the GUI's had no handoff at all, so the question had no legal home; both were created.
- **The protocol text for GUI page state and history** (`A-15`) — [GUI Handoff → Page state and history have no protocol text yet](./protocols/gui/gui.handoff.md#page-state-and-history-have-no-protocol-text-yet). The stance is stated and attributed in the protocol's own changelog; what is missing is the text under it — what a page's state is, and what a URL is in a model where state is the protocol's own.
- **Authentication, session, and authorization as three protocols** (`A-16`) — [Framework Handoff → Does Memar need authentication, session, and authorization protocols?](./framework.handoff.md#does-memar-need-authentication-session-and-authorization-protocols). The largest structural absence found: the separation appears in the corpus only as an example of bundled protocols, and no document, handoff, or vocabulary owns the subject.
- **Revocation and integrity of a delegated credential** (`A-17`) — [SDK Handoff → Revocation and integrity of a delegated credential](./protocols/modules/sdk.handoff.md#revocation-and-integrity-of-a-delegated-credential), beside the agreement-artifact question the same handoff already carried.
- **What must be demonstrated before a protocol is exposed, and where admission control lives** (`A-20`) — [Networking Handoff → What must be demonstrated before a protocol is exposed on a network, and where does admission control live?](./protocols/net/networking.handoff.md#what-must-be-demonstrated-before-a-protocol-is-exposed-on-a-network-and-where-does-admission-control-live).

### Claims the sources contain that no earlier record listed
The end-to-end inventories surfaced material the cluster-level manifest had not separated out. One of these became an entry of its own; the rest are recorded here, because a claim with no artifact to own it is a claim that gets lost, and the point of this record is that nothing is lost silently.
- **A value whose validity is a window must not be measured against a correctable civil clock** — one-time codes, signed grants, session deadlines. Not in any document, and it is the first requirement the Time protocol's deliberately deferred monotonicity question has produced: [Time Handoff → What protects a time-limited value from a clock that moves?](./protocols/time/time.handoff.md#what-protects-a-time-limited-value-from-a-clock-that-moves) now carries it.
- **The GUI's half of the shared validation shape** — the GUI protocol states that validation is generated once for both server and client, and the SDK handoff holds the question of how; the GUI side of that one question had no home, so [GUI Handoff → The GUI's half of the shared validation shape](./protocols/gui/gui.handoff.md#the-guis-half-of-the-shared-validation-shape) carries it and points at the SDK's entry instead of restating it, so the two cannot become different mechanisms.
- **The framework is a product and is managed as one** — carrying product-management practice over the framework itself. Related to, but broader than, [Framework Handoff → Formalizing framework goals as a named section](./framework.handoff.md#formalizing-framework-goals-as-a-named-section); not merged into that entry, because merging would have left the larger claim invisible inside a narrower question.
- **Everything is possible in software; only development cost, maintenance cost, and per-request resource cost differ** — the criterion the owner uses when arguing about tools, and the reasoning behind [Framework Handoff → Mental model of the substrate before tool selection](./framework.handoff.md#mental-model-of-the-substrate-before-tool-selection) without being stated there. It is a criterion for arguing rather than a doctrine, so no entry was written; it belongs in that entry's next revision if the owner wants it stated.
- **A dependency is trusted whole or not at all, the alternative being reimplementation** — no home. It belongs beside [Dependency and Version Management](./protocols/modules/dependency-management.md)'s treatment of what a consumer may do with a dependency it does not own, which is a body-text claim and therefore an owner decision.
- **A service whose many inputs each produce an error signals a broken architecture** — an owner position on service design with no protocol home. It belongs wherever service declaration settles, which is the same session as [SDK Handoff → What the declaration artifact is](./protocols/modules/sdk.handoff.md#what-the-declaration-artifact-is).
- **A panic at any line is a defect in reasoning, not a style choice** — adjacent to the panic question [Control Flow Handoff](./protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md) already carries, and stronger than it. Not merged, because merging would have quietly strengthened a question its owner framed as still to be evaluated.

### Clusters deliberately not carried
- **Measurements and observations about one ecosystem** — goroutine and buffer costs, timer accuracy, escape-analysis weakness, allocator-count folklore, a self-reported halving of binary size, an unrigorous "userspace TCP cannot improve throughput" result. The Memory protocol's measurement discipline already governs claims of this kind; a number from one ecosystem's runtime, with no corpus, version, or platform attached, is not an architectural fact and would become one by being written down.
- **A goroutine pool described as wasteful, against the settled worker-pool default** — [Concurrency → Execution units are pooled, not spawned](./protocols/computer/concurrency.md#execution-units-are-pooled-not-spawned) states the pool as the framework's default, and the objection in the sources is scoped to a language whose per-unit cost is unusually low. Re-opening a settled position needs new evidence, not a restatement.
- **The fixed-width character-encoding argument, the server-rendered-versus-client-rendered document question, and the forwarded data-governance material** (`A-24`) — the encoding question already has an owner and an open list ([Characters Codec → Still considering](./protocols/codec/characters-codec.md)), where a fixed-width proposal belongs if it is ever adopted; the rendering question has no selected requirement to attach to; the governance material is visibly forwarded from a named other author, which under this record's attribution limit is evidence of transmission only.
- **The build, security, and working-tree snapshot of the archived `memar-go` repository** (`A-26`) — every path in it belongs to a different repository, whose current state this checkout cannot confirm. Its one durable residue is already carried correctly: [Networking Handoff → The userspace transport realization's scope](./protocols/net/networking.handoff.md#the-userspace-transport-realizations-scope) cites the archived goals as unratified by any document here.
- **Personal, epistemological, and life-advice material, and paradigm comparisons treated as open questions** (`A-23`, `A-25`) — carried by their governing principles, not as claims.

## Conclusion
Nothing in this research changed a governing document, and that is the finding rather than an omission: the corpus turned out to be the documents' own thinking, already written down, in most cases years earlier and in some cases by earlier audits of these same groups. What the corpus held that the documents did not were thirteen open questions, and those now sit in thirteen handoff entries across ten artifacts, each stating the owner's position and its reasoning in the vocabulary of the artifact that owns it, marked with the date and provenance of the audit. Two handoff files were created for artifacts whose contested vocabulary had no home at all — the GUI and Media Type protocols.

With that, the deletion condition was met: every source-backed architectural claim is either carried by a governing document, or recorded as a self-contained open question with its rationale, or explicitly not carried with the reason stated. The raw exports, the preservation plan, and the cluster-level manifest were then deleted, so no claim now depends on a line reference into a file that no longer exists.

What remains is the owner's: thirteen open questions, of which three are large enough to shape the framework — whether authentication, session, and authorization are protocols; whether storage capabilities are named; and what declares a service — and the rest are bounded. One dependency was found and left recorded rather than silently dropped: [SDK Handoff → Cache-strategy variables and invalidation signals](./protocols/modules/sdk.handoff.md#cache-strategy-variables-and-invalidation-signals) pointed at diagrams in the chat exports as the source for its missing content; the exports contain no images, so the strategy variables must come from the owner.
