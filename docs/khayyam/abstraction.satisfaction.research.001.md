## Methodology
A single reading pass over the two governing documents and the passages that bear on them, on 2026-10-09, prompted by the open question recorded at [Abstraction Handoff → Do declared Types and structural satisfaction both stand?](./abstraction.handoff.md#open-questions). The pass read [Abstraction in Khayyam](./abstraction.md) and [Type](../type.md) in full, and read the satisfaction-bearing passages of [Polymorphism in Khayyam](./polymorphism.md), [Inheritance in Khayyam](./inheritance.md), [Agency in Khayyam](./agency.md), [Method in Khayyam](./method.md), [Encapsulation in Khayyam](./encapsulation.md), [abstraction_p.Implements](../modules/computer/abstraction-implements.md), [Modularity](../modularity.md), [Protocol](../protocol.md), [Modeling](../modeling.md), [Compiler](../modules/computer/compiler.md), and the abstraction scaffolding rule (`bundles/khayyam/rules/abstraction-scaffolding/abstraction-scaffolding.md`). Regex passes over `docs/` for *satisfaction*, *satisfies*, *conform*, *nominal*, *impl*, *counterpart*, *boundary*, and *organization* established where the two subjects are said at all; every hit outside the files named above was re-read before use, and the hits that were only the same passage restated elsewhere are marked as restatements rather than treated as independent support.

**Known limitation, and it is a membership one.** [Documentation — Research → What Qualifies as an Inquiry Worth This Facet](../documentation-research.md#what-qualifies-as-an-inquiry-worth-this-facet) states that a research "brings in something from outside this project's own documents", and that "if every source consulted is a document of this project, the work is an audit of the project's own record, however carefully done, and it is not this facet". Every source consulted here is a document of this project. The work is therefore, by that document's own criterion, an audit — and it is filed as a research because the owner asked for a research record at this path. The tension is recorded rather than resolved; the criterion is the owner's to apply.

A second limitation: no document was asked what it means. Nothing here reports what either ruling's author intended, only what the text commits to. Where a passage could be read either way, both readings are given with the words that support each.

## Findings

### What *Deliberate Satisfaction Is Composed, Not Declared* commits to, and what it bounds itself to

**The ruling's positive content is a prohibition on grammar.** The section opens on it (Space Bunny Alpha — claimed; **Evidence**: `abstraction.md:142`): "The language does not intervene in satisfaction. A capsule cannot name the abstraction it satisfies, cannot seal a declaration that only a matching implementation could enter, and the grammar carries no intent marker of any kind." The prohibition is stated three times over — negatively at `abstraction.md:101` ("You **cannot** explicitly declare \"I implement this abstraction\" using any keyword"), in the Abstract's fourth property at `abstraction.md:19` ("Satisfaction stays structural"), and at `abstraction.md:134` ("Khayyam does not introduce any explicit syntax or keyword (such as `impl` or `implements`) to bind a capsule to an abstraction (`ab`). Abstraction realization is strictly implicit and structural.").

**Its positive replacement is an undertaking written in the satisfier's own source, and it is the capsule's act.** `abstraction.md:144`: "A capsule that wants its satisfaction of an abstraction to read as deliberate composes an intent abstraction and explicitly delegates the methods the abstraction it declares requires, naming a capsule that owns the behavior." [Agency in Khayyam → Agency Beyond Concurrency](./agency.md#agency-beyond-concurrency-intentional-vs-accidental-abstraction-satisfaction) supplies the reading of what that act is worth: "resemblance establishes nothing, and only a written undertaking does" (`agency.md:56`).

**The ruling's own bounding sentences, which are the load-bearing ones for the scope question.** Three, in order of directness.

1. What it grants is exactly what ordinary satisfaction grants, and no more: "The intent abstraction is then satisfied by the ordinary structural check — wherever an abstraction type is required, the same signature comparison decides, and a missing or unmatched method is the same failure it is for any other abstraction. Declaring the intent grants no exemption from satisfaction and no guarantee beyond it; what it adds is the undertaking itself" (`abstraction.md:162`). The undertaking therefore adds no checking power of its own; it is checked by the same rule (`abstraction.md:164`).
2. What it governs is the satisfier's writing and nothing further: "What the settled position governs is what the satisfier may write, and there it needs nothing further: the undertaking is the capsule's own act, taken in its own source beside the delegations that carry the responsibility. **What it does not reach is the receiving side.**" (`agency.md:60`).
3. What is deliberately left to another end of the relationship: "Whatever would — a rule or a tool's diagnostic surfacing at an abstraction-typed position which satisfiers undertook and which merely resemble — belongs to that other end of the `agent_for` relationship, and what stands there is a governance and tooling question that this document states as its boundary and does not cross." (`agency.md:60`).

**On the four candidate scopes.**

- *One implementation language?* Partly, and by subject matter rather than by a bounding clause. The word the ruling uses is "the language" (`abstraction.md:142`), and in that document the language is Khayyam throughout — the document's subject is the `ab` subtype keyword (`abstraction.md:11`, `abstraction.md:52`). But no sentence bounds the ruling to one language; the subject is one language, and the grammar-level prohibition is a statement about one grammar.
- *One compilation unit?* No such bound exists. The section names no file, module, package, or compilation unit, and the one place a unit is mentioned is about governance, not about satisfaction: "Delegation boilerplate reduction and abstraction-satisfaction assists for this repository are organizational rules in [abstraction scaffolding]" (`abstraction.md:187`). Satisfaction is instead located by *type position*, not by unit: "wherever an abstraction type is required — a `vr` declared as one, or a value passed to a position declared with one" (`abstraction.md:137`; same wording at `abstraction.md:80`).
- *One repository?* No such bound exists in the ruling. `abstraction.md:187` scopes repository-specific tooling to this repository, and nothing else in the section or document speaks of repositories.
- *Something else?* What the ruling is bounded by is the boundary between the satisfier's side and the receiving side (`agency.md:60`), and between grammar and governance (`abstraction.md:131`, which sends dispatch to [Compiler](../modules/computer/compiler.md)). It is silent — not bounded — on organizational boundaries, on cross-language realization, and on any other consumer of an abstraction it did not write. Silence is the finding; it is not a permission and it is not a prohibition.

### What *Type vs Implementation Type* commits to, and over what boundaries

**The ruling, quoted whole at its decisive sentence** (Space Bunny Alpha — claimed; **Evidence**: `type.md:112`): "What such a mechanism maps is already settled, and settled here rather than left to the mechanism's author. A counterpart across that boundary is either modeled as Types or is not modeled at all: there is no boundary between two type theories, only between a modeled counterpart and an absent one. A counterpart whose entities are modeled as Types is mapped under Memar Types, and a counterpart whose entities are not modeled as Types has no semantic entities in this framework — there is nothing on the far side to convert to, and nothing for a conformance rule to be lenient about. **Declaring the counterpart's vocabulary as Types is therefore the mechanism's first requirement rather than one option among several; no second or weaker notion of conformance is introduced at a boundary.** The shape of that declaration — how the Types are named, in what form they cross, and what a check against them looks like — belongs to the language that realizes these principles, which is where this document locates it and not here."

Four commitments are in that passage, and they are separable:

1. There is no middle position at a boundary — a modeled counterpart or none (`type.md:112`).
2. Declaring the counterpart's vocabulary as Types is the mechanism's **first requirement**, not one option among alternatives (`type.md:112`).
3. No second or weaker notion of conformance is introduced at a boundary (`type.md:112`).
4. The *shape* of that declaration and of the check against it is located in the realizing language, not in `type.md` (`type.md:112`).

**Which boundary the words name.** The demonstrative in "A counterpart across **that** boundary" (`type.md:112`) points back to the boundary the paragraph opened: "if the modeling Type exists at a different level, there must be a mechanism for mapping between them" — that is, the gap between a modeling Type and its implementation representation, the descent and ascent problems of `type.md:108` and `type.md:110`. On the text alone, that is the boundary `type.md` names.

**Does it claim to govern only inter-organization boundaries, or conformance generally?** The word *organization* does not appear in `Type vs Implementation Type`. Neither does *interoperability*. What appears is the counterpart at the modeling/implementation gap, and the sentence "no second or weaker notion of conformance is introduced at a boundary" carries no restriction to any named kind of boundary. So the narrower claim — that it governs inter-organization boundaries only — is not what the text says; but neither does the text say the word *conformance* in that section means the same thing as the word in [Protocol → Conformance](../protocol.md#conformance), where "Conformance is a decidable question, not a judgment" (`protocol.md:228`) and is decided by a party that runs the procedure "without having written the rules" (`protocol.md:228`). The two senses are not tied together anywhere in `docs/`, and a reader may take the prohibition in `type.md:112` to reach the protocol sense, the type-system sense, or both.

**Where the inter-organization reading of the ruling actually enters the record.** It enters through [Modeling → One Authoritative Location per Concern's Data](../modeling.md#one-authoritative-location-per-concerns-data), which cites this exact section for an organizational case: "Another organization's model of the same concept is authoritative at its own level and is not a derived copy of this one — it is a counterpart whose Types are declared in its own right ([Type → Type vs Implementation Type](../type.md#type-vs-implementation-type)), and what crosses the boundary is that counterpart's model, not a replica of this model's data" (`modeling.md:417`). So the organizational extension of the ruling is a modeling document's application of it; the ruling's own words do not contain it. This is the pivot the deciding case turns on, and it is worth the owner's attention that the extension lives in a third document rather than in the ruling.

### Where they meet, and where they do not collide

**One place where `type.md` itself removes the collision before it arises.** This is the strongest evidence that both rulings were meant to stand together, and it should be read before the collision list.

- `type.md:103` states structural satisfaction as a permitted realization, names the tension and resolves it by layer: "**Interaction with structural satisfaction**: A language may realize Abstraction conformance structurally... The Type Identity principle stated here — nominal identity within a context — applies at the declaration level; structural satisfaction operates at the conformance level. **The two are not contradictory, but their interaction requires careful design.**"

Note what `type.md:103` gives up: it does not say the interaction is already designed. It defers the design ("owned where the conformance mechanism itself is defined", `type.md:103`) and it identifies the place where the two would meet if they met — the conformance level, where satisfaction is judged and where, per `type.md:101`, "If the type system cannot observe this distinction, nominal identity is merely a naming convention". Read against `abstraction.md:162`, the nominal identity of an abstraction is not observed at the conformance level by the satisfaction rule itself.

**Restatements, not independent support.** The structural position is restated, without adding scope, at `polymorphism.md:193` ("a natural consequence of the structural satisfaction model"), `polymorphism.md:141`, `polymorphism.md:157`, `inheritance.md:112`, `khayyam.md:213`, `method.md:74`, and `abstraction-implements.md:11`. Each is the same sentence in a different document's argument; none of them bounds it to a unit, a repository, or an organization either, and none of them mentions an organizational boundary.

**The two rulings' shared premise.** Both rest on the same refusal: a relationship that resemblance establishes is not a relationship the framework treats as having been taken. `abstraction.md:139` gives the language-layer form ("Khayyam answers this risk with composition and delegation rather than with a nominal declaration"); `type.md:112` gives the boundary-layer form ("no second or weaker notion of conformance is introduced at a boundary"). Whether one refusal is one principle stated twice or two principles that happen to agree cannot be settled from the documents, and nothing here depends on settling it.

### The collisions

Four, given with file and line. Each is stated as what a reader following one document would write, and what a reader following the other would forbid.

**Collision 1 — the conformance level.** `abstraction.md:137` judges satisfaction "wherever an abstraction type is required — a `vr` declared as one, or a value passed to a position declared with one", with no unit, repository, or organization qualifier, and `abstraction.md:162` confirms the intent abstraction is judged by "the same signature comparison". `type.md:103` names that same level as where "structural satisfaction operates", and declines to have designed the interaction there. A reader following `type.md:103` to its conclusion is being sent to a design that does not exist; a reader following `abstraction.md` is being told the level is already settled. **Files**: `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md:137`, `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md:162`, `docs/type.md:103`.

**Collision 2 — what "a boundary" reaches.** `type.md:112` forbids a second or weaker notion of conformance "at a boundary" and makes declaring the counterpart's vocabulary as Types "the mechanism's first requirement". Read against `modeling.md:417`, which applies that ruling to another organization's model, a consuming organization must declare the producer's vocabulary as Types before anything maps. But `abstraction.md:134` and `abstraction.md:101` forbid the declaration that would make that mapping explicit, and `agency.md:60` states that the satisfaction position "does not reach the receiving side" — the receiving side being, for the deciding case, exactly the consuming organization. So the consuming organization is required by one document to have a declared counterpart vocabulary and forbidden by another from making the declaration that identifies which capsule conforms. **Files**: `docs/type.md:112`, `docs/modeling.md:417`, `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md:134`, `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md:101`, `docs/khayyam/agency.md:60`.

**Collision 3 — accidental satisfaction at a boundary.** `abstraction.md:139` accepts that "an unrelated capsule can accidentally satisfy a small, marker-like abstraction" and answers it "with composition and delegation rather than with a nominal declaration" — a resolution `abstraction.md:37` states as deliberate ("Khayyam keeps structural satisfaction and answers this risk by composition and explicit delegation rather than by grammar"). A capsule crossing an organization boundary is the case where the risk is largest: the producer's author composed no intent abstraction, and `type.md:112`'s first requirement is a declaration on the far side. Nothing in `docs/` says whether the composition remedy is available to a producer who never composed it, and `agency.md:60` says the two are "indistinguishable" at an abstraction-typed position. **Files**: `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md:139`, `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md:37`, `docs/type.md:112`, `docs/khayyam/agency.md:60`.

**Collision 4 — how the shape of the check is located.** `type.md:112` locates "what a check against them looks like" in "the language that realizes these principles". `abstraction.md:131` agrees the grammar names no dispatch strategy and hands that to [Compiler](../modules/computer/compiler.md), and `compiler.md:43` records that "**how** it does so at any given call site is a freedom". So both documents hand the check's shape outward, and neither says which document states the check for a counterpart that arrives from outside the language's own program. **Files**: `docs/type.md:112`, `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md:131`, `docs/modules/computer/compiler.md:43`.

### Where there is no collision

Recorded because an absence of collision is evidence, and because several of these are the passages a reader would expect to carry the tension and find that they do not.

- **`abstraction.md` says nothing about organizations, boundaries, other repositories, or cross-language realization.** A search of the whole document for those terms returns the governance sentence at `abstraction.md:187` and the compiler sentence at `abstraction.md:131`, and nothing else. The deciding case's vocabulary is absent from the ruling, which is why the ruling can be read either way on it.
- **`type.md` never mentions `impl`, interfaces, traits, or structural satisfaction as anything but a permitted realization.** `type.md:103` permits and states it, and `type.md:244` extends the same answer to implementation-intent metadata: "Structural conformance alone cannot provide this signal... The framework's answer is an ordinary first-class construct: the Type composes an intent-declaring Abstraction". `type.md` therefore does not forbid composed intent; it describes it.
- **`abstraction_p.Implements` is realization-agnostic and changes nothing.** `abstraction-implements.md:11`: "Structural satisfaction remains a realization property (the Khayyam example below uses its implicit structural satisfaction); **this document does not change that property**, and adoption is entirely opt-in per abstraction." `abstraction-implements.md:50` adds: "This applies equally across realizations; it is not a backend-specific concern." So the protocol document under which the intent declaration lives already draws no line between one realization and another, which removes one candidate place for the boundary the open question asks about.
- **`polymorphism.md`, `inheritance.md`, `method.md`, and `encapsulation.md` are consistent with both rulings and add no third position.** Each restates satisfaction or intent without bounding either. `method.md:74` extends the composed-intent answer from capsules to methods, which is consistent with `abstraction-implements.md:11` and with `type.md:244`.
- **`modularity.md` places no rule on satisfaction at a Module boundary at all.** Its table defines Abstraction in a different sense — "The degree to which internal detail is exposed or hidden" (`modularity.md:222`) — which is not the Khayyam `ab` and is not a competing account. The nearest boundary rule it holds is `modularity.md:215`: "Language-level cycle restrictions belong to the language layer — each language's own modularity document records them". So the document that would own a module-crossing question has not taken one.
- **The scaffolding rule is downstream and imposes nothing.** `abstraction-scaffolding.md:9` states the rule as "a governance rule of this repository" that "states what organizational tooling may generate or verify" — it cannot be the source of a declaration requirement, since it follows the language rather than bounding it.

### The deciding case, under each reading

The case as the handoff states it: a `Reader`-shaped capsule in one organization, consumed by another that never saw how it was written (`abstraction.handoff.md:28`). Stated as the case the documents put, the consumer holds a position declared `Reader` (`agency.md:60`), and the producer's capsule answers it by signature alone.

**The reading *Deliberate Satisfaction Is Composed, Not Declared* supports.** The producer's capsule satisfies `Reader` at the consumer's position, with nothing added, because satisfaction is judged "wherever an abstraction type is required" (`abstraction.md:137`) and the intent abstraction — had the producer composed one — "grants no exemption from satisfaction and no guarantee beyond it" (`abstraction.md:162`). The consumer cannot require a declaration: none exists in the language (`abstraction.md:101`, `abstraction.md:134`), and `agency.md:60` states that at that position the two capsules "are one value under one rule, and the Agent endpoint and the Principal endpoint are indistinguishable." On this reading the deciding case is already answered, and the open question's premise — that a declaration might be owed across an organizational boundary — has no standing, because `agency.md:60` places the receiving side outside what the ruling reaches.

**The reading *Type vs Implementation Type* supports.** The consumer's `Reader` is a modeling Type (`type.md:11`: "every semantic entity is a Type"), and the producer's capsule is a realization of it, so what crosses is a counterpart at the gap `type.md:112` names. On that reading the consumer must declare the counterpart's vocabulary as Types — "the mechanism's first requirement rather than one option among several" — and "no second or weaker notion of conformance is introduced at a boundary" rules out accepting the capsule because it resembles `Reader`. A consumer that cannot tell an undertaking from a resemblance is applying a second, weaker notion of conformance, which `type.md:112` forbids. Read this way the deciding case is answered too, and it is answered against the composed answer: the declaration would have to be a declaration the language recognizes, which `abstraction.md:142` forbids in the same breath.

**Do the documents as written already answer it?** Partly, and not on the point the question turns on. Three things are settled and do not need ruling: the language has no declaration mechanism (`abstraction.md:101`, `abstraction.md:134`, `abstraction.md:142`); `type.md` does not forbid structural satisfaction and states it (`type.md:103`, `type.md:244`); and `type.md` itself identifies the conformance level as the place where the two meet and says the design there is not done (`type.md:103`). What is not settled, and cannot be settled by reading, is the two questions on which the readings actually diverge: whether the producer's capsule crossing into the consumer's organization makes the consumer's abstraction-typed position "a boundary" within `type.md:112`'s meaning; and whether that sentence's prohibition on a second or weaker notion of conformance is the protocol-sense decidability of `protocol.md:228` or the type-system-sense conformance of `type.md:103`, or both. The first is unanswerable because `type.md:112` says "that boundary" and points at the modeling/implementation gap, while the organizational extension is introduced by `modeling.md:417`; the second is unanswerable because no document ties the two senses together. Both are the owner's.

## Open Questions
- **Is a consuming organization's abstraction-typed position "a boundary" for the purposes of `type.md:112`?** The deciding case turns on this alone. The ruling's own demonstrative points at the modeling/implementation gap; the organizational reading enters through `modeling.md:417`. Ruling that it does, or does not, is a decision this research does not make.
- **Which sense of *conformance* does `type.md:112`'s prohibition reach — the protocol sense, the type-system sense, or both?** If both, the prohibition and `abstraction.md:162` are in direct conflict and one document must yield. If the type-system sense only, `type.md:103`'s "The two are not contradictory" holds and Collision 1 dissolves.
- **Should the organizational extension of `type.md:112` live in `modeling.md:417` alone, or should the ruling itself name the boundary it governs?** Either way one of the two documents currently carries a commitment the other does not make, which is what makes the question look open when it may be under-stated.
- **Should `docs/khayyam/README.md` or a Khayyam document say what "a boundary" means for a language whose satisfaction is judged by type position?** `khayyam.md:158` records that "The precise boundary between \"what Khayyam specifies\" and \"what a Khayyam toolchain or module provides\" still needs worked examples before this can be considered settled", and the deciding case is a worked example of exactly that boundary.
- **The filename.** The facet specification pairs a research to its base by the dot ([Documentation — Research → File Format and Naming](../documentation-research.md#file-format-and-naming)), and this document's base is `abstraction.md`, which would pair as `abstraction.research.001.md`. It was written at the path the owner named. Recorded, not corrected.
- **Is this file a research or an audit?** [Documentation — Research](../documentation-research.md#what-qualifies-as-an-inquiry-worth-this-facet) says a project-internal reading is an audit and not this facet. It is filed as a research at the owner's request; the criterion is the owner's.

## Notes
The tension the handoff records is real but narrower than "two rulings in conflict" suggests. `type.md` already grants structural satisfaction permission (`type.md:103`) and already states that nominal identity and structural satisfaction "are not contradictory"; the composed-not-declared ruling adds no claim about boundaries, because it never makes one. What the two documents do is hand the same unresolved question to two different homes — `type.md` to the language, `agency.md:60` to governance and tooling — without either saying the question exists in both places at once.

The most consequential single fact found is that the inter-organization reading of `type.md:112` is not in `type.md`. It is in `modeling.md:417`, applied to another organization's model, citing `type.md` for it. A reader who follows only the ruling reads a modeling/implementation gap; a reader who follows the citation reads an organizational boundary. The deciding case turns on that difference and nothing in `docs/` says which reading the owner intends.
