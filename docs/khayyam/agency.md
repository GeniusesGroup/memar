# Agency in Khayyam

## Abstract
This document applies [Agency](../agency.md)'s vocabulary to Khayyam design decisions where it clarifies something that was not fully explicit before. Two instances are documented so far, in unrelated parts of the language: concurrency, and abstraction satisfaction. Both are retrospective applications of Agency's vocabulary to decisions reasoned through independently, before Agency existed as a document — this document states each original justification first, then states separately how Agency's vocabulary describes the same ground. Where the two accounts agree, that agreement is treated as evidence the original decision was sound, not as evidence that Agency was secretly present all along. See Methodology, below.

Concurrency is the more fully worked-out instance: Khayyam embeds no concurrency model into its syntax — no `go` keyword, no `async`/`await`. Whether a method blocks or runs asynchronously is an intrinsic property of the method's own definition (tagged via an abstraction, e.g. `tp Async ab`), not a choice made by the caller; how such a method is carried out once it is tagged belongs to the library that implements it, and how that library's work is scheduled belongs to the framework and runtime above the language ([Runtime](../modules/computer/runtime.md)). [Agency](../agency.md) and [Process](../process.md#concurrency) later established, independently of Khayyam's own design history, that a Worker, Actor, thread, or other unit of execution is a *representation* of an execution responsibility, not the concept itself — and that treating one of those representations as a language- or syntax-level primitive is a common source of unnecessary coupling and unnecessary synchronization machinery. Khayyam's refusal to bake `go`/`async`/`await` into its grammar reached the same conclusion first, from a different direction: it avoids the function-coloring problem.

Abstraction satisfaction is the second instance: [Abstraction in Khayyam](./abstraction.md) settles how a capsule whose satisfaction of an abstraction is to read as deliberate says so — it composes an intent abstraction into its own definition and explicitly delegates the methods that abstraction requires, and the language intervenes in satisfaction nowhere. Agency's vocabulary says what that answer is about, why that form of undertaking is the right one, and what it leaves open on the other side of the relationship — see *Agency Beyond Concurrency*, below.

This document does not restrict itself to these two; see the paired [handoff](./agency.handoff.md)'s Open Questions for whether further instances belong here.

## Introduction

### Motivation
Embedding a concurrency model directly into language syntax (Go's `go`, Rust's `async`/`await`) couples the language permanently to one specific concurrency philosophy and is the classic source of the "function coloring" problem, where async and sync functions become incompatible without explicit, often painful conversion machinery.

There is a second, independent reason to reject this coupling, visible only once Agency's own vocabulary is available: a language keyword that spawns a unit of concurrent execution is, functionally, declaring a primitive execution-Agent representation (a goroutine, a green thread, a task) directly into the grammar — before any question has been asked about who is responsible for what state, whether that responsibility can be partitioned, or whether synchronization is even the right tool. [Process → Concurrency](../process.md#concurrency) names this precisely: reaching for a mechanism (a keyword that spawns a `go`routine, in this case) before the responsibility question has been asked tends to produce exactly the queuing- and locking-heavy code that a deliberate Worker/responsibility model would have avoided. A language that bakes `go` into its grammar has made that mistake at the most permanent possible layer — its own syntax — for every program that will ever be written in it.

### Methodology
This document does not derive Khayyam's concurrency decisions from Agency after the fact and then rewrite history to claim they were always Agency-driven. The decisions documented below — definition-site tagging, the absence of any baked-in concurrency keyword, and the placement of scheduling above the language — were made and justified independently, before [Agency](../agency.md) existed as a document. What follows states the original justification first, in each case, and then states separately, and explicitly, how Agency's later, independently-developed vocabulary describes the same decision. Where the two accounts agree, that agreement is treated as evidence the original decision was sound, not as evidence that Agency was secretly present all along.

## Explanation

### Definition-Site Over Call-Site
A method's creator, not its caller, decides whether that method is asynchronous, by tagging it with an abstraction at definition time. Callers do not choose sync-vs-async behavior at the call site; they simply call the method, and its nature is already fixed by its own definition.

The tag does not change what a caller observes: a method call remains a statement whose influenced variables are written when the statement completes, exactly as for a synchronous method — otherwise definite assignment and the uniform call syntax would not survive the tag. What the tag changes is what the owning library does with the method underneath it; that is behavior the method's own library defines, changeable by using a different library, never by changing Khayyam's syntax. Each such library specifies its own method's behavior at the level that method needs.

Read through Agency's vocabulary, this is a specific, non-obvious claim about *where responsibility for a method's execution properties belongs*. A method is the thing that knows what it needs — whether it blocks, what it depends on, what invariants it touches. The caller is a different party, often with no visibility into any of that. Letting the caller choose sync-vs-async behavior at the call site (as `async`/`await`-style languages effectively do, by requiring the caller to `await` or not) makes the calling Agent responsible for a decision that properly belongs to the method's own definition. Definition-site tagging keeps that responsibility where the relevant knowledge actually is. The tag is the method implementing an abstraction: it defines that abstraction's methods on itself, and it may compose an intent abstraction such as [`abstraction_p.Implements`](../modules/computer/abstraction-implements.md) so tooling can see the implementation. See [Method → A method implements an abstraction by methods of its own](./method.md#a-method-implements-an-abstraction-by-methods-of-its-own).

### User-Space Scheduling (Unikernel Synergy)
The grammar states nothing about how execution is scheduled or what substrate it runs on. A method tagged asynchronous is performed by the library that implements it, and the scheduling of that work — threads, fibers, kernel facilities, whatever the environment provides — is the framework's and the runtime's contract, one layer above the language ([Runtime → Concurrency is hosted, not defined, here](../modules/computer/runtime.md#concurrency-is-hosted-not-defined-here), [Concurrency](../modules/computer/concurrency.md)). This is the same placement [Framework → Memar's Framework: Design Space Over Implementation Layers](../framework.md#memars-framework-design-space-over-implementation-layers) gives the scheduler: a framework-level component, not a language-level or OS-level given.

### Avoiding Function Coloring
Asynchronous behavior is declared as an abstraction the method composes, not as a keyword, so the function-coloring problem that plagues `async`/`await` languages does not arise: nothing in the source distinguishes a call that may suspend from one that may not, because no declaration makes that distinction at a call site. This mirrors [Control Flow](../modules/process/control-flow.md)'s own governing principle — the compiler exposes minimal primitives, and every named behavior built on them is a library decision, never a grammar one — applied here to concurrency specifically rather than to branching, looping, or error propagation. This document does not restate that general argument; see Control Flow's [changelog](../modules/process/control-flow.changelog.md) (entry "Absorption of Control Flow in Khayyam", Carried audit record) for why compiler-owned syntax is rejected as a category, independent of which feature is in question.

The cost of this placement is real: without a privileged concurrency keyword, the static guarantees a language like Rust derives from concurrency being baked into its type system (e.g. compile-time prevention of blocking calls inside asynchronous contexts) must be reconstructed elsewhere — by a rule or a tool's check rather than by the grammar — which places the same opt-in/swappable trade-off discussed in [Memory → Safety enforcement is governance](../modules/memory/memory.md#safety-enforcement-is-governance) onto concurrency safety as well.

### Concurrency Decisions as Agency, Not Syntax
A language keyword that spawns a unit of concurrent execution (Go's `go`, most explicitly) is, in Agency's terms, hard-coding one particular Execution Agent representation — a goroutine — directly into the grammar, before any question has been asked about who is responsible for what state. This is the same error [Agency's own Common Modeling Errors](../agency.md) and [Process's Common Modeling Errors](../process.md#common-modeling-errors) name under a different framing: treating Worker, Actor, or another execution representation as the primitive concept, rather than as one possible representation of a bounded execution responsibility.

Khayyam's refusal to add `go`/`async`/`await` as keywords already avoids this, for reasons that had nothing to do with Agency at the time — the function-coloring problem, and a general reluctance to let the compiler privilege one paradigm. Stated through Agency's vocabulary now that it exists: adding such a keyword to Khayyam would mean deciding, once, permanently, in the grammar, which execution-Agent representation every concurrent Khayyam program must use — exactly the decision [Process → Concurrency](../process.md#concurrency)'s escalating question chain (shared state → shared mutable state → shared invariant → common ownership → partitionable responsibility → scheduling → synchronization → locking) argues should be reached only after the responsibility question has actually been asked, not assumed by reaching for a keyword first.

This does not mean Khayyam has no need for some library-level representation of an execution Agent. It means that representation, whatever shape it takes and whatever it ends up being called, is built the same way `IF`/`ELSE` and `PANIC()` are — as an ordinary, named, importable abstraction, not as syntax — so that the choice of execution-Agent representation remains a library decision a project can inspect, replace, or govern by rule.

### Agency Beyond Concurrency: Intentional vs. Accidental Abstraction Satisfaction
Concurrency is not the only place Agency's vocabulary bears on a Khayyam design decision. [Abstraction in Khayyam → Deliberate Satisfaction Is Composed, Not Declared](./abstraction.md#deliberate-satisfaction-is-composed-not-declared) settles how a capsule whose satisfaction of an abstraction is to read as deliberate says so: the language does not intervene in satisfaction — no `impl`-style keyword, no sealed declaration, no grammar-level intent marker — and the capsule composes an intent abstraction into its own definition, explicitly delegating the methods that abstraction requires to a capsule that owns the behavior. That section states how the undertaking is written. What Agency's vocabulary adds is why that form of undertaking is the right one, why it needs nothing from the language, and what remains unprovided by it.

Agency requires an undertaking before a relationship is an Agent relationship at all: an `agent_for` edge is constituted by one system taking responsibility on behalf of another ([The `agent_for` Relationship](../agency.md#the-agent_for-relationship)), and being directed to act does not make a thing an Agent ([What Distinguishes Agency from Mere Behavior](../agency.md#what-distinguishes-agency-from-mere-behavior)). Khayyam's structural satisfaction reaches that bar with nothing on its own: a capsule that happens to match `Reader`'s signatures is a `Reader` at every position that asks for one, whether or not its author ever intended to act as one. So the settled answer's substance is an Agency answer — resemblance establishes nothing, and only a written undertaking does — and its form is Agency's own: the undertaking is the capsule's act, taken in its own source beside the delegations that carry the responsibility, where [Delegation](../agency.md#delegation) places it. Composing an intent abstraction is also what keeps the declaration off the grammar's list, and what a language-level marker would have added is not a smaller amount of the same thing but a different kind of thing: the compiler's recognition of an undertaking, making the relationship exist because a declaration was accepted rather than because the capsule took responsibility.

This is also why the answer needs no exemption and no check of its own. The intent abstraction is judged by the ordinary structural rule, wherever an abstraction type is required, like any other abstraction; the delegations are ordinary source under [Type → Explicit Behavior Ownership](../type.md#explicit-behavior-ownership) and [Method → A method implements an abstraction by methods of its own](./method.md#a-method-implements-an-abstraction-by-methods-of-its-own), so a deliberate declaration is found by exactly the means any satisfier is found. A declaration the language recognized would need a rule of its own for what counts as complete and who may enter it; an undertaking the capsule composes needs only the check that already existed, and declaring the intent grants nothing beyond ordinary satisfaction.

What the settled position governs is what the satisfier may write, and there it needs nothing further: the undertaking is the capsule's own act, taken in its own source beside the delegations that carry the responsibility. What it does not reach is the receiving side. A position declared `Reader` is satisfied equally by a capsule that composed its intent abstraction and delegated its methods, and by one whose author never had `Reader` in mind; at that position the two are one value under one rule, and the Agent endpoint and the Principal endpoint are indistinguishable. Nothing in the language will tell them apart, the language being uninvolved in satisfaction by settled design. Whatever would — a rule or a tool's diagnostic surfacing at an abstraction-typed position which satisfiers undertook and which merely resemble — belongs to that other end of the `agent_for` relationship, and what stands there is a governance and tooling question that this document states as its boundary and does not cross.
