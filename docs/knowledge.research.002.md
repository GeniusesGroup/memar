## Question and Goals
- **Question 1:** Does Memar need a named category for material that exists in the shared medium without any decision having produced it — distinct from fabricated content, which the Content Rule already forbids? — `Open`
- **Question 2:** If such a category is owed, which kind of thing is it: a kind of material Knowledge models, a Rule stated against the repository, an obligation a check must discharge, or a term Memar deliberately leaves undefined? — `Open`
- **Question 3:** Is the condition decidable at all by a participant reading the medium, and if so what counts as evidence that no decision produced a given artifact? — `Open`
- **Question 4:** What follows from the category for material found in it — surface it, delete it, adopt it by recording the decision after the fact, or decline to say? — `Open`
- **Question 5:** Is the question Memar-wide, or a statement about this project's own record and the projects that install it? — `Open`
- **Goal 1:** establish what Memar's live documents already say about material lacking a producing decision, so that any category is placed against what exists rather than beside it — `Partially met`: the grounding pass is recorded under Findings; the placement questions are Question 2 and Open Questions below.
- **Goal 2:** keep the questions open rather than answering them here — `Met`: no finding below proposes a category, and every finding is a statement about what a document already says.
- **Goal 3:** record the origin and the blind spots of this inquiry so its reach can be audited — `Met`.
- **Scope:** what Memar's `docs/` owns about material in the shared medium that no decision produced, and what kind of thing a named category for it would be. Excludes: whether this repository actually contains such material; any survey of external practices for detecting or classifying it; the disposition of any specific artifact; and the other threads the originating comparison raised ([Comparison: Memar and GitHub Spec Kit → Separate research](./comparisons/spec-kit.md#separate-research)).

## Methodology
**Sources consulted.** The governing specifications for the facets this record belongs to, read in full: [Documentation — Research](./documentation-research.md), [Documentation Practice → Written surface](./documentation.practice.md#written-surface). The candidate base documents, read far enough to decide ownership: [Knowledge](./knowledge.md) in full, [Process](./process.md) in full, [Documentation](./documentation.md) in full, and [Protocols → Membership criterion](./protocols/README.md#membership-criterion) to confirm that `docs/protocols/` is not the home — a category for a kind of material is a concept, and that folder's criterion admits a protocol contract, a rule set Memar owns, or Memar's position on an external protocol surface. Also read: [Rule](./rule.md) in full, [Knowledge Practice](./knowledge.practice.md) in full, [Content](./content.md) in part, the originating [comparison](./comparisons/spec-kit.md), three existing research companions for shape ([Knowledge Research 001](./knowledge.research.001.md), [Process Research 001](./process.research.001.md), [Process Research 002](./process.research.002.md)), and the sections of [Terminology](./terminology.md), [Documentation Changelog](./documentation-changelog.md), [Test Practice](./protocols/process/test.practice.md), and [Modeling Practice](./modeling.practice.md) named at the findings they support.

**Procedure.** The question was put to each candidate base document as *what is this document's subject, and does the question fall inside it*, before any of them was read for what it says about the phenomenon. The vocabulary of the phenomenon was then searched across the whole doc set — `unrequested`, `unowned`, `orphan`, `no decision`, `decision producing`, `producing decision`, `provenance`, `accumulated`, `who introduced`, `produced by`, `dead code`, `unused`, `vestigial`, `leftover`, `prune`, `retire`, `no rule`, `without a stated` — and each hit was read in its document rather than taken from the line. Where a document's own scope statement decided the placement, that statement is the evidence and it is cited at the finding.

**Limits and blind spots.**
- Spec Kit's own source was not consulted. The four-way classification of divergence, and the `unrequested` member of it, are taken as [the comparison records them](./comparisons/spec-kit.md#separate-research), not re-read from `/converge`. This inquiry therefore inherits whatever that comparison's own accuracy is, and has not verified that its account of the categories is complete.
- No external prior art was consulted: no provenance standard, no dead-code or unreferenced-artifact detection literature, no taxonomy of how other systems name material that no decision asked for. The space of candidate names, and the possibility that the condition is already named somewhere Memar has not looked, are both unexplored.
- `docs/protocols/` was not read beyond its README's membership criterion and the headings of [Test](./protocols/process/test.md). A protocol may bear on Question 3, since whether a condition is decidable is the kind of thing the protocol layer states.
- Five concept documents were not read: [Agency](./agency.md), [System](./system.md), [Modeling](./modeling.md), [Modularity](./modularity.md), [Type](./type.md). They were reached only through search hits. A document that owns this subject and does not use any of the searched vocabulary would not have been found by this method.
- `modules/` was not examined at all. Whether this repository in fact holds material no decision produced is untested, so nothing here rests on the condition being common, frequent, or harmful; the frequency and severity of the failure are unmeasured, and the question is currently reachable only because a comparison reported it.
- The working tree carries uncommitted work from earlier sessions. Nothing in it was read as evidence about this question and nothing in it was reconciled with this record.
- This is a single reasoning pass by one participant, with no second reader and no review. Under [Cognition → Report absence rather than supplying it](./cognition.md#report-absence-rather-than-supplying-it), the searches reported under Findings establish what was searched, not that Memar holds no such category.

## Findings
These are statements about what Memar's live documents already say. None of them proposes a category, and none of them is offered as the inquiry's answer.

### What the placement decision rested on
- **Knowledge is the base artifact this research is paired to**, because the question's subject is the provenance of material in the shared medium, and that is Knowledge's declared subject: what a system must do to preserve, retrieve, and evolve knowledge, and how knowledge differs from its representations ([knowledge.md](./knowledge.md), Abstract; [Scope boundary](./knowledge.md#knowledge-in-memars-concept-web)).
- **Process disqualifies itself in its own words.** "A process describes something that happens, may happen, or is being carried out — it is concerned with behavior and progression, not with the static existence of things" ([Process → Abstract](./process.md#abstract)). A category for material that exists is a category of static existence.
- **The Content Rule cannot host it without changing its stated scope.** Its four rules "apply to every Explanation- and Practice-facet document (and to pointer files such as directory READMEs that guide readers into those documents)" ([Documentation → Content Rule](./documentation.md#content-rule-no-fabricated-or-redundant-provenance)). The shared medium holds modules, code, and generated artifacts as well as documents — [Knowledge and Code](./knowledge.md#knowledge-and-code) treats prose and executable modules as complementary projections of one knowledge — so a category covering all of it reaches past what that rule says it governs. This was the first assumption, and it did not survive the scope statement.
- **`docs/protocols/` is not the home**, per its own membership criterion: concept documents model general concepts and stay at `docs/` root ([protocols/README.md → Membership criterion](./protocols/README.md#membership-criterion)).

### What Knowledge already holds about a producing decision
- **The decision is already the primary unit of knowledge evolution.** "The primary unit of knowledge evolution is the Task (or its equivalent: Decision, Investigation, Experiment, Case Review), not the Snapshot or Commit" ([Knowledge → Task-Centric Knowledge Evolution](./knowledge.md#task-centric-knowledge-evolution)). The unit a piece of material would be missing is therefore not an unfamiliar one.
- **The knowledge unit is already defined as more than the artifact.** "A knowledge system must treat the document/artifact as ONE component of a knowledge unit, alongside its provenance, discussion history, decision rationale, evolutionary context, and relational connections. Storing only the artifact is storing only the conclusion, not the knowledge" ([Knowledge → Knowledge Requires Explicit Context Recovery](./knowledge.md#knowledge-requires-explicit-context-recovery)). Material with no producing decision is describable in this vocabulary today, as a unit that is only its artifact.
- **The relation whose absence is at issue is already a first-class relationship type.** Attribution — "A was created/modified/approved by B" — appears in the table of relationship types a knowledge model must support natively ([Knowledge → Relationships Are First-Class Citizens](./knowledge.md#relationships-are-first-class-citizens)), and Provenance ("who, why, based on what?") is one of the content-model concerns the document contrasts against storage concerns ([Storage Model ≠ Content Model](./knowledge.md#storage-model--content-model)).
- **Memar already owns a rule requiring authorship, for one class of artifact.** "A Rule is an **authored, replaceable claim**" ([Rule → What a Rule Is](./rule.md#what-a-rule-is)), introduced by an owner ([Rule → Who Owns and Introduces Rules](./rule.md#who-owns-and-introduces-rules)). Material that is not a Rule carries no such requirement anywhere in the doc set.

### The nearest named condition, and why it is a different failure
- **Context decay is about context lost, not context never held.** Of the six knowledge problems, the fourth is "artifacts created with rich context (decisions, designs, protocols) become orphaned from the discussions, rationales, and assumptions that gave them meaning" ([Knowledge → Problem](./knowledge.md#problem)). The artifact was produced by a decision whose rationale has since decayed. Material with no producing decision never had one to lose, so this problem is adjacent rather than covering.
- **Process already admits that purpose can arrive after the fact.** "A process may have been established for one purpose and later acquire additional purposes or functions through interaction with other systems, participants, or processes. These later effects may become relevant to understanding the process even when they were not part of its original intent" ([Process → Intent](./process.md#intent)). This bears directly on Question 3: Memar already treats unrequested effect as a real and legitimate thing, which makes the boundary between "no decision asked for this" and "a decision's later effect" a distinction Memar has reason to care about.
- **The nearest thing Memar owns to a check for material with nothing connecting it is scoped to a session's model, not to the medium.** The Completeness Check asks "Are there orphaned concepts with no relationships at all?" ([Modeling Practice → Completeness Check](./modeling.practice.md#completeness-check)). The word is the same and the subject is different: concepts in one model under construction, not artifacts in the shared medium.

### The two adjacent rules that do not reach this case
- **The empty-result rule governs what a check adds, not what it finds.** "An empty result leaves the medium unchanged" ([Documentation → Written surface](./documentation.md#written-surface)) requires that a report, review, check, or convergence producing nothing produce no artifact. The question here runs the other way: material already present that no check asked for.
- **A change with nothing to audit gets no entry, which is not the same situation.** "A change with nothing to audit receives **no entry**" ([Documentation Changelog → Trivial changes](./documentation-changelog.md#trivial-changes)) — there a change happened and was judged not worth recording. Material with no producing decision is not a change at all, and nothing in that rule reaches it.
- **Retention of an artifact whose reason has gone is governed, for one artifact kind, by a protocol.** "Keep the Test. It stays in the tree, is re-checked whenever the Process's defining document changes, and is retired only when the Process it names no longer exists" ([Test Practice → The Cycle](./protocols/process/test.practice.md#the-cycle)). This is the shape an obligation over continued existence already takes in Memar, and it is scoped to Tests.

### Two constraints any answer must respect
- **Naming it is a governance act with a stated cost.** [Terminology → A Term Deliberately Left Open](./terminology.md#a-term-deliberately-left-open) holds that taking a word settles by definition the part of the design space the word names, before the work that would have explored that space has been done, and records `Task` as the standing instance of a term used without being defined. A category name for this condition would carry that cost whichever way it went.
- **Whether the discipline belongs to Memar at all may not be Knowledge's to settle.** Knowledge's scope boundary states that it does not own knowledge management as an organizational discipline — creation workflows, tacit-to-explicit conversion practice, sector adoption patterns, and change management — and that these belong to the Organization project ([Knowledge → Scope boundary](./knowledge.md#knowledge-in-memars-concept-web)). A category and a practice for triaging material in it are two different things and may have two different homes.

## Open Questions
- **Is a name needed at all, or is "a knowledge unit that is only its artifact" already the description?** [Knowledge → Knowledge Requires Explicit Context Recovery](./knowledge.md#knowledge-requires-explicit-context-recovery) states the requirement an artifact with no producing decision fails. Whether naming the failure adds a rule or only a label is Question 1, and nothing read so far settles it.
- **What kind of thing is the category — a concept Knowledge models, a Rule about the repository, an obligation a check discharges, or a deliberately undefined term?** [Rule → What a Rule Is](./rule.md#what-a-rule-is) supplies the two-tier test that would decide the Rule option: if turning the check off would admit something the owning specification says cannot exist, it is ontology wearing a governance label. Whether that test reaches this condition at all is untested.
- **Is the condition decidable from the medium?** [Cognition → Report absence rather than supplying it](./cognition.md#report-absence-rather-than-supplying-it) holds that a lookup finding nothing is a fact about the lookup and never the statement that the project holds no such rule. That norm is written about what a *participant* may report; here it would bear on what a *category* can require, which is a different use and is unexamined.
- **What is the evidence that no decision produced an artifact?** Memar's own record keeps decisions in changelog entries, in research companions, and in handoffs, but nothing requires every file in the medium to have one — so absence of a record is weak evidence in the direction the category would need it. Whether a stronger obligation is possible without the coverage rule it would depend on is Question 3.
- **What is the disposition of material found in the category?** Surface, delete, adopt retroactively by recording the decision, or decline to say. [Test Practice → The Cycle](./protocols/process/test.practice.md#the-cycle) is a precedent for retention, and [Documentation → Written surface](./documentation.md#written-surface) is a precedent for a check that changes nothing — neither states an obligation about material nobody decided to keep.
- **Does the category reach projects that install Memar, or only this repository's own record?** [README → Where Memar Stops, and Where Work Continues](../README.md#where-memar-stops-and-where-work-continues) was not read for this inquiry; the boundary between what Memar states about its own documentation and what it states about a consuming project is unexamined here.
- **Is the phenomenon Memar's at all, or is a project free to have material nothing decided on?** [Cognition → Ask rather than assume](./cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model) does not require asking permission for every artifact, and nothing read states an obligation to account for an artifact's existence at all. Whether the honest answer is "Memar needs a category" or "Memar needs a statement that it declines to require one" is unasked by any document consulted.
