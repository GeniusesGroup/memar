# Judge Assignment Handoff

Open work for who may sit. The ballot, the margin's job, and the consensus proposal are on the [case handoff](../../../modules/rights/case/case.handoff.md).

## Topic & Purpose

This module writes judge edges. One edge serves an organization that calls itself arbitration, a state court, or a social network. Who may sit is the pluggable part.

## Status

Active

## Decisions

- **Decided (2026-09-27).** Who names the person is the organization's list, or the parties in advance. Party-named judges are a rule this module may implement. There is no second adjudicator type. He rejects handing a seated, paid judge's case to an arbitrator.
- **Decided (2026-09-27).** A judge may accept the maximum a party will pay, or decline the seat. There is no wealth cap on how long the case continues. Continuation stops on the vote margin, not on a fixed count of 1, then 3, then 5, and not at 5 or at 11, 13, or 15. The examples of about 20 percent from the middle and about 80 percent the same way are examples. This draft does not adopt those numbers as its schedule. [case.research.004.md](../../../modules/rights/case/case.research.004.md) question 3 asked for first, second, and third filing counts. The 2026-09-26 doubling note was not adopted.
- **Decided (2026-09-27).** A party score is not put on a vote. No scoring formula is designed. Specialty, rank, and fee are self-declared and must carry evidence. The kernel does not compute a fee from rank. Conflict of interest and anti-fraud apply and are not designed here.
- **Decided (2026-09-27).** Seated persons must not see each other's votes or reasons before casting their own. Until every required judge has voted, assignment does not reveal those votes or texts to a party.

## Open Questions

1. **Whether any person may be a judge.** He doubts that "anyone" is right. He left it open on 2026-09-27 and model v5 records it as still open. Model v6 does not close it. [case.research.007.md](../../../modules/rights/case/case.research.007.md) question 4's remainder is this question. A pluggable rule may still name judges from the parties or from the organization's list. Matters for who may sit. Does not block the judge edge, and does not add a second type. Suggested path: he says whether "any person" is allowed, refused, or left as an organization rule. This handoff does not pick.

2. **The three score examples that are not a vote weight.** On the afternoon of 2026-09-27 he named four examples and said they are not the justice rule: a new judge may enter outside the seated count and record an opinion; closeness to the majority may raise a score; a party's complaint may lower a score; a party's score might affect the judge's vote. The fourth is decided: a party score is not a vote weight. Critique v2 searched for support for that fourth example and recorded: **Not found** in either research file: party reputation, rating, or score weighting an adjudicator's ballot. What would falsify that rejection: A sourced rule or ratified pilot norm that a party's score of a judge multiplies or divides that judge's vote on the merits — **not found**. Critique v2 also named where a party's score could go instead, as a proposal nobody adopted: policy metadata on assignment, on the fee, or on later seating, inside this module, without entering the vote tally. Its reason is that weighting a vote by a party's score mixes the evaluation of the judge with the aggregation of dispositions. The first three stay examples. He said they were left open, and model v6 does not close them. No formula is stated. Matters because an example can be read later as a rule. Blocks any score rule and blocks "a new judge outside the seated count" as assignment policy. Suggested path: he adopts one, rejects it, or leaves it an example. This handoff does not design a formula.

3. **A higher rank later.** On the morning of 2026-09-27 he asked whether judge rank changes the fee, and whether parties can summon a higher rank at a later stage. The fee half is decided: rank does not compute the fee; the fee is self-declared with evidence. The later-stage seat was not answered. The outside-count example in question 2 is not that answer. Matters for a later seat. Blocks a rank-summons rule. Suggested path: he says whether a later higher rank is allowed. This handoff does not infer it from the example.

4. **A seat nobody fills.** What happens when the margin allows continuation and no eligible user accepts the seat, for example because every candidate declines the maximum the party will pay? Model v1, under the kernel pool that was later dropped, said the filing is not opened, the previous shortcut stays, and that is an assignment failure. The margin rule replaced that pool and did not restate the failure. He did not answer. Matters because the case could otherwise wait on a seat with no rule saying it has stopped. Blocks an assignment-failure rule. Suggested path: he says whether an unfilled seat leaves the current shortcut standing, or is an organization rule.

## Assumptions

- **Strong.** Who may sit can change without a new edge type.
- **Weak.** Leaving the numeric margin examples unadopted means an organization may set its own thresholds later. No default schedule is implied.
