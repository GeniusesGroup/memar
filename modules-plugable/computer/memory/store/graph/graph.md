# Graph
In discrete mathematics, particularly in graph theory, a [graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_(discrete_mathematics)) is a structure consisting of a set of objects where some pairs of the objects are in some sense "related". The objects are represented by abstractions called vertices (also called nodes or points) and each of the related pairs of vertices is called an edge (also called link or line). Typically, a graph is depicted in diagrammatic form as a set of dots or circles for the vertices, joined by lines or curves for the edges.

Graphs are the basic subject studied by graph theory. The word "graph" was first used in this sense by J. J. Sylvester in 1878 due to a direct relation between mathematics and chemical structure (what he called a chemico-graphical image).

## Edge types
- The edges may be directed or undirected. For example, if the vertices represent people at a party, and there is an edge between two people if they shake hands, then this graph is undirected because any person A can shake hands with a person B only if B also shakes hands with A. In contrast, if an edge from a person A to a person B means that A owes money to B, then this graph is directed, because owing money is not necessarily reciprocated.
- [Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loop_(graph_theory)) are edges that join a vertex to itself.
- Shortcut (or derived) edge is just short traversal for two or more edges. ## Relationship-distance queries: traversal vs precomputed edges
The recurring design question behind shortcut edges: answering "how is user A related to user B?" (LinkedIn-style friend-of-friend, friend-of-friend-of-friend) without traversing the social graph on every read. The resolved direction from the modeling discussion (2025-01-16):

- **Traversal (BFS/DFS) is always needed** to discover relations dynamically, but it is too expensive to run per read when the read is a user opening another user's page.
- **O(1) read time requires compute-and-store**: the distance/relation is precomputed and stored as a derived edge, paid for at write time (each new or removed relation invalidates and recomputes affected derived edges). There is no free lunch — the trade-off is write-time cost vs read-time cost, and for social-profile reads O(1) read wins.
- **Derived edges are caches, not truth**: a shortcut/derived edge (e.g. `connection_2nd`) must always be re-derivable from the authoritative base edges (`friendship`), must never become the source of truth, and is owned by the capability module that gives it meaning (shortcut edges are not separate modules; analytics that materialize derived knowledge is always pluggable).
- Degree labels are automatic (users cannot assign them); they are maintained by the system when the base relations change.

Algorithm inventory for the engine side (reference for implementers; the modeling decision above is independent of the choice): BFS (shortest path / degree discovery), DFS (reachability), incremental distance-update algorithms and dynamic all-pairs methods (Floyd-Warshall, Johnson) for write-time maintenance, distance matrices or key-value pair stores for precomputation (O(n^2) vs sparse), graph partitioning/clustering (Louvain, Girvan-Newman) for very large graphs, distance trees and metric-based indexing, and approximation approaches (sampling, ML-based link prediction) where exactness is not required.

## Graph theory
In mathematics and computer science, [graph theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_theory) is the study of graphs, which are mathematical structures used to model pairwise relations between objects. A graph in this context is made up of vertices (also called nodes or points) which are connected by edges (also called arcs, links, or lines). A distinction is made between undirected graphs, where edges link two vertices symmetrically, and directed graphs, where edges link two vertices asymmetrically. Graphs are one of the principal objects of study in discrete mathematics.

## Graph drawing
[Graph drawing](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_drawing) is an area of mathematics and computer science combining methods from geometric graph theory and information visualization to derive two-dimensional (or, sometimes, three-dimensional) depictions of graphs arising from applications such as social network analysis, cartography, linguistics, and bioinformatics.

A drawing of a graph or network diagram is a pictorial representation of the vertices and edges of a graph. This drawing should not be confused with the graph itself: very different layouts can correspond to the same graph. In the abstract, all that matters is which pairs of vertices are connected by edges. In the concrete, however, the arrangement of these vertices and edges within a drawing affects its understandability, usability, fabrication cost, and aesthetics. The problem gets worse if the graph changes over time by adding and deleting edges (dynamic graph drawing) and the goal is to preserve the user's mental map.

## What it is to the memory contract
An edge remains a **value under an address** of the memory contract ([Memory Store → The contract](../../../../../modules/computer/memory/store/store.md#the-contract)): the reverse side of an edge is written in the same atomic `batch`, and a derived (shortcut) edge is a cache rebuilt from `changes`. Graph adds **no** new address kind and **no** new durability or isolation guarantee.

What it **does** add is a **capsule of layouts and services** over that contract (and, for high-degree layouts, over [Key-Value](../key_value/key_value.md)): how many edges of one relation on one vertex are addressed, how their sides are listed without a world scan, and how a consumer names which layout it uses. That responsibility evolves alone from Record’s “one domain key → one part” and from a raw key-value engine, which is why the capsule is justified ([Modeling → Abstract](../../../../../docs/modeling.md#abstract); owner decision 2026-11-01 on task `20261101-T123647Z-15992`). Domain modules supply relation names and edge-field meaning; they do not each invent adjacency.

The abstract datatype of a graph (primary node, secondary node, edge) stays at [`modules/computer/adt/graph/`](../../../../../modules/computer/adt/graph/protocol/graph.kh). Relations live in each domain’s `edges/` folders ([Modules → Edge folder](../../../../../modules/modules.md#edge-folder)). This module stores them.

### What stays opaque, what Graph frames, what a medium adapter does
Four responsibilities must not be collapsed:

1. **Domain edge Fields** are opaque caller bytes. Graph does not interpret them as JSON, YAML, FlatBuffers, or any domain schema. Domains give Fields meaning; Graph only carries them under a side.
2. **Layout-value framing** — how compact-adjacency state and per-edge side state become the **value-bytes** of the memory contract — is Graph’s. It is a named Pack/Unpack seam ([`Graph_Layout_Value_Codec`](protocol/graph-layout-value-codec.kh)), not private helpers locked inside the capsule spine. One default realization holds today’s framing; there is no bind-time family of interchangeable layout codecs. Address tags that name `(vertex, relation)` under a layout are address layout, not this seam’s subject.
3. **Medium adapters** (files or other) map store addresses and value-bytes onto a medium. They store and retrieve; they do not invent relation vocabulary, nest edges under a domain map, or decide edge-field meaning. Graph is developed against the memory contract without fixing which medium holds the bytes; when Graph needs a capability of the store below (variable-length values, listable key prefixes, compare-and-swap), it declares that need on the contract / layability rules so the lower layer can provide it.
4. **Presentation / display** — formatting Graph or store answers (sides, parts) as JSON, YAML, or other text **for a human or agent to read** is not storage and not Path encoding. It calls Graph/store services and formats for eyes. It must not invent dump wrappers (`domains`, `placements`, sibling `relation` / `reverse` metadata) that pretend to be a medium body schema, and it is not a bind-time family of graph-json storage codecs. A dedicated projection (family of [Memory View](../view/view.md)) may own that formatting; until one exists, callers print structured service answers without inventing a second on-disk shape.

## Layouts for many edges of one relation
A vertex may hold **many edges of the same relation**, added over time. The capsule offers more than one layout; **the consumer chooses** which fits its degree, append pattern and read shape. Graph states the costs; it does not pick for the domain.

What is **searched by** (the relation) is part of the address key; what is only shown (far vertex, edge fields) is in the value — the same rule ownership already binds for edges generally ([Ownership → Contributor relations](../../../../../modules/rights/ownership/ownership.md#contributor-relations)).

### Compact adjacency
One address per `(vertex, relation)` (and the reverse relation on the far vertex). The value holds **all** current sides of that relation from that vertex.

- **Retrieve** every side: one `get` of that address (cost grows with the value size, not with the store).
- **Add** a side: read the value, append, `batch` under the prior token (compare-and-swap on the whole adjacency). Reverse side in the same `batch`.
- **Fits** low-to-medium degree and infrequent appends.
- **Breaks** when the value or CAS contention grows with degree (the old sketch’s “millions of followers” case).

### Per-edge keys
One address per edge instance. Keys are ordered so that all edges of `(vertex, relation)` share a **listable prefix**; each value holds one side’s fields (and names the far vertex when it is not already in the key).

- **Retrieve** sides: `list` (paged) over that prefix — bounded neighbours without loading every side into one value.
- **Add** a side: write one new key (+ reverse) in one `batch`; no rewrite of a giant adjacency.
- **Fits** high degree and append-heavy relations.
- **Needs** variable-length, prefix-ordered keys — the [Key-Value](../key_value/key_value.md) layout — not a per-domain encoding.

### Choosing
| Prefer compact adjacency when… | Prefer per-edge keys when… |
| --- | --- |
| Degree stays small enough for one value and one CAS | Degree is large or unbounded |
| Readers usually want the whole neighbour set | Readers want a page / window of neighbours |
| Appends are rare relative to reads | Appends are frequent and must not rewrite all sides |

**Layout is bound when the store is laid** (or per relation in that binding), not chosen ad hoc on each `put_edge`. Mixing layouts for one `(vertex, relation)` is refused. A later policy may migrate one relation (or one vertex) from compact to per-edge under the **same** services; that migration is this module’s, not a domain fork.

### Multiplicity and `side_ref`
Industry property graphs are multigraphs by default (GQL; Neo4j `CREATE`; SQL Graph edge rows): many edges may share the same `(from, relation, to)`. Each occurrence has a stable **`side_ref`** returned by `list_sides` / `get_side` / `put_edge` — the per-edge address, or a slot identity inside a compact value — so patch and end target one occurrence.

A consumer that needs **at most one** edge for a given far vertex uses `put_edge` in replace-by-far mode (MERGE-like): `get_side` without `side_ref` answers that unique side. The capsule does not invent domain uniqueness rules beyond what the call asks.

## When not to use a graph layout
Not every sequence of facts is a graph. Forcing a graph view where the natural access is something else inflates both write cost (forward + reverse, relation keys) and retrieval cost (adjacency or prefix scans that a specialised structure would answer directly).

Prefer a **time-series** (or another non-graph) structure when **most** of the following hold:

- Each new fact is meaningful on its own and does **not** primarily mean “this vertex is related to that vertex” ([Modeling → Modeling State Change as Events](../../../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates) — inherently time-series readings).
- The dominant query is a **time range** (or latest-N) on one stream, not “sides of relation R from vertex V” in both directions.
- Maintaining a reverse edge would be noise: nothing asks “which streams mention this sample?” as a first-class neighbour read.

Graph remains right when the durable question is pairwise (or multi-way) **relatedness** between identities and both directions matter — follow, confirmation, membership, substitute, and the same class of edges. A domain that needs **both** relatedness and a cheap temporal window still chooses a graph layout for the relation and may keep a separate time-series (or derived index) for the window; it does not invent a third adjacency scheme.

## Services
The capsule’s protocol ([`protocol/graph-store.kh`](protocol/graph-store.kh), Tentative until implement) exposes these operations over an opened store. Edge-field bytes are the caller’s; the capsule orders addresses and batches. **This module owns the full graph surface** — adjacency writes/reads **and** pattern match / path services — not a thin half that leaves MATCH to another domain. Industry splits (SQL/PGQ’s `GRAPH_TABLE`, GQL/Cypher `MATCH`) are capabilities **of graph**, implemented here over the layouts below. Cross-check: task research `research-graph-apis-services` (2026-11-01); owner correction 2026-11-01 (MATCH is in scope).

- **`put_edge`** — add one directed side (or replace under expectation / by far vertex); always schedule the **reverse** write in the same `batch` when the relation has a reverse name. Answers conflict / refused / unavailable as the contract does. Returns `side_ref`.
- **`put_edges`** — the same for a bounded list in one call → one memory `batch` (importers; Neptune/Gremlin-style batch adds).
- **`list_sides`** — page sides of `(vertex, relation)` with cursor, without a world scan. Order is **by `side_ref` ascending** unless the binding declares another total order (e.g. append order encoded in the ref). Each page element carries far vertex, fields token, and `side_ref`. Compact: one get, then page in process. Per-edge: `list` on the prefix. Optional cheap total when the layout can answer it.
- **`count_sides`** — cardinality of `(vertex, relation)` without returning members (degree-style checks and empty-vs-missing).
- **`get_side`** — one side by far vertex and/or `side_ref`: membership and fields without pulling every neighbour when the layout can answer cheaper.
- **`set_side_fields`** — replace the payload of an existing side (by `side_ref`) without changing endpoints; maps to industry SET/UPDATE on relationship properties.
- **`end_edge`** — mark a side ended (tombstone / validity end) so history remains readable; reverse ended in the same `batch`. This is Memar’s counterpart to industry DELETE while the memory contract still has **no erase** ([Memory Store → The contract](../../../../../modules/computer/memory/store/store.md#the-contract)): dropping a neighbour from a **compact** value is a replace of that adjacency blob (current view), not erasure of a past fact — domains that need the fact keep `end_edge` history instead of rewriting it away.

**Undirected** relations are two directed names written together (or one name used as its own reverse); a distinct undirected address kind is not required for v1. **Vertex create/delete** is out of this capsule — nodes exist when another module writes their parts (same split as SQL Graph node table vs edge table).

### Pattern match and paths
Domain agents need a **complete** graph surface here so they do not invent adjacency or ad-hoc BFS. The first implement ships a usable match/path set; the dialect grows **inside this module**.

- **`match`** — evaluate a **structured** pattern (Khayyam types: steps, directions, relation filters, optional field predicates, bound/unbound vertex slots). Returns rows of bindings (`vertex` / `side_ref` per slot). This is the responsibility Cypher/GQL name `MATCH` and SQL/PGQ expose as `GRAPH_TABLE` — owned here, not elsewhere.
- **`path`** — bounded variable-length expansion from a start vertex over one or more relations (min/max hops; trail or acyclic mode). Answers reachability windows.
- **`exists`** — boolean/`limit 1` form of `match` or `path` without materialising every row (cheap “is there any?” for domain rules).
- **`expand`** — one hop from an already-bound vertex set (pipeline step for agents composing reads without re-stating a full pattern).

Patterns are **structured values**, not an opaque query string. A text dialect (Cypher-like) may later compile *into* these types; the protocol agents call is the structured form so requirements stay decidable ([Protocol](../../../../../modules/knowledge/protocol/protocol.md#abstract)).

### Inherited graph languages are references, not authority
GQL, openCypher, SQL/PGQ, Gremlin and kin are **evidence of repeated needs**, not models to copy wholesale ([Resist defaults that arrive with the medium](../../../../../docs/cognition.md#resist-defaults-that-arrive-with-the-medium)). Weaknesses to keep out of Memar’s protocol:

| Inherited habit | Why it fails Memar | What we do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Query as an opaque string (`GRAPH.QUERY`, Cypher blob) | A protocol whose requirements cannot be decided against a stated input is not a Protocol | Structured `Graph_Pattern` / path request types |
| One storage shape for every degree | Forces “millions of followers” into one mechanism | Consumer-chosen **layouts** (compact vs per-edge) |
| `DELETE` / `DETACH DELETE` as normal | Memory contract has **no erase** | `end_edge` / replace of current adjacency view |
| Graph DML mixed with arbitrary analytics in one language | Behavioural complexity; unclear ownership | Graph owns relatedness + match/path; shortcuts/analytics stay with their owners |
| SQL/PGQ graph as a thin view over arbitrary tables | Couples relatedness to relational packaging | Graph lays out over the **memory contract** (and key_value when needed) |
| Treating “everything connected” as graph | Inflates write/read cost | [When not to use a graph layout](#when-not-to-use-a-graph-layout) (time-series warning) |

Inspire adjacency and match **operations** from those systems; **critique** their packaging before adopting a name or syntax.

Derived / shortcut edges remain caches owned by the module that gives them meaning ([Relationship-distance queries](#relationship-distance-queries-traversal-vs-precomputed-edges); [Modules → Edge buckets](../../../../../modules/modules.md#edge-buckets)); their **upkeep** may call `match`/`path` here, but the cache’s meaning stays with that owner.

Adapters (files or other media) map addresses and values to their medium **after** these services are stable; they do not interpret relation vocabulary.
