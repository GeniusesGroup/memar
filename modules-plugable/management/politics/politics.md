# Politics
Politics is who gets what, when, and how, when people with different interests make one choice together.

## Abstract
Harold Lasswell's sentence, as [OpenStax](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-political-science/pages/1-1-defining-politics-who-gets-what-when-where-how-and-why) uses it, places this activity in a family deciding where to live and in a government deciding a tax. Michael Laver's line, as [Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics) quotes it, is the boundary: pure conflict is war, pure cooperation is love, and politics is the mixture. ## Explanation

### Definition
**Politics** is the mixture of conflict and cooperation through which people decide who gets what, when, and how.

OpenStax states the condition under which that activity has existed: scarcity, different beliefs and preferences, and the need to resolve the difference while allocating what is scarce. On that condition, more than one claim can be sound. The mixture can add a later share — who gets it, and when — so the people whose interests differ still join. It is not limited to picking one of the claims that were named first.

The same chapter separates a game whose rules are fixed and known to every player from this activity. In chess each piece moves in known ways, and the rules do not change during the game. In politics the people making the choice can change the rules while the choice is underway. A contest that keeps a fixed scoring rule stays that contest. The same people are in politics when the stake is scarce, their interests differ, and the rules of the share are themselves part of the choice.

A friendship, each week, has one scarce turn as host. A larger home, a better cook, and a richer host are each a sound claim on that turn. Weighing what each friend gives and receives is how the group decides who gets the turn.

Two villages each gain if a new road passes beside that village. A shorter road on a better slope can be the better line. The mixture adds a spur to each village in a later phase, so the landowners of both villages join. Who gets the line, who gets a spur, and when, is the choice.

[David Easton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Easton) names a narrower sentence: the authoritative allocation of values for a society. This page uses Lasswell's sentence for the activity at every scale the OpenStax chapter already gives. Whether a friendship's choice is "authoritative" in Easton's sense is open on the handoff.

OpenStax states that the discipline of political science focuses primarily on these interactions as they involve governments. This page keeps the activity. The apparatus of a government is a different module.
