## Findings

### The activity is wider than the government, and the discipline concentrates on government
OpenStax, using Harold Lasswell, defines politics as "who gets what, when, how" and states that politics "exists wherever people interact with one another to make decisions that affect them collectively." The same section gives a family decision — parents deciding where the family will live — as politics, and then says political science "focuses primarily on these interactions as they involve governments." [Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics) opens by calling politics the settling of affairs in an organized society, "usually" through a government, and in the next paragraphs places the same activity in clans, companies, institutions, and states. Adrian Leftwich, as that page reports him, separates an extensive view (politics across human social relations) from a limited view (politics as governance). The slogan "the personal is political" is the extensive view's rejection of a private/public wall. (OpenStax; Wikipedia, Politics)

The owner's examples sit in the extensive view. A weekly host is who gets the house, when, and how. A road is who gets the alignment, when the spur is built, and how the landowners join. The university field described on [Political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) studies systems of governance, power, political behavior, constitutions, and laws, and Munroe Smith in 1886 called it "the science of the state." That is the limited view. This module keeps Lasswell's activity at every scale OpenStax already names, and leaves the state's apparatus to its own module. (Grok, from those pages)

### Conflict and cooperation together, not either alone
[Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics) reports Elmer Schattschneider: "at the root of all politics is the universal language of conflict." The same section quotes Michael Laver: "Politics is about the characteristic blend of conflict and co-operation that can be found so often in human interactions. Pure conflict is war. Pure co-operation is true love. Politics is a mixture of both." The road case is that mixture. Each village's interest conflicts with the other's. The shorter line on a better slope, plus a later spur to each village, is the cooperation built on that conflict. Pure conflict would be forcing one village's line. A case with no conflict of interest is Laver's other pole, not this science. (Wikipedia, Politics; Grok, applied to the owner's road)

### Easton's sentence is the narrower one
David Easton's widely used definition, as [his Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Easton) and the Politics page both state it, is the authoritative allocation of values for a society. OpenStax's family example needs no office. Easton's word "authoritative" may require one. This research does not stretch his sentence to cover the weekly host. Lasswell's sentence already covers it. (Grok)

### Scarcity and several sound answers
OpenStax's section opens: politics has existed as long as humans have faced scarcity, different beliefs and preferences, and have had to resolve those differences while allocating scarce resources. One host each week is a scarce turn. A larger home, a better cook, and a richer host can each be a sound claim on that scarce turn. The weighing is who gets it, when, and how. (OpenStax; the owner's host example)

### The discipline describes and also recommends
[Political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) says the field emphasizes description and explanation of what is, and favors empirical evidence over ethical judgment, and in the same article says some political scientists advance normative theses and policy recommendations. This page's sentence is normative: a better answer the group can carry. The sources do not decide whether this module is that craft, the descriptive study, or both. (Wikipedia, Political science)

## Conclusion
The statement that the sources support, and that [politics.md](politics.md) now carries, is Lasswell's: politics is who gets what, when, and how, wherever people with different interests make one choice together, including in a family. Laver's mixture of conflict and cooperation is the boundary. The academic discipline concentrates on governments. Easton's authoritative allocation, the positive/normative split, scarcity without a stake, and the meaning of "better" under social-choice impossibility stay open and are not written as settled.
