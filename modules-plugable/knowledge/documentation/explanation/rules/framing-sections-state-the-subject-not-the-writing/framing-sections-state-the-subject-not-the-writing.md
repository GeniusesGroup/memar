# Explanation Rule — Framing Sections State the Subject, Not the Writing
A rule of this repository, held for [Explanation](../../explanation.md), which owns the claim in [Relevance discipline](../../explanation.md#relevance-discipline). This rule states the check a reviewer applies to the Abstract, the Motivation and the Methodology of a document before it is left.

## The rule
Each sentence of the three openings changes the reader's understanding of the subject, or it is dropped or moved to a content of the task.
- **The Abstract** states the claim, not how the claim was reached.
- **The Motivation** names the failure mode that makes the document necessary, not who noticed it or how the writing unfolded.
- **The Methodology** states a method the reader must hold to understand or verify the claims, or the section is omitted.
- **What is dropped** is session dates, who argued or reviewed, which drafting pass changed what, "see the changelog for the alternatives rejected", and every other sentence about the document's production.

## Why it exists
Agents repeatedly filled these three openings with what only an auditor needs. It teaches a later reader nothing about the subject, and the reader of the current state pays for it in every reading.

## Keep it or drop it
An organization may let the openings tell how the document came to be. It then has two readers served by one text and the current-state reader served worse.

## What this rule does not claim
- It does not say where the dropped material goes beyond naming a content of the task: the outcome is a `changelog` content, a rejected alternative the Options of a `decision` content.
- It does not make the Methodology mandatory; a document with nothing methodologically distinctive has none.
