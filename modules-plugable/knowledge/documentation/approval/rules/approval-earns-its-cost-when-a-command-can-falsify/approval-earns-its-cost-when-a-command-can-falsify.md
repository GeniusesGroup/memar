# Approval Rule — An Approval Earns Its Cost When a Command Can Falsify the Claim
A rule of this repository, held for [Approval](../../approval.md), which owns the claim in [Approval Requires a Reviewer Who Did Not Do the Work](../../approval.md#approval-requires-a-reviewer-who-did-not-do-the-work). This rule states the check a task applies before it asks for an approval.

## The rule
- **Ask for an approval when a command can show the claim false if it were false.** An independent verdict earns what it costs there, because the reviewer's finding is then a command's result and not a reading.
- **A claim that only reading decides** (a wording fix, a broken link, a rename) can still be approved on a verdict that rests on reading, but it rarely earns the second party: the task's own check of it is what decides whether it is right.
- **When it is unclear which of the two it is, ask the reviewer.** That question is one of the things a reviewer is for.

## Why it exists
A reviewer is a second cognitive system, and its start-up costs the task's context and the owner's budget. Spent on a claim the task could check with one command of its own, the cost buys nothing the command does not.

## Keep it or drop it
An organization may ask for an approval of every content. It then pays the second party's cost for claims that reading settles, and the approvals it collects say no more than the task's own check.

## What this rule does not claim
- It does not forbid an approval of a claim that reading decides; it says such an approval rarely earns its cost.
- It does not say who the reviewer is; [A reviewer has not seen the work](../../../../../management/task/rules/reviewer-is-a-fresh-sub-agent/reviewer-is-a-fresh-sub-agent.md) does.
