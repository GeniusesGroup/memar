# Documentation Rule — A Rejected Position Leaves the Body
A rule of this repository, held for [Documentation](../../documentation.md), which owns the claim in [Written surface](../../documentation.md#written-surface), where the body affirms what holds. This rule states the check a reviewer applies to a body and the three steps taken when the owner rejects a position.

## The rule
- **The body states what holds.** A sentence whose job is to warn the reader off a formulation the edit replaced ("revised", "formerly", "retired", "is not the rule") is removed, whether or not a prior snapshot exists. A snapshot changes whether the old text can be opened; it does not license leaving the removed formulation in the body as a negation.
- **A position the owner has rejected is not a record,** and the work it generates is three steps:
  1. Delete the entry, section or sentence whose claim is that position. Do not annotate it, hedge it, or keep it as history.
  2. Give the alternative that was examined and not taken one home: the Options of a `decision` content, phrased as that alternative and why it was not taken ([Handoff → The kinds](../../handoff/handoff.md#the-kinds)).
  3. State the decision affirmatively in the document that holds the claim. The changelog content records the outcome of the change, never the position it replaced.

## Why it exists
A reader who loads the body without the task's contents, an AI-assisted reader above all, cannot tell a negated position from an affirmed one, and treats the negation as a live claim. A rejected position kept as history is read as a position still open.

## Keep it or drop it
An organization may keep rejected positions in the body, marked as rejected. It then makes every reader of the current state read the past as well, and accepts that some will take it for the present.

## What this rule does not claim
- It does not remove the alternative's reasons; they live in the `decision` content, where the audit reader looks.
- It does not forbid a body sentence that is tentative about a specific claim; a claim that is genuinely tentative says so in its own statement ([Explanation → Relevance discipline](../../explanation/explanation.md#relevance-discipline)).
