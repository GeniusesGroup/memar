# Text Likeness
## In one sentence
Keep a short weighted [label](/modules/knowledge/label/label.md) signature per held text, then rank **holders** by likeness without reading every text again.
## Ask / answer
| You give | You get |
| --- | --- |
| Scope, a query text or holder, likeness store, holders to exclude, limit, minimum likeness | Ordered `Likeness_Hits` (holder + `Text_Likeness`) |
`Text_To_Labels` → sign → keep updates the store when `Text_Place` response faces fire; `Likeness_Ranker` / `Likeness_Rank` read postings of the query’s labels only. Not retrieval (no Type_Identifier field preds, no full inverted index score service).
## Abstract
Likeness is what this pluggable module keeps so that a text can be ranked against every other text of a kind without reading them. For each text it keeps a short signature of its most telling [labels](/modules/knowledge/label/label.md) (a label is a word or phrase), updated when writers' services dispatch their own request/response faces (not parallel Event DTOs), never rebuilt for a call. A likeness is the cosine of two signatures, a ratio between 0 and 1 that means the same in every call, declared as `Text_Likeness`. Labels cover any script, Persian among them. The abstraction is declared in Khayyam under the module [`protocol/`](protocol/) only for shared values and the store port (`Text_Likeness`, `Likeness_Weight`, `Likeness_Signature`, `Likeness_Counts`, `Likeness_Store`; label bags imported from Label). Each service's and widget's own `protocol/` holds that owner's surface — Hit/Hits under rank, source under renewer, entry writes under keep (`Likeness_Entries`) ([Protocol folder → Placement test](../../../../modules/knowledge/protocol/rules/protocol-folder/protocol-folder.md#placement-test-refuse-the-main-folder)). Single-act services use Request/Response/`Do`; Event is a characteristic of those faces ([Modularity → Event as a Module Capability](/modules/knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)). This document says what it is for, why it has the shape it has, and what was measured. It restates none of the declarations.

## Introduction
### Motivation
The owner's statement, recorded in [A task is compared with the others by everything it holds](/modules-plugable/knowledge/text/rules/a-task-is-compared-by-everything-it-holds/a-task-is-compared-by-everything-it-holds.md), asks the text domain to rank, for an organization with millions of tasks, the others that a task is most like, from everything each holds, without a pass over all of them and without a rebuild per call. [Finding words belongs to text](/modules-plugable/knowledge/text/rules/text-owns-search/text-owns-search.md); the task domain hands over what a task holds and understands none of it. The ranking in kernel [`services/index.py`](../../../../modules/knowledge/text/services/index.py) cannot serve this. It ranks the sections of one checkout, builds its index in memory on each call, scores without a bound, and reads words as `[a-z0-9]` only, so a Persian text yields no word at all. The critique of the first design (task 20261030-T092950Z-18468) named the same limits.

### Stepping back
What becomes better is that a maker of a task, human or intelligence, learns of a repeat before making it; the problem belongs to whoever makes work, not to the task domain. The larger question the piece belongs to is how an organization finds what it already holds. The text domain answers it for any kind of text, which is why no declaration here names a task; the documents use tasks as the example.

## Explanation
### What the domain knows
- **A text** has an identity, and the text domain does not know which subjects hold it: the caller answers that through the holders abstraction, and the keeper keeps an entry for each holder when `Text_Place` dispatches a write or remove response. For a removed text the store lists the subjects it kept the text for. It is kept once for each subject that holds it: the entry is the pair of a subject and a text, so a document that many tasks name is an entry of each of them under one text identity. This costs one signature a citer and makes every citer of a common document like every other in that document's words; the alternative, a text of one subject, would leave a document out of all but one of the tasks that name it.
- **A scope** is the population within which words are weighed together, the kind of text. Tasks are one scope. It is the kind the `text_type` edge already gives a text, a label of the label domain, and is declared here as a thing identity until that edge is unblocked; a likeness is only ever asked within one scope. The `tokens` edge's analyzer and token are set aside, not absorbed: they describe the tokens of an index of positions and phrases, which a ranking by signature does not need.
- **A subject** is the thing a text belongs to. A task is a subject and its title, plan, each message and each document it names are its texts. A hit names a subject, because the caller asks for tasks and not for texts.
- **The caller owns relations.** The text domain excludes the subjects it is given and knows no parent, child or sibling.

The text a ranking reads is the plain value of a text, through the view of plain characters (`CharacterRun`) the owner ruled on, so the annotations and the structure of a text ([Text → Structure of a text](../../../../modules/knowledge/text/text.md#structure-of-a-text)) are not words. Where a text is a node of the store, `Text_Place` itself exposes the request and response `EventTarget` faces keepers listen to.

### Words of every script
A word is found in two steps that are services of their own. The first folds the characters: the Unicode compatibility form, one case, the Arabic and Persian variants of one letter written as one (the Arabic yeh and kaf as the Persian ones), diacritics and the stretching mark dropped, digits of any script written as the Latin ones, and the zero-width non-joiner treated as a separator. The second cuts the folded characters into words: a run of letters and digits is a word; in a script that does not separate words by space (Han, kana, Thai, Lao, Khmer, Myanmar) the run becomes overlapping pairs of characters, so no dictionary of any language is needed. Neither step carries a list of stop words: a word every text holds weighs little by its rarity, and a language the project has never met is handled as the first.

The check on one line of Persian, Arabic-script variants, Persian digits and Chinese gave `می`, `خواهم`, `یک`, `ساختار`, `برای`, `رتبه`, `بندی`, `متن`, `ها`, `بسازم`, `کتاب`, `الگوی`, `123` and the pairs `世界`, `界你`, `你好`; the present `WORD` gave none. The two rules about the zero-width non-joiner (`می‌روم` becomes `می` and `روم`, so a text typed with a space and one typed with the joiner meet) and about the length of a word (two characters at least, forty at most) are choices measured nowhere yet.

### What is kept
For each text, a **signature**: at most K words (64 by default) with a weight each, the weights taken to unit length. The weight of a word in a text is `(1 + ln frequency in the text) × rarity`, the rarity being `ln(1 + (n + 0.5) / (c + 0.5))` for n entries of the scope and c of them keeping the word, the text weighed counted among both, so the first text of a scope has a weight and not zero. The K highest weights are kept and the rest are dropped. Kept with the signatures are, per scope, the count of entries, for each word the entries that keep it with the weight (the count of the second is the word's count), and the words each entry keeps, so that retiring an entry lowers exactly what it raised. Counting the words a signature keeps and not every word of the text was measured against counting all of them: the top ten share 0.77 of their members, and a planted passage is found as its own text first in 0.59 of 338 queries with the counts of every word, 0.59 with those of the kept words after one pass and 0.60 after three, so the cheaper count, which the store can keep and undo alone, is chosen. This measure is stricter than the one below (the text itself, not the task, must be first, and other tasks hold copies of the same documents). The signature of a long text is as small as a short one's, so the cost of a text is bounded whatever its length, and the largest text found in the existing tasks (248,190 characters) costs the same to keep as the smallest.

The structure is **derived**: every signature can be made again from the texts, which the text domain holds. It is therefore not a file beside the documents, and a document is never a copy of it. Where it is persisted is the storage domain's, as a record of the scope; losing it costs a renewal of the scope (below), not a loss of any text.

### How it is updated
`Text_Place` exposes request and response `EventTarget` faces; the keeper listens to the response face for WriteText and RemoveText. It folds, cuts and signs the text and hands the signature to the keep service, which puts it in the structure and counts it, or retires the text's earlier signature and puts the new one. A retired signature is marked, not searched out of the lists. `Likeness_Keep` likewise exposes request and response faces: when more than half of a scope's entries are retired, the keep response carries `compaction_due` and Compact removes them; when the scope's text count has doubled since its weights were last derived, the response carries `renewal_due` and the renewer signs every entry of the scope again from the source of texts and marks the scope renewed. No parallel Event DTO is introduced for either signal ([Modularity → Event as a Module Capability](/modules/knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)). This is one pass over a whole scope, and it departs from the wording of the rule that no pass reads every task: it is paid once for each doubling, so its cost for a text is constant, and the departure is the owner's to accept. No call of a ranking does any of this.

### What a likeness is
A likeness is the sum, over the words two signatures share, of the product of their two weights. Both signatures have unit length, so the value lies between 0 (no word shared) and 1 (identical signatures) and does not depend on how long either text is. It does depend on the weights, which depend on the scope's counts when each signature was made. A signature is therefore re-derived whenever the count has doubled, so no kept weight was derived from a scope less than half its present size, and a likeness read in two calls differs only by what the renewal and the changed texts changed.

### How a ranking is asked
The ranking service takes a scope, a query signature, the store, the subjects to exclude, a limit, and the module-relation facets a holding module registers when it keeps a text — module identity, an optional role, and a minimum likeness — and returns subjects with a likeness each (a query signature carries no identity, so a typed text that is not yet kept can be ranked; a title and a text typed together are ranked as two queries and the subject keeps the better of its likenesses, as for a given subject), best first, the subject's likeness being that of its most like text. It reads the postings of the query's words only, so its cost follows the query and the lists it meets, not the number of texts. Two shapes are asked through the ranker widget. The subject's likeness to a subject asked about is the greatest likeness between any of the asked subject's kept signatures and any entry of the other. Because a given text is signed now and a kept one at an older count, the same pair can score a little differently by the shape asked: a **given text**, which is folded, cut and signed first (the typed title and request before a task exists), and a **given subject**, whose kept signatures are used as they are, with nothing folded or cut again. Both exclude the subjects the caller names, the subject asked about among them, and a text that has no word is refused with a named error and is not ranked.

### The services and the widgets
One responsibility each, in [`services/`](services/) and [`widgets/`](widgets/):
- **Text_To_Labels** (kernel Text widget) folds, cuts via Lexer (`Lex_Source` + bound IR-words model), and interns surfaces to **labels**. **Sign** weighs those labels against the scope's counts and keeps the highest. **Keep** puts a signature in the store or retires an entry, and on its response face carries `renewal_due` / `compaction_due` when a scope has doubled or more than half its entries are retired; those two thresholds are the rule it holds. **Rank** reads the lists and scores. **Compact** removes what was retired when keep's response says so.
- **Keeper** listens to `Text_Place` response; **Renewer** listens to keep's response when `renewal_due` and runs the keeper over the source of texts; **Ranker** composes the pipeline and the ranking for the two shapes.
- Shared values and the store port live in [`protocol/`](protocol/); each service/widget owns its Request/Response and one-service surfaces under its own `protocol/` ([Protocol folder → Placement test](../../../../modules/knowledge/protocol/rules/protocol-folder/protocol-folder.md#placement-test-refuse-the-main-folder)); the two errors are in [`errors/`](errors/likeness.kh).

## Measurements
Everything was measured with the Python standard library alone, on the owner's machine, to the abstraction rather than to a realization; a native one is faster and smaller, and these are the upper bounds of the first one. The scripts are in the temp folder of task 20261030-T094102Z-8640, which is not archived; the figures are kept here.

**The figures below were made with the weights derived from the whole scope at once, as a renewal leaves them (a renewal signs against a snapshot of the counts taken when it begins, so the order of the pass does not change them).** Weighing the texts one by one in arrival order, with no renewal, found a planted passage as its own text first in 0.56 of 234 queries against 0.59 with the weights derived at once; by this measure the renewal gains little, and the figure that argues for it is the top-ten agreement of weights derived on a tenth, a quarter and a half of the scope below. The agreement and staleness figures were made over whole tasks as one text each and carry to the unit of the text by analogy; the planted queries are taken only from texts above 2,000 characters; a cap of 5% on the lists was run on the real tasks and its result is not reported. "Upper bounds" and "a native one is faster" are expectations, not measurements.

**The existing tasks** (live folders and the records branch, 2026-10-30): 575 tasks, 2,445 texts, 14.3 million characters, a median text of 2,513 characters, 99 in 100 under 63,368 and the largest 248,190.
- Fold, cut and count: 3.2 million characters a second, 4.5 s for all of them.
- Sign and keep all texts once, with weights derived once: 0.94 s; 138,366 list entries, about 57 a text, in 13,531 lists.
- A typed text of 2,500 characters, folded, cut, signed and ranked against all: 2.6 ms.

**Agreement and recall** (the words of the existing tasks; no set of labelled repeats exists, so the figures say how close the cheap structure stays to the dearest one and whether a planted passage is found):
- Against the cosine of whole vectors with every word, with a typed text as the query, the top ten share 0.69 of their members at K=32, 0.76 at K=64 and 0.79 at K=128; the first is the same in 0.65, 0.74 and 0.78 of the queries. The signature is a loss, taken for a cost bounded by K rather than by length.
- A passage of 500 characters taken from inside one text of a task as the query, 468 such queries: with each text a signature and a task ranked by its best text, the task is first in 0.76 of them, among the first ten in 0.95, mean reciprocal rank 0.82 at K=64; at K=128 0.79, 0.96 and 0.85. With the whole task as one signature it is 0.68, 0.90 and 0.75 at K=64, which is why the unit is the text and not the subject. Cutting each text into windows of 1,500 characters at K=32 gives 0.82, 0.97 and 0.87 for twice the entries; it is not adopted and is the first thing to try if a miss matters. The remaining misses are very probably tasks that hold copies of the same document, which no ranking can tell apart; this was not checked.
- Weights derived when the scope was 10%, 25% and 50% of its present size give top-ten lists that share 0.81, 0.89 and 0.92 of their members with those derived on the present size. This is the reason for the renewal at each doubling: the weights a ranking meets were derived from at least half the present scope, where the agreement is at least 0.92 in this sample.

**A synthetic corpus of 1,000,000 texts** (64 words each, signatures drawn directly: 8 from 200 words that a few in a hundred texts keep, 56 from a long tail; the existing tasks hold about 4.25 texts a task, so this is about 235,000 tasks):

| | 10,000 texts | 100,000 texts | 1,000,000 texts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Put one signature, mean | 491 µs | 389 µs | 240 µs |
| Entries / longest list | 640,000 / 436 | 6.4 million / 4,114 | 64 million / 39,790 |
| Structure held | | | about 1.77 GB |
| Query of 40 words, median, every list read | 1.5 ms | 12.9 ms | 147 ms |
| The same, lists kept by more than 1% of texts skipped | 0.3 ms | 1.2 ms | 13 ms |

A change of one text (retire and put) took a median of 215 µs and 445 µs at the 99th percentile at 1,000,000. Compacting after 20% of the entries were retired took 54 s and peaked at 2.85 GB, the old and the new held together; a realization builds the new while the old answers, and the compaction runs once the retired share passes a half, so it is paid for by the changes before it. A run at 4,250,000 texts, the size of a million tasks, was not made because the machine had 5 GB free; cost per text, entries and memory are linear in the figures above, and the query is linear in the longest list, which the skip bounds.

**What the figures do not show.** The synthetic lists follow an assumed distribution of words, not a measured one at that size; the real distribution is known to 575 tasks. The query time at a million tasks is not measured and is the first thing the first implementation is held to. A skipped word is a word of a query that reads nothing, so the cap is also a loss of recall nobody measured on the real tasks.

## What this does not claim
- That the signature finds what a reader must read, nor that a likeness means a duplicate: it ranks by words, and a paraphrase that shares none is not found. Ranking by meaning is a recorded TODO of `services/index.py`.
- That the thresholds a gate would need exist. A likeness is comparable across calls; where a maker is held is decided after a gate is measured on the existing tasks ([the rule](/modules-plugable/knowledge/text/rules/a-task-is-compared-by-everything-it-holds/a-task-is-compared-by-everything-it-holds.md)).
- That this replaces `memar content_find`. That ranking is of sections of one checkout for an agent; this one is of the texts of a scope, kept. Whether the first should read the second is not decided here.
- That the names are final. `Likeness`, `Scope`, `Subject` and `Signature` are working names; the abstraction is not ratified until a fresh reviewer and the owner approve it ([Abstraction First](../../../../modules/computer/software/rules/abstraction-first/abstraction-first.md)).
