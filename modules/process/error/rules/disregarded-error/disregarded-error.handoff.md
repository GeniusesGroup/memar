# Error Rule — Disregarded Error Handoff
Open state for [disregarded-error.md](./disregarded-error.md). See the repository's [documentation-handoff.md](../../../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/documentation/handoff/handoff.md) for what a handoff is.

## Status
Active — drafted 2026-10-02 in the Khayyam rules catalog, rewritten the same day, relocated on 2026-10-03 to the control-flow module, and relocated again the same day to this module on the owner's ruling that the concern the Rule governs is failure and that `modules/process/error/rules/` is the folder held for it. The first form of this Rule was drafted 2026-10-02 on the owner's ruling that a method whose operation can create an error carries that failure in the method's own variables; the owner has since ruled that this is the wrong Rule, on the ground that narrowing or an obligation must not happen for error rules at all — creativity must not be blocked — and that the only thing to be a Rule is that in a process an error must not be disregarded at all, especially when there is one, and that the rules are to be written logically and definitely.

## Placement
The Rule has moved twice, both times on the criterion at [Rule → Which Module a Rule Belongs To](../../../rule/rule.md#which-module-a-rule-belongs-to): from `bundles/khayyam/rules/disregarded-error/` to `modules/process/control-flow/rules/disregarded-error/` on 2026-10-03, and from there to `modules/process/error/rules/disregarded-error/` the same day. The first move read the subject as execution after a failure and cited [Control Flow → Error Propagation](../../../control-flow/control-flow.md#error-propagation) as the claim, against [The Error](../../error.md), which `:139` of the former says is concerned only with how execution continues once an error has occurred. The second move is the owner's ruling, and it rests on a distinction that first move missed: **what an error is and what a failure's being reported owes its caller are two claims, and the Rule states the second.** [The Error](../../error.md) states at `:130` that an error is a contract addressed to the immediate caller whose sole purpose is to let that caller decide what to do next — a claim about the failure, not about the execution — and at `:88` which class a failure carries. A report that reaches past the point where the failure could have been accounted for empties that contract, so the refusal is a statement about the error and not only about the flow that carries it. The Rule therefore cites both documents, with The Error as the owner of the subject and Control Flow's `:141` and `:143` as the same requirement stated from the execution side.

**The name is unchanged, and it fits its new home better than the old one.** The subject did not change in the move; only the module that owns it did, and the naming rule this repository follows — a name locates a subject and the document then speaks about it, recorded under the withdrawn framings below — keeps `disregarded-error` as the subject's name. In this module the word `error` is also the module's own noun, so the name reads as a condition on what this module is about rather than as a borrowed term from the neighbouring protocol. The names refused earlier are refused here for the same reasons: `Error Not Disregarded` and `Failure Not Disregarded` put the verdict in the name, `Error Propagation` is the name of the neighbouring protocol topic that states part of the claim, and `Error Slot` named the mechanism the withdrawn framing required.

`modules/process/error/` holds no README of its own and this folder's index is new with this move; both were written this session because the folder that owns the Rule now holds it.

## Withdrawn Framing
The earlier Rule was named **Error Slot**, and its requirement was that where an operation can fail, the influenced group carries a variable of an error type and the call reports the failure by writing it, a failure reported by a signal reaching past the call being refused. That framing is superseded in its requirement and withdrawn; the trigger, the refusal, and the protocol anchors are unchanged and are now the Rule's whole content.

- **What it would have foreclosed.** A named slot in every signature is one shape forced on every declaration, so a designer who reports a failure some other legitimate way — through a result type, through a mechanism a second realization's language provides, or through a contract that states the condition in prose beside the declaration — would be writing a correct program and receiving a finding. That is the obstruction the owner named: an obligation here narrows the field before a designer has chosen a design. Its reasoning also named `panic` as the thing to avoid, which a reader could take as a claim about halting as such rather than about reaching past the point where a failure could have been accounted for.
- **What replaced it, and what it deliberately does not decide.** The Rule now requires that a failure be accounted for where the operation can see it, refuses disregarding — proceeding as though it had not occurred, or reporting it by a signal reaching past the point where it could have been handled — and says nothing about how the failure is carried, about halting, about a failure's class, or about an operation that cannot fail. The freedom of how to carry a failure is the point of the Rule and is stated as such. The halting half has its own Rule in the flow-control module, [halt declaration](../../../control-flow/rules/halt-declaration/halt-declaration.md), so that the concern is stated where it is the subject rather than carried here as a refusal.
- **A measurement that was carried as evidence is withdrawn from the Rule.** An earlier form of this Rule reported a scan of every `.kh` method declaration under `modules/` — how many carry an `Error`-typed influenced variable, how many carry none, and what the sources name that variable — and used the distribution to argue that both conformant forms are already in wide use. The Rule no longer states it, and the Rule's own *What this rule does not claim* now says that no declaration or count in this repository's sources is evidence for or against it. The reason is the owner's standing that the code under `modules/**` is residue of an attempt to develop these ideas in Go that failed, and is neither evidence nor a premise; the scan's method and its filters are recorded below as record, and no conclusion anywhere rests on them. What the sources did establish is recorded instead as what it is: a set of findings about the sources, with no claim attached.
- **The names that were refused are recorded here.** `Error Slot` named the mechanism the earlier framing required and no longer required. `Error Not Disregarded` and `Failure Not Disregarded` were refused because they put the Rule's verdict in the name and left the document nothing to say, where every other name in the catalog locates a subject the document then speaks about. `Error Signalling`, `Signalled Failure`, and `Out-of-Band Failure` were refused because each names the mechanism the Rule refuses rather than the subject it governs, which would make the Rule about signalling. `Silent Failure` was refused because "silent" is one target's reading of what a tool reports and not a word this repository defines. `Error Propagation` was refused because it is the name of the protocol topic that owns part of the normative claim, and a Rule must not take the name of the document whose subject it cites. `Failure Accounted` and `Accounted Failure` were refused because "accounted for" reads as a record kept somewhere, which is a requirement about documentation rather than about a process.

## Open Questions
- **Where a fallibility judgment is recorded, if it is.** The trigger is a per-method decision, and nothing in the language, the protocol, or this repository's Rules says where that decision goes. The answers are still the author's judgment alone, a declaration-level annotation the signature carries, or the abstraction's own contract text — and the third has nowhere to live today, since a body-less method on an abstraction has no body. This Rule does not depend on the answer, which is the change from the earlier form: it asks only that the failure be accounted for where the operation can see it, and a method whose operation can fail satisfies that in whatever form its own contract already uses.
- **Whether a linter may check the refusal half, and what it compares against.** The visible case is a body that signals a failure where its own signature states a path for one. What a tool compares is the body's signalling against the contract the signature states, and a tool must say what it exempts: a body that halts where its signature states nothing is not this Rule's finding, because nothing stated that the operation could fail — and whether such a halt is conformant is the [halt declaration Rule](../../../control-flow/rules/halt-declaration/halt-declaration.md)'s question, not this one's.
- **Whether an organization that prefers one form states it as a Rule of its own.** The Rule is silent on the form, which leaves the choice to the organization that writes the code, and the catalog's membership criterion asks that a Rule be executable as a condition over analysis input. A Rule prescribing a form for a narrower case — one Type category, one module, one organization — is therefore defensible where the general Rule is not, and whether any catalog should hold one is the owner's.
- **Whether the protocol's own sentence needs narrowing.** [Control Flow → Error Propagation](../../../control-flow/control-flow.md#error-propagation) at `:141` says the realization "may express it as an output value, a result type, or another explicit mechanism", and this repository's realization uses more than one. Nothing needs an edit, and that was checked rather than assumed; recorded so a later reader knows the question was opened and closed.
