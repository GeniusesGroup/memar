# Synchronization Model

## Abstract
This document states what the implementation module under [`modules/process/sync/`](./) is for, and models it. Its central finding is that the responsibility has **two** guarantees in it and not a third: that an effect is **atomic** — no other activity observes it part-way — and that one activity's completed effects become another activity's next premise in a stated order, which this document calls **sequencing**. It refuses exclusion as a third concept on the repository's own ground, that locking is "one possible mechanism for satisfying a concurrency constraint; it is not the definition of concurrency and should not become its default representation" ([Process → Concurrency](../process.md#concurrency) at `:200`), which leaves the two lock declarations the folder already holds outside the module's stated responsibility and records them as open state rather than deleting them. It places the indivisibility of a ring's release on **a method of the buffer module** that obtains this module's guarantees, as the owner ruled, and records what the owner's ruling does not reach: **atomicity alone does not make a release correct**, because a freed portion is only safe to refill once everything written before the release is observable to the other party, and no document in this repository states that half at all. Nothing here is implementable today as a guarantee. What a Khayyam source can write is a body-less method on a capsule whose implementation arrives from outside, and the one protocol document whose abstract claims to state "the synchronization and signaling primitives it exposes" ([Concurrency Realization](../../computer/concurrency/concurrency.md) at `:11`) names no primitive in its body. The normative claim this module's Rules would cite therefore has no home document, which is why the module carries no `rules/` folder. Eight questions are open and carried in the paired [handoff](./sync.handoff.md), each with what it blocks and what would close it.

## Introduction

### Motivation
The owner asked who carries atomicity in a design where a network driver and an application share one ring buffer, and answered that it is "a method of the buffer module, using a module similar to Go's `sync`" — and that the synchronisation module itself may be generated. Two things then had to be established before anything could be built, and neither had been. First, what a synchronisation module in this language can honestly promise, given that the language has no primitive types, no literal, and no construction form, that there is no concurrency in the grammar, and that the memory protocol calls data races relaxable governance. Second, what atomicity means for the buffer design specifically — a head or tail that changes atomically, so a processed region is freed and the other party can refill it — which is narrower than "concurrency" and turns out to require more than the owner's word alone carries.

A folder already existed at [`modules/process/sync/`](./) holding three declarations and no document, which [`modules/README.md:12`](../../README.md) makes a folder whose intent is carried by its name until one is written. Reading those three declarations first was necessary: it establishes that the folder's intent is synchronisation primitives, and that the intent was reached for through a lock rather than through indivisibility — a fact about the repository's history rather than about the responsibility, per the residue rule the Error module states in its [README](../error/README.md) and that every other module in this repository states as well.

### Methodology
The candidate concepts were taken from the owner's own word, from the two guarantees the language and protocol documents actually state or omit, and from the three declarations the folder holds; each was then run against the challenge questions in [Modeling Practice](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#challenging-a-proposed-concept). Node-or-edge standing was decided by the audit in the same practice, and every surviving candidate was searched against the whole documentation set for a concept that already does its job under another name — `Mutex`, `WaitGroup`, `happen-before`, and `strong consistency` each return nothing, which is a fact about what those queries reached and not a finding about the project's silence ([Cognition → Report absence](../../../docs/cognition.md#report-absence-rather-than-supplying-it)). Three standing rules shape what follows. **A claim is recorded only where a document states it**, with the section and the line. **Nothing here designs an implementation**; where a declaration is named, what it must mean is stated and not how it is written ([Modeling → Modeling Produces Conceptual Abstractions, Not Implementation Structures](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-produces-conceptual-abstractions-not-implementation-structures)). **The code under `modules/**` is residue** of an attempt to develop these ideas in another language that did not succeed, which the owner has ruled neither evidence nor a premise; what those files contain is described, and no conclusion rests on it.

## Explanation

### What this module is for
**Synchronization is the responsibility by which two activities that may progress at the same time are given a dependable statement of when each one's effect on state they both touch has taken effect.** That is the whole of its reach, and the reach is narrow for the same reason Construction's is narrow ([Construction Model → What construction is for](../construction/construction.md#what-construction-is-for)): the language cannot express the arrangement, so what a source may do is declare it and the execution arrives from elsewhere. The responsibility is a *guarantee one party may rely on*, never a decision about whether one is needed — that decision belongs to [Process → Concurrency](../process.md#concurrency), whose chain of questions runs from shared state down to "is locking one possible mechanism?" (`:204`–`:221`) and which [Agency → Agency Before Synchronization](../../../docs/agency.md#agency-before-synchronization) at `:584`–`:602` requires to be walked before any mechanism is reached for.

**Membership criterion.** A declaration belongs under this module when its subject is *what one activity may rely on about the completion, and about the order of arrival, of another activity's effect on state both touch* — nothing coarser and nothing finer. Each of the four neighbours fails that test on its own subject rather than on its subject's wording:

- **Which state a party may touch at all** is admission, and admission is a term on the crossing that states it, which no document owns today ([Binding Model → The relations](../../computer/binding/binding.md#the-relations)) — a different relation, not a guarantee about an effect.
- **When each activity runs** is the substrate's and the framework's, per [Agency in Khayyam → Concurrency Decisions as Agency, Not Syntax](../../../docs/khayyam/agency.md#concurrency-decisions-as-agency-not-syntax) at `:39`: "the scheduling of that work … is the framework's and the runtime's contract, one layer above the language".
- **What the state means** belongs to the type that owns it, and its rules are [Modeling → Constraints Belong to the Constraining Concern](../../../docs/modeling.md#constraints-belong-to-the-constraining-concern)'s subject rather than this module's.
- **Whether a race occurred** is a detector's, and [`modules/process/race/`](../race/) holds exactly that — `Detector_Static` at [`protocol/detector.kh:14`](../race/protocol/detector.kh), and a `Race_Event` carrying a thread identifier and a stack ([`protocol/event.kh:9`](../race/protocol/event.kh)–`:10`). Detection observes what happened; this module supplies what was guaranteed. A folder holding both would hold a guarantee and an audit of it, which is two responsibilities under one name.

**What would have to be true for someone to split it.** The two guarantees this module owns answer one question — *when has that other activity's effect taken effect?* — so today they are one responsibility. The module splits the moment either of two things becomes true. Either one half acquires a lifecycle of its own that the other does not share — for instance if a ruling requires exclusion to be stated as a guarantee in its own right, with its own failure cases (a holder that never releases, a holder that acquires twice, a holder that is abandoned), which is a contract this module's two guarantees have no term for. Or the indivisibility of one effect turns out to need a mechanism whose own subject is not a guarantee — which the two lock declarations the folder already holds point at directly, since [`protocol/locker.kh:6`](./protocol/locker.kh) records that the requesting thread which calls `Lock()` "CAN `yield`", a statement about the substrate's suspension rather than about any effect's completion. Under [`modules/README.md:11`](../../README.md) — "no folder here is final. Names may be merged, split, moved, or retired whenever the responsibility they name is better served another way" — that split would produce a peer folder beside this one and not an extension of this one.

### The concepts

#### Atomic Operation
An effect on shared state that no other activity observes part-way: either it has not begun, or it has completed, and no activity can be placed between the two.

This is the concept the owner's own word names, and it is not owned anywhere in this repository's documents. What exists instead is a *mention*: [Khayyam → Syntactic Atomicity and Semantic Clarity](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#syntactic-atomicity-and-semantic-clarity) at `:234`–`:235` uses the word of grammar — "each syntactic construct should correspond to a single semantic intent" — so a reader following the owner's word into the language documents lands on a principle about syntax rather than about execution. That collision is recorded here so that neither document is read as contradicting the other, and the naming question it raises is carried in the paired [handoff](./sync.handoff.md).

The boundary of the concept is drawn by the difference between an effect being indivisible and an effect being *arrived at*. The residue's own vocabulary shows the two being confused: [`modules/computer/adt/hash_table/protocol/atomic.kh:7`](../../computer/adt/hash_table/protocol/atomic.kh) declares `HashTable_AtomicAccessor ab` with `Load` at `:8`, `Store` at `:10`, `Swap` at `:12` and `CompareAndSwap` at `:15`, all body-less and unimplemented, and the same sources raises a shareability flag whose comment names what supplies the guarantee — "it use some sync mechanism like atomic operation to let other access" ([`modules/computer/function/protocol/access.kh:47`](../../computer/function/protocol/access.kh)–`:50`). Both are records of what the residue attempted; neither states a contract.

**What it owns.** The claim that the effect admits no intermediate observation, and the terms that say which effects are indivisible and what each one indivisible step covers. **What it does not own.** How the indivisibility is realized; whether anything checks it; what the effect was applied to; who may apply it.

**Node.** It has an independent lifecycle — the set of indivisible operations can grow, shrink, or be realized differently without any structure that uses one changing — and it enforces a rule nothing else in this repository owns: that an effect claimed to be indivisible is indivisible with respect to *every* other activity, not only with respect to those using the same realization. **Collides with** nothing this repository declares, and **is not declared** by any `.kh` file; the nearest declaration is the residue's, and it is scoped to a hash table's accessor rather than to the guarantee.

#### Sequencing
The order in which one activity's completed effects become another activity's next premise: once activity A has completed a step, activity B — having observed that step — reads A's earlier writes as having happened.

This is the concept the owner's word does not name and the ring cannot do without, and it is the sharpest finding of this session. **An atomic head-or-tail update is not sufficient to free a region.** The application's advance of the head says the portion is free; what makes refilling it safe is that the application cannot advance the head before its own writes to the portion have completed, and the driver cannot observe the advanced head before those writes are visible to it. A store that is indivisible but unordered delivers the second without the first, and the driver reads a head that has moved over bytes the application had not finished writing. So indivisibility and order are not two ways of saying one thing, and a module supplying only the first would supply a guarantee its sharpest consumer cannot use.

Nothing in this repository states it. There is no memory-ordering document, no happens-before relation, and no rule for the arrival of writes: `happen-before`, `memory order`, and `strong consistency` each return nothing from this repository's documentation on the queries run, and the third is notable because the residue asserted it — [`locker.kh:5`](./protocol/locker.kh) reads "Lock use to reach strict consistency" — in a comment no document supports. The nearest statements are two obligations that *require* the term to be written without supplying it: [Memory → Copy and ownership semantics are contract members](../../computer/memory/memory.md#copy-and-ownership-semantics-are-contract-members) at `:100` requires that how long a returned view remains valid be stated "because it is exactly the term callers otherwise assume to be the opposite", and at `:102` requires that "every buffer-reuse, pooling, or zero-copy optimization they perform must be paired with an explicit, documented contract term". A shared ring is a zero-copy optimization in that document's own vocabulary, so the obligation is already in force and the term has nowhere to live.

**What it owns.** The claim that an observed step implies the writes that preceded it, and the terms that name which side establishes the order and which side relies on it. **What it does not own.** Whether the order is stronger than one party's reliance requires — nothing states whether ordering is guaranteed absolutely or only between the parties that share the guarantee; what a party may conclude about a *different* party's writes when the two did not synchronise; how the order is realized on a substrate.

**Node.** It has an independent lifecycle and an independent rule — that a step whose completion another party relies on must be ordered before that party's read of it — and removing it leaves [Atomic Operation](#atomic-operation) with a claim the ring cannot use, which is the test [Modeling Practice → Challenging a Proposed Concept](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#challenging-a-proposed-concept) applies. **Collides with** `Ordering` as [Process → Ordering](../process.md#ordering) uses it, which is a relation among activities' occurrences and not a relation between an effect and its observation; the collision is recorded rather than resolved, and `Visibility` is refused for the same adjacency, since this repository already uses that word for name scope ([Encapsulation → Sovereign Encapsulation](../../../docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md#sovereign-encapsulation) at `:71`) and for behavioural visibility ([Khayyam → Self-Documenting Code and Naming](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#self-documenting-code-and-naming)).

#### Mutual Exclusion
Not a concept of this module, and refused on the repository's own ground rather than on this model's preference.

[Process → Concurrency](../process.md#concurrency) at `:200` states that "Locking is one possible mechanism for satisfying a concurrency constraint; it is not the definition of concurrency and should not become its default representation", and at `:220` places "is locking one possible mechanism?" at the end of a chain whose every earlier step may resolve the concern without it; `:232` records that pinning a bounded part of a process to one fixed Worker "can resolve a concurrency hazard without a lock". [Agency → Agency Before Synchronization](../../../docs/agency.md#agency-before-synchronization) at `:602` states what the order is for: it "prevents a lock, queue, mailbox, scheduler, or transactional mechanism from silently becoming the model of responsibility itself", and at `:604` that "A Single Writer arrangement, an Actor-style mailbox, or a Worker assigned to a partition can each be useful realizations of this principle. None should be mistaken for the principle itself." Exclusion is therefore a *mechanism this module's guarantees can be obtained through*, not a guarantee this module owns — the same standing [Binding Model → Adaptor, Wrapper, Shim, Marshal, Bridge](../../computer/binding/binding.md#adaptor-wrapper-shim-marshal-bridge) gives a name that names a mechanism.

**Node.** Refused: an equivalent concept exists under a name that covers the guarantee, and a module that owns the guarantee and the mechanism has two responsibilities under one name, which [Modeling Practice → Single Responsibility Check](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#single-responsibility-check) resolves by decomposition.

#### Concurrency
Not a concept of this module, and already owned. [Concurrency Realization](../../computer/concurrency/concurrency.md) at `:11` names its own boundary in the first sentence of its abstract: "The *concept* — concurrency as a property of how a process's activities progress relative to each other, the escalating decision chain before any synchronization, the Worker/core distinction — is defined in [Process → Concurrency](../process.md#concurrency) and is not repeated here."

**Node.** Refused: an equivalent concept exists, is defined elsewhere, and this module's reach begins after that concept's decision chain has been walked.

#### Shared State
Not a concept of this module, and refused because it is the occasion rather than the responsibility. It is data and a condition, not a concern: it holds no rule of its own, and the question a reader actually has about it — *who may touch it, and when* — is answered by admission and by scheduling, both named above as exclusions. Keeping it as a node here would be [Modeling → Common Modeling Anti-Patterns](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#common-modeling-anti-patterns)'s *data as concept* in its plain form, and would give the module a second subject.

**Node.** Refused: it is the occasion a guarantee is stated against, not a responsibility that states one.

### What each concept owns, and what it does not own
**Atomic Operation** owns the claim that an effect admits no intermediate observation, and the terms naming which effects are indivisible and what one indivisible step covers. It does not own how the indivisibility is realized, whether anything checks it, what the effect was applied to, or who may apply it.

**Sequencing** owns the claim that an observed step implies the writes that preceded it, and the terms naming which side establishes the order and which relies on it. It does not own whether the order is absolute or holds only between parties that share the guarantee, what a party may conclude about a party it did not synchronise with, or how the order is realized.

Three concerns a reader will expect here are owned elsewhere and this module does not absorb them: **whether synchronisation is needed at all**, which is [Process → Concurrency](../process.md#concurrency)'s chain and [Agency](../../../docs/agency.md#agency-before-synchronization)'s order; **whether a race happened**, which is [`modules/process/race/`](../race/)'s; and **whether a value crossing to another execution unit must be copied**, which is [Memory → The boundary copy convention, stated](../../computer/memory/memory.md#the-boundary-copy-convention-stated) at `:105` and which a shared ring deviates from rather than satisfies.

### The relations
**An Atomic Operation establishes nothing about order on its own; an ordered step is both.** This is the relation the ring turns on and the one this module exists to state. Indivisibility says no observer sees a partial effect; ordering says an observer that has seen the step also sees what preceded it. A step may have the first without the second, and the ring's release needs both — a head advanced indivisibly but unordered lets the driver refill bytes the application was still writing.

**A guarantee is stated by one party and relied on by another, and neither may state it for the other.** The direction runs from the establishing side to the relying side because the order is established once and read many times. This is the same shape as the [Binding Model → What a binding is](../../computer/binding/binding.md#what-a-binding-is) test — *a term is worth stating only if one party can check it without the other's cooperation* — and by that test **no guarantee in this module passes today**: indivisibility and order are properties of a realization, not of anything a party declares, so the only evidence available is that an implementation arrived.

**Sequencing is established by the party that completes a step and observed by the party that depends on it; the two are ends of one relation and not two concepts.** Naming them as a pair would produce a node whose name uses "and", which is the tell [Modeling Practice → Single Responsibility Check](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#single-responsibility-check) gives for a mixed node. The residue did name them as a pair — `Access_SafeToSend` and `Access_SafeToShare` ([`access.kh:45`](../../computer/function/protocol/access.kh) and `:50`) — as two flags on one `uint32`, which is a record of an attempt and not a reason.

**Exclusion may deliver either guarantee, and no guarantee requires it.** The relation runs from mechanism to guarantee, one way: a critical section delivers ordering to its holders by construction, and a lock-free algorithm delivers ordering by an explicit ordered step instead. This relation is why exclusion is refused as a concept rather than merely deprioritised.

**A guarantee that nobody relies on is not this module's business, and this module does not decide who relies on what.** That is admission's, carried as an open question at [Binding Model → Open questions](../../computer/binding/binding.md#open-questions) rather than restated here.

### The names to use and the names to refuse
**Use `Synchronization`** for the responsibility, and **`sync`** for the folder and as the qualifier on this module's own declarations, which is what the folder already carries. [Documentation → File Naming](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#file-naming) states that the human-readable form of a document's name is its front-matter `Title`, "which is the slug's source", so this document's `Title: "Synchronization Model"` is where the full word lives while its address stays `sync.md` beside a folder named `sync`.

**Use `Atomic Operation`** for one indivisible effect, and **`Sequencing`** for the order in which completed effects become another's premise. `Atomic` is the owner's word for the first and needs no defence; it is the *word*, and not the concept, that collides with [Khayyam's syntactic use](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#syntactic-atomicity-and-semantic-clarity).

**Refuse `Mutex`, `RWMutex`, `WaitGroup`, `Once`, and `atomic` as names here.** None is a Memar term, all five are one ecosystem's spellings of mechanisms, and none states a guarantee: a mutex is exclusion, a wait group is a counter with a completion condition, a once is a one-shot idempotence guard, and none of the four is indivisibility or order. `Mutex` and `WaitGroup` return nothing from this repository's documentation, which is why no name here collides with a declared one; [Terminology → The Default Meaning of an Unreferenced Term](../../../docs/terminology.md#the-default-meaning-of-an-unreferenced-term) gives an unreferenced word "exactly its general meaning", and for all four that general meaning is Go's.

**Refuse `Lock`, `Locker`, and `ReadLocker` as this module's concepts**, while leaving the two declarations that carry them where they are. The refusal is [Process → Concurrency](../process.md#concurrency)'s, quoted above; the declarations are residue, and this session neither edits nor deletes them.

**Refuse `Concurrency` as this module's name**, on [Concurrency Realization](../../computer/concurrency/concurrency.md)'s own statement at `:11` that the concept belongs to Process.

**Refuse `Visibility`, `Publication`, and `Memory Order`.** `Visibility` is already two other things in this repository — name scope at [`encapsulation.md:71`](../../../docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md#sovereign-encapsulation) and behavioural visibility at [`khayyam.md:185`](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#self-documenting-code-and-naming). `Publication` is the research-publication sense at [`protocol.md:65`](../../../docs/protocol.md) and [`protocol.md:143`](../../../docs/protocol.md). `Memory Order` is one ecosystem's term for a strength this repository has never ruled on, and adopting the word would import the question of strength as though it were answered.

**Refuse `Strict Consistency`, `Sequentially Consistent`, and `Linearizable`.** The residue asserts the first in a comment ([`locker.kh:5`](./protocol/locker.kh)) and no document in this repository defines any of the three; each names a strength, and no strength has been ruled on for anything in this repository.

**Refuse `Race`** as this module's subject. The repository already owns it: [`modules/process/race/`](../race/) holds detectors, a report event, and a sanitizer, and [Memory → Safety enforcement is governance](../../computer/memory/memory.md#safety-enforcement-is-governance) at `:85` places data races among checks an organization may relax. This module supplies the guarantee those checks look for.

### What an atomic head-or-tail advance needs from this module
The owner's requirement is exact: when a packet has been read and processed, the application must **atomically** change the ring's head or tail so the portion is free for the driver to fill. The owner has ruled where that lives: **a method of the buffer module**, using this module. Worked out, such a method needs five things, and only the first is what the owner's word carries.

1. **A cursor the buffer owns.** The advance is a change to the ring's own state, so the method belongs to the buffer module and not to the abstraction that describes atomicity generally — [Modeling → Constraints Belong to the Constraining Concern](../../../docs/modeling.md#constraints-belong-to-the-constraining-concern) puts the rule about when a portion is free with the concern that constrains, which is the ring. The sources' nearest is [`modules/computer/buffer/protocol/index.kh:2`](../../computer/buffer/protocol/index.kh), `tp Buffer_Index ab`, an empty abstraction composed by nothing; `Computer_Buffer ab` at [`buffer.kh:22`](../../computer/buffer/protocol/buffer.kh) composes `Observer_Buffer_Indexes` and `Mutator_Indexes` and no cursor.
2. **Indivisibility of the advance.** One indivisible step, not a read of the head, an increment, and a write — the three-step form is precisely what another activity may interleave with, and it is why `Load` and `Store` as two separate operations ([`atomic.kh:8`](../../computer/adt/hash_table/protocol/atomic.kh) and `:10`) do not compose into an advance without a further guarantee.
3. **Order, which the advance does not carry by being atomic.** Everything the application wrote into the portion must be visible to the driver before the driver observes the advanced head. This is the half the owner's word does not carry and the half the ring cannot do without, per [Sequencing](#sequencing).
4. **The rule about what the advance means, stated as a term.** How long a portion is valid, what the driver may assume about the region between the two cursors, and what each side may assume the other does with it. [Memory → Copy and ownership semantics are contract members](../../computer/memory/memory.md#copy-and-ownership-semantics-are-contract-members) at `:100`–`:102` obliges the term and no document owns it; [Binding Model → Open questions](../../computer/binding/binding.md#open-questions) question 24 carries that.
5. **A statement that the guarantee is relaxable.** [Memory → Safety enforcement is governance](../../computer/memory/memory.md#safety-enforcement-is-governance) at `:85` places data races among checks "relaxable by configuration", so "atomically" is a property of an organization's posture and not of the design. An agreement may therefore state the requirement and never guarantee it — which is the [Binding Model](../../computer/binding/binding.md#what-a-binding-is)'s own checkability finding arriving from the other direction.

The module supplies points 2 and 3 as guarantees the method obtains, and **owns none of points 1, 4, and 5**: the cursor and its meaning are the buffer's, the contract terms are a crossing's, and the posture is an organization's.

### What the documents already state
Seven things are stated, and each is usable without extending the grammar.

1. **The language embeds no concurrency model.** [Agency in Khayyam → Concurrency Decisions as Agency, Not Syntax](../../../docs/khayyam/agency.md#concurrency-decisions-as-agency-not-syntax) at `:13` — "Khayyam embeds no concurrency model into its syntax — no `go` keyword, no `async`/`await`" — and at `:39`, "The grammar states nothing about how execution is scheduled or what substrate it runs on."
2. **Concurrency safety is reconstructed by a rule or a tool, not by the grammar.** The same document at `:44`, which places "the same opt-in/swappable trade-off discussed in Memory → Safety enforcement is governance onto concurrency safety as well". So this module's guarantee is a library's and a tool's, and never the grammar's.
3. **Whether a method blocks is the method's own property, tagged by an abstraction.** [Agency in Khayyam](../../../docs/khayyam/agency.md#concurrency-decisions-as-agency-not-syntax) at `:13`: tagged "via an abstraction, e.g. `tp Async ab`), not a choice made by the caller", and "how such a method is carried out once it is tagged belongs to the library that implements it". An indivisible operation and a method that waits for one are therefore both declarations, distinguished by what they compose.
4. **A body-less method on a concrete capsule asks for an externally provided implementation.** [Khayyam → Method](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#method) at `:91` and [Method in Khayyam → Body-less Methods](../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#body-less-methods-ffi-and-contracts) at `:80`. **This is the whole of what this module can express today.**
5. **Every variable of a call passes strictly by reference** ([Method in Khayyam → Key Rules](../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#key-rules)), which is what lets a method name an indivisible effect on a value the host produced rather than one it made.
6. **A method's contract includes the memory semantics of its inputs and outputs, and the term must be written.** [Memory](../../computer/memory/memory.md#copy-and-ownership-semantics-are-contract-members) at `:100`–`:102`, quoted above. This is the obligation that forces points 3 and 4 above to be stated rather than assumed.
7. **The framework's own concurrency mechanics are libraries the runtime loads.** [Runtime → Concurrency is hosted, not defined, here](../../computer/runtime/runtime.md) at `:37`: "those mechanics are libraries the runtime loads, not keywords a language core owns". So the realization of this module is the runtime's to supply, and a module that had to invent one would be inventing the runtime's subject.

### What neither states, and what this module is therefore blocked on
Four gaps, and the first one blocks the module's own protocol surface.

**Which document states that an indivisible, ordered step exists and what it guarantees.** [Concurrency Realization](../../computer/concurrency/concurrency.md) at `:11` states that the document covers "the execution substrate a Memar system provides … the **synchronization and signaling primitives it exposes**, and the rules their use must follow", and its body then states the substrate (`:27`–`:30`), that channels are signals rather than stores (`:32`–`:39`), and that execution units are pooled rather than spawned (`:41`) — and **names no synchronization primitive at all**. Its paired [handoff](../../computer/concurrency/concurrency.handoff.md) carries three questions, none of them this one. This is not a missing topic in an unrelated document; it is a gap between a document's stated scope and its content, and it is this module's blocker because a Rule here must cite the document that owns its normative claim ([Rule → Normative Claim and Executable Form](../rule/rule.md#normative-claim-and-executable-form) at `:66`).

**Whether indivisibility and order may be stated on the same declaration, and whether an ordered step may be declared at all.** Nothing states that a provision carries contract terms. [Control Flow → Error Propagation](../control-flow/control-flow.md#error-propagation) requires every fallible operation to expose its error path as an ordinary named part of the call contract, and [Disregarded Error](../error/rules/disregarded-error/disregarded-error.md) requires that a failure be accounted for where the operation can see it, while prescribing no form of carriage at all; neither speaks to ordering, and both are protocols' requirements over a library rather than a grammar obligation ([Khayyam → The Grammar Refuses Protocol Semantics](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#the-grammar-refuses-protocol-semantics)).

**Whether the indivisibility of a release is also a term of the crossing.** Three homes are candidates and none is settled: a term of the crossing ([`memory.md:99`](../../computer/memory/memory.md#copy-and-ownership-semantics-are-contract-members)–`:102`), a provisioned method — the shape the owner has now ruled — or a configured posture ([`memory.md:85`](../../computer/memory/memory.md#safety-enforcement-is-governance)). The owner's ruling settles the second and leaves the other two open; [Binding Model → Open questions](../../computer/binding/binding.md#open-questions) question 23 carries it there and this document does not duplicate it.

**What the indivisible step is called, and what its cursor is called.** Nothing states it, and the qualified-names rule is what a naming answer must satisfy ([qualified names](../../../bundles/khayyam/rules/qualified-names/qualified-names.md)). This blocks the first declaration rather than the design.

### The code this module would hold
This module is to have code, and what it is has been decided; what it is not yet is writable, and the two are separate statements.

**What it would hold.** One declaration file for the indivisible step over a value the host produced — a body-less method owned by a capsule, per point 4 of [What the documents already state](#what-the-documents-already-state) — carrying the terms that say the effect admits no intermediate observation. ### Implications

- **The owner's ruling is necessary and not sufficient, and the gap between them is a whole concept.** Placing the indivisibility on a method of the buffer module is what makes the design writable: the method's receiver is a capsule, so it may name a capsule and take the region, which [Abstraction in Khayyam → What You Cannot Do](../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do) at `:100` would forbid an abstraction-owned signature to do ([The sharpest instance → One module, both an abstraction and an implementation](../../computer/binding/binding.md#one-module-both-an-abstraction-and-an-implementation)). What it does not deliver is order, and without order the freed portion is not safe to refill. A model of synchronisation that stops at indivisibility produces a ring that is correct on one core and wrong on two, which is precisely the case [Process → This Document Treats Concurrency as One Concept, Not Two](../process.md#this-document-treats-concurrency-as-one-concept-not-two) at `:228` says needs no second core to arise.
- **The guarantee this module can offer is a promise about posture, not about the machine.** "Atomically" is relaxable by configuration ([`memory.md:85`](../../computer/memory/memory.md#safety-enforcement-is-governance)), the language cannot enforce it ([`agency.md:44`](../../../docs/khayyam/agency.md#concurrency-decisions-as-agency-not-syntax)), and the protocol that would state it does not. So what a declaration honestly says is *this step was declared indivisible and ordered, and an implementation arrived* — and what a crossing may honestly require is the same, which is why the [Binding Model](../../computer/binding/binding.md#the-execution-method-an-atomic-head-or-tail) records this requirement as failing its own checkability test. The gap is not in the design; it is that this repository has no document in which "atomic" means anything at run time.
- **A shared ring is a deviation from the memory protocol's default, not an optimization within it.** [Memory → The boundary copy convention, stated](../../computer/memory/memory.md#the-boundary-copy-convention-stated) at `:105` makes the producing side's copy "the framework's default" for a value handed to another execution unit; `:102` requires every zero-copy optimization to be paired with an explicit documented contract term, and `:107` adds that a library handing out retained views "must present it as prominently as its performance numbers". The ring is therefore lawful and expensive in obligations: three terms — how long a portion is valid, what the driver may assume about the region, and what each side may assume the other does with it — and no document yet owns any of them. This module's contribution to that is one of the three: the order in which a freed portion becomes the other party's premise.
- **Two concerns that look like this module's are not, and naming them is what keeps the module one thing.** Exclusion is a mechanism ([`process.md:200`](../process.md#concurrency)) whose own contract already depends on the substrate the module refuses to speak for ([`locker.kh:6`](./protocol/locker.kh)); detection is an audit ([`modules/process/race/`](../race/)) of what this module guarantees. A folder holding both would be the shape the owner refused when refusing `os` — "everything is dumped in without care" — reproduced one level down, and the reproduction is worth naming because both folders already exist and both sit under `process/`.
- **The indivisibility half is already declared twice in the residue under two names, and neither declaration is a contract.** `Sync_AtomicAccessor` at [`sync/protocol/atomic.kh:3`](./protocol/atomic.kh) and `HashTable_AtomicAccessor` at [`computer/adt/hash_table/protocol/atomic.kh:7`](../../computer/adt/hash_table/protocol/atomic.kh) are one concept under two owners' names, both body-less, both unimplemented, and under [Khayyam → Import Mechanism](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#import-mechanism-in) that is a collision whether or not both are reachable. The first is this module's subject filed inside it; the second is the same guarantee filed inside a data structure, which is where a guarantee does not belong.
