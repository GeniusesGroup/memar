# Control Flow Model Answers
## Abstract
This document is what the documents and the `.kh` corpus of this repository do and do not settle about the nine open questions the [Control Flow model handoff](./model.handoff.md#open-questions) carries. It answers each from the evidence, cites the decisive sentence at every claim, and names the specific missing sentence wherever nothing answers. Of the nine, the repository settles none outright and constrains all nine; the four that decide whether any declaration in this module can be written — the owner of the forms, the type of a scope parameter, the type of a condition, and whether a form carries an error output — turn on one gap that no document in this repository fills: a code scope is the `sc` subtype of Khayyam, a fourth Type category rather than an Abstraction, and an Abstraction's method signature may name only Abstractions, so a conditional pair declared as an Abstraction has no way to name the scope its members receive. One conflict is recorded rather than resolved. The JavaScript target's handoff reports, under Ambiguities Resolved, that the control-flow model declares the type of a scope argument, while that model carries the same question as open; the measurement both rest on survives in the tree only as a compiled build product under `modules/khayyam/targets/js/build/`, its `.kh` source absent.

## Introduction
### Motivation
The model's handoff carries nine questions whose answers are the only thing standing between the model and the first declaration under `protocol/`: no signature in that folder can be written until the owner, the scope parameter's type, the condition's type, and the error output are each settled. A question left open in a handoff is answered by whoever writes first rather than by the evidence, and that is not hypothetical here — `modules/process/control-flow/protocol/if.kh:6` is exactly that outcome, carrying three names that no declaration in this repository binds. Each question below was therefore put to the documents rather than reasoned about, and the result is recorded in the form a declaration can use: a citation, or a named absence.

### Methodology
Each question was put to the evidence in the order the repository's own division of subjects requires — the language documents for anything about the language, the protocol for the contract, the `.kh` corpus for what has been declared, and the toolchain's own measurements for what has been compiled. A claim is recorded only where a document or a declaration states it; where the corpus is the only witness, the declaration is named by file and line; where nothing answers, the silence is reported as the document, the section, and the sentence that would have to be written, together with the other document that would change with it. The corpus was read file by file rather than sampled, and the searches were run over the whole tree.

Two standing rules shape what follows. Nothing here is chosen: a question the evidence cannot decide is left to the owner and carried in the paired [handoff](./answers.handoff.md), and where a document says a question is open, that sentence is quoted rather than paraphrased. And where the documents conflict, both sides are named with their citations rather than reconciled; a reconciliation is itself a decision.

## Explanation
### The nine questions and the state of each
The handoff carries nine questions under [`Open Questions`](./model.handoff.md#open-questions), at lines 29, 35, 41, 47, 53, 59, 65, 71, and 77 of [`model.handoff.md`](./model.handoff.md). Their state against this repository's evidence is:

1. **Which type owns the forms, and what category is it?** Not answered. The owner may be any Type category and the method name need not repeat it, and the library is replaceable per organization; nothing names the owner.
2. **What type does a scope parameter take?** Not answered. The position is admitted and the spelling of its type is unstated in the language documents and in the protocol.
3. **What type does a condition take, and does the generic form take one at all?** Not answered. The generic form's standing is stated and the condition must be a named type, but no declaration binds either.
4. **Does a form carry an influenced `err`?** Not answered. The visibility requirement is conditional on the operation being fallible, and nothing states that a branch decision is.
5. **Is `Conditional` in `modules/math/logic/conditional.kh` this module's concept?** Not answered. No document defines the declaration, and the folder's own membership rule does not list its subject.
6. **Which error class justifies a Halt, and does Recovery exist?** Not answered. The protocol registers halting semantics as a topic it does not hold.
7. **Where do `OnAbsent`/`OnPresent` and `OnFailure`/`OnSuccess` live, and under what name?** Not answered. The protocol records which of its two questions is prior, and both candidate pairs are declared in two other modules at once.
8. **Which type owns the condition's two-branch contract?** Not answered, and constrained by the Abstraction-signature rule named in the Abstract.
9. **Is a branch's own driver a jump the library must declare, or the compiler's?** Not answered. The compiler is excluded from holding it, the protocol permits either packaging, and the one measurement chose the library.

### Which type owns the forms, and what category is it?
**The repository does not answer this**, and one protocol sentence says so in its own body.

- `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:61` (under [Library-Defined Control Flow](../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#library-defined-control-flow)): "Which type owns the forms in Khayyam, and what category that owner has, is open in the [handoff]; until it is settled, the Khayyam examples in this document write the call without its owner and its influenced group, and their `in` lines stand for wherever that owner is declared."
- `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md:8` records four spellings and none decided, and states that the owner raised on 2026-09-28, without deciding, that the forms may sit under `tp CF sc`. `:9` puts the choice to the owner as three options, and `:11` states the consequence: "Until then no module depends on a declaration under that folder, and no form declaration is added there."
- [`README.md:4`](./README.md) restates it where a reader of the folder arrives: "Which type owns its forms, and how they are laid out across files, is open in its handoff."

What the repository does settle is every constraint the decision must satisfy:

- The owner is not restricted to a capsule. `docs/khayyam/method.md:29` (under [Method Structure](../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#method-structure)): "That owner is not limited to capsules (`cp`); a method can be attached to *any* type (`tp`), including an abstraction (`ab`) or even another method (`mt`)" — "wherever a receiver is genuinely warranted".
- The call must name it. `docs/khayyam/method.md:52` (under [Method Invocation Rules](../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#method-invocation-rules)): "Every method names its parent type in the owner group. The call writes both groups."
- The owner is replaceable per organization. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:130` (under [Mechanism Summary](../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#mechanism-summary)): "organizations can configure their Linter to block raw intrinsic jump methods and mandate their own approved control-flow libraries, without changing the protocol."
- The forms' method names need not repeat the owner. `modules/khayyam/rules/receiver-method-names/receiver-method-names.md:12` (under [The rule](../../khayyam/rules/receiver-method-names/receiver-method-names.md#the-rule)): "A method that no other file brings in by name keeps the bare name." That rule is advisory by its own [Keep it or drop it](../../khayyam/rules/receiver-method-names/receiver-method-names.md#keep-it-or-drop-it) at `:39`.
- No rule requires one form per type or one form per file. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md:8`: "No document requires one type per form or one form per file"; `modules/khayyam/rules/file-layout/file-layout.md:25` (under [What this rule does not claim](../../khayyam/rules/file-layout/file-layout.md#what-this-rule-does-not-claim)): "It does not claim one file equals one capsule or one abstraction".

Each of the two candidates on the table breaks one of this repository's own rules, which is a finding about the candidates and not a choice between them:

- `tp CF sc` is refused by this toolchain as written. `modules/khayyam/rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.md:12` (under [The rule](../../khayyam/rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.md#the-rule)): "A `sc` declaration appears only inside a method body. A top-level `tp {name} sc { }` is refused by this toolchain with the implementation label `scope-placement`." That rule's own [handoff](../../khayyam/rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.handoff.md#open-questions) at `:13` states what dropping it would cost: "No document in this repository supplies that, and the honest form of the answer is that an organization dropping the rule also has to supply the meaning" — driven by whom, entered how.
- A Flow abstraction is constrained by the Abstraction-signature rule, quoted under the eighth question below.

**What is missing.** One sentence naming a Type category as the owner of the flow forms in Khayyam. It would sit in `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope` or a new topic under `docs/khayyam/` if the answer is a `sc`, and in `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md` as a statement about this realization if the answer is an `ab`. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#library-defined-control-flow` and [`README.md:4`](./README.md) would change with it, and so would the three worked examples at `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:39-57`, `:66-88`, and `:90-118`, which today write an owner-less call.

### What type does a scope parameter take?
**The repository does not answer this.** Three documents touch it and each stops one sentence short.

- The position is admitted. `docs/khayyam/method.md:71` (under [Type-level arguments for `sc` and `mt`](../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#type-level-arguments-for-sc-and-mt)): "A method's influencing group may receive a `vr`, an `sc`, or an `mt`. The compiler distinguishes the three from the callee's signature and performs the behavior that position requires… An `sc` is a code scope: a library method that drives a scope receives that scope here". `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:132` (under [Scope](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope)) repeats it from the grammar's side: "An argument position may be satisfied by a `vr` of the declared type or, for `sc` and `mt`, by the type itself passed as a type-level argument."
- The spelling is unstated, and the toolchain's own reading of that gap is recorded. `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.handoff.md:50` (under [Ambiguities Resolved](../../khayyam/targets/js/target.handoff.md#ambiguities-resolved)): "**The type of a scope argument is unstated in the documents, so the model declares one.** The control-flow model declares the scope as the shape the compiler emitted and carries the open question of what a scope is in the language itself".
- The repository's rule catalog does not close it either. `modules/khayyam/rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md:15` states only that "Type-level arguments for `sc` and `mt` positions that the signature explicitly allows remain valid", and `:24` states only what it "does not claim".

The one declaration in this repository that wrote such a parameter chose a capsule: `modules/khayyam/targets/js/test/emit.test.ts:15` declares `tp Scope cp {` in the fixture it compiles, and every branch parameter is written `(branch Scope)` at `:23`, `:29`, `:33`, `:35`, and `:39`. The same shape survives as the artifact `modules/khayyam/targets/js/build/modules/khayyam/targets/js/lib/control-flow.js` and as the stored representation `modules/khayyam/targets/js/build/.cache/modules_khayyam_targets_js_lib_control-flow.kh.json`. The unit's source is absent from the tree: `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.changelog.md:57` records "Removed: `targets/js/lib/` — the Khayyam libraries this folder held, and the demo that branched through them."

The corpus holds one witness to the question, and it is a comment rather than a declaration: `modules/computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh:3` reads `// OnAbsent(call scope) (err Error)` and `:6` reads `// OnPresent(call scope) (err Error)`. No `.kh` file under `modules/` declares a method whose parameter is a scope; the only two `sc` declarations in the tree are the probe bodies at `modules/khayyam/targets/js/bootstrap/repeat.kh:25` and `compose.kh:20`, and `modules/khayyam/rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.handoff.md:12` records that at this milestone a parser carries an `mt` body as opaque text, so those two are "carried as body text and never parsed as a declaration".

**What is missing.** A sentence in `docs/khayyam/method.md#type-level-arguments-for-sc-and-mt`, or in `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope`, stating whether a scope parameter's type is written as the grammar's own `sc`, as a named abstraction over scopes, or as a capsule. `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope` would change with it, since that section is where the `sc` subtype is declared (`tp {name} sc { ___ }` at `:128`), and `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.handoff.md:50` would change too, since it reports the question as settled by the model.

### What type does a condition take, and does the generic form take one at all?
**The repository does not answer either half.**

On whether the generic form is declared at all:

- Its standing is stated. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:64` (under [The Preferred Form](../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#the-preferred-form-domain-specific-conditional-methods)): "`IF`/`ELSE` are a generic default only — a last resort when no more specific name exists."
- The reach is stated. [`model.md:107`](./model.md#the-relations): "The Preferred Form makes this the expected reach and `IF`/`ELSE` the last resort, so a type with a nameable condition should have no generic branch in its source at all."
- Whether this library declares it is not stated anywhere. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:37` says only that "A realization may expose a generic conditional abstraction that receives an explicit branch scope", which is permissive and not a requirement, and `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:48` shows a scope passed positionally beside a condition, a shape the language's own rule discourages.

On the condition's type:

- It cannot be a primitive. `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:209` (under [Type Principles Realized](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#type-principles-realized)): "**No primitive types**: Khayyam does not have primitive types. Even values that other languages represent as `int`, `bool`, or `string` are wrapped in named capsules (`W32`, `Bool`, `String`)."
- It cannot be a method, because nothing can hold one. `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.handoff.md:86` measures it: "The language admits a method in an argument position, and no unit can declare the type that holds one."
- The truth-value concept already has an owner, and it is split. `modules/math/boolean/protocol/boolean.kh:24` declares `tp Boolean ab` composed of `Conjunction`, `Disjunction`, `Logic_Equivalence`, and `Negation`; `docs/protocols/math/math.md:26` (under [The number tower](../../../docs/protocols/math/math.md#the-number-tower)) states the split: "Truth values follow the same split: the capsule of one truth value (1 for true and 0 for false) is distinct from the concept of Boolean truth."
- In the preferred form there is no condition argument to type, because the receiver carries it. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:100` reads `saveErr.OnAbsent(PaymentRecorded)` on a value the example's own `vr saveErr StorageError` declared at `:97`.
- The one measured signature wrote a capsule: `modules/khayyam/targets/js/test/emit.test.ts:12` declares `tp Condition cp {`, used as `(condition Condition)` at `:23`, `:25`, `:29`, `:33`, `:35`, and `:39`. No `.kh` file in the tree declares a `Condition`.

**A conflict, recorded rather than resolved.** [`model.md:123`](./model.md#the-names-to-use-and-the-names-to-refuse) refuses `Condition` as this library's type name and [`model.handoff.md:18`](./model.handoff.md#decisions) records that refusal as Decided, on the ground that a type here names a domain concept and `modules/math/boolean/protocol/boolean.kh` already owns the truth-value concept. The only measured realization of a generic conditional in this repository names a capsule `Condition`. Neither side is a document claim about the protocol: one is a naming decision in a Draft model, the other a build product of a unit whose source is absent. If a ruling adopts a capsule named `Condition`, then [`model.md`'s names section](./model.md#the-names-to-use-and-the-names-to-refuse) and the handoff entry above it must both change together.

**What is missing.** A sentence stating whether `modules/process/control-flow/` declares the generic form, and, if it does, which named type its condition parameter takes. It cannot be written before the owner question is settled, because the owner and the parameter's type are one signature.

### Does a form carry an influenced `err`?
**The repository does not answer this.** The requirement it states is conditional on the operation being fallible, and nothing states that deciding to run a body is fallible.

- `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:143` (under [Error Propagation](../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#error-propagation)): "**Visible Error Paths:** every fallible operation exposes its error path as an ordinary, named part of the call contract." The sentence before it, at `:141`, gives the realization its choice of form and puts the check on tooling: "the realization may express it as an output value, a result type, or another explicit mechanism, and its tooling checks that the path is handled or routed".
- The same section disclaims the question at `:139`: the document "is concerned only with how execution continues once [an error] has occurred."
- The process layer declines to decide what a failure of a decision does. `docs/process.md` (under [Failure](../../../docs/process.md#failure)): "a failure may result in another attempt, a request for human intervention, a pending state, compensation, cancellation, an alternative path, a partial but valid outcome, or termination — rollback is only one possible response among these." Under [Cancellation](../../../docs/process.md#cancellation): "A process may be cancellable, but cancellation is not synonymous with rollback."
- A search of `docs/` for `fallible` returns only those two sentences in the Control Flow protocol and the arithmetic discussion at `docs/khayyam/variable.md:18`. Nothing anywhere says a branch decision can fail.

Four witnesses disagree, and none of them is a ruling:

- [`modules/process/control-flow/protocol/if.kh:6`](./protocol/if.kh) writes `(err Error)`.
- [`modules/computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh:3`](../../computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh) and `:6` carry the commented `(err Error)`.
- `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:44-56` and `:68-88` write no influenced group at all on their `IF`/`ELSE` examples, which `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:61` explains as a consequence of the owner being unsettled.
- The one measurement writes an empty influenced group on every branch: `modules/khayyam/targets/js/test/emit.test.ts:23`, `:29`, `:33`, `:35`, and `:39` all end in `()`, and the only non-empty influenced group in that unit is `:25`, `(reversed Condition)` on `Revise`.

The language constrains the shape if the answer is yes. `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.handoff.md:87` measures that "the parameters of the method itself are not slots a call may write (a body binds them with `vr`)", so an `err` on a form must be an influenced variable, which the caller declares and hands over: `docs/khayyam/method.md:39` states that "Every variable passed to a method — the owner, each influencing variable, and each influenced variable alike — is passed strictly by reference", and `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.md:50` (under [The artifact of one unit](../../khayyam/targets/js/target.md#the-artifact-of-one-unit)) states that "the influenced group is handed over as the slot itself".

**A conflict, recorded rather than resolved.** `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:143` requires the error path of every fallible operation to be visible, while the measured signature at `modules/khayyam/targets/js/test/emit.test.ts:23` exposes none. Both cannot be right unless the protocol's sentence is read as not reaching a branch, which is precisely what the protocol does not say.

**What is missing.** A sentence in `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#error-propagation` stating whether a Flow Decision is a fallible operation. If it is not, [`protocol/if.kh:6`](./protocol/if.kh) and the commented signatures at [`listeners.kh:3`](../../computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh) and `:6` are wrong as written. If it is, the protocol's two `IF`/`ELSE` examples are incomplete and `docs/protocols/process/error.md#multi-cause-returns` would have to say which class a failed decision returns, since it currently distinguishes only the one-cause case from the multi-cause case.

### Is `Conditional` in `modules/math/logic/conditional.kh` this module's concept?
**The repository does not answer this**, and no document in it defines the declaration.

- [`modules/math/logic/conditional.kh:4`](../../math/logic/conditional.kh) declares `tp Conditional ab`, with no method and no `in` line. The enumeration is inside the `TODO(go-migrate)` comment at `:6-22`: `Conditional_Unset`, `Conditional_Then`, `Conditional_ActionAndContinue`, `Conditional_Continue`, `Conditional_Break`, `Conditional_Return`.
- The declaration is not orphaned. [`modules/math/logic/conditional.observers.kh:3,5,6`](../../math/logic/conditional.observers.kh) includes it and declares `tp Observer_Conditional ab` with `tp GetConditional mt (self Observer_Conditional) () (c Conditional)`. Whatever happens to `Conditional` happens to this too.
- A second declaration of the Flow Decision concept would be an architecture error. `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:62` (under [Import Mechanism (`in`)](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#import-mechanism-in)): "**Name collision is an architecture error, not a language disambiguation problem.**" The rule that applies it names the case and its answer: `modules/khayyam/rules/qualified-names/qualified-names.md:23`, under [When qualification cannot separate two declarations](../../khayyam/rules/qualified-names/qualified-names.md#when-qualification-cannot-separate-two-declarations), "One concept declared twice … its answer is one declaration that the other module includes, not a second name … Which module keeps the declaration is a question of which one owns the concept, and it is not answered by naming."
- The folder's own membership rule does not list this subject. `docs/protocols/math/math.md:33` (under [Realization membership](../../../docs/protocols/math/math.md#realization-membership)): "A declaration belongs under `modules/math/` when its subject is a number or a truth value: a magnitude, an operation over magnitudes, a truth value, or the logic over truth values." A branching decision is none of the four. This is the strongest thing in the tree bearing on the question, and it is a placement rule rather than a naming one.
- No `.kh` file under `modules/` declares a method named for a `Conditional`, and no document under `docs/` uses the word as a Memar concept; the search returns only the protocol's own `IF`/`ELSE` vocabulary and unrelated prose about conditionals in the general sense.

**What is missing.** A sentence naming the subject of [`modules/math/logic/conditional.kh`](../../math/logic/conditional.kh). If the answer is that it enumerates Flow Decision outcomes, then `docs/protocols/math/math.md#realization-membership` must also change, because that rule is what places a truth value's logic under `modules/math/` and does not list a branching outcome. If the answer is that it is unrelated to control flow, then the file's own commented enumeration names `Break`, `Continue`, and `Return` — the derived-target Flow Decisions of [`model.md#flow-decision`](./model.md#flow-decision) — and that coincidence is itself unexplained by any document.

### Which error class justifies a Halt, and does Recovery exist?
**The repository does not answer this.** Two documents hold the facts a ruling would need and neither connects them.

- The halt is an operation in the realization's layer. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:144`: "**Explicit Halt Operations:** abrupt execution halts are explicit operations in the realization's library or contract layer, with governance supplied by the appropriate tooling."
- The protocol does not hold the topic. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:152`, under [Topics to be developed here](../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#topics-to-be-developed-here): "**Halting semantics** — abrupt control transfer (`PANIC()`-family behavior) as an ordinary method call, its contract, and its relationship to error propagation".
- The error classes exist and are unrelated to halting as written. `docs/protocols/process/error.md:57-68`, under [Optional capability interfaces](../../../docs/protocols/process/error.md#optional-capability-interfaces), gives `Internal` as "caused by this process's own logic, not the caller's input", `Temporary` as "retrying later may succeed", and `Timeout`, then states at `:68` that "`Internal`, `Temporary`, and `Timeout` are independent dimensions — an error may be any combination of them, or none", framed as letting infrastructure "reason about a *class* of errors". Nothing in that section connects a capability to a control path.
- The owner's position is recorded as a position. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md:14`: errors "**temporary and outside the program's control** (e.g. momentary memory unavailability) warrant abrupt halting (panic-class behavior), while input-validation failures return errors addressed to the caller", and the entry closes "the temporary/uncontrollable criterion is the owner's working position to evaluate, not a decided rule".
- Neither process concept decides it. `docs/process.md#failure` lists termination as one of several possible responses and `docs/process.md#cancellation` states that cancellation "does not necessarily mean that every effect already produced by the process should be removed".
- Whether a halt carries an `Error` at all is unstated, and it is the prior question: nothing in `docs/protocols/process/error.md` mentions halting, and nothing in `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md` says the halt's argument is an error.

On Recovery, the repository holds no concept and two names. [`modules/process/control-flow/protocol/panic-recovery.kh:3`](./protocol/panic-recovery.kh) declares `tp RuntimePanicRecovery cp {` with no field, no method, and no importer. [`modules/computer/runtime/thread/protocol/thread.kh:31-33`](../../computer/runtime/thread/protocol/thread.kh) carries the whole thing commented out — `// type Execution_Exception interface {` / `// 	Panic(e Event)` / `// 	Recover() (e Event)` — with the author's own question in the comment above at `:30`: "TODO::: Is it good idea to have these logics? Why not just Errors??". The diagnostics belong elsewhere: [`modules/computer/runtime/protocol/stack.kh:5,8`](../../computer/runtime/protocol/stack.kh) declares `tp Stack ab` and `tp RuntimeStack mt (self Stack) () (s String)` under the comment "if log need to trace, specially in panic situation", and [`modules/process/log/panic_handler.kh`](../log/panic_handler.kh) is a Go-draft `PanicHandler` that recovers a halt and turns it into a log event. [`model.md:131`](./model.md#the-names-to-use-and-the-names-to-refuse) refuses the name `Recover` and says why: what observation of a halt means "is [the protocol's own open topic]".

**What is missing.** `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md` must gain the halting-semantics topic it registers at `:152`, and that topic must say which of the three dimensions at `docs/protocols/process/error.md:57-68` conditions a halt, and whether a Halt carries an `Error` at all. If it names `Temporary`, then `docs/protocols/process/error.md` needs a sentence connecting a capability to a control path, because that section currently states the capabilities as opt-in per concrete error and says nothing about what any consumer may do with one. If observation of a halt is a concept, its owner is this module's question and the answer would need `modules/process/log/panic_handler.kh` and `modules/computer/runtime/protocol/stack.kh` to point at it.

### Where do `OnAbsent`/`OnPresent` and `OnFailure`/`OnSuccess` live, and under what name?
**The repository does not answer this.** It answers which of the protocol's two questions is prior, and it answers that holding both candidates at once is what the language's collision rule forbids.

- Both candidates are declared, method-less and import-free, in two other modules. [`modules/computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh:3-7`](../../computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh) reads `// OnAbsent(call scope) (err Error)` / `tp OnAbsent ab` / `// OnPresent(call scope) (err Error)` / `tp OnPresent ab`. [`modules/process/operation/protocol/on-failure.kh:3`](../operation/protocol/on-failure.kh) reads `tp OnFailure ab`; [`on-success.kh:3`](../operation/protocol/on-success.kh) reads `tp OnSuccess ab`.
- The precedence is stated. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md:16-18`: "Should the success/failure pair live on the Error abstraction or on a shared abstraction? … Not yet decided. Next: resolve before finalizing the pair naming." `:20-22`: "No final naming choice between the candidate pairs … Next: decide alongside the shared-abstraction question above." The placement question is prior by its own text.
- A shared abstraction has a cost already recorded. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md:24-26`: "if the shared-abstraction approach is chosen, how does it interact with [Abstraction in Khayyam]'s rejection of default implementations? Each implementing capsule would need its own Explicit Delegation Verification line to a shared internal implementation, to avoid duplicating the same check logic across every implementer without resorting to inheritance." The rejection it names is at `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md` under [Rejection of Default Implementations](../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#rejection-of-default-implementations).
- The corpus cannot keep both. `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:62`: a file needing both "has no `as` alias syntax", and the correct fix is "a root-cause redesign so that one abstraction is the single owner of that concept".
- The protocol itself points away from the error-specific spelling. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:29` frames the presence/absence candidate as "built around the presence/absence behavior a type may expose as a contract term", citing `docs/protocols/memory/memory.md#absence-is-a-types-contract-not-a-universal-machine-condition` — a different document claiming the concept. `:120` states the naming convention for "such presence/absence or success/failure branch pairs is still an open question".

**A fourth spelling exists and nothing declares it.** `docs/protocols/process/error.md:81-82` writes `yes.OnTrue(WaitAndRetry)` and `yes.OnFalse(ReturnErr)` on a `Boolean` — `yes` is the influenced variable typed `Boolean` at `:75` and written by the call at `:80`. No `.kh` file in the tree declares `OnTrue` or `OnFalse`, and [`modules/math/boolean/protocol/boolean.kh:24`](../../math/boolean/protocol/boolean.kh) composes only `Conjunction`, `Disjunction`, `Logic_Equivalence`, and `Negation` into `Boolean`. The Error protocol's worked example therefore calls two methods that no abstraction in this repository requires.

**What is missing.** A ruling on the shared abstraction, then a name. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md` under [Candidates for the success/failure branching pair](../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#candidates-for-the-successfailure-branching-pair) would change, as would the `Boolean` abstraction's method set if `OnTrue`/`OnFalse` are to be the pair, and `docs/protocols/process/error.md:81-82` would have to be written against whatever that set becomes. One of the three `.kh` files then moves or is deleted; `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:62` and `modules/khayyam/rules/qualified-names/qualified-names.md:23` are the two rules that decide which.

### Which type owns the condition's two-branch contract — a Type, a Flow Library, or neither?
**The repository does not answer this**, and one rule it does state makes the question harder than the handoff records.

- Replaceability is what makes a contract the right owner, and the protocol states it. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:130`: "organizations can configure their Linter to block raw intrinsic jump methods and mandate their own approved control-flow libraries, without changing the protocol." `docs/modeling.md:394`, under [Extensible Behavior Belongs to Pluggable Modules](../../../docs/modeling.md#extensible-behavior-belongs-to-pluggable-modules), gives the general form: "a concern's model should expose attachment points for pluggable Modules rather than growing new fields or branches to absorb every variation one might need", and `docs/modularity.md` under [Pluggable Behavior](../../../docs/modularity.md#pluggable-behavior) defines what that is: "A Module may expose relationships through which independently modeled Modules can attach behavior to it."
- Any Type can carry the methods. `docs/type.md:181` makes Scope a fourth Type category beside Capsule, Method, and Abstraction, and `docs/khayyam/method.md:29` admits a method on any of them.
- The constraint that decides it is an Abstraction's signature. `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md:101`, under [What You Cannot Do](../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do): "You **cannot** use a capsule type as an argument or return type in an abstraction's method signature — only other abstractions are allowed, ensuring the specification remains implementation-agnostic."

The pair's members must name a scope. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:59` requires that "`ELSE` always takes an explicit reference back to the same condition value it pairs with; the construct carries its dependencies in its own arguments", and `:37` describes the generic abstraction as one that "receives an explicit branch scope". A branch scope in Khayyam is the `sc` subtype — `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:128` declares it as `tp {name} sc { ___ }` — and `docs/type.md:181` makes Scope a category that is not an Abstraction. So an Abstraction-declared conditional pair has no way to name the scope it receives, and the eighth question and the second are one problem rather than two.

The measured shape works around exactly this, and the workaround is what removes replaceability. `modules/khayyam/targets/js/test/emit.test.ts:18-20` puts `IF` and `ELSE` inside `tp Flow cp {`, satisfying the Abstraction rule by making the owner a capsule; the abstractions' own contract methods at `:27-33` are satisfied by that capsule's methods at `:35-43`. The target then refuses any call whose receiver is typed as an abstraction — `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.md:58`, under [The three kinds of body-less method](../../khayyam/targets/js/target.md#the-three-kinds-of-body-less-method): "No artifact carries one, and a call whose receiver is typed as an abstraction is refused where it stands". So the one measured realization of a two-branch contract is reachable only by not going through the contract, which is the opposite of what `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:130` claims the library is for.

**What is missing.** `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md` must gain the conditional-pair topic it registers at `:150` — "the contract a well-formed conditional method pair must satisfy: how the condition is evaluated, whether the two branches are symmetric, how the receiver's state is read, and how the pair relates to the presence/absence abstraction family" — and that topic must say what the pair's signature names. For such a contract to be an Abstraction at all, `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do` must first say how a scope is named in an Abstraction's signature, which means `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope` and `docs/khayyam/method.md#type-level-arguments-for-sc-and-mt` must state it too. This is the single dependency the first declaration in this module waits on, and it is why the first, second, third, seventh, and ninth questions cannot be answered independently of each other.

### Is a branch's own driver a jump the library must declare, or the compiler's?
**The repository does not choose for this library**, and one protocol sentence excludes the compiler from holding it. What the repository answers is that the jump cannot be a grammar form, that a host mechanism beneath a library jump is the language's own FFI form, and that the protocol deliberately leaves the packaging to the realization.

- The jump is the compiler's lowering, not the branch's grammar. `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:131` (under [Scope](../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope)): "There is no `goto` keyword: a compiler of this language lowers `sc`-driven branches to internal jumps, and `return` is an IR marker that must itself end with a line break so it cannot be written as `return 0`."
- The compiler may not hold a library's jump. `docs/protocols/computer/compiler.md` under [The compiler recognizes language primitives, not library names](../../../docs/protocols/computer/compiler.md#the-compiler-recognizes-language-primitives-not-library-names): "It does not treat library-provided operations as intrinsics because they are common, useful, or imported from a privileged package. Framework control-flow methods, numeric helpers, and lifecycle hooks are ordinary library code."
- The form a library jump takes is settled by the language. `docs/khayyam/method.md:80`, under [Body-less Methods (FFI and Contracts)](../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#body-less-methods-ffi-and-contracts): "When the receiver is a concrete capsule (`cp`), a body-less method signals to the compiler that the implementation will be provided externally during the linking phase"; `:79` gives the other case, the contract form on an `ab`, where "The method body is provided by each capsule that implements the abstraction".
- The protocol permits all three packagings and names one as the common denominator. `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:32`: "A realization may package these operations as library methods, compiler intrinsics, or another explicit mechanism … Across realizations, the common denominator is the branch-grouping primitive, not how a particular library or compiler drives it." `:124` says a realization "can provide low-level compiler-intrinsic jump methods for repetition and use its collection-iteration contract", and `:134` makes a jump "exactly as valid, and exactly as unprivileged, as an `IF`/`ELSE` pair", governable by a Linter rather than the grammar.
- The one measurement declared it, as a body-less method on a concrete capsule. `modules/khayyam/targets/js/test/emit.test.ts:23` reads `tp Drive mt (self Flow) (condition Condition, branch Scope) ()` with no body; the stored representation at `modules/khayyam/targets/js/build/.cache/modules_khayyam_targets_js_lib_control-flow.kh.json` records it as `"kind":"supplied"`; the two conditional methods are built on it at `modules/khayyam/targets/js/test/emit.test.ts:35-43`.
- What the host supplies under it was stated at the time. `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.changelog.md:83` records that the hosts "supply the jump, a condition the other way, and what a text can be asked, and nothing that decides what a branch means", and `:94` records that "A JavaScript implementation of the branch library was not kept … the answer is a Khayyam library, and only the jump under it comes from the host."
- That unit is gone. `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.handoff.md:85` names "the jump this target's control-flow unit is built on", and `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.changelog.md:57` records the folder's removal, so what remains of it is a build product under `modules/khayyam/targets/js/build/`.

**What is missing.** A sentence in `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md` saying whether Memar's own library declares the jump, because the protocol states the choice is the realization's and this repository is one realization (`docs/protocols/README.md` under [Where implementations live](../../../docs/protocols/README.md#where-implementations-live)). Given `docs/protocols/computer/compiler.md#the-compiler-recognizes-language-primitives-not-library-names`, declaring it is what remains. The declaration form then follows from the first question rather than from this one: a body-less method is FFI only on a capsule (`docs/khayyam/method.md:79-80`), and a body-less method on an abstraction carries no implementation at all, which `modules/khayyam/targets/js/target.md:58` shows the toolchain refusing at the call site.

### Implications
If the questions above are answered in the direction their evidence points, four things follow that no session has yet recorded.

- The first declaration in this module cannot be an Abstraction until item 2 above is closed, because the pair's signature must name a scope and no Abstraction may. That makes the scope parameter's type the critical path rather than a detail of one signature, and it is why the first and the eighth questions are the same decision wearing two hats.
- If the owner is a concrete capsule, the library loses the replaceability the protocol states at `docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:130`, and the Linter cannot mandate an organization's own library without the call sites changing. The measured shape at `modules/khayyam/targets/js/test/emit.test.ts:18-20` is that shape.
- Three of the four declarations a conditional pair needs already exist in two other modules and one in a third. Under `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:62` and `modules/khayyam/rules/qualified-names/qualified-names.md:23` one declaration survives and the others are deleted or moved, so the placement ruling is a change to files this module does not own — the propagation [`model.handoff.md:87`](./model.handoff.md#assumptions) already names as unexamined.
- The corpus in this folder cannot be a starting point. [`protocol/if.kh:6`](./protocol/if.kh) binds three names that no declaration in this repository provides, and its `Error` inclusion at `:3` names a path that declares nothing; whichever question is answered first, that file is superseded by it rather than by this document.
