# Flow Management Model Handoff
Open work for [flow-management.md](./flow-management.md). Entries are mutable current state — revised as each item moves, removed when it resolves or is dropped. See the repository's [documentation-handoff.md](../../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/documentation/handoff/handoff.md) for what a handoff is.

## Topic & Purpose
The model of what a program in this language can be asked to do about its own flow, and of the smallest set of things a compiler must provide that between them serve every one of those asks. It exists because [the Control Flow model](./control-flow.md) settles what the library is made of and never claims that any set is minimal, and because the [compiler protocol](../../../computer/compiler/compiler.md) states seven guarantees about the branch body without stating which flow operations need them or whether they are all of them. The document enumerates seven operations, derives five compiler obligations from them, argues that no proper subset of the five serves all seven, and separates what is language-level from per-target from protocol-level. It states no signature, no owner type, no mechanism, and no name.

## Decisions
- **This is a new model beside [control-flow.md](./control-flow.md), not a section of it** — Decided. The two have a different subject (what the library is made of, against what a program can be asked to do and what the compiler must therefore be), a different derivation direction (protocol inward, against program outward), a different layer (the library's, against the compiler's), and the minimality claim belongs to none but this one. Reason: merging them would put a library's concept decomposition and a compiler's obligation in one document, which is the umbrella the owner refused. The document argues this at `Why this is a second model and not a second list`, and states the relation between the two lists once, in `How the two lists map`.
- **The document is named `flow-management.md`, after the concept rather than after the layer** — Decided. "Flow management" is the repository's own term for the responsibility rather than for a construct: the compiler protocol speaks of a module's "obligation to flow management" (`:42`) and of a "flow-management module" (`:77`). Naming it after `control-flow` would have addressed the protocol's subject and this document's protocol; naming it after a single operation would have addressed a member of the list rather than the derivation. Reason: [Documentation → File Naming](../../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#file-naming) fixes the name to the concept the model is about, and requires that the name not stand where the subject belongs.
- **The criterion is two-part: a program needs it, and something must decide or sequence it** — Decided. Reason: it is checkable, it excludes sequence (which the language already performs, since a body's commands run in order) and it excludes inspecting a value and acting on it (which is an ordinary call). Every exclusion under `What no operation is` follows from the criterion rather than from taste. Rejected: a criterion keyed on what a mainstream language offers, and one keyed on what the `.kh` files under `protocol/` contain; the owner has ruled the second neither evidence nor a premise.
- **Seven operations: Select, Repeat, Continue, Leave, Exit, Transfer, End** — Decided as the enumeration. Reason: each is a thing a program can be asked for and each has something outside the value's own meaning deciding it. Four are counted separately rather than merged with a neighbour on the ground that they land in different places — Continue at the start of a body's next run, Leave past the nearest enclosing body, Exit past the call, End past every frame — which is what [control-flow.md](./control-flow.md) at `:52` calls a written or derived target.
- **Multi-way selection is not an operation; it is a member count of Select** — Decided. Reason: no compiler guarantee distinguishes a two-member selection from a five-member one, so counting it separately would have added an operation and no obligation. [The Preferred Form](../control-flow.md#the-preferred-form-domain-specific-conditional-methods) at `:122` already generalizes the branch methods to a multi-way interpretation, and that generalization is a property of the declaration rather than of what the compiler owes.
- **A failure's route is Select on the error value, not an operation of its own** — Decided. Reason: [The Error](../../error/error.md) at `:130` states that an error's sole purpose is to let the immediate caller make a correct decision about what to do next, so the decision is made at the call site, which is Select. Nothing in the repository requires a failing call to divert by itself, and the [disregarded-error Rule](../../error/rules/disregarded-error/disregarded-error.md) requires only that the failure be accounted for where the operation can see it. Rejected: automatic diversion, which would have been a ninth operation and would have needed a compiler obligation nothing else needs.
- **Deferred execution and teardown on the way out are excluded, and carried as a protocol question** — Explored-but-unresolved. Reason for exclusion: no document asks a program for it as a flow operation, and [Compiler → Entry and lifecycle are not grammar](../../../computer/compiler/compiler.md#entry-and-lifecycle-are-not-grammar) places a program's beginning and end outside the grammar. The criterion excludes it as well as the vocabulary. Reason it is not dropped: the sources' `defer.kh` shows the shape is one somebody reached for, and a protocol ruling could put it back.
- **The minimal compiler surface is five obligations: body, drive, reach and end, landing, decision** — Decided, as the enumeration's derivation. Reason: three of the five are the compiler protocol's own guarantees at `:54`–`:60`, regrouped; the other two are the two the protocol records as unmet — the landing, which appears in that document only inside the mechanism it would commit a compiler to (`:68`), and the decision, which `:77` names as unimplemented and which no one of the four options covers. The contribution is the count: the enumeration asks for two obligations the protocol does not state and for none it does.
- **Minimality is argued per member, by what fails without it** — Decided. Reason: a set is minimal only when no proper subset is complete, which cannot be shown by exhibiting the set. Each of the five paragraphs under `Why the surface cannot be smaller` states the operations that fail without that member and why no other member covers the need. The member most at risk of being called a restatement of another is the third: a compiler can hand over a detached body, satisfy the first, and leave every body-taking operation servable in form and empty in substance.
- **Nothing in the enumeration asks for first-class re-entry, mid-body resumption, or a value returned by a body** — Decided. Reason: Continue's target is the *start* of a body's next run, which the re-drive clause already gives; Transfer's and Repeat's targets are starts or named points, not resumptions. The compiler protocol says of the returned-continuation option that "**No other option delivers that, and it is what a repetition driven from two different decisions would need**" (`:70`), and this enumeration establishes that nothing here needs it — so the two open questions the protocol records at `:72` block none of the seven, and block only the two things excluded above. This is a negative result about the protocol's option list and is offered as such.
- **The seven operations share a primitive and a guarantee set, not an abstraction** — Decided. Reason: an umbrella enforces no rule the members do not already obey elsewhere, has no independent lifecycle (any member may be forbidden by one organization's tooling without touching the rest, per [control-flow.md](./control-flow.md) at `:111`), and — realized as a type — is designed to be absent from every call site, which is what the protocol's preferred form does at `:100`. Sharing the branch body and its guarantees is a relation the operations hold to Scope, not a node above them.
- **Nothing was written to `protocol/`, and no file outside this folder was touched** — Decided. Reason: the document models operations and obligations and declares nothing; [Modeling → Modeling Produces Conceptual Abstractions, Not Implementation Structures](../../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-produces-conceptual-abstractions-not-implementation-structures) and the owner ruling recorded in the Control Flow protocol's changelog both hold that a module's protocol settles before anything depends on it.

## Ambiguities Resolved
- **Whether `return` is still a command a body may hold** — Answered by reading, not by choosing. It is not. [Khayyam → Scope](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope) at `:131` states that there is no keyword for leaving a method early and that until a library declares one, a program written in this language has no way to leave a method early; the compiler protocol records the same at `:86`, describing the representation as carrying three commands for a body — declare, scope, invoke — with no jump among them; and `bundles/khayyam/core/src/body.ts` dispatches only `tp` to a scope, `vr` to a declare, and anything else to an invoke. Every enumerated operation therefore avoids assuming `return`, and Exit is counted as an operation a program cannot yet perform.
- **Whether the operations can be enumerated at all while the residue sources exist under `protocol/`** — Answered. They can, and the sources were not read as evidence for or against any shape. The owner has ruled the `.kh` code under `modules/**` the residue of an attempt to develop these ideas in another language that did not succeed, and neither evidence nor a premise; the enumeration was derived from the protocol documents, the language documents, and the criterion, and every claim is cited to a document rather than to a declaration. The fact that the sources hold sixteen ecosystem spellings and that fourteen of them declare nothing was not used, and one sentence in the document says so.
- **Whether the compiler's seven guarantees already cover the operations** — Answered, negatively, and this is the document's second central finding. They cover everything about the body itself, which is C1 through C3, and nothing about where control lands or about where the decision comes from. [Compiler → What no option delivers](../../../computer/compiler/compiler.md#what-no-option-delivers) at `:77`–`:79` records the decision half as unimplemented; the landing is not named as a guarantee anywhere and appears only at `:68`, inside the mechanism it would commit a compiler to.
- **Whether an operation that names no body needs a body value** — Answered: no, and the service table records it as `inherited`. Continue, Leave, and Exit compose with Repeat and need nothing the composition does not already need; the only member they require that Repeat's own service does not give them directly is the landing, because their landing is not the next command.

## Open Questions
### How does a program name a point to continue at? (language)
- State: Transfer's target is written in the operation's arguments, and the grammar ships no control-flow keyword and no label — [Khayyam → Scope](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope) at `:129`. [control-flow.md](./control-flow.md) at `:119` refuses both `Label` and `labelType`, and its argument for refusing `Label` is that the word's ordinary sense is a jump target, which is exactly what is wanted here and is a different thing from a branch body.
- What a ruling must decide: whether a point is named by a declared scope, by a declared scope plus a position within it, or not at all. The first makes Transfer's target a Branch Scope, which the existing model already owns; the third makes Transfer unimplementable and the operation refused, which [Structured and Unstructured Flow as Libraries](../control-flow.md#structured-and-unstructured-flow-as-libraries) at `:134` would permit and this document would report as a finding.
- Blocks continuation: yes, for any Transfer declaration, and for the refusal option if it is chosen.
- Path: the owner of the language documents, then the grammar. What would close it: a language document stating how a program names a point it may transfer to, or a ruling that it may not.

### Do the language's documents state a body's reach and its end? (language)
- State: [Compiler → The branch body a flow-control module receives](../../../computer/compiler/compiler.md#the-branch-body-a-flow-control-module-receives) at `:59` states the rule — a body sees outward and is not seen outward — and in the same sentence says "those rules are this protocol's, and no language document states them." The JavaScript target records its own reading of the reach as a measurement and states the silence ([target](../../../../bundles/khayyam/targets/js/target.md#the-commands-a-body-may-hold) at `:42`). C3's bucket in the separation table turns on the answer: protocol-level today, language-level if a language document states it.
- What a ruling must decide: whether the reach is a property of the language's `sc` — in which case every realization must honour it and the compiler protocol cites it — or a property of each compiler's contract — in which case two targets may read it differently and a flow module is written against whichever it targets.
- Blocks continuation: yes, for whether a flow module may be written once for every target or once per target.
- Path: the language documents, with the compiler protocol as the citing party. What would close it: a language document stating the reach and the end of a scope, or a ruling that they stay the compiler protocol's and a flow module is written per target.

### Are deferred execution and teardown on the way out flow operations? (protocol)
- State: excluded from the enumeration under the criterion and under the operation family [Library-Defined Control Flow](../control-flow.md#library-defined-control-flow) names; `protocol/defer.kh` exists in the residue sources and was not used as evidence. [Compiler → Entry and lifecycle are not grammar](../../../computer/compiler/compiler.md#entry-and-lifecycle-are-not-grammar) places a program's beginning and end in configuration plus the runtime. The [Method Exit Rule](../rules/method-exit/method-exit.md) at `:28` records that what happens to a scope's teardown on the way out is the protocol's and the library's and is not designed.
- What a ruling must decide: whether an operation that runs a body on the way out of an execution is a flow operation. If it is, it adds nothing to the compiler surface — a body value and a landing already serve it — and what it needs beyond those belongs to the runtime, not to this model.
- Blocks continuation: no; nothing in the seven depends on it. It blocks any claim that the enumeration is closed.
- Path: the Control Flow protocol, with [The Error](../../error/error.md) where a deferred body reports. What would close it: the protocol adding deferred execution to the family it names, or leaving it out.

### What does a halt carry, and may a branch decision carry an error? (protocol)
- State: [Control Flow → Error Propagation](../control-flow.md#error-propagation) at `:144` requires halts to be explicit operations and at `:152` registers halting semantics as a topic not designed; the compiler protocol at `:81` records that what leaves a method early is a library's business and that none of its four options states it. [control-flow.md](./control-flow.handoff.md)'s own handoff carries the same ground for Halt and for the `err` slot on every signature.
- What a ruling must decide: whether End carries an `Error` and of which class, and whether deciding to run a body is itself fallible. The second decides whether an error path appears on a branch signature at all.
- Blocks continuation: yes, for End's declaration and for every signature; no for the landing, which End needs regardless of what it carries.
- Path: the Control Flow protocol jointly with [The Error](../../error/error.md). What would close it: the halting-semantics topic being written, and the protocol's ruling on whether a decision can fail.

### Which mechanism serves a drive in this repository? (toolchain)
- State: [Compiler → What this obligation does not decide](../../../computer/compiler/compiler.md#what-this-obligation-does-not-decide) at `:64`–`:72` names four options and states that the document names no preference. The compiler in this repository serves a drive by a call, its representation carries three commands for a body and no jump among them, and a scope is emitted as a `function` declared inside the method that holds it (`:86`; `emit.ts:423`–`:429`). Under that lowering a nested function's only exit is its own return, so Leave, Exit, Transfer, and End have no expression from inside a body. The protocol says of the jump option that it is what makes the derived-target decisions "expressible at all" (`:68`).
- What a ruling must decide: which of the four this repository's toolchain uses. The first three are not equivalent for the four derived-target operations, and the fourth buys a capability no enumerated operation needs.
- Blocks continuation: yes, for four of the seven operations — they cannot be written, let alone declared, under the current lowering.
- Path: the toolchain owner. What would close it: a ruling on the mechanism, after which either the lowering changes or the document records that those four operations are unservable here.

### Which type owns the operations, under what names, and which conditional pair survives? (naming)
- State: four spellings of an owner are undecided across this repository's documents and are recorded in [control-flow.md](./control-flow.handoff.md)'s handoff; the seven names used in this document are illustrative and are checked against that document's `The names to use and the names to refuse` (`:115`–`:135`) rather than chosen beside it. Of the seven, `Continue` and `Exit` are also the spellings the residue sources used, `Select` is a word another language reserves, and `End` is a word another language made a keyword — none of which is decisive under [Terminology → The Default Meaning of an Unreferenced Term](../../../../docs/terminology.md#the-default-meaning-of-an-unreferenced-term), since all seven carry their ordinary English sense in the sense wanted here, and the question is the owner's.
- What a ruling must decide: the owner type, the seven names, and which of the two candidate conditional pairs survives and which module it is declared in. The last is prior, because it decides which file the pair belongs in.
- Blocks continuation: yes, for every declaration in this module.
- Path: the owner, jointly with [control-flow.md](./control-flow.handoff.md)'s owner question; the two are one decision wearing two hats.

## Assumptions
- **The criterion yields the same seven operations another reader would derive from the same documents** — Stability: Weak. What changes it: a candidate the criterion admits and this pass missed, or one it refused that a reader holds the language needs. Mitigation: the exclusions are each stated with the criterion clause they fail, so a disagreement is about the criterion or the reading rather than about taste.
- **The compiler protocol's seven guarantees are still current, and the grammar's Scope section is the later of the two** — Stability: Strong for the guarantee list, Weak for their relationship to `control-flow.md`. What changes it: an edit to `modules/computer/compiler/compiler.md` or to `docs/khayyam/khayyam.md` while this document was being written; both were being edited by other agents during the session, and neither was touched here. Mitigation: every claim is cited to a line, so a reader can re-verify each against the document as it now stands.
- **`return` has left the language's body commands and nothing replaces it** — Stability: Strong. What changes it: a ruling restoring a command for leaving a method early. Mitigation: Exit is counted as an operation in either case; only its *performability* changes, and the document states that in the operation's own paragraph.
- **No document the enumeration depends on was edited during the session** — Stability: Unexamined. Three folders — `docs/`, `modules/computer/**`, and `bundles/khayyam/**` — were under other agents' edits while this document was written, and this session read from them and wrote to none of them.
