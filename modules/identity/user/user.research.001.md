## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The user domain gives the application one answer to the question *who*: of all the things it knows, which are the ones that can be the **who of an act** or the **whom of a standing** (a party, an owner, an heir, a member), and for each of them three things: *which one it is* (an identity that persists through every change and is never reused), *how it is recognised when it acts* (something it holds that the application can check), and *what its standing in this application has been* (admitted, active, blocked, closed, with the events that moved it). Everything else the industry stores on a "user" record, such as a phone number, a name, a picture, a verified badge, friends, a home address, a role in a company, a preference, is a relation to another module. A working definition follows from it: **a user is an animate thing as the application knows it: identified, recognisable when it acts, and answerable through the recorded events of its admission and standing.**

This restates the repository's own placement ("Animates will introduce in `user`", [thing.md](../thing/thing.md)) and sharpens it with Memar's agency model: the thing does not become a new entity by being a user, exactly as "a human does not become a new entity when becoming an Agent" ([Agency](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/agency.md#agent-and-agency)).

**The word hides three things (agent, Tentative; the evidence is in the repository).**
1. **The subject** — the endpoint a relation names when it needs a *who*. The repository uses it in this sense far more than in any other: eleven `user-*` edges in invoice ([agent](../../economics/invoice/edges/user-agent/user-agent.md), applicant, broker, buyer, consumer, customer, franchise, orderer, payer, provider, seller), eight in contract (arbitrator, expert, five numbered sides, witness), `owner_is` and `heir_is` in ownership, `vote_agree_by` in law, `limit_to_user` in content, the per-user timeline. In all of them "user" is a party and the role is the other domain's.
2. **The actor** — whoever causes a request. The legacy records all carry a `RequestID` "to prove how this object created" (see [PersonNumber](internal/storage/storage.kh)); the content research separates the requester from the identity a content speaks as ([content research, "Who is the author"](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md#open-questions)).
3. **The account** — the application's record of one admission: a credential, a phone number, a status, a name. This is what the Go domain actually built (finding 5), and it is the sense in which "a user registers", "a user is blocked", "a user closes".

The three come apart in the scenarios below. A child, a company and a dead person are subjects and not actors; a bot is an actor with no account in the human sense; a refugee registered without documents has an account and no credential strong enough to call it recognition. A domain that treats them as one thing is forced to give a company a public key and a dead person a password.

**Scenarios.** Each says what it *breaks* in the obvious model (one human, one account, one phone, one key) and what it *says* about the domain. The cultures and industries are named because the obvious model came from one of them.

1. **A mobile number that changes owner (telecoms in Iran, India, Kenya, Nigeria).** A person registers by one-time code sent to a SIM. The legacy service accepts a time-based code, refuses a number already registered or "blocked by justice", and carries a note that the previous person's number must be "un-registered" ([services/register.kh](services/register.kh), `// TODO::: un-register last person number`). Two years later the operator reissues the number to a stranger who now asks to register it. *Breaks:* the number as the identity; "a number is registered by exactly one person, forever"; the stranger taking over the first person's standing. *Says:* a number is a claim of reachability and a proof of present control, valid for a period ([the handoff](user.handoff.md#shown-verification-after-reading-a-real-onboarding-contract) already says "possession of a SIM proves control of a mobile, nothing more"); identity and standing must not move when a number moves. Persons and numbers are many-to-many over time.
2. **One handset, three people (rural Nigeria, India, Pakistan; savings-group and mobile-money agents).** A father, a mother and a daughter use the same phone and the same SIM for a savings app, each with their own profile. *Breaks:* the sentence "new CPU structures have a unique immutable key that we use" if the key is read as *the user's*; "one active user per instance" ([user-active](../../computer/instance_of/edges/user-active/user-active.md)) if the instance is a household device. *Says:* a key belongs to a user and a device holds many keys; switching person on a device is a new authentication, not a property of the device; the hardware key can be one anchor among several, never the identity.
3. **A company acts (Iran: a joint-stock company whose gazette lists the authorized signatories and the "two of three, with the seal" rule; Japan: the registered corporate seal with a seal certificate).** The company must sign a contract. It cannot hold a key or press a button; a manager signs, and the manager is replaced by a new gazette notice while the company's open contracts continue. *Breaks:* "each user must have a valid public key" ([user.md](user.md)); the assumption that the one who signs is the one who answers; the idea that an organization user authenticates. *Says:* the organization is a user with **no recognition of its own**; it acts only through other users by an acting-for relation whose authority is a fact registered elsewhere, with dates, and sometimes a threshold. [Delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md) already holds "Organization usually delegate just to some department" and the open question "Authority != Permission".
4. **A child, and an adult under guardianship (Iran: *vali* and *qayyim*; Sweden: *god man* and *förvaltare*; Germany: *Betreuer*; a school learning platform).** A nine-year-old has a profile; a parent consents for her; at the age of majority the parent's authority ends; for a protected adult a court limits authority by matter (may vote, may not sign a loan). *Breaks:* user equals capable actor; authority as a permanent flag; the ending of authority by someone's request. *Says:* acting-for is an edge with a scope, a period and an *ending cause* (age, court order, death); the ward's identity and history persist unchanged across the change; who may register a child is the guardian, not the child.
5. **A user who dies (Iranian inheritance; the German court ruling that heirs may read a deceased person's social-media account; platforms that "memorialize"; population registers that cancel a national card at death).** The person's contracts, invoices, signatures and messages remain facts; the heirs may need to continue some and the family wants others closed. *Breaks:* "closed means no further activity"; "never delete" against a family that asks for removal; the dead having no key while their old signatures must still verify. *Says:* death is a standing event with evidence, not a deletion; the user remains a subject of past facts; authority moves through the [heir relation](../../rights/ownership/edges/heir/heir.md) and never by transferring the identity; old keys are revoked but kept so past signatures stay verifiable. The legacy revoke service already says so: "Due to legal history purpose never delete any public key" ([revoke-person-public-key.kh](../../computer/token/edges/key_public/services/user-public-key/revoke-person-public-key.kh)).
6. **A machine or an AI as the one who acts (a billing agent reconciling the day; a smart meter; a robot arm on a factory floor; a shop's chatbot answering customers; the membership document's "synthetic minds").** The legacy mask has `Type_Bot` ("any device or robot with specific intelligence - AI") and `Type_App` ("same app with difference instance"). *Breaks:* registration by SMS and a person's papers; the assumption that the actor is the answerable party; one bot being one process (a bot is copied; its instances come and go). *Says:* the kind is a classification of the user, the bot acts as an agent for a principal who answers ([delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md): "Persons can delegate to things (as a user type)"), and a key belongs to an *instance* while the identity belongs to the bot.
7. **Two identities for one human, one identity for two humans (a pseudonymous campaigner under a repressive state; a trader in Lagos who keeps a personal and a business account; a sole proprietor in Japan or Iran who is one human but two legal capacities; twins merged by a registry because the birth data matched).** *Breaks:* one human, one user; uniqueness as a service the domain performs; "merge" as an overwrite. *Says:* several users per human must be allowed without proof they are the same human; linking is a *claim* with evidence that the human may or may not make; a wrong merge must be undoable, which means it is a new fact that supersedes, not an edit ([supersedes](../thing/edges/supersedes/supersedes.md)).
8. **A guest and an undocumented person (a clinic kiosk used by a walk-in; a refugee registered by a humanitarian agency who has no national papers).** The legacy mask has `Type_Unset` ("don't know anything about user yet e.g. connection not fully established") and `Type_Guest` ("Unknown user type"). *Breaks:* user equals authenticated; user equals verified. *Says:* existence, recognition and verification are three independent facts; a guest is a state of a *connection* and not a kind of user (agent, Tentative); a user can exist with no verdict at all and the application must still be able to serve and protect them.
9. **Losing the key (a stolen or confiscated phone; a hardware-bound passkey whose device dies; a national card renewed; recovery by trusted contacts; an in-person check at a notary office in Iran).** [user.md](user.md) itself lists "losing or replacing the hardware directly threatens the User's identity, so a recovery/rotation story is mandatory". *Breaks:* identity rooted in a key. *Says:* the identity must survive every key; recovery is a capability with its own rules (who may approve, how long a delay, what the old devices are told) and it needs the user to have recorded recoverers while still able to authenticate.
10. **One person, many deployments, and the right not to be joined (Austria's sector-specific identifiers; Germany's post-1983 aversion to a single personal number; versus India's universal number and Estonia's personal code).** A person has a hospital account and a bank account on two deployments of this system. If both see the same identifier, their records can be joined; if each mints a new one, nothing joins unless the person says so. *Breaks:* "UUID MUST be unique in all domains" read as "one human is one UUID"; the follow relation between organizations ([follow](../thing/edges/follow/follow.md)) read as a join. *Says:* this is a choice the owner must make visibly (question 2); the two options are both expressible if identity is minted per registration and linking is a claim.
11. **Joining, moving and leaving a society (emigration; a change of citizenship; an employee's transfer between companies; a bank customer closing one branch's account).** The legacy lifecycle has `ChangedDomain` and a service `change-domain`; the membership bundle says "every sentient being that accepts the laws holds permanent citizenship". *Breaks:* one home society for life; leaving as deleting; moving as a property of the user. *Says:* standing in a society or an organization is the *membership's*, with its own history, and the user outlives each membership ([citizen](../organization/edges/citizen/citizen.md), [membership-and-identity](../../../bundles/government/modules/membership-and-identity/membership-and-identity.md)).

**What the domain is for, stated against the scenarios (agent, Tentative).**
- **Continuity of identity under every life event** — number changes (1), device changes (2, 9), authority changes (3, 4), death (5), moves (11). The identity is the one thing that must not change.
- **Recognition kept apart from existence** — a user may exist and be a party without being able to act (3, 4, 5, 8), and may act through several keys (2, 6, 9).
- **Acting-for as a relation, not a type** — companies, wards, heirs and bots all act through someone, with scope, period and an ending (3, 4, 5, 6).
- **Standing as events** — admission, suspension, closure, reopening, with their causes and evidence, never a flag overwritten (5, 8, 11), as [Modeling State Change as Events](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates) requires.

**The second layer (agent, Tentative).** Stepping back from "design the user record", the underlying problem the owner's sentences point at is **attributable acts with the least possible knowledge about people**: the application must know *which one* did or was affected by an act, and must survive every change in that one's life, while knowing as little as it can about who they "really" are. Verification is deliberately optional and separate ([the handoff's decision](user.handoff.md#decisions)), the membership document refuses a secret rank, and the survey's own question "kind of user: one representation?" asks for the same thing. The reading assumes the application should not itself decide who is a real person; if the owner wants the application to be the registry of real persons, scenarios 7, 8 and 10 change, and the verification capability moves toward the kernel. That is question 2.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

**Placement tests applied.** For each candidate: (a) the foundational test, *attempt to express it through the remaining abstractions* ([What Earns Foundational Status](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status)); (b) the pluggable test, an independent responsibility with its own lifecycle attaching through an explicit relationship; (c) the edge test, [an edge becomes its own module only when it carries independent semantics](../../modules.md#placement); (d) no bare edges; (e) the kernel never references `rule` directly.

**The challenge the test raises first (agent).** Does `user` itself earn kernel status? Attempt: *who requested this* is a thing named on the request; *who owns* is a thing in ownership; *recognised by* is a credential in token; *acts for* is delegate; *kind* is a loop; *standing* is a status loop. Nothing here forces fabricating or bending a foundation. [thing.md](../thing/thing.md) already speaks of "a subject or an object", so subject-hood is not new. What is left that no other module owns is **admission and answerability**: the act by which a thing comes to be treated as a *who* in this application, and the record that lets every later act be attributed to it. That is thin, and the proposal below keeps it thin. Whether a thin `user` stays a kernel domain or becomes a classification of thing is the central choice among the rival models (finding 3, M2 against M4); the repository has already placed "Animate" here, 93 outside files depend on `UserUUID` ([identity survey](../../../temp/sessions/20261014T071355Z-22804-0/report.md)), and [person](../person/protocol/model.kh) composes `user_p.Model`, which is why the proposal keeps the domain and does not claim the test proves it.

**Proposed kernel (agent, Tentative): three elements.** Each states who may create and remove it and what it triggers, because a relation without both does not exist.

| # | Element | What it is | Who may create | Who may remove | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | **Identity** | The user *is* a thing: its identifier is the thing's identifier, minted once, never reused, carrying no kind and no time | The admission event (K2) alone | No one. An identity is never removed; a user ceases through standing (K3) | Nothing by itself; it is what every other relation names |
| K2 | **Admission** | The event by which a thing becomes a user of this application: who requested it, when, under what rule, and for whom (itself, a ward, an organization) | The thing itself (self-registration), an organization's enrolment rule, a guardian for a ward, a registry process; each leaves its requester on the event | No one; an admission is history. A wrongly made admission is superseded or closed with a cause, never erased | `user_admitted`; opens the standing log (K3); for a kind that acts, starts the recognition requirement (below) |
| K3 | **Standing** | The status loops on the user and the events that move them: at least *closed* (with a cause), and the shared *block*, *lock*, *deleted-for-display* markers where they apply | The user (closing or reopening itself), the organization (suspending under its rule), a justice or fraud process (blocking), a registry event (death, dissolution); the actor and the cause are recorded | A status loop is cleared by a later event with its own actor and cause; the earlier one stays in history | `user_standing_changed`; consumers re-evaluate their rules at the next evaluation; no consumer stores a copy |

**Two requirements the kernel states but does not own.**
- **Kind is a classification, not part of identity.** The existing `(user)-[:person]->(---)` and `(user)-[:organization]->(---)` loops ([person](../person/edges-loop/person/person.md), [organization](../organization/edges-loop/organization/organization.md)) are the kernel's kind vocabulary, and the [organization document already decides](../organization/organization.md#protocol) that kind "is a label on the organization, not a type of its UUID". The proposal extends the same rule to the user: the one-byte type inside the legacy identifier ([internal/user.kh](internal/user.kh)) is retired. Several kinds at once are allowed (a sole proprietor is a person and a business).
- **An acting user must be recognisable, or must be acted for.** This replaces "each user must have a valid public key" with a form the scenarios survive (agent, Tentative; contradiction C1 below). A recognisable user has at least one current key that the application can check; a user that cannot hold one (a company, a ward, the dead, a guest) is acted for by a user that can. The *binding* of a key to a user is not the user's: a device, a bot instance and a company seal hold keys too, so the relation is needed by several domains and under [A Relation Several Domains Need Is Defined Once](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.md#a-relation-several-domains-need-is-defined-once) it is defined once, in the token domain, which already owns the credential kinds and the legacy key services (finding 6, item 3). The kernel states the requirement and consumes the `key_added` and `key_revoked` events; it holds no key.

**Not in the kernel by this proposal:** name, display name, handle, picture, phone, email, address, verification, recovery, the roles a user plays in an invoice or a contract, membership of an organization, friends, preferences, activity and presence, consent, retention. Each is below as a pluggable or as another module's relation.

**Pluggable and neighbouring capabilities.** A capability owns its relation and its processing together ([Capability Completeness](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modularity.md#capability-completeness)). "Home" says where it lives or would live; "Status" is the confidence mark.

### 3. Rival models

Four models of what the node is, then one axis that cuts across them (how far an identity reaches). Each is marked; none is Decided. The criteria are Memar's: an equivalent concept already exists (Challenging a Proposed Concept), independent lifecycle, rule ownership, and whether the model has to fabricate or bend a foundation.

- **M1 — user as the account record (the legacy bundle; Explored, not recommended).** The node is what the Go built: an identifier with a type byte and a clock, a registered phone number with a justice-block status, a display name, a user name, a picture, a domain status with six services. *For:* it is what exists and what the five services serve. *Against:* it collapses at least eight concerns into one record (identity, kind, reachability, name, picture, membership, standing, authority), which is the pattern [Rules → Scope Matters](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modularity.md#scope-matters) warns about; it makes the phone number the key to the person (scenario 1); it needs a public key for every record (3, 4, 5, 8); its `Blocked` means "had been inactive and can't be used now" ([status.kh](_import/user-domain/status.kh)) while the shared [block](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/block/block.md) means blocked "by justice (admins) or fraud detection", one word with two meanings.
- **M2 — user as a thin admitted subject (Tentative, recommended).** The node is the thing, admitted. The kernel is identity (K1), admission (K2) and standing (K3); a user that acts must be recognisable or acted for; every other fact is a relation owned by another module or a pluggable. *For:* it survives all eleven scenarios (grid below); it matches the shape the four sibling researches found (identity, one or two structural relations, events, pluggables owning relation and processing); it keeps `UserUUID`'s 93 outside consumers pointing at one concept; it makes the verification draft in the handoff a pluggable with a stable attachment point. *Against:* the kernel is thin enough that the foundational test does not prove it is a foundation (finding 2); it leaves open what `UserModel` must require (question 14); it asks the owner to give up the sentence "each user must have a valid public key" in its present form (C1).
- **M3 — user as a persona over a global subject (Explored; strongest for privacy).** There is a global *subject* (the human or the company) and, per organization or per sector, a derived *user* that is only that organization's face of the subject, in the manner of sector-specific personal identifiers (scenario 10). *For:* unlinkability is built in, not promised; the follow relation between organizations becomes an explicit mapping. *Against:* it needs a new foundational concept, the subject, that is neither thing nor user; it reverses the direction the documents already use, where person and organization are classifications *of a user* (`(user)-[:person]->(---)`), not the user a face of a person; it makes death, closure and key loss ambiguous (does the subject or the persona end?); and the subject registry is exactly the thing scenarios 7, 8 and 10 ask the application not to be. It can be offered later as a pluggable (`pairwise identity`, Deferred) without changing M2.
- **M4 — no user node: "user" is a position or a loop on thing (Explored; the Memar agency reading).** Under [Agency](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/agency.md#agent-as-a-relational-concept) an agent is relational and under [System → Abstraction](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/system.md#abstraction) "user" is perspective-bound; so `(thing)-[:user]->(---)` marks a thing that has been admitted, and the domain dissolves into thing, token, delegate and status. *For:* nothing is fabricated; it is the minimal reading of the foundational test; it agrees with [thing.md](../thing/thing.md)'s "subject or object". *Against:* admission, the recognition requirement and standing then have no owner except thing, which would grow the very record this repository is trying to keep thin (distortion of a foundation, the test's own condition); the rename costs 93 consumers; and scenarios 2 and 9 lose the module that states what an acting subject must have. It differs from M2 mostly in where the three kernel elements are filed, which is why the choice is the owner's (question 1).

**Identity scope, the axis (agent, Tentative on the default).** Independent of the four models:
- **G — one global identity per human or company**, as a national number does. Joins are free; privacy and scenario 7 suffer; a single registry must exist.
- **L — identity minted per registration, linking only by claim.** Each deployment (or each time the human chooses) mints a new thing; `same_as` (finding 6) records the human's own statement that two are one. Scenario 7 is natural, scenario 10 is the default, and G is still reachable by the human choosing to present one identity everywhere.
- **P — derived pairwise identities** over a hidden root, as M3.
The default proposed is **L**: identity is minted at admission and is never derived from a national number, a phone number or a key; joining is a visible act. It answers the handoff's fork question differently from "every domain imports the thing declaration": under L the user has no identifier *of its own kind*; it has the thing's, and the question of whether two users share it is a registration choice, not a property of the type.

**The scenarios against the models** (ok, strain, fails; agent's reading, not a measurement):

| Scenario | M1 account | M2 thin subject | M3 persona | M4 no node |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 recycled number | fails | ok | ok | ok |
| 2 shared handset | strain | ok | ok | strain |
| 3 company acts | fails | ok | strain | ok |
| 4 ward and guardian | fails | ok | ok | ok |
| 5 death | strain | ok | strain | ok |
| 6 bot | strain | ok | ok | ok |
| 7 many accounts / merge | fails | ok | ok | ok |
| 8 guest, undocumented | fails | ok | strain | ok |
| 9 key loss | fails | ok (with recovery) | strain | strain |
| 10 non-linkability | strain | ok (choice, default L) | ok | strain |
| 11 moving society | strain | ok | ok | ok |

**Recommendation (agent, unratified): M2 with identity scope L.** It is the one model with no scenario it fails and the one whose open choices are visible questions. Its weakness (a kernel too thin to prove foundational) is stated, not hidden. Items already Decided in the domain documents (application layer only; public key as the baseline method; verification not a field and not a kernel domain) are consistent with it, with the single refinement C1.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirement questions

These are requirement questions, written so that no answer is favoured where the documents leave the matter open. "Settled" marks the few that a document already answers.

**Identity**
- What must be true of the identifier: unique among users only, among all things, among all deployments? ([thing](../thing/thing.md) says one UUID "MUST be unique ID in all domains"; [user.md](user.md) says the user is known at the application layer only.)
- May an identifier ever be reused after closure, or reissued to the same human after loss? (Settled for the number case by scenario 1: a number may be reissued; whether an identifier may is not.)
- May a user's identity ever differ from its thing identity? ([handoff, "What the conversion did not settle"](user.handoff.md#what-the-conversion-did-not-settle).)
- What may an identifier carry: a time, a kind, a deployment? The legacy layout carries a clock reading and a type byte; the organization document already says kind is a label.
- Who may ask whether two users are one human, and who may answer?

**Lifecycle**
- What are the standing states, and which are reversible? Is *closed* one state with a recorded cause (own choice, death, dissolution, expiry, sanction), or several states?
- Who may move a user between states, with what evidence, and may the user object? The legacy `Blocked` ("inactive") and the shared `block` ("by justice or fraud") name different things; which does the domain mean?
- What happens at death or dissolution to the user, its keys, its acting-for relations, and what its heirs or successors may continue?
- Is erasure a state, an event on data, or outside the domain? ([handoff Q3](user.handoff.md#open-questions): immutability of evidence against revocability of the verdict and data minimization.)
- Is a *guest* a kind of user, a state of a connection, or nothing the user domain knows?

**Recognition and authorization**
- Must every acting user hold a key, or may it be acted for? (C1.)
- Who may add, rotate and revoke a key, and what stops an attacker who holds one key from replacing all of them?
- Is hardware-rooted identity a kind of key or the identity? ([user.md](user.md): "recorded here as intent, not design".)
- Is an authentication requirement Code or a per-organization Rule? (Open in [user.md](user.md).) Who decides how many factors a given action needs?
- Which part of "what may this user do" does the user domain own? The reading proposed is none: authorization belongs to [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md) and [access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md), and a *kind-set* test such as the legacy `CheckUserType` is a rule input. Is that right?
- How does acting-for reach an organization: by threshold, by office, by named person? Who records the external fact it rests on?

**Rules**
- Which rules does the domain itself need, and which belong to the organization that admits the user: a minimum age, a required kind, a uniqueness of handle, an edit window on a name ([_import/user-picture](_import/user-picture/set-user-picture.kh) has a one-change-per-day username rule), a limit on accounts per human?
- Where does each attach: to the admission, to the standing, to the key, to the reachability relation?
- Is "how many accounts one human may hold" a rule an organization may make, or forbidden by the domain?

**Events** (the domain exposes them as an EventTarget capability, [Event as a Module Capability](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability))
- Which occurrences are the user's own: admitted, kind set, standing changed, merged or split (`same_as` claimed or revoked), erased? Which belong to others: key added or revoked (token), acting-for started or ended (delegate), verdict issued (verification), reachable-at added (uri)?
- Is *authenticated* an event of the user or of the connection? (Volume: one per session, so probably the connection's; open.)
- Which events carry the requester and the cause, and which may a subscriber learn about without learning the user's identity?

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each is a candidate sketch (agent, Tentative or Explored as marked). None exists in the tree; none is created by this research.

1. **admission (kernel event, with the register service).** *Explored.* The one act by which a thing becomes a user. It records the requester, the time, the rule under which it was permitted, and for whom it was made (itself, a ward, an organization). It is also where the first key proves possession by signing the admission request. It replaces the missing "register user" service named in the handoff ("not yet designed"). Created by the thing itself, an enrolment rule, a guardian or a registry process; never removed; superseded or closed with a cause. Triggers `user_admitted` and the recognition requirement for a kind that acts.
2. **standing (the `status/` folder of this domain).** *Tentative.* Shared statuses (`block`, `lock`, `deleted`) plus one domain status, *closed*, with a cause drawn from a small vocabulary kept as data (own choice, death, dissolution, expiry, sanction). The cause is a parameter, not a new status ([parameters versus semantics](user.handoff.md#decisions)). Cleared by a later event with its own actor; the previous standing stays in history. Triggers consumers' re-evaluation at their next evaluation; no consumer stores a copy.
3. **credential binding (in the token domain, used by user).** *Tentative on the shape, Explored on the home.* `(user)-[:holds]->(token)` where the token is a public key, a one-time-code seed, a card or a passkey. Owns proof of possession, rotation, revocation that keeps the key for verifying the past, and the refusal to leave a user with no current key by an administrator's act. Defined once for any thing that holds a key (device, bot instance, company seal) per [A Relation Several Domains Need Is Defined Once](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.md#a-relation-several-domains-need-is-defined-once). Seeded by the three legacy services under `key_public/services/user-public-key`.
4. **recovery (pluggable `modules-plugable/identity/recovery`).** *Explored.* Trusted contacts or an authority recorded by the user while it can authenticate; a request by a claimant opens a case with a delay and a notice to every old device and every recoverer; a threshold of approvals binds a new key and revokes the old. The delay and the threshold are rules of the organization. Directly answers [user.md Future possibilities](user.md#future-possibilities). Wrinkle: `modules-plugable/identity/` holds identity *kinds* today ([handoff](user.handoff.md#capability-draft) notes the same).
5. **same-as (an edge, probably thing-level).** *Explored.* `(user)-[:same_as]->(user)`: a claim that two users are one human or one company, with its evidence and who made it. Needed by scenario 7 (merge, undo a wrong merge) and by scenario 10 (opt-in linking). Never an overwrite: a later claim supersedes, and the two histories remain distinct. Created by either user when it can act, or a verification process; both consent unless a ruling orders it.
6. **reachability (pluggable over the uri pluggables).** *Tentative.* `(user)-[:reachable_at]->(uri)` with a validity period and a proof of present control: phone, email, postal address. Owns the one-time-code check (the legacy register service), the reassignment of a number (scenario 1: the old claim ends, nothing else moves) and the "blocked" status on the *number*, not on the person. Triggers a notification to the old holder on reassignment, where a channel exists.
7. **representation scope (an amendment to rights/delegate, not a new module).** *Explored.* The acting-for relation needs three things the scenarios ask for and the delegate documents do not yet state: a **threshold** (two of three signatories, scenario 3), an **ending cause** (age, court order, death; scenario 4, 5) and a **scope** by matter. Whether delegate takes them or a new pluggable does is the delegate handoff's own open question 4 (record or lifecycle).
8. **retention and erasure.** *Deferred.* The legal duty to keep evidence against a person's request to be forgotten, with the mechanism that satisfies both by shredding the profile data and keeping identity, standing and signatures. No home is proposed because it touches law and knowledge as much as identity; recorded so it is not rediscovered.
9. **pairwise identity.** *Deferred.* The persona model M3 as an optional capability for deployments that must prevent joining, built on `same_as` being absent by default. Offered only if the owner answers question 2 toward unlinkability.
10. **`UserModel` requirement.** *Explored.* What a user must offer beyond a thing: the standing accessor, the recognition requirement as a question the model can answer ("is this user recognisable now?"), and the kind loops. Not written; question 14.
11. **kind loops for bot and app.** *Tentative.* `(user)-[:bot]->(---)` and `(user)-[:app]->(---)` next to the existing `person` and `organization` loops, if the owner keeps the identity pluggables as classifications until a consumer exists (catalogue P9 default). Each would need its own who-may-set: the kind is set by the admission and changed only by an event that names the cause.

### 7. Test scenarios

Written as given, when, then, so that another session can turn them into tests of the Khayyam model once one exists. Status in brackets is the proposal's own mark.

1. **Identity survives a number change (K1, reachability) [Tentative].** Given a user with a registered number N. When N is reassigned to another person who registers it. Then the first user's identity, standing and history are unchanged, the first user's claim to N ends on the date of reassignment, and the second registration is a different user.
2. **The identifier carries no kind and no time (K1) [Tentative].** Given two users admitted at the same instant, one a person and one a bot. When their identifiers are compared. Then nothing in either identifier reveals kind or time, and changing the kind later does not change the identifier.
3. **A company acts only through another user (acting-for) [Tentative].** Given an organization user with no key and a delegate relation to a manager with a key. When the manager signs a contract for it. Then the act is attributed to the organization by way of the manager, both appear on the record, and the organization is not asked for a key.
4. **A threshold for an organization [Explored].** Given an organization requiring two of three signatories. When one signs. Then the act is incomplete and attributable to no one yet; when a second signs, it completes.
5. **A ward's registration is made by a guardian (K2) [Tentative].** Given a nine-year-old who needs a profile. When the guardian admits her. Then the admission records the guardian as requester and the child as the subject, and the child has no key of her own.
6. **A guardian's authority ends by an event [Tentative].** Given a ward who reaches the age of majority. When the ending event is recorded. Then future acts by the guardian for her are refused, her history is unchanged and a past act the guardian made remains valid.
7. **Death is a standing, not a deletion (K3) [Tentative].** Given a user with signed contracts. When a registry event records death. Then the standing is *closed* with cause death, every key is revoked and kept, old signatures still verify, and an heir authorized through the heir relation can continue the user's open matters.
8. **No administrator can strand a user (credential binding) [Tentative].** Given a user with one current key. When an administrator tries to revoke it. Then the revocation is refused unless another key exists or a recovery is open.
9. **A revoked key keeps verifying the past (credential binding) [Tentative].** Given a signature made before a revocation time. When it is checked after. Then it verifies as valid-at-the-time and every signature after that time with that key fails.
10. **Recovery has a delay and a notice [Explored].** Given a user who recorded three recoverers and lost all keys. When a claimant requests recovery. Then every old device and every recoverer is notified, no key is bound before the delay passes, and two approvals bind a new key and revoke the old.
11. **Hardware key is one key among several (credential binding) [Tentative].** Given a user with a hardware-bound key on a lost device and a second key elsewhere. When the first is revoked. Then the user stays recognisable and the identity is unchanged.
12. **A shared device switches user by authentication (scenario 2) [Tentative].** Given a device with three users. When the second authenticates on it. Then acts on that device are attributed to the second and not the first, and the device holds no standing.
13. **Several users for one human are allowed (scenario 7) [Tentative].** Given a human who admits two users with no `same_as` claim. When either is queried. Then nothing reveals that they are one, and neither can act for the other.
14. **A wrong merge is undone without erasure (same-as) [Explored].** Given two users claimed the same and then the claim revoked as wrong. When both histories are read. Then they are distinct again, the claim and its revocation are visible, and nothing was deleted.
15. **A guest is not a user (scenario 8) [Tentative].** Given a connection that has not authenticated. When it asks for a service allowed to guests. Then it is served as a connection with no user, and no user is created until an admission occurs.
16. **A user with no verdict can still be served (scenario 8) [Tentative].** Given a user admitted with no verification verdict. When a service requires none. Then it proceeds; when a consumer's rule requires a verdict, it refuses that action only.
17. **Closed is not blocked (K3) [Tentative].** Given a user who closes itself and another blocked by a justice process. When each is read. Then the first is *closed* with cause own-choice and may reopen itself, the second is *blocked* and only the blocking authority can clear it.
18. **The standing change is an event with an actor and a cause (K3) [Tentative].** Given any standing change. When the log is read. Then each entry names the actor, the cause and the time, and the earlier standing remains readable.
19. **A kind-set test is a rule, not a type (kind loops) [Tentative].** Given a service allowed to persons and organizations. When a bot calls it. Then it is refused by the rule that tests the user's kinds, not by a type embedded in the identifier.
20. **A user's kind can be several and can change by event (kind loops) [Tentative].** Given a sole proprietor admitted as a person. When the business is registered. Then the user also carries the business kind, the identifier is unchanged and the event names who made the change.
21. **A mention of a closed user shows a tombstone (events) [Tentative].** Given content that mentions a user later erased. When the content is read. Then the mention resolves to the user's identity and shows that the user has closed or been erased, and does not resolve to a reassigned identifier.
22. **Unlinkability by default (scenario 10, axis L) [Tentative].** Given a human with a hospital user and a bank user on two deployments and no `same_as`. When either organization queries the other. Then no common identifier is returned; when the human makes the claim, the link exists only for the parties the claim names.

### 8. Open questions for the owner

Ordered by what they change. Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. **The first three change what the domain is.**

1. **Must a user be able to act, or may it be only a subject?** Default: a user is an admitted subject; an acting user is recognisable (holds a current key) or is acted for. This replaces "each user must have a valid public key" with that sentence for acting users. Blocks: the authentication section of [user.md](user.md), `UserModel`, admission of organizations, wards and the dead, and the delegate scope of finding 6 item 7.
2. **Is a user's identity minted per registration (several users per human allowed, linked only by claim), or is one human or company one user everywhere?** Default: minted per registration, with `same_as` as an explicit claim (axis L). Blocks: the `UserUUID` fork ([handoff](user.handoff.md#the-uuid-is-one-concept-under-many-names)), the cross-organization [follow](../thing/edges/follow/follow.md) and the product-identity question in [organization handoff item 6](../organization/organization.handoff.md), the uniqueness the verification pluggable may assume, and whether the persona capability is ever built.
3. **Where does the key-to-user binding live, and is a hardware key ever the identity?** Default: the token domain owns the binding for any thing; a hardware-bound key is one kind of key and never the identity; recovery is a pluggable. Blocks: [user.md](user.md)'s unresolved CPU-key question, recovery, bots with many instances, and the three legacy key services' destination.
4. **Is the kind a loop and not part of the identifier?** Default: yes; the identifier carries no kind and no time; the type mask is a derived selector. Blocks: retiring `dt-type.kh`, `dt-id.kh`, `internal/user.kh`, and the survey's Q4.
5. **Is *closed* one state with a recorded cause, and is *blocked* only the justice or fraud status?** Default: yes to both; the legacy `Blocked` ("inactive") becomes closed with cause inactivity. Blocks: the `status/` folder, the shared `block` loop's meaning, the legacy lifecycle services.
6. **Are the legacy `UserDomain` statuses the user's own or its membership of a society?** Default: membership (the status texts say "in this society"), so they move to [citizen](../organization/edges/citizen/citizen.md) and only *closed* stays. Blocks: the destination of [_import/user-domain](_import/user-domain/status.kh); this reading is the agent's inference and the owner may know otherwise.
7. **Who may admit a user?** Default: the thing itself, an organization's enrolment rule (including [invite](../../../modules-plugable/management/invite/invite.md)), a guardian for a ward, or a registry process, each leaving its requester. Blocks: the missing register service and the admission event.
8. **Is a guest a kind of user or a state of a connection?** Default: a connection's state; no user exists until admission. Blocks: the `Guest` and `Unset` constants and the rule that services are "allowed to guests".
9. **How does an organization act, and where is the authority it rests on recorded?** Default: only through other users by acting-for; the external fact (a gazette, a seal certificate) is a verification-capability record with dates. Blocks: organization admission, signing, and the first two handoff questions on authority registration.
10. **What happens to a user at death or dissolution?** Default: standing *closed* with cause; keys revoked and kept; heirs act through the heir relation; nothing is deleted. Blocks: the dead scenario, ownership's heir edge, retention.
11. **Is erasure a state, an event on data, or outside the domain?** Default: an event on the profile data that keeps identity, standing and signatures. Blocks: [handoff Q3](user.handoff.md#open-questions), the mentions tombstone, any retention capability.
12. **Where do per-user preferences live (mute, receipts off, who may mention me)?** Default: with the capability that reads them, per Capability Completeness, as a relation the user sets (an acquired edge, not a shortcut). Blocks: content receipts and limits, the "Labels to find users" idea, and the notion of a user settings record.
13. **Is the Go's "domain" in `UserDomain` a society, an organization or a deployment?** Default: society or organization (membership). Blocks: question 6 and the `ChangedDomain` verb.
14. **What must a `UserModel` require beyond `ThingModel`?** Default: the standing accessor and the answer to "recognisable now?"; the kind through loops. Blocks: any `UserModel` declaration (rule 6) and `PersonModel`, `OrganizationModel`.
15. **Does the application keep a derived "labels to find users" lookup?** Default: derived, as an analytics query; stored only if read cost demands, and then as a shortcut naming its sources. Blocks: the backlog idea in [user.md](user.md).
16. **Does `user` stay a kernel domain, or become a loop on thing?** Default: stays a thin kernel (M2). Blocks: the folder, the 93 consumers, and `person`/`organization` composing `UserModel`. Stated last because the owner has already placed "Animate" here; the foundational test does not by itself settle it.

## Reconciliation with the four sibling researches

**Agreements (stated once, to be ratified together).**
1. **One shape.** The [concept synthesis](../../../temp/sessions/20261014T071207Z-28148-0/concept-synthesis.md) found in product, task, content and text: kernel is identity, one or two structural relations and events; every other behavior is a pluggable owning relation and processing. M2 is the same shape: identity, admission, standing; the rest pluggable.
2. **Names and handles are not identity.** [Text scenario 5](../../knowledge/text/text.research.001.md) ("a username that changes hands") and this research's scenario 1 are the same failure at two layers: a string or a number used as the key to a someone. Both conclude that the stable identifier is a thing's and that the string is a relation with a period.
3. **A mention resolves to an identity, not a string.** [Text TM4 / reference-resolution](../../knowledge/text/text.research.001.md) and [content mentions and receipts](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md) both create `(x)-[:mentions]->(user)`. That requires K1 (never reused) and creates the tombstone requirement of test 21: a mention of a closed or erased user must not resolve to anything new.
4. **The actor is not the speaker or the answerer.** Content separates the requester from the identity a content speaks as (an agency employee posting for a brand); this research's acting-for separates the one who signs from the one who answers. One relation serves both.
5. **A software agent is a first-class delegate and accountability stays with the principal.** [Task question 10](../../management/task/task.research.001.md#open-questions) (default yes and yes) is scenario 6 and question 9 here. The user-type question the task research left in delegate is answered by the kind-loop proposal.
6. **Eligibility from outside, no flags on the host.** [Product](../../economics/product/product.research.001.md) refuses a prescription flag and uses a rule; this research refuses a `kyc` field or a `verified` flag on the user and uses the verification pluggable read by the consumer's rule.
7. **Participation retires into Group, Role and delegation.** Task's default for the four ownership files matches finding 5 (those files are not user relations).
8. **A change is a new fact that supersedes.** Content's immutable revisions, text's `supersedes`, and this research's `same_as` and standing events all keep the earlier fact readable.

**Contradictions across domains.**
- **X1 — identity that carries information.** The legacy user identifier carries a time and a kind; the [organization document](../organization/organization.md#protocol) decides a kind is not part of the identifier; the text research derives an identity from content; the content research keeps persistent work-level identity. These cannot all be "an identity". Recorded; proposal for the user only (K1).
- **X2 — who owns "who".** Content's "author", task's "assigner", invoice's eleven roles and contract's eight each define a party. Under the proposal the user owns none of them. The parts do not yet agree on whether a role is an edge from the document to the user or from the user to the document (the invoice `user-agent` document records both directions, C7).
- **X3 — what a deletion means.** Text keeps an erasure event that preserves the fact it happened; content keeps seals and withdrawals; this research defers erasure. Three domains need one answer to the retention question (question 11).

## Contradictions found

Reported, not resolved. Each names the documents that disagree.

- **C1 — "each user must have a valid public key" against the other documents.** [user.md](user.md) says every user has a key; the same repository has organization users (a company cannot hold one), wards, the dead, guests, and bot instances (the key is the instance's). The proposal refines the sentence for *acting* users; the sentence itself is not edited here.
- **C2 — identifier layout against the organization decision.** [internal/user.kh](internal/user.kh) puts a type and a clock reading inside the identifier; [organization.md](../organization/organization.md#protocol) says kind is a label, not a type of its UUID; [thing](../thing/thing.md) calls the UUID the base identifier for every domain.
- **C3 — where person and organization sit.** [thing.md](../thing/thing.md): "Animates will introduce in `user` and its subdomains as `organization`, `person`". The folders put `person` and `organization` beside `user` under `identity/`. [person.md loop](../person/edges-loop/person/person.md) and [organization loop](../organization/edges-loop/organization/organization.md) make them classifications *of a user*. Subdomain, sibling and loop are three readings.
- **C4 — the meaning of `Org` in the type mask.** [dt-type.kh](protocol/dt-type.kh) describes `Type_Org` as "other applications", while the organization domain defines an organization as any collective non-person identity. The comment and the domain disagree.
- **C5 — application layer only against cross-organization identity.** [user.md](user.md) scopes the user to the application layer; the [organization handoff](../organization/organization.handoff.md) and the [follow](../thing/edges/follow/follow.md) relation assume a user or its identifier crosses organizations; 93 outside files use `UserUUID` across layers.
- **C6 — empty abstractions.** [UserUUID is empty](user.handoff.md#useruuid-is-empty-and-that-is-the-question) and so is `ThingModel`, which `UserModel` extends; rule 6 calls this a design smell; eleven domains already compose them.
- **C7 — direction of a relation to a user.** [invoice user-agent](../../economics/invoice/edges/user-agent/user-agent.md) records `(invoice)-[:agent_is]->(user)` and `(user)-[:agent_for]->(invoice)` in one document; [Agency](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/agency.md#the-agent_for-relationship) says the direction convention must be stated explicitly.
- **C8 — "Blocked" has two meanings.** `UserDomain_Status_Blocked` ("had been inactive and can't be used now") against the shared [block](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/block/block.md) ("by justice (admins) or fraud detection mechanism") and `PersonNumberBlockedByJustice`.
- **C9 — "one user active per instance".** [user-active](../../computer/instance_of/edges/user-active/user-active.md) is true of a single-user machine and false of an application instance serving many users; its own open question (OS-user versus app-user) is the boundary [user.md](user.md) draws by saying "application layer only".
- **C10 — the services are not user services.** [services/](services/) holds `registerPersonNumber`, `getPersonNumber`, `getPersonNumberStatus`, an error for a number already registered and a bulk SMS send; none registers a user; the handoff's "Register new user ... not yet designed" is still true.

## Recommendation (unratified)

Adopt, as the working proposal, **M2 with identity scope L**: the user is the thing, admitted; the kernel is identity (never reused, no kind, no time), admission (an event with a requester) and standing (status loops with a cause); an acting user is recognisable or acted for; credential binding lives in the token domain; recovery, verification, acting-for, reachability, membership, same-as and retention are capabilities owning their relations and processing. Do the work in this order, none of it before the owner answers questions 1 to 3:

1. Answer questions 1 to 3 (what a user is; identity per registration or global; where the key binding lives).
2. Write the user domain's Model and Policies from the three kernel elements, amend the authentication sentence (C1), and add `status/` for standing; do not write a `UserModel` before question 14.
3. Settle the `UserUUID` fork under question 2 and sweep the consumers as the organization handoff item 3 lists.
4. Harvest and retire the Go clue and `_import` residue per finding 5, after the destinations (reachability, membership, delegate, text, uri, notification) exist or are ruled out.
5. Open the credential-binding and recovery sketches in their own homes.
