## Findings

### 1. What the domains are for

#### A. Estate — the plain statement (agent, Tentative)
The estate domain gives every other domain one answer to a question each of them keeps asking and keeps answering alone: **which place is this about, and how does it relate to the other places?** Evidence that the question is asked by many, found by search and read at the file: the department's `Office` and `Meeting` ([department/edges/estate](../../management/department/edges/estate/estate.md)), a task's `Site` ([task/edges/site](../../management/task/edges/site/site.md)), a staff member's `Attendance` ([staff/edges/attendance](../../management/staff/edges/attendance/attendance.md)), a product's service area `LimitTo` and a point-of-sale's `ExactLocation` ([service/edges/estate](../../../modules-plugable/economics/service/edges/estate/estate.md)), a voucher's `Field_EstateID` ([voucher/protocol/estate.kh](../../../modules-plugable/economics/voucher/protocol/estate.kh)), an inventory notification's `WarehouseID` ([inventory handoff](../../../modules-plugable/economics/inventory/inventory.handoff.md)), an address's identifier, which the Go declared as `estate_p.UUID` ([address/protocol/dt-uuid.kh](../../../modules-plugable/uri/address/protocol/dt-uuid.kh)), and the occupancy words `Reserved`, `Occupied`, `Vacant`, `Closed` ([estate-status](../../knowledge/status/edges/estate-status/estate-status.md)). That is at least eight domains with a place pointer and no shared place identity but this one.

The statement has three jobs, kept separate because the scenarios pull them apart:
1. **The referent of "where".** A stable identity that other domains name, never a street name, an address string or a coordinate pair (coordinates and addresses are out of the domain; Decided).
2. **The subject of territorial rules.** Density, open space, shadowing, ventilation, certificates: rules that bind to an estate and to the relations between estates, not to a jurisdiction's bureaucracy ([estate handoff, Tentative design insights](estate.handoff.md#decisions)).
3. **The bearer of physical history.** An estate is built, divided, merged, rebuilt, demolished; the history is the graph (Decided: no overwrite).

**The second layer (agent, Tentative).** Stepping back from "model the plot", the underlying problem the owner's sentences point at is **making territorial and economic rules replaceable policies over a few stable physical referents, instead of one jurisdiction's procedure hard-coded**. The positions that follow from it are the Decided ones: two estates (land, building), observations not properties, runtime qualification. The reading assumes the domain should *not* decide what is physically real; it records what an organization declares and keeps physical evidence beside it. If the owner wants the domain to be the authority on what physically exists, question ES.3 changes and the kernel gains a verification element.

**The challenge the test raises first (agent).** Does `estate` earn a domain at all? Attempt without it: the object domain already gives any thing a boundary and a model (`coordinate_boundary_gps`, `model_volume`, `model_physical`, `coordinate_gps_on`), so "a thing with a boundary" exists; `thing` gives identity. What is left that no other module owns? (a) the *admission* of a thing as a place a rule can bind to; (b) the *declared extent*, as distinct from drawings and coordinates, because law declares extents that geometry would not (finding 1, scenarios E3 and E4); (c) the *composition and succession of extents* (parcel into parcels, building into units); (d) the rule that a change of declared extent is a new estate. None of these is data of the object domain (a boundary is a shape; a declared extent is an authority's statement about what the shape counts as), and none is carried by thing alone (its `supersedes` is the mechanism, not the rule about when it applies). The test passes weakly: the fabrication is not of a new foundation but of a bounded capability. The survey's default, that estate stays kernel (Q1), is therefore consistent with this research, with the reasons stated here rather than assumed.

#### B. Person — the plain statement (agent, Tentative)
[person.md](../person/person.md) is one encyclopedia sentence: a being with certain capacities, part of culturally established forms of social relation, "the defining features ... differ widely among cultures and contexts". The domain's folders show what it has actually been used for: **kinship** (father, mother, brother, sister), **social ties** (friendship with its circles, known, mentor, mentee, works-with, the derived degrees) and **a life record** (the Go `LifeLog`: user, a type, a start, an end, a save time, the request). The `person` loop is `(user)-[:person]->(---)`, a classification of a user. The plain statement the research proposes: **the person domain holds what is true of a human as a human and of no other kind of user: the facts of a life (born, died, declared absent) and the ties that arise from being someone's child, sibling, friend or mentee.** It does not hold admission and answerability (the user's), the organism's biology (taxon's), legal capacity or ownership (rights'), names (text and uri's `name-*`), or a collective (organization's).

The challenge: does `person` earn a domain? Attempt without it: a human is a thing; being a *who* is the user's admission; being an organism of a kind is taxon's; kind is a loop. What is left is **the life** and **kinship**, which have their own rules for beginning and ending (civil registration, a presumption of death, adoption) and their own authorities — which is the independent-lifecycle test (X0 in the [synthesis](../../../temp/sessions/20261018-T195830Z-17032/base-model.md)). It passes, thinly, on those two; the social graph passes as a pluggable (finding 2B), not as kernel.

#### C. Taxon — the plain statement (agent, Tentative)
**Taxon answers "what kind of living thing is this, according to whom?"** A kind is a group an authority has circumscribed under a classification scheme; the same group may carry different ranks and even different boundaries under different schemes ("taxon concepts"), and the group is revised: split, lumped, renamed. The individual that belongs to the kind is an organism, a thing in its own right with a life. The domain's folders point at this reading without stating it: ten rank loops `(taxon)-[:taxonomic_rank_*]->(label(taxonomic_rank))`, so a taxon *has* a rank as a label; `organism`, `plant`, `virus`, `eukaryote` as groups; `gender` and `sexuality` as the odd ones out, which describe individuals and mostly humans, not groups.

The challenge: does taxon earn a kernel? Attempt without it: "a person is a human, a pet is a dog, this crop is wheat, this animal may not be moved" are all classification of a thing by a label that rules attach to; nothing forces a new foundational abstraction. What taxon adds is **the scheme-and-authority structure of a biological kind and its revision history**, which a general application needs only when an organization works with living things (health, agriculture, food, ecology, a veterinary or quarantine rule). That is the removal test for a pluggable, not a kernel (finding 2C). This differs from the survey's default (Q1: estate, taxon, uri, time stay kernel) and is reported as contradiction TN-C2, not resolved.

#### D. Scenarios — the place (eleven)
Each states what it breaks. Illustrations are from general knowledge and are not looked up.

**E1. A shrine rebuilt every twenty years on alternating plots (Ise, Japan; the same pattern for many sacred buildings).** The building is replaced by a new one, of the same design, on the adjacent plot; the old is dismantled; twenty years later it returns. The community says it is the same shrine. *Breaks:* the idea that a building's identity is its materials or its plot; and the Decided rule that any change makes a new estate. *Says:* the building estates B1, B2, B3 are three, each superseding the last; the land estates L1, L2 are two and recur; the "same shrine" is carried by something that is not an estate (the shrine as an institution, an organization or a thing with a seat), whose seat moves between estates. Physical continuity is not what people mean by sameness here.

**E2. A parcel divided in three, two parcels merged (cadastral practice everywhere; Iran's registers distinguish subdivision, *ifraz*, from merger, *tajmi'*; Japan's land readjustment reshuffles whole districts).** The old parcel number is retired, three new ones appear, a mortgage on the old one must now mean something on the three. *Breaks:* a one-to-one `supersedes` ([supersedes](../thing/edges/supersedes/supersedes.md) as written; thing asks whether succession may be many-to-one and one-to-many, [thing question 6](../thing/thing.research.001.md#8-open-questions)); and the assumption that a relation to the old estate silently applies to its successor. *Says:* many-to-many succession with a successor rule recorded in the event: which relations pass, to which successor, in what share (the organization research reaches the same shape for mergers).

**E3. A boundary that moves by itself (a river changing course; the shifting river islands of Bangladesh; the Chamizal strip between two countries, settled by treaty in the 1960s after a river shift).** Nobody built or demolished anything, yet the land the law attributes to a parcel changed, and in some systems only slow change (accretion) moves the line while sudden change (avulsion) does not. *Breaks:* "any change to an estate produces a new estate" read as physical change, because then nature, with no actor and no request, would mint estates; and the idea that the geometry is the extent. *Says:* the declared extent is the identity-bearing fact and an authority declares it; the observed geometry is evidence beside it; when they differ the model records a discrepancy that a rule may react to (order a survey), and no estate is created by erosion.

**E4. A house the border runs through, and an island two states share by turns (Baarle-Hertog and Baarle-Nassau, where houses are assigned to one state or the other by the front door; Pheasant Island on the French–Spanish border, held by each in alternating half-years).** *Breaks:* one authoritative boundary; one jurisdiction per estate; a stored `within` derived from geometry (the door, not the polygon, decides). *Says:* an authority's assertion overrides geometry; jurisdiction is a relation with an interval and an authority, not a field of the estate; two organizations may state different things about one estate and neither is wrong (the synthesis' X7: one global identity, organization-authoritative facts).

**E5. A place with no boundary on a map (a Mongolian herding family's winter camp and its seasonal rights; an Aboriginal Australian Country whose places are known by relation and some of whose names must not be spoken; customary land in much of West Africa and the Pacific held by lineage).** *Breaks:* estate as a bounded polygon with a coordinate; spatial relations as derived from geometry; a name as the handle. *Says:* extent may be stated by relation (rights of use, kin, ritual) with no boundary; the spatial relations must be allowed to be *acquired* because there is nothing to derive them from; and the domain must not require a name (thing: a thing need not be named).

**E6. Stacked, buried and fractional places (a Hong Kong tower where a floor is a market; an apartment, a parking space and a storage cellar each held separately; air rights sold above a neighbour's roof in New York; mineral rights below a farm held by another party; a mosque or church shared by hours).** *Breaks:* land and building as the only two layers; "the apartment is part of the building" when the apartment's owner is not the building's; "an estate is something you can stand on". *Says:* an estate may be a volume, an interval of time on a volume, or a share; ownership of any of them is the rights domain's, not estate's (Decided); the composition relation must allow many parents (a floor is part of the building and part of a market's footprint) and a time dimension.

**E7. A place that moves (a houseboat in Amsterdam, a ger that is struck and raised elsewhere, a ship under a flag, a container converted to a shop, an offshore platform, a space station).** *Breaks:* the object domain's `movable` rule "IT CAN not use for `estate`" ([movable](../object/edges-loop/movable/movable.md)), which the estate session itself called an open question, and the Decided position that what may become an estate is each organization's runtime policy. *Says:* qualification as an estate and rendering on a map are different questions; a moving estate has a declared extent relative to a moving base; its "within" relations change by event, not by identity; the `movable` rule is a default of one organization's policy, not a law of the domain.

**E8. A place that is not built and not physical (a design never built; a planned capital; a plot in a game world sold for money; a district in a map of a future).** *Breaks:* "capable of being represented as a physical environment" as the criterion, which the handoff marks Weak, and the Assumption that an estate may be virtual. *Says:* the extent is stated in a *frame* (a coordinate system, a game world, a plan); physical Earth is one frame. A design is a different thing from the estate that realizes it ([thing's lineage relations](../thing/thing.research.001.md#2-minimal-kernel-and-what-must-be-pluggable)); a virtual estate is admitted by whichever organization runs that frame.

**E9. A place that is a legal subject (the Whanganui River in New Zealand and the Ganges and Yamuna, granted legal personhood by courts or statute; Ecuador's constitutional rights of nature).** *Breaks:* the Decided exclusion of ownership and law, if "who may sue for the river" lands in estate; and the boundary between estate and organization. *Says:* the river estate is one thing, its guardians and its standing are another (the legal person is an organization or a user recognised by a society; [organization research, scenario 4](../organization/organization.research.001.md)); estate keeps only the extent, which for a river is dynamic (E3).

**E10. A place destroyed and rebuilt (Warsaw's Old Town, rebuilt after 1945 from drawings, paintings and survivors' memory; Bam's citadel after the 2003 earthquake; Notre-Dame after the 2019 fire).** *Breaks:* every criterion of identity by matter. *Says:* under the Decided rule each rebuilding is a new estate superseding the destroyed one; a heritage listing or a name or a tourist's belief that it is "the same" is a relation to the *place as a concept* (a named, culturally persistent referent), which is not any one physical estate (Explored, model M4 in finding 3).

**E11. A settlement no register recognises (informal neighbourhoods in many cities, such as Kibera or Dharavi; Iran's informal peripheral settlements; squatted buildings).** People live there, deliver there, pay for services there. *Breaks:* an estate stated only by a land authority; one authoritative declaration. *Says:* any organization may admit an estate by its own runtime policy (Decided); a humanitarian agency, a utility and the municipality may each state a different extent of the same settlement; the domain keeps all statements and the authority of each, and never merges them silently.

#### E. Identity through change — the known contradiction, argued through scenarios (agent, Tentative)
The contradiction ([synthesis C2](../../../temp/sessions/20261018-T195830Z-17032/contradictions.md); [thing research, choice 4](../thing/thing.research.001.md#3-rival-models)): estate says a change makes a new thing and `supersedes` links them; text and content say one identity survives revision; organization and group say rename and change of officers keep the identity while merger and split create successors. The thing research proposes both mechanisms with a per-relation owner and the test *every reference to the thing would still be true if read after the change; otherwise the change ends it*. The scenarios let that test be run on estate:

| Scenario | The change | Which references stay true after it | Verdict under the test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wall moved** (a house extended into its yard; the handoff's wall-moving case) | declared extent changes | a mortgage on "the house", the postal address, a resident's `lives_in`, the electricity contract stay true; a 1995 photograph, a safety certificate issued for the old walls, the as-built plan, a sale contract for the old floor area stop being true of the new thing | **Both kinds of reference exist on the same change.** The change ends the identity for the second kind and not for the first |
| **Repainted** | none of extent, composition or use | everything | no change of identity; and this is a change the Decided sentence "any change" would otherwise seem to include |
| **Tenant leaves** | none; an observation over a relation changes | everything | no change; Decided: occupancy is not a property |
| **Parcel divided** (E2) | one extent becomes three | the mortgage is true of what? | needs the successor rule: it passes, in shares |
| **River moves** (E3) | observed geometry only | declared extent unchanged | no change until an authority declares |
| **Shrine rebuilt** (E1) | a new building on a different plot | the shrine's rites are true; the old timber is not | the *building* ends; the *institution* continues; two different referents |
| **Born in Danzig** (a person's `born_in` to a city later renamed and re-governed) | the city estate is superseded by successors | "born in that city at that time" is a fact about the estate as it was | must be pinned; a following reference would rewrite a birth certificate |
| **Text corrected** (the sibling case) | words change | all comments stay true | identity kept; a different relation, not a different rule |

Findings from the table (agent, Tentative): (1) *the Decided rule and the persistent-identity domains do not actually collide*, because they answer different relations. What collides is the unstated question of **which facts of an estate are identity-bearing**. The Decided text names no such list; it names only what is *not* a property (observations over relations). Reading "any change" as any change of extent or composition is consistent with it; reading it as any physical change would make a repaint a new estate and would be the one domain in the repository with a new identity per edit (question ES.1). (2) *Every reference needs a mode, pinned or following*, set by the relation's owning domain and carried by the successor rule on the succession event; without it, a succession silently changes what a reference means. (3) *The sameness people feel* (shrine, old town) is usually carried by something that is not the estate, and the estate model is not wrong for not carrying it. These are proposals; the Decided rule is untouched.

#### F. Scenarios — the human (seven)

**H1. Fathers who are not one man (fraternal polyandry in Tibetan and Nepali Himalayan communities; adoption everywhere; gestational surrogacy with a different genetic mother; a donor's child; the Islamic category of milk kinship, which creates marriage prohibitions between children nursed by one woman; kinship vocabularies that name paternal and maternal elder and younger siblings separately, as Chinese does).** *Breaks:* `father_of` and `mother_of` as two edges with a built-in meaning, and `brother`/`sister` as stored relations: the relation that matters (who may marry, inherit, consent, be a guardian) is a *kind* of parentage that each society defines, and a sibling is derived from shared parents. *Says:* parentage is one relation with a kind and an authority and an interval; siblings and cousins are discovered.

**H2. A person who is not, and may never be, a user (a grandmother who died before the application existed and is the root of a family tree; a newborn; a person who refuses an account; a missing person).** *Breaks:* the loop `(user)-[:person]->(---)`, which makes personhood a classification of a user, and so every kinship edge between two persons that are not users. *Says:* the human is a thing first; being a user is a separate admission (the user research: "kind is a classification, not part of identity"); the person classification attaches to the thing.

**H3. Death, absence and a return (courts in many systems declare a missing person dead after a statutory period; a person declared dead and found alive; a hospital record of brain death with a body still supported; a reincarnation tradition, as in the Tibetan recognition of a successor to a lama, where a person ends and an office continues).** *Breaks:* a single "date of death" field and an irreversible tombstone. *Says:* life events are facts stated by an authority with an effective time and a recorded time (the synthesis' X4), reversible only by a later fact; "living" is discovered; what continues after an end (an office, an estate in the law's sense, a name that may no longer be spoken) is another thing's.

**H4. Born where the place no longer exists, or nowhere (a person born in Danzig, Leningrad, Czechoslovakia or Yugoslavia; a child born on an aircraft or a ship; a foundling).** *Breaks:* `born_in` as a standing relation to a place that is read as it is today; and a mandatory estate endpoint. *Says:* a birth place is a pinned reference to the estate as it was, optional, with the authority's own words when no estate exists (the Danzig row in the identity-through-change table).

**H5. Who a person is in law is not what a person is (the Whanganui River, a Hindu temple idol held by courts to be a juristic person that owns land, a company; contrasted with a person with no registration at all, a stateless child, a person in witness protection whose two identities must not be linkable).** *Breaks:* [person.md](../person/person.md)'s sentence, which includes "legal responsibility" and so, in law, includes the company; against [organization.md](../organization/organization.md), which makes an organization "any collective, non-person identity"; and the Go `PersonModel`, which composes `TaxonModel` (a person is an organism) and `UserModel`. *Says:* the repository's *person* can only mean the natural human individual if the other two are not to contradict it (contradiction PS-C1); the legal-person cases are organizations recognised by a society.

**H6. Gender, a marker and a body (hijra in South Asia, muxe in Oaxaca, fa'afafine in Samoa and two-spirit in Indigenous North America as social categories; legal change of sex marker permitted by court in Iran, a third legal marker recognised in India after a 2014 ruling and in Germany after 2017–2018; a marker changed twice in one life, with a diploma issued under the first).** *Breaks:* `gender` as a value list attached to a taxon (a group of organisms has no gender, an individual has a sex, a person has a gender identity and a legal marker, which are four different facts); and any overwrite of the marker, because earlier documents must still resolve. *Says:* biological sex is a trait of an organism, with time (scenario T4 below); self-identified gender is a fact the person states; the legal marker is a fact an authority states, with history; none is identity.

**H7. Ties that are not "friendship" (guanxi in China and wasta in the Arab world, ties of obligation and favour; a patron and a client; a mentor who does not know the mentee has listed them; an acquaintance one wants to hide; a blocked stalker).** *Breaks:* `friendship` as the one symmetric tie, "automatic labels that user can't assign by itself" ([friendship](../person/edges/social_connections/friendship/friendship.md)), and a precomputed degree graph as harmless: a derived "second connection" is itself a disclosure. *Says:* a tie has a kind, a direction, a visibility, an authority that may set it; the circles and degrees are discovered and are never authority for access (the family document already says so); sensitivity is a label and a rule (the synthesis: "sensitivity is the thing-labels registry plus a rule, not a field in any domain").

#### G. Scenarios — the kind of living thing (six)

**T1. A name that changes what it names (Brontosaurus described in 1879, folded into Apatosaurus in 1903, reinstated by a 2015 study; a species split into two, or two lumped; a plant moved to a new genus; a virus species renamed).** *Breaks:* a taxon as a stable record with a name, and nine rank loops that treat a rank as intrinsic. *Says:* a taxon is an assertion by an authority under a scheme, and the same name can refer to different circumscriptions ("taxon concepts"); revision is many-to-many succession plus `same_as`/`distinct_from` ([thing, TH5](../thing/thing.research.001.md#2-minimal-kernel-and-what-must-be-pluggable)); a rule bound to "the taxon" must say under which scheme and as of when.

**T2. Kinds that matter to a rule but are not taxa (pork under halal and kosher rules; the sacred cow in much of India, whose slaughter law differs by state; a weed; a pest; livestock; crops; an allergen group such as "tree nuts", which crosses botanical families and excludes the peanut, a legume; "fish", which some traditions apply to sea creatures a biologist would not).** *Breaks:* the assumption that a biological taxon is the kind a rule needs. *Says:* a functional kind is a classification an organization declares by rule over organisms (and over products made from them); taxa are one input to it, not the same thing ([Modeling → Classification Emerges From Rules and Relations](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels)).

**T3. One tree (the Sycamore Gap tree beside Hadrian's Wall, felled in 2023, and the saplings grown from it; a Tree of Life in a desert village; a bonsai handed down four generations).** An individual organism with a name, a place, a story, an owner, a death that is an event. *Breaks:* the domain having no individual: `taxon.md`'s first sentence says "living things", and the folders have rank labels, no organism. *Says:* an organism is a thing; it is related to a taxon, it stands at an estate (E2/E8 relation), it ends by a tombstone, and its offspring are `derived_from` it; the taxon is not the tree.

**T4. Sex that changes or is not binary (a clownfish that changes sex when the dominant female dies; species that reproduce without a male; people born intersex).** *Breaks:* `gender` as a taxon attribute with the candidate values `Male`, `Female`. *Says:* sex is a time-varying trait of an organism, stated by whoever observes it with an authority, an event per change; for people it is one fact beside the self-identified gender and the legal marker (H6), never a precondition for identity or for being a user.

**T5. Where "organism" ends (viruses replicate only inside cells and are excluded by the domain's own organism text yet have a folder; the human microbiome; a lichen, which is two or three organisms acting as one; a clonal forest that is one genetic individual spread over many trunks).** *Breaks:* a bright line between organism and not, and between one and many. *Says:* the model must not decide the line; an authority's circumscription decides, and a count unit of "one" for a living thing (thing's `count_unit`) is a context question.

**T6. A classification whose data is dangerous (sexual orientation recorded as one of 35 label constants in `user-sexuality.kh`; states that criminalise same-sex relations; an analytics module that counts users by gender per UTC minute).** *Breaks:* a taxon-domain attribute that anyone with read access to the graph can join with a user; the principle that the application should know as little as it can about who people are (the user research, second layer). *Says:* sexuality is not part of any identity kernel; where it is stored at all it is optional, consented, labeled sensitive, and subject to the erasure principle (X1); a count is discovered data and its smallest cell is itself a disclosure.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

**Placement tests applied (agent).** (a) The foundational test: attempt to express the part of reality through the remaining abstractions ([What Earns Foundational Status](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status)), with its two guards; (b) the pluggable test: an independent responsibility with its own lifecycle attaching through an explicit relationship; (c) the edge test: an edge becomes its own module only with independent semantics ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement)); (d) no bare edges: who may create, who may remove, what it triggers; (e) the kernel never references the rule domain directly (`condition_is`, once defined by the rule domain); (f) a relation several domains need is defined once ([Placing a relation](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation)).

**Is taxon kernel? (agent, Tentative: no.)** The foundational test asks whether expressing the part of reality taxon covers forces the fabrication or distortion of another foundation. "A pet is a dog; a tree is a plant; this animal is protected" is classification by a label that a rule attaches to; thing, label and rule carry it. The need for the scheme-and-authority structure arises only for organizations that work with living things. That is a pluggable: **taxonomy** (this proposal), attached to thing, in the identity category's pluggables beside bot, society, government and business, or in knowledge. The survey's default (Q1) says all stay kernel; this is reported as contradiction TN-C2 and left to the owner (question TN.6). Its consequence: `PersonModel` should not compose `TaxonModel` (a person's species is a relation, not a requirement).

**Taxon pluggables (agent, Tentative).** *organism-trait* (sex, life stage, health as events on an organism, authority-stated; scenario T4); *functional-kind* (an organization's classification of organisms and products by rule: livestock, allergen group, halal, pest; scenario T2); *scheme-import* (a published scheme adopted read-only through thing's `follow`, never copied, with local additions allowed).

### 3. Rival models

Six choices, each separable. The order is the order in which they change what the domains are.

#### Choice 1: what an estate is
- **A1 — a classification of thing; no estate kernel (Explored).** `(thing)-[:estate]->(---)` as a bare loop, with the object domain's boundary and model carrying the rest. *For:* smallest; no new domain; consistent with "classification emerges from rules and relations". *Against:* it cannot hold the declared extent, containment, succession obligation or the admission event, which are exactly what the scenarios (E2, E3, E4) test; the loop would be a `type` column ([Edge Types](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.md#edge-types-and-their-traditional-counterparts)); eight consumers would still need a shared referent.
- **A2 — a located thing with a polygon (Explored; the generic geographic-information pattern, general knowledge, unverified).** An estate is a feature with a geometry and attributes. *For:* immediately useful for maps and spatial queries. *Against:* the Decided exclusion of coordinates; E5, E7 and E8 have no geometry or no Earth; the geometry would be taken as the extent and E3 and E4 fail; "Resist defaults that arrive with the medium" ([Cognition](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)).
- **A3 — an admitted thing with a declared extent, authority-asserted containment, and succession on every change of either (Tentative, recommended).** The kernel of finding 2A. *For:* keeps every Decided statement true (two estates, observations not properties, immutable identity, multi-typing, runtime qualification, exclusions); lets the scenarios resolve; geometry is evidence, not fact; gives rule 6 a requirement. *Against:* the identity-bearing list (ES.1) is the owner's to settle; every reference needs a mode; the amount of declaration is higher than for a plain polygon.
- **A4 — a place concept with physical realizations (Explored).** Two layers: a persistent named *place* (Notre-Dame, the Ise Shrine, the Bazaar of Tehran) and the immutable estates that realize it over time. *For:* carries E1 and E10 without making the institution do the work; a name, a heritage listing and a pilgrimage attach to something persistent. *Against:* two identities for one site; the place concept is not physical, so it is closer to an organization or a named thing; it reintroduces a persistent identity the Decided rule did not want for estates. Its function is available without the layer: the persistent referent is an ordinary thing with a `situated_at` role "seat". Recorded as Explored, to be revived if scenarios need a persistent place without an institution.

How each meets the scenarios (agent judgment): A1 meets E8 and E7 and fails E2, E3, E4. A2 meets E2, E6 and fails E4, E5, E8. A3 meets all eleven in outline at the price of ES.1, ES.2, ES.4. A4 meets E1 and E10 better than A3 and adds a layer for the other nine.

#### Choice 2: identity through change (the known contradiction)
- **C1 — a change makes a new estate; `supersedes` links them (Decided in estate.md).** *For:* no overwrite to defend; the history is the graph. *Against (unresolved by this research):* every reference must follow supersession or be pinned; what "change" ranges over is unstated.
- **C2 — one identity, a change is an event (Explored for estate; the text and content stance).** *For:* references stay valid. *Against:* contradicts the Decided rule; identity can drift until the thing is another (E1, E10); a boundary that changes by event contradicts E3's "no estate is created by erosion" only if the event is physical; it does not contradict it if the event is an authority's declaration — so C2 differs from C1 in whether the *successor is a new identity*, not in whether history is kept.
- **C3 — both, chosen per relation, with the test and a successor rule on the succession event (Tentative, recommended, and consistent with C1 as Decided).** Estate keeps C1 for the *estate's own identity-bearing facts* and gives every reference a mode (pinned or following), set by the relation's owner and carried in the succession event as a successor rule, as the organization research already does. For references that are pure pointers (address, a task's site) the rule is "following, resolved at read time"; for references that carry an obligation (a mortgage, a lease) the rule is recorded with the event ("nothing passes silently"). *For:* makes the contradiction a per-relation matter; no estate domain edit is needed; E1, E2, E3 and the Danzig row resolve. *Against:* complexity moves to the successor rule; two ways to resolve a reference (read time, recorded) need one statement.

#### Choice 3: spatial relations — stored, derived, or both
- **R1 — all derived from geometry (Explored).** *For:* nothing stored can go stale. *Against:* E4 (door decides), E5 (no geometry), E6 (volumes and times), E8.
- **R2 — all stored (Explored).** *For:* works without geometry. *Against:* `neighborhood` and adjacency stored for every pair is a quadratic cache; staleness; the edge-bucket rule makes a stored derivable relation a shortcut, never the source of truth.
- **R3 — by source: an authority's assertion is acquired, geometry yields discovered readings; containment may be asserted, adjacency and neighborhood are discovered (Tentative, recommended).** *For:* matches the acquired/discovered distinction and the handoff's own hint ("adjacency may be derived, not stored"). *Against:* when both exist and differ, a rule must say which governs (default: the assertion, with the discrepancy recorded).

#### Choice 4: what the 26 `type-*` loops are
- **T1 — 26 loops on estate (the status quo, Explored).** *Against:* they are not one kind of thing; see the families below; a second set of 28 would follow.
- **T2 — one loop `type` with a label vocabulary, per the survey default (Tentative).** *For:* one folder; the vocabulary is data. *Against:* it hides that the labels are of five different structures.
- **T3 — classification is an organization's statement, with a vocabulary of five families (Tentative, recommended).** Reading the 26 folder names (agent): (i) *level in a hierarchy of territory* — planet, continent, country, state, province, region, city, district, zone, block: the names and the number of levels are a society's (an Iranian *ostan*, a Japanese prefecture, an English parish are not the same level); (ii) *built form* — building, house, apartment, hall, lobby, parking, storage, warehouse, space; (iii) *a way* — way, street, boulevard, alley: a linear feature whose extent is a line with a width, per the folder's own OpenStreetMap reference; (iv) *a use under rules* — residential, commercial: zoning, a rule of a society attached to a plot, not a class of plot; (v) *the plain* — land. A warehouse is a warehouse *for an organization that admits goods to it*; the same building is a shop for another. It is a classification that emerges from a rule and a relation ([Modeling](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels)). Families (i) and (iv) are the false taxonomy of [False Taxonomies Hide Shared Structure](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.md#false-taxonomies-hide-shared-structure): useful local classes mistaken for the structure. *Against:* the vocabulary and the families are the owner's to approve; folders for individual labels stay only where processing exists (warehouse).

#### Choice 5: where the person-to-place relations live (born, lives, works)
- **P1 — three edges on estate with taxon as target, as filed (the status quo, rejected).** `(estate)-[:born_in]->(taxon)`, `live_in`, `work_in`. The direction runs from the place to the living thing; the target is a group of organisms, not a person. The survey records the defect and its default.
- **P2 — owned by person: `(person|organization)-[:lives_in]->(estate)` (Explored; the survey default, Q2).** *For:* one relation, one owner, consistent with "owned by person". *Against:* seven other domains need the same relation with other subjects (a task's site, a department's office, a staff attendance, a service area, a warehouse's admission, a voucher's area); copying it into each domain is the failure [One Authoritative Location per Concern's Data](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.md#one-authoritative-location-per-concerns-data) names.
- **P3 — one `situated_at` record on the estate side, roles as vocabulary, each consumer keeping its rules (Tentative, recommended).** *For:* defined once in the domain that covers every target; `born_in` is pinned, `lives_in` follows; residence processing (domicile versus registered address) is a person-side pluggable on top. *Against:* it departs from the survey default; role semantics could grow into a second type system; reported as contradiction ES-C3.

#### Choice 6: what a person and a taxon are
- **Person.** *PA — a subtype of user (the status quo `(user)-[:person]->`; Explored)*: fails H2. *PB — a classification of thing with life facts and parentage (Tentative, recommended)*. *PC — the human subject behind per-organization user personae (Explored; the user research's M3)*: strong for privacy, adds a global subject, which a "know as little as possible" stance resists. *PD — no person domain; a loop on user plus taxon (Explored)*: leaves life facts and kinship with no home (H1, H3).
- **Taxon.** *TA — an individual living thing (taxon.md's sentence; Explored)*: contradicts its own quotation. *TB — a group asserted by an authority under a scheme, with organisms related to it (Tentative, recommended)*. *TC — as TB plus functional kinds as a separate organization classification (Tentative, recommended with TB)*.

### 4. What the domains must own, as requirements questions
These are questions the domains' own documents must answer before implementation; each favours no answer where the repository has not settled it ([Ask rather than assume](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). A line marked *settled* cites its source.

#### Estate

##### Identity
- *Settled:* identity is immutable; any change produces a new estate; the old one is never deleted; there is no "latest version" ([estate.md](estate.md)). *Settled:* land and building are two estates; multi-typing.
- Which facts of an estate are identity-bearing: its declared extent, its composition, its parts, its materials, its use, its name? Which are not? (The Decided sentence says "any change" without the list.)
- Does a creator-made change differ from a third party's? The estate document says creator-made changes are "audit-logging semantics" and third-party or no-change forks create new identities; it does not say whether a creator-made change creates a new identity or only a log line.
- Is a part of an estate (a room, a floor, a flat) an estate of its own, or a view of the whole?
- Is a boundary-less estate (E5) admissible; is a frame (a coordinate system, a plan, a virtual world) part of the extent?
- Unique in what scope? (Inherited from thing: one global opaque identity, organization-authoritative facts, [thing question 7](../thing/thing.research.001.md#8-open-questions).)

##### Lifecycle
- Planned, under construction, realized, altered, vacated, demolished, rebuilt: which are estate events, which are another domain's (a permit, an inspection)?
- May a succession be many-to-many (E2)? Does a split pass a mortgage in shares?
- Is demolition a tombstone ([thing TH3](../thing/thing.research.001.md#2-minimal-kernel-and-what-must-be-pluggable)), a succession to "land only", or both?
- When a thing stops being an estate for one organization and stays one for another, what does the first organization's withdrawal change?

##### Authorization
- Who may declare an extent: the organization that qualifies the thing, a land registry, a court, a surveyor, the neighbour? When two declare differently, is there a rule, or is the discrepancy simply recorded (E4, E11)?
- Who may assert containment where geometry exists and says otherwise?
- Who may view an extent whose disclosure is itself a risk (a safe house, a sacred site whose location may not be given, a military installation)?
- *Settled:* ownership and law are not the domain's (→ rights); rights come first, ownership arises from rights.

##### Rules
- Which rules does the estate own and which does it only carry? (Candidates: a change of extent requires a successor; a declared extent is a reference to a boundary; both are the domain's; zoning, density and open-space numbers are policies of a society or organization.)
- What is the metric behind a word such as "density"? *Settled-Tentative:* a word naming several quantities is badly defined; one metric per rule ([estate handoff](estate.handoff.md#decisions)).
- Is "movable" a rule of the organization's policy or of the domain ([movable](../object/edges-loop/movable/movable.md))?

##### Events
- `estate_admitted`, `extent_declared`, `containment_asserted`, `estate_superseded`, `estate_withdrawn`, `discrepancy_found`: are these the domain's event vocabulary, and is estate an EventTarget naming the points a rule may attach to (before realization, before change of extent, before change of use)?
- Are valid time and recorded time both kept for an extent (a boundary settled in 2020 that was in effect from 1990)? ([synthesis X4](../../../temp/sessions/20261018-T195830Z-17032/base-model.md))

#### Person

##### Identity
- *Settled:* none beyond "the defining features differ widely among cultures and contexts" ([person.md](../person/person.md)).
- Does *person* mean the natural human individual only, or any legal subject (H5)? Is a person necessarily a user (H2)? Is a dead person still a person?
- One person, several identities (witness protection, pseudonym) and several persons, one identity (twins registered once by error): is any of it the person domain's, or only user's and thing's `same_as`?

##### Lifecycle
- When does a person begin and end, and who says so: the registry, the family, a court, a religion? Are the beginning and ending reversible (H3)?
- Is a declared absence a tombstone, a status, or a separate fact?

##### Authorization
- Who may record a birth, a death, a parentage, a marker? Who may see them? Who may erase a life fact (X1: detach identity, keep effect and fact)?
- Who may assert a tie (social graph) on another person's behalf?

##### Rules
- Which parentage kinds exist in which society, and which rights follow from each? (The kinds are data of a society; the consequences are rules.)
- Is gender a self-statement, an authority's marker, or both?
- What does the person domain refuse to know (the user research's principle: as little as possible)?

##### Events
- `person_classified`, `person_born`, `person_deceased`, `person_declared_absent`, `life_event_corrected`, `parentage_recorded`, `tie_made`, `tie_ended`: the vocabulary, and whether they are one event family with a kind.

#### Taxon

##### Identity
- *Settled:* the accessor `Field_TaxonID` returns `ThingUUID`; `TaxonModel` composes `ThingModel` only ([taxon handoff](../taxon/taxon.handoff.md#decisions)).
- Is a taxon a kind (a group asserted by an authority) or an individual (TN.1)? Is the name ever the identity?
- Is an organism a thing that the taxon domain owns (the folder `organism` exists as text only)?

##### Lifecycle
- How is a lump, a split or a rename recorded: many-to-many `supersedes`, with `same_as`/`distinct_from` (thing TH5)?
- When a scheme is published in a new edition, does every treatment supersede or only the changed ones?

##### Authorization
- Who may assert a taxon or a treatment: a code's authority, a database, an organization's own agronomist? Who may determine an organism's taxon?
- Who may record an organism's sex or other trait?

##### Rules
- Which rules bind to a taxon, which to a functional kind, which to both (quarantine, allergen, halal)?
- Is the rank an intrinsic property or a relation to a scheme (TN2)?

##### Events
- `taxon_asserted`, `treatment_published`, `organism_determined`, `trait_observed`, `taxon_superseded`.

### 6. Missing edges and modules
Each is a sketch, not a design, and each states who may create or remove it and what it triggers.

**Estate**
1. **`situated_at` (shared record).** *Tentative.* A subject thing, an estate, a role (born, resides, works, stores, serves-area, attends, seat), an interval, a declarer. Created by the subject's authorised party or the declaring organization; ended by an event. Triggers whatever the consuming domain's rule attaches at that role (an eligibility, an attendance entry, a delivery). Replaces the born/live/work edges and gives the task, department, staff, service, point-of-sale, voucher and inventory place pointers one target and one meaning; each keeps its own rule. Evidence: finding 1A.
2. **`within` (containment).** *Tentative.* Child extent lies in parent extent; many parents; an interval. Asserted by an authority or discovered from geometry; the assertion wins and a discrepancy is recorded. Triggers derived readings of adjacency, overlap and district membership. Replaces the `part` and `neighborhood` candidates.
3. **`territorial-policy` pluggable.** *Tentative.* See finding 2A. Without it, nothing in this domain says what a rule on an estate looks like; it is the handoff's Next Step 4 and the first policy to prototype.
4. **`demarcation` pluggable.** *Explored.* Surveys, tolerances, discrepancy records; the place where observed geometry is compared with a declared extent (E3).
5. **`warehouse-admission` pluggable.** *Tentative.* From `type-warehouse/edges/product`; the organization's admission decision for a product at an estate; stock stays with inventory.
6. **`jurisdiction` record.** *Explored.* An organization holds authority over an estate for a scope and an interval; covers the enclave, embassy and shared-island cases (E4) without making jurisdiction a field of the estate.
7. **`estate-classification` vocabulary.** *Tentative.* The five families of choice 4 as label registries declared by an organization; one folder per label only where processing exists.
8. **`certificate` (cross-domain).** *Explored.* The handoff's Next Step 3: issuer, scope, trust, evidence, validity; approval is not certification. Likely a capability that attaches to thing with `satisfies`; estate is one consumer.
9. **`frame` (reference system).** *Explored.* The coordinate system, plan or virtual world in which an extent is stated (E8). May be only a label on the extent; held until a second frame is in use.

**Person**
10. **`parentage` record.** *Tentative.* See finding 2B. Created by the recognising authority; ended by event; triggers guardianship and inheritance rules, discovered siblings.
11. **`life-event` family.** *Tentative.* Birth, death, absence, reversals. Created by registry, court, family or physician; the earlier stays. Triggers the user's standing check (a rule) and an effective-time read.
12. **`social-graph` pluggable.** *Tentative.* The whole `social_connections` family as one capability with tie kinds, offer and acceptance, blocking, and derived degrees.
13. **`legal-marker` for gender.** *Explored.* Authority-stated marker with history, separate from self-stated gender identity; the owner decides whether the application holds it at all.
14. **`residence` processing (person-side pluggable).** *Explored.* Domicile, registered address and habitual residence as different roles of `situated_at` with different authorities (tax, voting district); on top of the shared record, not instead of it.

**Taxon**
15. **`taxon-treatment` (scheme).** *Tentative.* A scheme with an authority and editions; a treatment states a taxon's name, rank, parent and circumscription; adopted by `follow`.
16. **`organism-determination`.** *Tentative.* An observer determines an organism's taxon with a confidence; later determinations supersede.
17. **`organism-trait`.** *Explored.* Sex and other time-varying traits of an organism as events with an observer.
18. **`functional-kind`.** *Tentative.* An organization's classification of organisms and products by rule (livestock, allergen group, pest, halal).

### 7. Test scenarios
Given/when/then; each is a test another session can write once the owner answers. Numbers in brackets refer to the findings. All are **Tentative** unless marked kept.

#### Estate
1. **A thing becomes an estate by declaration (ES2; kept: runtime policy).** Given a waterfall that no organization has declared. When it is read. Then it is a valid thing and not an estate. Given a park authority that declares it. Then `estate_admitted` names the declarer and its rule, and the identity is unchanged.
2. **Two organizations, two declarations (ES2, E11).** Given a municipality and a humanitarian agency that each declare an estate for the same informal settlement with different extents. When both are read. Then both statements and both authorities are present and neither is merged or hidden.
3. **A change of extent makes a successor (ES5, kept: Decided).** Given a house whose declared extent is extended into its yard. When the new extent is declared. Then a new estate supersedes the old, the old remains readable, and no estate is edited.
4. **A repaint changes nothing (ES.1).** Given an estate whose use, occupancy, name and colour change. When the changes are recorded. Then no successor exists, because none is a change of declared extent or composition.
5. **A river moves; no estate is created (E3).** Given a parcel whose declared extent borders a river and an observed shoreline that moves. When the discrepancy exceeds a rule's limit. Then `discrepancy_found` is recorded and the rule may order a survey; no estate is created until an authority declares a new extent.
6. **A parcel is divided (E2).** Given parcel A with a mortgage and a successor rule that passes it in shares. When A is divided into B and C. Then B and C both supersede A with their dates, A remains readable, a reader asking "what is the mortgage on now" gets it on B and C in the stated shares.
7. **A door decides (E4).** Given a house on the line between two states' parcels and an authority's assertion that it lies in one. When containment is read. Then the assertion governs, the geometry's overlap is recorded as a discrepancy, and the jurisdiction record names the authority and the interval.
8. **A birth place is pinned (H4).** Given a person born in a city whose estate is later superseded by two successor cities. When the birth is read. Then it refers to the estate as it was, and a following reference such as "lives in" for a resident who stayed resolves to the successor by the successor rule.
9. **A moving estate keeps its identity under a new `within` (E7).** Given a houseboat admitted as an estate and moored in two harbours in a year. When the mooring changes. Then no successor exists, a `within` statement ends and another starts, and the `movable` rule appears only in the declaring organization's policy.
10. **A virtual estate is admissible in its frame (E8).** Given a parcel in a game world sold between players and a planned district in a future plan. When each is declared with its frame. Then each is an estate of the declarer; nothing requires a physical Earth coordinate.
11. **A stacked estate has many parents (E6).** Given a parking space that is part of a building's basement and of a separately held car-park. When containment is read. Then both parents are present; ownership is not read from this domain.
12. **An address is not an estate (kept: Decided out of domain).** Given an address string that changes when a street is renamed. When the estate is read. Then it is unchanged and the address relation is the uri domain's.
13. **A relation is read with its mode (finding 1E).** Given a photograph pinned to an estate and a postal address following it, both attached before a wall was moved. When each is read after the move. Then the photograph shows the old estate and the address resolves to the successor.
14. **A warehouse is a warehouse for someone (T3, choice 4).** Given one building classified as a warehouse by organization X and as a shop by Y. When each organization reads it. Then each sees its own classification and the building has one identity.
15. **A refused change (rule, via `condition_is`).** Given a rule attached to "before change of use" that refuses a use below a stated open-space share. When a change of use is requested. Then the refusal names the rule and the metric, and the estate is unchanged. *(Waits for the rule domain's attachment, [synthesis RU.2](../../../temp/sessions/20261018-T195830Z-17032/decision-graph.md).)*

#### Person
16. **A person who is not a user (PE1, H2).** Given a grandmother recorded as the parent of a user, never admitted as a user. When her classification and parentage are read. Then both exist and no user record does.
17. **A parentage with a kind (PE3, H1).** Given a child with a gestational mother, a genetic mother, a legal father by adoption and a milk-kin sibling. When parentage is read. Then four records exist with their kinds and authorities and a rule decides which kind counts for inheritance.
18. **Siblings are discovered (PE3).** Given two persons with a common recorded parent. When asked who their siblings are. Then the answer is computed; no `brother` or `sister` edge is stored.
19. **A death that is reversed (PE2, H3).** Given a person declared dead by a court after a statutory absence and later found alive. When the later fact is recorded. Then both events remain, "living" is true from the later event, and a reader at a date between sees the death.
20. **Gender and marker with history (H6).** Given a person who states a gender identity and later has the legal marker changed by an authority. When a document dated before the change is read. Then it resolves under the earlier marker; nothing is overwritten.
21. **A tie that cannot be assigned (social-graph; kept: automatic labels).** Given a user who tries to give another user the label friend. When the request is made. Then it is refused until the other accepts; a derived degree is never used for authorization.
22. **Sexuality is not a default (T6, PS.7).** Given an application that has not enabled the optional capability. When a user is created and read. Then no sexuality value exists, none can be inferred, and an analytics count by gender excludes any cell below its smallest allowed size.

#### Taxon
23. **A rename is not a new kind (T1).** Given a species name reassigned by a later scheme edition. When a rule bound to the taxon is read as of two dates. Then it applies to the same circumscription before and after, or to a stated successor, and the name is never the identity.
24. **Split and lump (T1).** Given one species split into two and two lumped into one. When recorded. Then both successions are many to many, with `same_as`/`distinct_from` between concept-level pairs.
25. **A functional kind is not a taxon (T2).** Given an allergen group declared by an organization that includes organisms from several families and excludes a legume. When an organism is read. Then it belongs to the group by the organization's rule and to its taxon by an observer's determination, independently.
26. **One tree (T3).** Given a notable tree felled. When recorded. Then its tombstone and the `derived_from` link to saplings grown from it exist, and the taxon is unchanged.
27. **A trait that changes (T4).** Given a fish that changes sex. When observed twice. Then two trait events exist with observers and dates; the species is unchanged.
28. **A scheme is adopted, not copied (scheme-import).** Given a published scheme adopted by `follow`. When a new edition appears. Then the treatments update by succession; the local additions remain.

### Reconciliation with the sibling researches and the synthesis

#### Agreements
1. **The shape.** The thing, user, organization and group researches, and the synthesis' twelve domains, all reach identity plus one or two structural relations plus status as events plus an attachment plus a stated invariant ([base-model §1](../../../temp/sessions/20261018-T195830Z-17032/base-model.md)). The estate kernel (five elements), the person kernel (three) and the taxon kernel (three) fit it. Proposed invariants: *an estate's declared extent is never edited; a change is a successor* (estate); *a life fact is never overwritten; a death is reversible only by a later fact* (person); *a name is never identity; a treatment is superseded, never edited* (taxon).
2. **Kind is a classification, not part of identity.** The user research puts `person` and `organization` as loops on the user and proposes the same for bot and app; the organization research does the same. This research moves the person loop one step further (on the thing) and keeps the principle (user research K1, organization O1).
3. **Admission.** Product, user, group and account share one admission pattern (an acquired fact with declarer, time and status event). Estate's ES2 is the fifth instance. The handoff's "runtime policy of each organization" is that pattern.
4. **Succession is many-to-many and carries a successor rule.** The organization research (merger, split, rename) and the thing research (question 6) ask for it; estate's parcel division and merger (E2) is the physical case, and the successor rule is the same data. This research uses the organization research's term rather than a second one.
5. **A relation several domains need is defined once.** Thing defines `supersedes`, `derived_from`, `satisfies`; the rule domain `condition_is`; this research proposes `situated_at` in estate on the same ground ([Placing a relation](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation)).
6. **Geometry and coordinates are not identity** (estate Decided; user: handles and names are never identity; text: values are content-addressed). Each of the four treats an externally governed token (a postal address, a phone number, a username, a parcel number) as a relation with a period, not as the identity.
7. **Presentation is a view.** A map layer, a feed and a profile are views; this research keeps density, occupancy and the social degrees as discovered readings.
8. **Erasure detaches and keeps the fact** (X1). Applied to a birth place (a pinned reference), a life fact and a parentage record: the identity link goes, the fact that the event happened remains.
9. **Authority is one capability** (X3) and the **organization is the authority boundary** (X7): estate's declarations are organization-authoritative with one global identity; every `situated_at` and `jurisdiction` record is organization-authoritative.
10. **Layering.** Estate, person and taxon would sit at the same layer as user and group, *who* and *where*, reading thing and rule and read by task, product, inventory and timeline; none names a higher layer (the synthesis' rule: a lower layer never names a higher one).

## Recommendation (unratified)
The owner has not reviewed this, and nothing in it is decided.

1. **Define an estate as a thing an organization has admitted, with a declared extent in a named frame, containment by an authority's assertion (geometry as evidence), and a successor on every change of extent or composition (Tentative, medium confidence).** Five kernel elements; everything else a relation, a pluggable or a view. All Decided statements stay true.
2. **Settle which facts of an estate are identity-bearing before anything else (ES.1).** It is the blocking question and also the one whose answer dissolves most of the known contradiction.
3. **Give every reference to an estate a mode, pinned or following, carried by a successor rule on the succession event (Tentative).** Use the organization research's term; share one sentence with content's quoting.
4. **Define `situated_at` once on the estate side and retire `born_in`, `live_in`, `work_in` as filed (Tentative).** Roles are vocabulary; each consumer keeps its rules.
5. **Run the boundary suite as the table in finding 7 and stop adding `type-*` folders (Tentative).** One classification relation declared by an organization with five families of vocabulary; a folder only where processing exists (warehouse).
6. **Define a person as a natural human individual, classified on a thing and not on a user, with life facts and parentage as the kernel (Tentative).** The social graph becomes a pluggable; gender and sexuality leave taxon, sexuality as Deferred.
7. **Define a taxon as a circumscription asserted by an authority under a scheme, with organisms related to it; treat taxonomy as a pluggable, not a kernel (Tentative; Explored for the pluggable placement).**
8. **Do not move, delete, convert or rename anything on this evidence.** The mapping in finding 5 is a reading list for later sessions. `EstateModel`, `PersonModel`, `TaxonModel` and the `.kh` files wait for ES.1, ES.12, PS.1 and TN.6.

## 8. Open Questions
This is the research's eighth section. Only the owner can decide these. Ordered by what they change: those that change what the domains are first, then those that change how a part is done ([Ask rather than assume](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. The estate questions are also entered in the [estate handoff](estate.handoff.md#research-001-questions-and-next-steps-2026-10-18); the person and taxon ones are pointed to from their handoffs.

#### Estate
1. **ES.1 — Which facts of an estate are identity-bearing, so that "any change produces a new estate" has a range?** Default: declared extent and composition (parts); use, occupancy, ownership, name, address, status and condition are not. Blocks: the estate definition, the wall-moving case, the `supersedes` policy text, every consumer of `Field_EstateID`, the identity-through-change contradiction.
2. **ES.2 — Does each reference to an estate carry a mode (pinned, following), set by its owning domain and recorded in the succession event as a successor rule?** Default: yes; pointers follow, resolved at read time; references that carry an obligation are recorded with the event; evidence about a past state is pinned. Blocks: voucher, inventory, discount, address, task site, department office, staff attendance; thing's `supersedes` text; content's `quoting`.
3. **ES.3 — What makes a thing take the estate identity: the declaration of an organization (admission), and is there any hard exclusion (the `movable` rule)?** Default: only declaration, by runtime policy of each organization; no hard exclusion; `movable` becomes one organization's default. Blocks: the definition, the waterfall case, the `movable` document, `EstateModel`.
4. **ES.4 — Are spatial relations stored or derived?** Default: by source; `within` may be asserted by an authority and wins over geometry (a discrepancy is recorded); adjacency and neighborhood are discovered. Blocks: `edges/estate`, `part`, `neighborhood`, the statuses.
5. **ES.5 — May a succession be many-to-many with shares?** Default: yes. Blocks: parcel division and merger, mortgages, thing question 6.
6. **ES.6 — Where do the person-to-place relations live?** Default: one `situated_at` record on the estate side with role vocabulary, each consumer keeping its rules; `born_in`, `live_in`, `work_in` retired as filed; residence processing a person-side pluggable. Blocks: the three edges, the survey's Q2, task `site`, department `estate`, staff `attendance`, service `LimitTo`, point-of-sale `ExactLocation`.
7. **ES.7 — Are the 26 `type-*` loops a property of estate, or classifications declared by an organization in five families?** Default: the latter; a folder only where processing exists (warehouse); the vocabulary a label registry. Blocks: 26 folders, `WarehouseID`, `type-warehouse/edges/product`, inventory.
8. **ES.8 — Is the definition sentence replaced by one drawn from the positions, and is the name kept?** Default: replace; keep the name provisionally; record the collision with the law's sense. Blocks: estate.md, every consumer's wording; nothing else.
9. **ES.9 — Are Occupied and Vacant stored or discovered; Reserved and Closed acquired?** Default: discovered, acquired. Blocks: the `estate-status` move.
10. **ES.10 — When two organizations declare different extents, who governs?** Default: each is authoritative for what it states; one global identity; a discrepancy is recorded; no silent merge (X7). Blocks: cross-organization follow, territorial policy, enclave and informal-settlement cases.
11. **ES.11 — Is a virtual or unbuilt place an estate, with a physical frame only one frame among others?** Default: yes, admitted by the declarer of the frame; a design is a different thing. Blocks: the definition criterion ("capable of being represented as a physical environment"), the virtual-estate hypothesis.
12. **ES.12 — What does `EstateModel` require beyond `ThingModel`?** Default: the declared extent as a reference; held until ES.1. Blocks: `protocol/model.kh`; the inventory handoff's `Field_EstateIDs` consumer.
13. **ES.13 — Is a persistent place concept (Notre-Dame, the Ise shrine) a separate layer, or an ordinary thing with a seat?** Default: an ordinary thing; revive model A4 if a scenario needs a persistent place with no institution. Blocks: the heritage and pilgrimage cases only.

#### Person
14. **PS.1 — Is a person a classification of a thing (so a person need not be a user)?** Default: yes. Blocks: the `person` loop, every kinship edge's endpoints, `PersonModel` (which composes `UserModel`).
15. **PS.2 — Does "person" mean the natural human individual only?** Default: yes; legal persons are organizations recognised by a society. Blocks: person.md, organization.md.
