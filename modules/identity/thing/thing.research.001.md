   - *Kernel / pluggable* — [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement) and Memar's placements: kernel is what, if removed, forces a foundational abstraction to be fabricated or another distorted; a kernel module "does not reference a pluggable one"; pluggable is an optional capability that attaches through an explicit relationship, "owns its complete capability", and whose "relationship and its processing sit in one module and are never split across modules". The removal test is [What Earns Foundational Status](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status) with its two guards: the fabrication must land inside the foundational set, and the test runs against requirements that exist, not anticipated ones.
   - *Edge / loop-edge / shortcut* — [Modules → Edge buckets](../../modules.md#edge-buckets): `edges/` join two distinct nodes; `edges-loop/` classify one node; `edges-shortcut/` are derived caches and never the source of truth. *No bare edge*: an edge with no stated who-may-create-or-remove and what-it-triggers does not exist; an edge becomes its own module only when it carries independent semantics — "identity, lifecycle, authorization, rules, processing".
   - *Acquired / discovered* — [Modeling → Acquired Data vs. Discovered Data](../../../docs/modeling.md#acquired-data-vs-discovered-data): acquired is stated by an external source and cannot be recomputed; discovered is derived and is a view.
   - *Defined once* — [Placing a relation](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation): a relation several domains need is defined once "in the domain that already covers every record those domains are", and each domain keeps the rule for when its own records may take it.
   - *Thing (this domain)* — the settled statements only: the definition quoted above; ThingUUID is "the base identifier for any other domains", "MUST be unique ID in all domains", "can be same with any other UUID domains" (the Go comment carried in [dt-uuid.kh](protocol/dt-uuid.kh)); a thing's identity is the one identifier every domain reuses and no domain declares a competing one (Decided in the conversion, [handoff](thing.handoff.md#decisions)); multi-typing, "a node takes identities from several domains simultaneously (thing + product + estate + ownership)", is a confirmed position of the [estate domain](../estate/estate.md).
   - *Quiddity and haecceity* — [thing.md](thing.md#quiddity): quiddity is "the essence ... what it is", the properties a substance "shares with others of its kind"; it is contrasted with haecceity, "thisness", "a positive characteristic of an individual that caused it to be this individual, and no other".
   - *Registration event, tombstone, assertion* (agent's terms, used in this research only) — a *registration event* is the recorded fact that an identity came to exist in an organization's graph, with its time and the request that caused it; a *tombstone* is the recorded fact that an identity has ended, which leaves the identity itself in place; an *assertion* is an acquired fact that one identity says something about another. They are defined here because no Memar document names them.

## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The thing domain answers one question for every record in an organization's graph and for every organization that points at one: *what is it that can be pointed at, regardless of what anyone has yet decided it is?* It gives each such referent one identity that carries no name, no kind and no owner, and it owns the few relations that change what that identity means: that this takes the place of that, that these two are the same, and that this was made from that. Everything a domain adds — a price, a task state, a body of words — is a classification and a set of relations attached to a thing, with the rules for when it may be attached kept by that domain.

Three reasons the plain statement is the right size. First, the documents already treat it as the unclassified base node: an edge is a thing ([edge](../../knowledge/edge/edge.md#identity)), a text is a thing (text TK1), a URI "is a thing whose text is the URI string" ([uri](../uri/uri.md)), an account is a thing ([account](../../economics/account/account.md)), and about ninety `dt-uuid.kh` files restate its identifier under their own name ([edge](../../knowledge/edge/edge.md#identity)). Second, the retired `_import/quiddity-status/storage.kh` shows what the domain used to be: a record with an id, an organization, a language, a URI, a title and a status — and each of those fields has since left for the domain that owns it (title to [text](../../knowledge/text/text.md), URI to [uri](../uri/uri.md), status to [status](../../knowledge/status/status.md), language to language-human). What stayed is the identity, and the registration record `internal/storage/struct.kh` still carries: id, the domain that registered it, the time, the request. The decomposition is therefore already the history of the domain; this research states where it ends. Third, the [Attribute-or-Edge Test](../../../docs/modeling.md#the-attribute-or-edge-test) applied to the four candidates of the handoff gives, for each, a domain that already owns it (finding 3, choice 2).

**The word hides three things (agent, Tentative).** *The individual*: this mountain, this account, this edge — the haecceity, "thisness"; its identity is `ThingUUID`. *The kind*: what the individual is, which is quiddity in [thing.md](thing.md#quiddity)'s sense and which, under Memar's classification section, is not a property but what emerges from rules and relations, so it is derived and plural. *The browsing level*: [modules.md](../../modules.md#top-or-thing-levels-labels)'s "top or quiddity levels labels" are the few classifications allowed to name a module. The Go code used "quiddity" for the first (`quiddityID`), the philosophy section uses it for the second, and modules.md for the third. Under thing.md's own definition, the identifier is the individual's, so "quiddity" is the wrong name for it; this is consistent with the survey's recommended default (A2, retire "quiddity" as an identifier name) and adds the reason.

**Eleven scenarios (agent; general knowledge, illustrative).**

1. **One mountain, many names (Iran, Alaska, Nepal and Tibet, Australia).** The peak in the Zagros range called Dena in Persian ([thing.md](thing.md#subject-and-object-philosophy) names "Dena mountain"); the Alaskan peak named Denali by the Koyukon people and McKinley in a later official usage, with the official name changed by decision of authorities more than once; the world's highest peak with Nepali, Tibetan and colonial-survey names; a rock in central Australia officially dual-named. The names differ by language, by community and by decade, and the naming is sometimes political. *Breaks:* a title as a field of the record (which language, which decade, whose authority); a canonical name; the old Go record, which kept one `Title` and one `URI`. Also breaks the edge of the object: where the mountain ends is a convention. *Says:* the identity must not depend on any name; every name is a relation to a text with its own language, authority and period; the thing exists with zero names (it is "a thing one need not give a specific name to"). Supports finding 3 choice 2 (name not required).

2. **A bus route that is renumbered and rebuilt (any city; Tehran, London, Mexico City).** Route 73 runs for fifteen years; every bus is replaced, half the stops move, the number is reassigned in a network reform, and a different route later gets number 73 ([subject](edges-loop/subject/edges/subject/subject.md) lists "Bus route #73" as a subjective thing). *Breaks:* identity as the sum of its parts; identity as its label; "a change makes a new thing" (the [estate](../estate/estate.md) position) and "a change is an event on one identity" (the content and text proposals) both fit part of the story, and neither says which parts of the story. *Says:* a rule is needed to say when a change keeps the identity and when it ends it; thing must supply both mechanisms (events on one identity, and `supersedes` between two) and the owning domain picks per relation; and a number is an external identifier with a validity period, never the identity.

3. **A person across a life, a name system and two organizations (Iceland, China, Iran).** A person born with a patronymic, married into a second family name, transliterated differently by each bank, dead for three years yet still a party to an inheritance, and known to a hospital and to a bank that have never exchanged a record. *Breaks:* one record per person per organization with no relation between them; a name as an identity; "the thing ends when the person dies" (obligations and rights continue). *Says:* the same real-world referent may be a thing in several organizations; an identity assertion across organizations is needed and must be an authorized, recorded fact; ending (death) is an event about the referent, not the end of the identity. Related: the `UserUUID` fork, which the [user handoff](../user/user.handoff.md) records as undecided.

4. **Merging and splitting (a municipality merger in Japan, the end of a federal state into two, a land parcel subdivided, two banks becoming one).** One record takes the place of two, or two take the place of one, and a court case later must apply the parcel as it stood on the day of the offence. *Breaks:* `supersedes` stated as a one-to-one replacement ([supersedes](edges/supersedes/supersedes.md) says "this record takes the place of that record"); a single surviving identity; deletion. *Says:* many-to-one and one-to-many succession are needed as sets of facts, each with a time; the old identity remains and stays readable; references to the old one must be able to learn what replaced it.

5. **A thing nobody can or will name (an unidentified patient in an emergency room, a found object, an anonymous donor; the name of a deceased person that some Aboriginal Australian communities do not speak or write).** The record must exist before a name does, and sometimes after one must no longer be shown. Later the patient is identified, or turns out to be two people mixed up in one chart. *Breaks:* the handoff's fourth candidate (every thing has a name); the Go service "find quiddity by title"; any model where identification is a precondition of existence. *Says:* this is exactly thing.md's "need not, cannot, or does not wish to give a specific name to"; identification afterwards is an assertion (this is that), and a wrong merge is corrected by a new fact that splits, not by editing.

6. **A design and its realizations (a carpet pattern and a woven carpet, a car model and one car with a chassis number, a contract template and a signed contract).** The pattern is a thing; each carpet is another; the pattern "MUST not [be used] in direct manner" ([template](edges/template/template.md)); a building conforms to a design; a product meets a specification ([product research 001](../../economics/product/product.research.001.md), `satisfies`). *Breaks:* one record for the kind and the individual (the philosophy section's quiddity against haecceity); a single "instance of" relation (there are at least three: made from, conforms to, replaces). *Says:* the kind is a thing too, with a template classification; the relation from instance to template is missing from the domain, and it differs from `supersedes` and from `satisfies`.

7. **A river, an idol, a ship, a company as legal subjects (New Zealand, India, maritime law, any commercial register).** A river is granted legal personhood by statute; a temple idol is treated by courts as a juristic person that holds property; a ship can be a defendant in some legal systems; a company is a person for contracts; some decisions of this kind have been stayed or reversed on appeal. *Breaks:* the philosophy section's subject, "a being that exercises agency, undergoes conscious experiences" — none of these does; and the [subject](edges-loop/subject/edges/subject/subject.md) rule "Thing MUST get just `object` or `subject` tag not both in one time", which is exact about time but silent about purpose: a river is a subject for standing in court and an object for a survey. *Says:* "subject" in the repository's examples (Persia, Bus route #73) is a standing in relations, not consciousness; it is a classification that completes the Memar sentence — *a thing may be classified as a subject because rules give it standing to be a party, and therefore it can be an owner, a creator, a follower* — and it can begin and end by a dated rule.

8. **Gold in a bazaar, eggs by the tray, rice by the traditional weight (Iran, Japan, India).** One quantity of gold is quoted by one dealer per coin, by another per gram, by a third in an old weight; eggs are sold singly or by a tray of thirty; rice is quoted in a regional weight in one market and in kilograms in the next. *Breaks:* [count_unit](edges/count_unit/count_unit.md): "Almost all `thing` MUST have just one `count_unit`" — a unit is a property of the counting, not of the thing; the compound-unit question (kg·km, passenger-km) is the same defect from another side. *Says:* the unit belongs to the relation between a counting context (an account, an offer, a stock) and the thing, and a conversion between units is processing, not identity.

9. **A thing that exists in intervals (a weekly market, a festival rebuilt every cycle, a seasonal road, a recurring meeting).** The market of Friday is the same market as last Friday; the festival of this cycle is a different gathering from the last; both are "the same thing" in speech. *Breaks:* identity that persists as long as the thing exists; "a copy is a new thing". *Says:* the occurrence and the series are two things, related; recurrence of a task is already modeled in [task](../../management/task/task.research.001.md), and a thing that is not a task needs the same relation without the task.

10. **An identity that must stop being readable (a person in a protected shelter, a sealed adoption record, a witness, an erasure order).** The identity is referenced by a hundred records; the duty is that nothing may disclose who this is, or that this record exists. *Breaks:* "nothing is ever deleted" ([supersedes](edges/supersedes/supersedes.md), [follow](edges/follow/follow.md): a revocation "is a new fact, not a deletion"); a follower list that anyone can read (follow's own open question on who may see who follows). *Says:* the identity must be able to end without its references dangling or leaking; erasure is an authorized event that removes readability and keeps the fact that it happened; whether existence itself may be hidden is a question the domain must ask, not answer by default.

11. **Whose is it, and who may say so (a seized phone passing from police to a laboratory to a court; an endowed building whose revenue is for the poor and whose manager cannot sell it; land held by a community; a rented machine inside a factory).** Several organizations touch one thing; none of them is "the owner" in a sense that decides who may state facts about it. *Breaks:* [follow](edges/follow/follow.md)'s "the followed thing's owner decides" — the thing domain defines no owner; the [ownership](../../rights/ownership/ownership.md) domain says rights exist first and ownership arises from them; and the `object` edge's "Some rule CAN limit user". *Says:* the authority over a fact about a thing is per relation and per organization, set by rule, with the creating organization authoritative for the facts it states; the thing domain needs the requirement question, not an owner field.

**The reading the answers assume (second layer; agent, Tentative).** The underlying problem is not "what does a base type contain" but "how can the graph keep one stable referent while the knowledge about it keeps changing, arriving from several organizations, and sometimes turning out wrong". The answers below therefore favour: an identity with almost nothing in it; every statement about the identity as a recorded fact with an author and a time; corrections as new facts; and kind, name, unit and owner as relations, not fields. Where the owner's underlying problem is different (for example, if the domain is chiefly meant as a storage directory, as `services/get` suggests), questions 1 and 10 change first.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

#### The removal test run on the thing itself (agent, Tentative)
Remove the thing domain and try to express the rest. The [edge](../../knowledge/edge/edge.md) domain has no endpoint type for the two things it joins (its graph data model is typed `Graph[thing UUID, thing UUID, label UUID]`, see the [organization handoff](../organization/organization.handoff.md)); each of text, uri, account, product and task would mint its own identifier, which is the ninety-file duplication the collapse removed; the relations that need an endpoint of any kind (label, supersedes, follow, count unit) would be copied into each domain. The fabrication lands inside the foundational set (the graph's endpoints), so the thing passes the test. The second guard also holds: these are existing requirements, not anticipated ones. The test is silent on how much the thing contains, which is the next step.

#### Proposed kernel (agent, Tentative)
| # | Element | Who creates / removes | What it triggers | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TH1 | **Identity** — one opaque identifier per referent, minted without a name, kind or owner; unique across domains; never reused; stable. | Created by an authenticated request of any domain that needs a referent; never removed (see TH3). | A registration event (TH2). | Decided as the single owner of the identifier ([handoff](thing.handoff.md#decisions)); "MUST be unique ID in all domains" ([dt-uuid.kh](protocol/dt-uuid.kh)). The behaviour (order, format, scope) is not stated anywhere. |
| TH2 | **Registration event** — the recorded fact that the identity came to exist: time, the request that caused it, the domain that asked. | Created with the identity; cannot be removed or edited. | Makes the identity readable to every domain; the first fact other facts can cite. | The Go `Storage_Fields` (id, domain, time, request) in [protocol/storage.kh](protocol/storage.kh); [Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates). |
| TH3 | **Tombstone** — the recorded fact that the identity has ended (the referent is gone, or the identity is retired), with its time and the authority; and its reverse, a reinstatement, as a separate fact. | By the authority named by the rule for that kind of thing, never by the thing itself; never physically removes the identity. | References learn that the thing has ended; followers receive it as a new version. | The [deleted](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/deleted/deleted.md) status ("Soft Deleted") is the existing home of the word; survey R4 decides whether statuses are `edges-loop/` or `status/<name>/`. |
| TH4 | **Succession** — `supersedes` (exists): this takes the place of that, the earlier stays. | By the authority of the domain that owns the rule for the kind (a law's amendment, a product's replacement). | The earlier becomes "not current" in the reading of every reference; nothing is deleted. | [supersedes](edges/supersedes/supersedes.md); used by the product, content and text researches and by the estate domain. |
| TH5 | **Sameness assertion** — `same_as` / `distinct_from` between two identities (does not exist). | By an identity with authority over both, in the same request; `distinct_from` is the reverse that stops a later candidate from being proposed again. | Readers may follow `same_as` to treat the pair as one; nothing is merged or deleted. | No document states it; the need is in scenarios 3, 4, 5 and in follow's open questions on outside-platform things and identity proof across organizations. **Explored**: the existing requirements are real but few. |

Not in the kernel by this proposal: a name or title, a kind or media type, an owner, a status other than the tombstone, a count unit, a picture, a language, a rule, a price, a location, a deliverable. Each of these has a domain that owns it already or a pluggable below.

**What `ThingModel` requires if the proposal is taken (agent, Tentative).** The identity accessor only, `ThingModel ab { Field_ThingUUID }`: a thing can say which it is. Everything else in the table is a fact in the graph, not a requirement on the model of a capsule. This ends the empty-abstraction smell of [Khayyam rule 6](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md) with one real requirement, and it does not give every importing domain a storage surface (the handoff's reason for leaving the model alone). It is a design recorded here, not an edit; the `.kh` stays as it is.

#### Shared relations defined once, which stay in this domain without becoming modules (agent, Tentative)
The test for admission, a gate proposed here because [modules.md](../../modules.md#top-or-thing-levels-labels) warns that "we MUST think very deeply" before adding to this level and nothing yet says how: a relation is defined on thing when (a) at least three domains that do not otherwise depend on each other need it with the same meaning, (b) it holds for any kind of thing with no kind-specific precondition, and (c) its who-may-create-or-remove and what-it-triggers can be stated without naming any one domain's content ([Placing a relation](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation)). A relation that fails (c) is a bare edge ([Modules](../../modules.md#placement)).

| Relation | Passes? | Who creates / removes | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `substitute` — this can be used in place of that (directed, with the asserter and the scope: an organization, the system, a period) | Yes: product, task, account, knowledge unit, form and question each list their own copy of the line ([substitute](edges/substitute/substitute.md)); survey Q5 proposes the collapse | An identity with authority over the use, in a request; removable by the same, as a new fact | Search and replacement readings may offer it; a safety-critical substitute (a generic medicine, an approved alternate part) is an assertion with an authority, never a recommendation |
| `complement` — used together with | Yes, same evidence ([complement](edges/complement/complement.md)) | Same | Same; the Go record also carried a type (`TimeBased`, `Org`, `System`), which is the asserter and scope above |
| `count_unit_is` — a counting context counts this thing in this unit | Yes for the relation; **no** for "one per thing" (scenario 8) | The context that counts (an account, an offer, a stock) | The unit of every quantity stated about the thing in that context; conversion between units is a pluggable (below) |
| `template` (loop-edge) — not to be used directly; instantiate | Yes, with its missing instance side (`derived_from`, finding 6) | The author of the template | Instantiation copies; the copy is a new thing |
| `derived_from` — made from that (instance of a template, fork, copy) (does not exist) | Yes (survey lists the lineage edge as missing; the estate domain lists `derived_from` and `forked_from` as "separate candidates for `thing`") | The maker, in the request that makes it | Lineage is readable; a fork "does not by itself supersede" (estate) |

Proposed home: the object domain (question 16), which this research does not move.

### 3. Rival models

Five choices, each separable. The order is the order in which they change what the domain is.

#### Choice 1: what a thing is
- **A1 — a bare identity token (Explored; the status quo).** `ThingModel ab` with nothing, an identifier with no behaviour. *For:* smallest kernel; nothing to migrate; the handoff's reason to leave it. *Against:* [Khayyam rule 6](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md) names an empty abstraction a design smell and the pilot note says "`ThingModel` is genuinely incomplete, not a deliberate empty extension point"; nothing can say that two identities are the same, that one ended, or what replaced it; fifteen domains compose a model that promises nothing.
- **A2 — a named entity: identity, name, kind (Explored; the generic knowledge-graph and web-vocabulary pattern, general knowledge, unverified).** Every node has a label, a description and a type. *For:* immediately useful to readers and to search; fewer relations to follow. *Against:* it is the old `Quiddity` record the repository decomposed (id, title, URI, status, language) and the decomposition went the other way for stated reasons; it contradicts "need not, cannot, or does not wish to give a specific name to"; it fixes one kind per node against multi-typing; it re-implements the text domain's i18n, history and validation, the very concern [the Attribute-or-Edge Test](../../../docs/modeling.md#the-attribute-or-edge-test) assigns to Text. "Resist defaults that arrive with the medium" ([cognition](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)) applies.
- **A3 — an identity with lifecycle and identity relations (Tentative, recommended).** The kernel of finding 2: identity, registration, tombstone, succession, sameness assertion; everything else attaches. *For:* keeps every Decided statement true (single identifier, shared by all domains, unique across domains, no kind or name imposed); answers scenarios 2 to 5 and 10 without a field; gives rule 6 its requirement; matches the history of the domain. *Against:* TH5 and TH3 add real obligations (who may assert, what a tombstone does to references) that the other domains must follow; the `ThingModel` requirement is small enough that someone will call it still empty.

How each meets the scenarios (agent judgment): A1 meets 1 and 5 (nothing is required) and fails 2, 3, 4, 10 (it cannot say what changed). A2 fails 1, 5 and 7 (a name and a kind forced). A3 meets all eleven in outline, at the price of questions 4 to 9.

#### Choice 2: what `ThingModel` must require (the handoff's four candidates)
| Candidate | Option | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| none | **M0** — nothing | Explored; survey R3(b) default |
| identity only | **M1** — `Field_ThingUUID` | **Tentative, recommended** |
| identity + name | **M2** — a text | Explored; rejected by scenarios 1 and 5 and by thing.md's definition |
| identity + kind | **M3** — a media type | Explored; contradicts multi-typing (estate, content K1: a course that is also a product) |
| identity + audit | **M4** — request and time | Explored; these are attributes of the registration *event* (TH2), not of the thing; the repository distinguishes record from thing nowhere, and this says where |
| all four | **M5** — the old storage record | Explored; the old `Quiddity` plus audit; A2's failures |

The media-type candidate has one real function worth keeping: the Go `get` service resolved an id to the domain that holds its record. That is a directory, and under multi-typing the answer is a set. It belongs to the registry question (question 10), not to the model.

#### Choice 3: can one identity be several kinds
- **I1 — one identity, many classifications (Tentative, recommended).** The estate domain's confirmed position, the content proposal (K1: "the same node may take other identities"), the Go comment ("UUID can be same with any other UUID domains"), and the collapse of per-domain `UUID` onto `ThingUUID`. *Against:* one identity means one authority question per relation, not one per kind; exclusivity (subject against object; template against instance) must be an edge rule.
- **I2 — one identity per kind, linked by a relation (Explored).** Each domain mints its record's identity and relates it to the thing it describes (a document and the thing it is about). *For:* each domain owns its own lifecycle; erasure per kind is simple. *Against:* reintroduces the per-domain identifier the collapse removed; every query joins; the relation is itself a bare edge until its rules are stated.
- **I3 — identity borrowed between kinds (Explored; text TM5).** The thing's UUID doubles as its title's text identity. *For:* nothing to mint. *Against:* one title per thing; contradicts "unique" if the same value names two nodes; the text research keeps it only as a convention.

#### Choice 4: identity through change
- **C1 — a change makes a new thing; `supersedes` links them (Explored).** The estate domain's confirmed position: "any change to an estate produces a new estate; the old one is never deleted. ... There is no 'latest version'". *For:* history is the graph; no overwrite exists to defend. *Against:* every reference must follow supersession; a counter, a price or a status changes constantly.
- **C2 — one identity; a change is an event on it (Explored).** The content and text proposals, and [supersedes](edges/supersedes/supersedes.md)'s own policy: "A domain that keeps one identity and records edits as events on that identity does not use this edge for those edits." *For:* references stay valid; fits a thing that is edited often. *Against:* the identity can drift until it is a different thing (Ship of Theseus; scenario 2).
- **C3 — both, chosen per relation by the owning domain, with a stated test (Tentative, recommended).** Thing supplies both mechanisms and requires that no relation uses both. The test the thing domain offers (agent, Tentative): *a change keeps the identity when every reference to the thing would still be true if read after the change; otherwise it ends it.* For example, a text's word-correction keeps a comment's reference to it true; a building's demolition and rebuilding does not.

#### Choice 5: identity scope across organizations
- **S1 — one global opaque identifier minted anywhere; each organization owns the facts it states; equivalence across organizations is an assertion (Tentative, recommended).** *For:* matches the stated rule "unique in all domains" and the follow edge (facts move between organizations, identities do not change); no registry required for uniqueness. *Against:* two organizations will mint two identities for one referent (scenarios 3, 4); correctness depends on TH5.
- **S2 — organization-scoped identity (organization, local id) with a global mapping (Explored).** *For:* each organization controls its space; erasure is simple. *Against:* every cross-organization reference carries two parts; the survey's collapse of per-domain identifiers (A1) goes the other way.
- **S3 — an externally governed identifier (ISBN, GTIN, VIN, national id, a domain name) as the identity (Explored; rejected).** *For:* already exists. *Against:* the authority is outside; such identifiers are re-issued, recycled or retired (a domain name, a phone number, a username), which is the argument in [uri](../uri/uri.md) for a thing whose text *is* the identifier while the identity stays ours; and a thing outside any standard has none.

#### Smaller choice: the registry
- **R1 — the registry is authoritative (mints and enforces uniqueness; `services/register`).** *For:* the Go design. *Against:* a central point; wrong if S1 holds.
- **R2 — the registry is a derived index of which domains hold the identity (an `edges-shortcut`; never the source of truth) (Tentative).** *For:* [Edge buckets](../../modules.md#edge-buckets) require a cache of a walk already in the graph to be a shortcut; uniqueness comes from minting. *Against:* a lookup must scan or index every domain.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions
These are questions the domain's own documents must answer before implementation; each favours no answer where the repository has not settled it ([Ask rather than assume](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). A line marked *settled* cites its source.

#### Identity
- *Settled:* one identifier shared by all domains, no competing per-domain identifier ([handoff](thing.handoff.md#decisions)); "MUST be unique ID in all domains" (Go comment in [dt-uuid.kh](protocol/dt-uuid.kh)).
- Is the identifier ordered (by time, by anything) or opaque? Who may mint one: any domain, any organization, only the platform?
- Unique in what scope: the organization, a deployment, every deployment? What is the collision policy?
- May an identity ever be reused after its tombstone?
- What does "the same thing" mean: who decides, by what rule, and is the verdict an assertion that can be wrong?
- May a thing exist with no name, no kind and no relation at all? For how long before it is garbage?
- May an identity exist before its referent does (a planned building, a bill that is not yet a law, an unborn child)?
- Is the identifier a value other organizations can see on the wire, and what does seeing it reveal (order, origin, time)?
- How is an external identifier (ISBN, VIN, phone number, domain name) related to the identity, when such identifiers are recycled?
- Is "quiddity" retired as an identifier name, kept for the kind, or kept for the browsing level?

#### Lifecycle
- When does a thing begin: on registration, on first reference, or on an act of the owning domain?
- Does a thing end in the graph, and what distinguishes the end of the referent from the end of its record from a legal erasure?
- Is a tombstone reversible (a person declared dead who is alive, a retired product reinstated)?
- What happens to relations that point at an ended, superseded or merged thing: stay, redirect, flag?
- May succession be many-to-one and one-to-many, and does it transfer the identity or only point?
- How is an intermittent or recurring existence modeled: one thing with gaps, or an occurrence per interval and a series above them?
- May a thing's kind change without ending it (a prototype that becomes a product, a design that becomes a building)?

#### Authorization
- Who may register, tombstone, supersede, assert sameness, and who approves a correction?
- Who may state which relation about a thing; is that authority per relation kind and per organization, and what decides it when two organizations disagree?
- Given the ownership domain's rights-first ordering, what does the followed side's "owner" in [follow](edges/follow/follow.md) refer to?
- May anyone attach a relation to a thing they do not control (a label, a follow, a comment)? Who owns the relation, who sees it, who may remove it?
- May the existence of a thing be hidden from some readers, and is hidden existence compatible with followers and with references?
- Which authorization is a rule attached through `condition_is`, and which is structural? The kernel does not reference the rule domain ([Modules](../../modules.md#placement)).

#### Rules
- Which rules does the thing domain own for itself (subject against object; the unit rule; the template rule), and which belong to a classification that merely sits in this folder?
- Where does exclusivity between classifications live, and does it ever change over time?
- Is a rule written here "MUST" a rule of every organization or a default one organization may change? ("What may become an estate is runtime policy of each organization" is the estate position.)
- What does the thing domain require of a domain that attaches a kind: a statement of the sentence *a thing may be classified as X because ... and therefore ...* ([Classification Emerges](../../../docs/modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels))?

#### Events
- Which events does the thing itself own (registered, tombstoned, reinstated, superseded, declared the same, declared distinct), and which belong to the owner of each relation?
- Are valid time (when it was true in the world) and recorded time (when the graph learned it) both kept for identity events (a death known late, a merger backdated)?
- Are events about a relation recorded on the relation (an edge is a thing) or on the thing at its end, or both?
- How are events ordered across organizations, and how is a redelivered one recognized (follow answers this for deliveries, unratified)?
- How is a correction recorded, and does a reader see the corrected state and the correction?

### 6. Missing edges and modules
Each is a sketch, not a design, and each states who may create or remove it and what it triggers.

1. **`same_as` / `distinct_from` (kernel candidate; Explored).** An assertion between two identities that they name one referent, or that they do not. Created by an identity with authority over both (or a pair of identities, one per side, when the organizations differ), recorded with its time and its basis; removed only by a later assertion that references it. Triggers: readers may follow `same_as`; followers of either receive it; accepting a candidate from identity-resolution creates one. Open: whether it is symmetric, transitive, and what happens when `same_as` meets `distinct_from`.
2. **`derived_from` (shared edge; Tentative).** This was made from that: an instance from a template, a copy, a fork. Created by the maker in the request that makes it; never removed. Triggers: lineage is readable; template changes may be offered to derived things; a fork does not supersede. A small `kind` field is the question (a closed list is a false taxonomy; a free label is a bare edge).
3. **Tombstone and reinstatement (kernel; Tentative).** The ended-identity fact (TH3). Created by the authority named by the rule for the kind; reversed by a separate reinstatement. Triggers: references show an ended thing; followers receive it; the status vocabulary question (survey R4) decides whether this is the [deleted](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/deleted/deleted.md) status or a lifecycle fact in this domain.
4. **`registry` (shortcut; Tentative).** `identity → set of domains that hold a record`, a derived cache of a walk across domains; replaces the Go `get`'s domain id. Never the source of truth; recomputed from the domains.
5. **Authority statement (rule, not edge; Explored).** *Which identity may state which relation kind about which thing.* Not a new field on thing: a rule attached with `condition_is` by the organization that states the facts, evaluated by [access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md) and [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md). What the thing domain owes is the requirement (finding 4), a default (the creating organization is authoritative for what it states), and an example.
6. **`follow` as a pluggable (reclassification; Tentative).** The existing relation and its processing as one capability attached to thing (finding 2).
7. **`identity-resolution` (pluggable; Explored).** Duplicate candidates as discovered data: a list of pairs, a score, the evidence; accepting one creates `same_as`. Kept from kernel for the reason the pluggable placement gives: analytics produces discovered data.
8. **`erasure` (pluggable; Explored).** Request, verification, rule, scoped removal of readability, and the record that it happened. See scenario 10.
9. **`measurement` (pluggable; Explored).** Units, conversion factors with a period, and compound units; resolves the compound-unit question in [count_unit](edges/count_unit/count_unit.md#open-questions).
10. **`evaluation` (pluggable; Tentative).** Ratings and estimates as events, with a type vocabulary and aggregation as discovered data. Replaces the criterion edge; leaves `Progress` to the completion capability (task research 13).
11. **`satisfies` (shared edge; Explored).** Taken from the product research: a concrete thing meets a specification. Open until a third domain evidences it.
12. **`name` as a relation, not a field (no new module).** A thing has zero or more relations to texts with a role (title, alias), owned by the text domain's `(thing)-[:text]` loop and relations; the modules.md note "default text as loop edge is title for each vertex" is a convention inside the text proposal, not a thing requirement.
13. **`existence period` (no new module; Explored).** A thing's interval of existence, held in the time domain; needed only for scenario 9 and scenario 3's death. Question 19.

### 7. Test scenarios
Given/when/then; each is a test another session can write once the owner answers. Numbers refer to the findings.

1. **A thing exists with no name (kept; scenario 1, 5).** Given a new identity registered with no relation to any text. When it is read. Then it is a valid thing, its registration event is present, and no field or relation is reported missing.
2. **Names are relations (scenario 1).** Given a mountain with names in three languages and one name changed by an authority in a later year. When all names are listed. Then each name is a relation to a text with a language, an authority and a period, and the identity is unchanged.
3. **Identity is minted without a kind (finding 2, TH1).** Given a request from any domain. When an identity is created. Then no kind is recorded on the identity; the registration event names the requesting domain only as a fact.
4. **Multi-typing (finding 3, I1).** Given a course that is also a product. When both classifications are attached. Then they share one `ThingUUID`; neither domain declares its own identifier.
5. **Exclusive classifications are edge rules (scenario 7).** Given a river classified as an object and, from a stated date, as a subject. When both are read for a date before and after. Then the rule in force on each date decides; nothing is overwritten.
6. **A succession keeps the earlier (kept: [supersedes](edges/supersedes/supersedes.md)).** Given a regulation that takes the place of another. When it is registered. Then the earlier remains readable and a reader on a date before the change gets the earlier.
7. **Merge and split (scenario 4).** Given parcel A split into B and C, and two banks D and E merged into F. When the successions are recorded. Then B and C both succeed A, F succeeds D and E, each with its time, and a reference to A learns of B and C.
8. **A wrong merge is corrected by a fact (scenario 5).** Given an identity declared `same_as` another and later found to be two people. When it is corrected. Then a `distinct_from` references the earlier assertion; nothing is deleted; both assertions are readable with their authors.
9. **Sameness needs authority (finding 4).** Given a requester with no authority over either identity. When it asserts `same_as`. Then the request is refused, and a candidate may be proposed as discovered data instead.
10. **A tombstone is not a deletion (scenario 3).** Given a person who has died. When the tombstone is recorded. Then the identity remains, relations stay readable, new relations about the referent still refer to it, and a reinstatement can reverse it.
11. **A number is an external identifier with a period (scenario 2).** Given route number 73 reassigned. When it is read before and after. Then the number resolves to the first thing before the date and the second after; no identity changed.
12. **The unit belongs to the counting context (scenario 8).** Given one quantity of gold counted by a dealer per gram and by another per coin. When both are read. Then each is a `count_unit_is` of its own context; the thing has no single unit; the conversion is a measurement fact.
13. **Instance from a template (scenario 6).** Given a carpet pattern marked as a template. When a carpet is made from it. Then the carpet is a new thing with `derived_from` to the pattern; the pattern is not used directly.
14. **Fork does not supersede (kept: estate).** Given a fork of a design. When it is registered. Then it has `derived_from` and the original is not superseded.
15. **Authority is per relation (scenario 11).** Given a seized object at three organizations. When each states a fact about it. Then each fact carries its author, and a reader can ask which organization's statements to use by rule.
16. **Existence may be hidden (scenario 10).** Given an identity under protection. When a reader without standing queries it or lists a thing's followers. Then the reader learns neither the identity nor the follower, by rule, without the reference breaking for those with standing.
17. **Erasure keeps the fact (scenario 10).** Given an erasure order. When applied. Then attached data becomes unreadable in each owning domain, the identity and the fact of erasure remain, and followers are told.
18. **Intermittent existence (scenario 9).** Given a weekly market. When this Friday's market occurs. Then it is an occurrence with `derived_from` to the market series, not a new series.
19. **Registry is a cache (finding 3, R2).** Given the registry is lost. When it is recomputed from the domains. Then it is identical, and no identity is lost or re-minted.
20. **A follower receives the tombstone (scenario 10, follow).** Given a follower of a thing. When the thing is tombstoned. Then the follower receives a new version that says so, with the time.

### Reconciliation with the four sibling researches
The [concept synthesis](../../../.agents/tasks/20261014T071207Z-28148-0/concept-synthesis.md) names the thing and `ThingModel` as unblocked by the twelve decisions on text, content, task and product. This research reads the four back for what each takes from thing.

**Agreements.**

This research's choice 3 (I1) is the same direction stated as a rule for the base.
3. **State change as events, not overwrite.** Text TM4 (immutable values), content (immutable revisions), task (state as events) and the follow edge (a revocation is a new fact) agree; TH2 and TH3 apply the same rule to the identity itself.
4. **Shared relations defined once on thing.** Product proposes `satisfies`; content proposes that `bio` ("a thing-level relation in the content domain") be defined once on thing; the survey proposes complement and substitute once; text and task use `template` and `supersedes` without copying them. The admission gate in finding 2 gives them one test.

**Contradictions or gaps between this research and a sibling (reported, not resolved).**

## 8. Open Questions
This is the research's eighth section. Only the owner can decide these. Ordered by what they change: those that change what the domain is first, then those that change how a part is done ([Ask rather than assume](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. They are also entered in the [handoff](thing.handoff.md#research-001-questions-and-next-steps-2026-10-18).

1. **What must a thing offer: nothing (M0), the identity accessor (M1), or also a name, a kind or an audit?** Default: M1; name, kind and audit are relations and events. Blocks: `protocol/model.kh`, the closing of [Khayyam rule 6](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md) for fifteen domains, `service-get`, survey R3(b) (whose default is M0 and contradicts rule 6's pilot note).
2. **Must every thing have a name?** Default: no; a name is a relation to a text with a role. Blocks: text TM5, the modules.md TODO on default title, a "find by title" service, the handoff's fourth candidate.
3. **May one identity carry several kinds (I1) or does each kind get its own identity linked to the thing (I2)?** Default: I1; exclusivity between kinds is an edge rule. Blocks: survey A1 (the `UserUUID` fork and the per-domain identifiers), product question 2 (domain or classification), text TM5, the estate positions.
4. **When does a change keep an identity and when does it end it?** Default: both mechanisms exist; the owning domain picks per relation; the test is "every reference would still be true after the change". Blocks: the `supersedes` policy text, the estate and content identity statements, text TM4, product's identity-survival question.
5. **Is a sameness assertion (`same_as` / `distinct_from`) a kernel fact, and who may make one?** Default: yes, with authority over both ends; detection is a pluggable. Blocks: follow across organizations, outside-platform things, mergers, the `UserUUID` fork's two-names question.
6. **May succession be many-to-one and one-to-many?** Default: yes, as a set of dated facts. Blocks: the `supersedes` document, law amendments, estate subdivision, mergers.
7. **What is the identity's scope across organizations: one global opaque value (S1), organization-scoped (S2), or an external identifier (S3)?** Default: S1. Blocks: `dt-uuid.kh` behaviour (order, format), follow's identity-proof question, the registry.
8. **What decides who may state which relation of a thing, given that ownership arises from rights?** Default: per relation kind and per organization by a rule through `condition_is`; the creating organization is authoritative for what it states; follow's "owner" reads as that organization. Blocks: follow's authorization, the tombstone, correction, maker-checker, [ownership](../../rights/ownership/ownership.md)'s bare-edge-or-node question.
9. **What does retiring an identity do, and how does an erasure differ from it?** Default: a tombstone is a dated fact by an authority and is reversible; erasure is an authority-gated event that removes readability in the owning domains and keeps the identity and the fact. Blocks: the tombstone, the `erasure` capability, scenario 10, the survey's R4 status-home ruling.
10. **Is the registry authoritative (mints and enforces uniqueness) or a derived shortcut?** Default: a shortcut; uniqueness comes from minting. Blocks: `services/register` and `get`, `protocol/storage.kh` and `service-register.kh`, the media-type identity abstraction (survey A7), `internal/storage`.
11. **Is `follow` a pluggable capability attached to thing, or a relation on thing with a separate processing capability?** Default: one pluggable. Blocks: the follow document's placement and its open question 2, price-index and replacement-cost, kernel purity, the delivery and metering design.
12. **Is the count unit a relation of the counting context, and is "one unit per thing" dropped?** Default: yes, with a default unit allowed on the thing; compound units in a `measurement` capability. Blocks: [count_unit](edges/count_unit/count_unit.md), the currency label, pricing's display basis, account.
13. **Are `complement` and `substitute` each one directed relation with an asserter and a scope, and are recommendations discovered data?** Default: yes to both. Blocks: the nine lines in each document, survey Q5, the `_import` Go records.
14. **What does `subjective` mean: agency, standing, or social construction; is it exclusive; and does it end?** Default: standing to be a party to relations, rule-governed and dated, exclusive only where a rule says; animate/inanimate leaves the kernel document. Blocks: the subject loop, thing.md's two sections, the user and bot/society questions (survey P9), scenario 7.
15. **Is "quiddity" retired as an identifier name; is it the kind; or the browsing level?** Default: the kind (derived), and retired as an identifier name. Blocks: the vocabulary of `services`, `_import`, modules.md's "quiddity levels".
16. **Do icon, picture and the user's object labels become one `depicted_by` relation in the object domain?** Default: yes; the rating is a label; access is a rule. Blocks: three edge documents, the picture-rating question in thing-labels.
17. **Does `criterion` leave thing as an `evaluation` capability, and does `Progress` leave it?** Default: yes and yes. Blocks: the criterion `data_model.kh` and `storage.kh` conversion (whose blocker `Field_TaskID` now exists), the handoff's Next Step 4, task question 13.
18. **Is `derived_from` defined once on thing, with what kinds?** Default: yes, with a small closed `kind` (template instance, fork, copy) reviewed after use. Blocks: the template instance side, the estate lineage candidates, product `satisfies`.
19. **How is a recurring or intermittent thing modeled?** Default: an occurrence per interval and a series related by `derived_from`; the interval of existence is in the time domain. Blocks: scenario 9, parity with task recurrence.
20. **Are valid time and recorded time both kept for identity events?** Default: yes. Blocks: late-known deaths, backdated mergers, the event model of every domain that records succession.
21. **What happens to the thing folder's residue (`_import/`, `internal/storage/mysql.sql`, `widgets/ai_generated`)?** Default: retire what is not about thing, after the findings here are kept; reclassify the AI label as a discovered thing-labels word. Blocks: survey A8, the next Phase 0 sweep.
