## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The organization domain answers, for any collective that is not a person, three questions the rest of the application keeps asking: *is this collective a party, an owner, a declarer, something that can be acted for — and from when to when?*; *who may act for it, and where is that written?*; and *which society, if any, treats it as a party, and under what identifier?* It does not hold the collective's members (that is [group](../../management/group/group.md)), its goods, accounts, products or tasks, its name or its credentials. A working definition follows: **an organization is a user that rules treat as a collective party: it can be a party, an owner and a declarer, it acts only through others, it is recognised or not by societies, and it begins and ends by recorded events.** This restates [organization.md](organization.md) and the user research's finding that a company is a subject with no recognition of its own ([user research, scenario 3](../user/user.research.001.md#1-what-the-domain-is-for)), and sharpens it with the Agency passage that the boundary of an agent need not coincide with a human's.

**The word hides four things (agent, Tentative; the evidence is in the repository).**
1. **The participant.** The collective as a party: the buyer on an invoice, the issuer of an ownership ([issuer](../../rights/ownership/edges/issuer/issuer.md): `(ownership)-[:issuer_is]->(organization)`), the organization that follows a thing ([follow](../thing/edges/follow/follow.md)). This is what [organization.md](organization.md) and [citizen](edges/citizen/citizen.md) describe.
2. **The scope.** The collective as the *declarer* in whose graph facts are authoritative. The siblings use the word this way all the time: product "one node per organization with a mapping" ([product handoff](../../economics/product/product.handoff.md)), account "each organization's ledger is authoritative for its own accounts" ([account, question 13](../../economics/account/account.research.001.md)), the invoice's "counterparty organization", thing's "the creating organization is authoritative for what it states" ([thing, question 8](../thing/thing.research.001.md#8-open-questions)). The domain's **only code** is this sense: `Field_OrganizationUUID` ("the organization the record belongs to") and `Field_OrganizationOwnerID` ("the organization that owns the record") in [protocol/dt-uuid.kh](protocol/dt-uuid.kh), used by about eighteen outside files ([survey](../../../temp/sessions/20261014T071355Z-22804-0/report.md)).
3. **The structure.** The collective as a group of groups, with departments ([organization.md](organization.md#organization-vs-group-conceptual-level)). The structure is group's, hierarchy and membership included.
4. **The deployment.** The collective as the tenant of a hosting system. [organization.md](organization.md#naming-rationale-resolved) refuses this sense on purpose and names a hosting layer **Government**.

The four do not share a lifecycle. A scope can exist before the participant is recognised by anyone; a participant can outlive its structure; a structure is rearranged under a stable participant; a deployment moves hosts under all three. The proposal below keeps the participant and the declarer scope (as a role of the participant, not a second thing) and sends structure and deployment to their owners.

**Scenarios.** Each says what it *breaks* in the obvious model (a registered company with a manager, a unique name and a stored status) and what it *says*.

1. **A company that acts only through others (Iran: joint-stock company whose gazette lists the authorised signatories and the "two of three with the seal" rule; Japan: a kabushiki kaisha with a registered representative director and a registered seal).** The company signs a contract; a manager signs; a new gazette notice replaces the manager while the open contracts continue. *Breaks:* an organization that authenticates (the Go media type already says it has no token); one fixed leader (the Go makes a single "leader" connection); authority as a flag. *Says:* the organization is a user with no recognition of its own and acts through other users by acting-for with a scope, a period, sometimes a threshold, resting on an external fact; its identity does not change when its officers do.
2. **A sole proprietor (Iran, Japan *kojin jigyōnushi*, the UK sole trader).** One human trades under her name; the tax office treats her as a business, the bank opens a business account; later she hires someone. *Breaks:* "an organization comprises one or more people" besides itself; "an organization and a person are different nodes"; "a group has two members" (group research, scenario 12). *Says:* one identity may carry both classifications; the day she hires, the existing identity gains a member and a second representative; nothing migrates. Whether this is one thing or two linked things is a legal choice of the society (in many systems the sole proprietor has no separate legal personality), so recognition, not identity, must carry the difference.
3. **No registry at all (an Iranian bazaar guild, a rotating savings circle, a DAO of pseudonymous token holders).** Nobody registers it; a spokesman is chosen by vouching; the DAO's "members" are keys, some of them other DAOs; the DAO holds a treasury and votes. *Breaks:* an organization is a registered thing; the Go's `Domain` unique name and `SocietyID`; formation by an administrator. *Says:* formation can be an act of its own participants or of a rule; recognition by a society is a separate, optional fact; the organization is real for the application when others treat it as a party, which is the Agency criterion and not a legal one.
4. **Collectives with no members and no owner (a waqf or religious endowment in Iran and the Islamic world, a German *Stiftung*, a temple deity treated by courts as a juristic person in India, a river granted personhood by statute in New Zealand).** The endowment's revenue is for the poor; its manager cannot sell it; nobody owns it; the deity "holds" land through a custodian. *Breaks:* the opening definition of [organization.md](organization.md) ("comprising one or more people"); "group of groups"; the idea that a collective has members and an owner. *Says:* what makes an organization is standing as a party under rules (the thing research reaches the same point for "subject": standing, not consciousness, [thing, scenario 7](../thing/thing.research.001.md#1-what-the-domain-is-for)); members and owners are optional relations; the custodian's authority is acting-for, not ownership.
5. **A merger, a split, a rename and a change of jurisdiction (two banks becoming one; a ministry's directorate moved to another ministry; a company that re-domiciles abroad; a name changed after a scandal).** Contracts, accounts, lawsuits and tax history must stay answerable on any past date. *Breaks:* "an organization is the same while it has the same name"; "a change makes a new thing" and "a change is an event on one identity" both fit part of the story; `Transferred` as one status. *Says:* rename, re-domiciling and change of officers keep the identity; merger and split create successors that supersede predecessors, many to many, each with a date ([thing, TH4](../thing/thing.research.001.md#2-minimal-kernel-and-what-must-be-pluggable)); which obligations pass is the successor's rule, not the identity's.
6. **A branch, a subsidiary, a franchise, an agent (a bank's branch with no legal personality; a subsidiary that is a separate legal person owned by a parent; a fast-food franchisee that is an independent company under a licence; a broker who acts for an insurer; a Japanese *keiretsu* of cross-holding companies).** *Breaks:* one relation `Branch | Agent | Franchise` as in [organization-relation-organization.kh](edges/organization-relation-organization.kh). *Says:* these are four different relations with different owners: a branch is part of the same party (a department; hierarchy); a subsidiary is ownership of shares; a franchise is a contract between separate parties; an agent is acting-for ([Nested Agency](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/agency.md#nested-agency)). A franchise *network* with members is a group of organizations (group question 10). Not one enum.
7. **A group of states and a cycle (the EU, the UN, a federation; a university on a ministry's board while the ministry sits on the university's committee).** The members are organizations; the Ministry sends a named official, replaced every two years. *Breaks:* members are persons; membership flows one way; a representative is the member. *Says:* an organization is a legitimate member of another and acts there through a representative; cycles through "member of" are real (group scenario 4) and harmless to the fact, harmful only to a derivation.
8. **Recognised here, blocked there, unknown elsewhere (a multinational with one legal entity per country; an organization under sanction in one society and trading in another; a foreign firm's representative office).** The Go has `Representative` (registered under another society), `Transferred` and `Blocked`, and `BlockOrg` reserved to "justice MS". *Breaks:* one status per organization; a global "blocked"; a single society. *Says:* recognition, suspension and block are facts *of a society about an organization*, plural, dated, and not facts of the organization; the organization may be a party in one society and refused in another. The block is a recognition's suspension by an authority, not an attribute.
9. **Dissolution, liquidation, revival (a bankruptcy under Chapter 11 or a liquidation; a company struck off a register and restored by a court years later).** Between the decision to close and the end, the organization exists only to pay creditors, sue and be sued, and hold records; it may later come back. [management.md](../../management/management.md#organization-lifecycle) records the owner's idea: a finish date, never a deletion, reopening restricted. *Breaks:* active versus deleted; closure as the end of activity; the end as final. *Says:* there is a stage in which acts are limited to a purpose (a liquidator's authority) and an end that can be reversed by an authority with its own event; nothing is erased.
10. **Whose graph is it (a hospital that self-hosts and refers a patient to a laboratory; a small shop that has no server and is hosted by a society's platform).** Both hold facts about the same laboratory; the shop's facts live on another's machine. *Breaks:* equating the participant with the scope with the deployment. *Says:* the laboratory is one participant, known in the hospital's graph by an identity the hospital minted; the hosted shop remains the declarer of its own facts though a host stores them; the host is a participant too and is not the owner of what it hosts. The sibling defaults ("one node per organization with a mapping") rest on how one organization is identified inside another's graph (question 3).
11. **Names and registry numbers that collide (name squatting; two societies registering the same name in Persian and in Latin transliteration; a registration number reissued after a dissolution; a lookalike).** The Go refuses a registered name (`ErrOrgNameRegistered`) and a registered domain (`ErrOrgDomainRegistered`). *Breaks:* a unique name as identity; a registry number as identity. *Says:* names and registry numbers are references with an issuer and a period, unique (if at all) per society by that society's rule; the identity never depends on them, the same finding as the text research on handles and the user research on phone numbers.

**What the domain is for, stated against the scenarios (agent, Tentative).**
- **A party that survives every change of its people** — officers (1), employees (2), members (3, 7), owners (4), structure (5, 6).
- **Standing apart from recognition** — an organization exists and acts before and without any society's recognition (3), is recognised by several, and is refused by some (8).
- **Acting-for as a relation, not a type** — and the external fact it rests on, dated (1, 4, 7, 9).
- **Beginning and ending as events, with a stage between** — formation, the liquidation stage, cessation, revival (5, 9).
- **One declarer role, not a second concept** — the organization that states a fact is the participant in a role, and the hosting host is another participant (10).

**The second layer (agent, Tentative).** Stepping back from "model the organization record", the underlying problem is **who may be held to a fact, and who may state one**: the application has to attribute acts and facts to collectives with the least knowledge about who they "really" are, and has to let facts cross from one collective's graph to another's without a global registry. The research assumes the application is not itself the register of lawful collectives. If the owner wants the application to be that register, scenarios 3, 4 and 8 change and recognition moves toward the kernel (question 2).

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

**Placement tests applied.** For each candidate: (a) the foundational test, attempt to express it through the remaining abstractions; (b) the pluggable test, an independent responsibility with its own lifecycle attached through an explicit relationship; (c) the edge test, an edge becomes its own module only when it carries independent domain semantics; (d) no bare edges; (e) the kernel never references the rule domain directly.

**The challenge the test raises first (agent).** Does `organization` earn a kernel domain? Attempt without it: identity is the thing's; being a *who* is the user's admission; being closed is the user's standing; having members is group's; acting for is delegate's; ownership of shares is ownership's; recognition is society's relation. What is left is (i) the **classification**: the fact that this user is treated as a collective party and not a person, with the completable sentence of [Classification Emerges From Rules and Relations](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels) — *a user may be classified as an organization because rules give it standing to be a party, an owner and a declarer without a body of its own, and therefore it may be acted for, recognised by societies, and a member of other collectives*; and (ii) the **declarer scope**, which the protocol already carries. Nothing here forces fabricating or bending a foundation. The organization domain is thinner than the user domain; the proposal says so and does not pad it. It stays a domain, not a loop only, because about eighteen outside files already use `Field_OrganizationUUID` and because the loop needs a home that states who may set it (Tentative; this is question 15).

**Proposed kernel (agent, Tentative): two elements, one Explored.**

| # | Element | What it is | Who may create | Who may remove | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | **Classification** | The loop `(user)-[:organization]->(---)`: this user is treated as a collective party. A user may carry it with `person` (sole proprietor) and with the kinds below | The admission event of a user that is a collective; or a later event by an authority over the user (the user's acting-for representatives, a registry process, a ruling) that names the cause | Never deleted; ended by an event with a cause (for example, found to be a person) | `organization_classified`; the consumers that accept an organization (issuer, follower, party) accept it from then; acting-for becomes the way it acts |
| O2 | **Declarer scope** | The two accessors `OrganizationID` and `OrganizationOwnerID` on a record, which say which organization declared it and which owns it; the organization is the participant in the declarer role | The record's creator, in the request that makes the record; the organization must be one the requester is authorised to speak for (delegate) | Never edited; a correction is a new fact by the same or a later declarer | Nothing by itself; every cross-organization rule (follow, invoice exchange, product mapping) reads it |
| O3 | **Dissolving stage** *(Explored whether kernel or the `liquidation` pluggable)* | A standing between active and ceased in which the organization may act only for the stated purpose | An authority named by the society or the founding rule | An authority, as a new event (revival) | `dissolution_started`, `organization_ceased`, `dissolution_revoked`; acts outside the stated purpose are refused by the consumers' rules |

**Not in the kernel by this proposal:** name and domain, kind vocabulary, officers, signatories, thresholds, recognition and registry identifiers, members, departments, shares and owners, branches and franchises, hosting, citizens, voting rights, block by justice, credentials, products, accounts. Each is below as a pluggable or another module's relation.

**Pluggable and neighbouring capabilities.** A capability owns its relation and its processing together ([Capability Completeness](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modularity.md#capability-completeness)). "Home" says where it lives or would live.

| Capability | Relation and processing it owns | Home | Who may create / remove the relation | What it triggers | Status |

### 3. Rival models

Five models of what the node is, then one axis that cuts across them (how an organization is identified from another's graph). Marks: none is Decided.

- **M1 — organization as the legacy record (Explored, not recommended).** An identifier, a unique `Domain` name, a society, a services type and a status of four values, a leader delegate connection, no token ([storage.kh](../../management/organization/_import/organization/storage.kh)). *For:* it exists and its services were written. *Against:* it makes a unique name the thing's key (scenario 11); has one society (8); one status (9); one leader (1); needs a person to form it (3, 4); a block that is an attribute and not a society's act (8).
- **M2 — organization as "a group of groups" (Explored; stated in [organization.md](organization.md#organization-vs-group-conceptual-level) as settled).** An organization is defined by the groups it contains. *For:* it is the owner's statement; it reuses group's membership and hierarchy. *Against:* it makes the organization a structure (meaning 3) and has no answer where there is no body of members (4), where the only member is the organization itself (2), or where the question is who may be held to a fact (10). Compatible with M3 if read as a claim about *membership* (an organization usually has groups as members), not about *type*; the group research reports the ambiguity as its own contradiction 4.
- **M3 — organization as a thin classification of a user, with recognition as a separate plural fact (Tentative, recommended).** Kernel O1 and O2 (and O3 Explored); the rest pluggable. *For:* it survives all eleven scenarios (grid below); it matches the shape the sibling researches found (identity, one or two structural relations, events, pluggables) and the user research's M2 and the group research's M3; it keeps the existing protocol surface (`Field_OrganizationUUID`, `Field_OrganizationOwnerID`) meaningful as the declarer scope; it gives the Go's `Representative`, `Transferred` and `Blocked` a home as recognition facts. *Against:* the kernel is thin, so the foundational test does not prove it is a foundation (finding 2); it leaves open what an `OrganizationModel` must require (question 14); it asks the owner to amend the opening definition and to treat "group of groups" as a claim about membership.
- **M4 — organization as the authority scope and tenant (Explored; the sibling usage).** The node is the owner of a graph, the unit of authority and hosting. *For:* it names what product, account, invoice and thing say when they write "the organization"; it is the SaaS reading. *Against:* [organization.md](organization.md#naming-rationale-resolved) rejects it deliberately; it fails every collective that has no graph of its own (3, 4, 8), or that is hosted by another (10); it turns a participant into infrastructure. Kept as a *role*, the declarer, in M3.
- **M5 — organization as the law's legal or juridical person (Explored; rejected as the definition).** The node exists when a society recognises it. *For:* it matches the encyclopedia text in [organization.md](organization.md#legal-person); it gives the Go's `Blocked`-by-justice a ground. *Against:* it makes existence depend on one society (8), excludes the unregistered and the non-legal-person sole proprietor (2, 3), and makes the application the register of lawful collectives. Kept as *recognition*, one fact among several, in M3.

**Identification axis (agent, Tentative on the default).** Independent of the models, for the organization **as seen from another organization's graph** (the thing the siblings' "one node per organization with a mapping" rests on):
- **G — one global identifier per organization**, minted by a registry or by the first graph that knows it. Joins are free; a registry exists; a re-registered or re-domiciled organization is a problem.
- **L — each graph mints its own identity for a collective it deals with; links are claims and registry identifiers are references.** The hospital's "Lab X" and the laboratory's own identity are two identities joined by `same_as` ([thing, TH5](../thing/thing.research.001.md#2-minimal-kernel-and-what-must-be-pluggable)) with evidence; a company number, tax number or LEI-style code is a reference with an issuer and a period (like a phone number in the user research).
- **S — the identifier of a recognising society** as the identity. Rejected: re-issued, recycled, retired, society-relative (thing choice 5, S3).
The default proposed is **L**, the same as the user research's, and compatible with thing's S1 (a global opaque value minted anywhere, equivalence by assertion). **The cost is larger here than for a user**, because a collective is dealt with by many other organizations and sameness will be asserted often; this is why `same_as` is a requirement of the organization case and not a curiosity (question 3).

**The scenarios against the models** (ok, strain, fails; agent's reading, not a measurement):

| Scenario | M1 record | M2 group of groups | M3 thin class + recognition | M4 scope/tenant | M5 legal person |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 acts through others | strain (one leader) | strain | ok | ok | ok |
| 2 sole proprietor | strain | strain (group of one) | ok | strain | fails |
| 3 no registry, DAO | fails | ok | ok | fails | fails |
| 4 endowment, no members/owner | fails | fails | ok | fails | ok |
| 5 merger, split, rename | fails | strain | strain (identity, Q10) | strain | ok |
| 6 branch, franchise, agent | strain | ok | ok (four owners) | strain | strain |
| 7 group of states, cycles | strain | ok | ok | fails | strain |
| 8 recognised here, blocked there | strain | strain | ok | fails | fails |

| 10 whose graph, hosted | fails | fails | strain (needs Q1) | ok | fails |
| 11 name collisions | ok | strain | ok | strain | strain |

**Recommendation (agent, unratified): M3 with identification axis L.** It is the only model with no "fails"; its weaknesses (a thin kernel, two scenarios strained) are stated. M2's statement is kept as a true remark about usual membership. M4 is kept as a role.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirement questions

Written so that no answer is favoured where the documents leave the matter open. "Settled" marks the few a document already answers.

**Identity**
- What must be true of an organization's identifier: unique among users, among all things, among graphs? (Settled in part: the UUID is the identity-domain UUID, kind is a label, [organization.md](organization.md#protocol).)
- What makes two identities the same organization — a shared registry number, an assertion by a party with authority over both, a ruling — and who may say so? ([Thing TH5](../thing/thing.research.001.md#2-minimal-kernel-and-what-must-be-pluggable) is Explored.)
- May a name or a registry number ever identify? (Proposed: never; references with an issuer and a period.)
- Does an organization keep its identity across rename, change of officers, change of society, merger, split? (Group question 14 asks the same of a group.)
- May an identifier be reused after cessation? (No document says.)

**Lifecycle**
- What are the stages of an organization's life and which are reversible: formed, active, dissolving, ceased, revived, blocked? Which of them are the organization's own and which are a society's facts about it?
- Is "finish date, never deleted" ([management.md](../../management/management.md#organization-lifecycle)) a lifecycle status or a registration rule (the idea's own question)?
- Who may form an organization, and what must a founder be? (The Go comment, the Go code and the update service's `UserType_Org | UserType_Person` disagree.)
- What happens at cessation to the organization's open obligations, accounts, ownerships, acting-for relations and memberships?
- May an organization be reopened, and by whom? (The idea: only the justice system.)

**Authorization**
- Who may act for an organization, by what authority, recorded where, with what threshold? Is the record of that authority a verification record (an external fact with dates)?
- Who may classify a user as an organization or remove the classification?
- Who may block an organization, in whose society, and who may unblock? Is a block global or a society's?
- Who may declare facts in an organization's name (declarer role), and what stops an impostor's declaration from being accepted by a counterparty?
- Who may host another organization and see its facts?

**Rules**
- Which rules does the organization domain itself need, and which belong to the society that recognises it or to the organization (a name format, a minimum number of founders, a minimum vote rule such as the Go's `MinVoteRule`)?
- Where does each attach: formation, recognition, representation, dissolution?
- Is "how many organizations one person may form" a rule of a society, of the application, or of nobody?

**Events** (an EventTarget capability, [Event as a Module Capability](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability))
- Which occurrences are the organization's own (classified, dissolution started, ceased, revived, superseded) and which belong to others (recognition granted, representative changed, membership changed, hosted)?
- Which events carry the requester and the cause, and which may a subscriber learn without learning the organization's members?
- Is a society's block an event of the organization or of the recognition?

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each is a candidate sketch (agent, Tentative or Explored as marked). None exists in the tree; none is created by this research.

1. **formation (kernel event, with the register service).** *Tentative.* The one act by which a collective becomes a user and an organization. It records the founders or the founding rule, the society if there is one, the basis (statute, deed, act of the participants, none), the requester, and the initial representatives. It is the user's admission carrying the organization loop, not a second event. Created by the founders, a registry process, a law or a parent organization; never removed; a wrong formation is superseded or ceased with a cause. It replaces the register service whose "who may form" is written three ways.
2. **recognition (pluggable over society).** *Tentative on the shape, Explored on the home.* `(organization)-[:recognized_by]->(society)` with the identifier the society issued, a period, and its status (granted, suspended, ended). Owns the check of the identifier, the suspension, and the block ("just justice MS"). Created by the society on the organization's request; never removed; ended with a cause. Triggers `recognition_*` events that rules of other domains read ("is this party recognised in this society?"). Gives `Representative`, `Transferred` and `Blocked` a home, and makes a block society-relative.
3. **representation (delegate's, defined once for user, group and organization).** *Tentative.* The acting-for relation with a scope, a period, an ending cause and a threshold, resting on an external fact. The user research asks delegate to take it (finding 6, item 7), the group research lists it as "probably delegate's" (item 5), and the organization case needs it most. It is listed here so that three researches do not each sketch a pluggable (agreement A4 below). Home: [rights/delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md), whose own open question 4 is the record-or-lifecycle choice.
4. **liquidation (pluggable or kernel stage).** *Explored.* The dissolving stage: the purpose limit, the liquidator's authority, the creditors' window, cessation, revival by a ruling. Created by a decision under the organization's rule or a ruling; removed (revived) only by a ruling. Triggers `dissolution_started`, `organization_ceased`, `dissolution_revoked`; open obligations continue unless a rule ends them. The finish-date idea of [management.md](../../management/management.md#organization-lifecycle) is its seed.
5. **succession (use of thing's `supersedes`).** *Tentative.* A merger is a new organization that supersedes two predecessors, a split two organizations that each supersede one, a rename an event on one identity. Which relations pass (contracts, accounts, ownerships) is a successor rule, kept as data in the succession record. Nothing new beyond answering thing's question on many-to-many succession.
6. **kind vocabulary (labels, not a module).** *Tentative.* School, Government, NGO, Business, Online and others, each with its completable sentence (*this organization may be classified as a school because rules ... and therefore ...*), recorded as data with the actor who classified it. A kind that has processing of its own becomes a pluggable ([business](../../../modules-plugable/identity/business/business.md) is the candidate); until then it is a label (catalogue P9).
7. **hosting (pluggable, named for the capability).** *Explored.* A host organization serves the GUI and stores facts of a hosted organization that has no deployment of its own; the hosted organization remains the declarer. Created when the hosted organization's representatives accept the host's admission rule; ended by either side with notice and an export. Triggers `hosting_started`, `hosting_ended`; the host acts for no one by hosting and holds no declarer scope for what it stores. [organization.md](organization.md#naming-rationale-resolved) names this layer Government; the name collides with an existing pluggable (C6), a decision for the owner.
8. **organization-to-organization membership with a representative.** *Explored.* Not a new module: group's unit membership with an organization as the member and the representation relation scoped to that membership (group, finding 6, item 5). Listed so that scenario 7 has a stated home.
9. **same-as for organizations.** *Explored.* The thing-level assertion that two identities are one organization, with evidence and the asserter, never an overwrite and revocable. Needed more here than for users because many graphs deal with the same collective (question 3). Home: thing (TH5).
10. **`OrganizationModel` requirement.** *Explored.* What an organization offers beyond a user: the classification accessor and, if O3 is kernel, the stage. The declarer accessors already exist. Not written (Khayyam rule 6, an empty abstraction); question 14.
11. **Plural `Field_OrganizationsUUID`.** *Tentative.* The array form that inventory was told this domain owns; added with the next protocol wave, after question 15.

### 7. Test scenarios

Written as given, when, then, so that another session can turn them into tests of the Khayyam model once one exists. The bracket is the proposal's own mark.

1. **An organization acts only through a representative (O1, representation) [Tentative].** Given an organization with no credential and a representative with one. When the representative signs a contract. Then the act is attributed to the organization by way of the representative, both appear on the record, and the organization is never asked for a key.
2. **A change of officer does not change the organization [Tentative].** Given an organization with open contracts and one representative. When a new gazette notice replaces the representative. Then the organization's identity, contracts and history are unchanged, the old authority ends at the notice date, and acts the old representative made before it remain valid.
3. **A threshold is respected [Explored].** Given an organization requiring two of three signatories. When one signs. Then the act is incomplete and attributed to nobody yet; when a second signs it completes.
4. **A sole proprietor carries two classifications [Tentative].** Given a person admitted as a user. When she registers a business. Then the same identity also carries the organization loop, the identifier is unchanged, and the event names who classified it.
5. **Hiring does not migrate the identity [Tentative].** Given the sole proprietor above. When she hires an employee. Then the existing identity gains a membership and a second representative, and nothing is created in its place.
6. **An organization may exist with no members and no owner (endowment) [Tentative].** Given an endowment formed by deed with a custodian. When it is read. Then it is an organization with a representative, no members and no owner, and the custodian's authority is acting-for.
7. **Formation without a registry (guild, DAO) [Tentative].** Given three vendors who vouch for a fourth to form a collective. When they record the formation. Then an organization exists with no recognition; asking whether any society recognises it returns none, not an error.
8. **Recognition is plural and dated [Tentative].** Given an organization recognised in society A from 2020. When society B grants recognition in 2024. Then both hold with their own identifiers and periods, and neither replaces the other.
9. **A block is a society's act, not an attribute [Tentative].** Given an organization recognised in A and B. When A's authority blocks it. Then its recognition in A is suspended with the authority and cause, in B it is unchanged, and only A's authority can lift it.
10. **A change of society ends one recognition and begins another [Tentative].** Given an organization recognised in A. When it moves to B. Then A's recognition ends with a cause, B's begins, the identity is unchanged, and the Go's `Transferred` reading is derivable from the pair.
11. **A name is not identity [Tentative].** Given two organizations that held the same name at different times. When the name is reassigned. Then the first organization's identity and history are unchanged, and the second is a different organization.
12. **A registry number is a reference with a period [Tentative].** Given a number issued to an organization that is dissolved and later reissued to another. When both are read. Then each number reference carries its issuer and period, and a lookup by number on a date returns the organization holding it on that date.
13. **A merger supersedes, never deletes (succession) [Tentative].** Given organizations A and B that merge into C. When the merger is recorded. Then C supersedes A and B with a date, A and B remain readable, and references to A and B can learn what replaced them.
14. **A split supersedes many to one [Tentative].** Given organization A split into C and D. When recorded. Then both supersede A, and the rule of which contracts pass to which is recorded with the event.
15. **A rename is an event on one identity [Tentative].** Given an organization renamed. When history is read. Then the identity is the same and both names appear with their periods.
16. **A branch is not an organization (hierarchy) [Tentative].** Given a bank branch with no legal personality. When it is modelled. Then it is a department of the bank (a group under it) with no organization loop; a subsidiary is a separate organization related by ownership of shares.
17. **A franchisee is a separate party [Tentative].** Given a franchise under a licence. When the licence ends. Then the franchisee organization continues; only the contract and the franchise network's membership end.
18. **Dissolution has a stage (liquidation) [Explored].** Given an organization that starts dissolving. When it tries to enter a new trade contract. Then the rule refuses it as outside the purpose; when it pays a creditor it is accepted; at cessation it stops acting and its records stay readable.
19. **Revival is an event by an authority [Explored].** Given a ceased organization. When a ruling revives it. Then a revival event with the authority and cause is recorded, the earlier cessation remains visible, and the identity is unchanged.
20. **The classification is removable by an event, not an overwrite [Tentative].** Given a user found not to be a collective. When an authority ends the classification. Then the end is an event with actor and cause, and past acts remain attributed as they were.
21. **A declarer is checked (O2) [Tentative].** Given a record claiming to be declared by organization X. When the requester has no authority from X. Then the declaration is refused; when it has, the record names X as declarer and the requester as the actor.
22. **A counterparty is known by the receiving graph's own identity (identification L) [Tentative].** Given a hospital and a laboratory that each hold the other. When the hospital reads its record of the laboratory. Then the identity is the hospital's own, linked to the laboratory's by an assertion with evidence, and no global identifier is returned.
23. **A hosted organization stays the declarer [Explored].** Given a small shop hosted by an organization. When the shop issues an invoice. Then the shop is the declarer, the host is named as the host, and the host cannot state or edit the shop's facts.
24. **An organization is a member of another through a representative [Explored].** Given a ministry that sits on a university committee. When the ministry replaces its official. Then membership continues, the representation changes, and a past vote by the old official remains attributed to the ministry through him.
25. **A cycle through membership is harmless to the fact [Tentative].** Given a university on a ministry's board and the ministry on the university's committee. When both memberships are recorded. Then both exist; a derivation that inherits authority through unit membership refuses to walk the cycle.

### 8. Open questions for the owner

Ordered by what they change. Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. **The first three change what the domain is.**

1. **Does "organization" name only the participant, with the declarer a role it plays, or also the authority scope (tenant) as a second concept?** Default: only the participant; "the declaring organization" of product, invoice, account and thing is the participant in the declarer role; no tenant concept here; hosting is a separate capability. Blocks: the meaning of `Field_OrganizationUUID` and `Field_OrganizationOwnerID`, the sibling defaults "one node per organization with a mapping", invoice exchange, [organization handoff item 6](organization.handoff.md), the Government naming.
2. **Must an organization have members, an owner, a body or a legal recognition?** Default: none of these; an organization is a user that rules treat as a collective party; members, owners and recognition are optional relations; the opening definition of [organization.md](organization.md) is amended. Blocks: scenarios 2, 3 and 4 (sole proprietor, DAO, endowment), the register service's founder rule, and whether the application ever becomes the register of lawful collectives.

**Contradictions across domains** (reported, not resolved; the domain's own are under [Contradictions found](#contradictions-found)).

Then an organization that is a "group of groups" is a user classified as an organization **and** a thing with members: two classifications on one identity, which is coherent, but [organization.md](organization.md) states it as a type relation ("higher-order") and calls it settled.
- **X4 — Uniqueness of the UUID.** Thing: "unique means no two different referents share a value" (contradiction 8); user: minted per registration; the organization handoff asks whether it is one UUID with every other domain's. Organization's cross-graph use makes the question more pressing (question 3).

- **X6 — "Unlinkability" against "known by many".** User scenario 10 (a person's right not to be joined across deployments) and this research's scenario 10 (a collective must be recognisable across graphs) pull opposite ways; per kind of user the default of `same_as` may differ. A person should be unlinkable by default; an organization is publicly recognised by design (registers are public). Not resolved here; a rule per kind would be a new fact for the owner.

## Contradictions found

Reported, not resolved. Each names the documents that disagree.

- **C1 — The opening definition against the scenarios.** [organization.md](organization.md): an organization is an entity "comprising one or more people". Endowments, a river or idol with personhood, a DAO of keys and a one-person business are collectives with no people besides themselves or none at all (scenarios 2, 3, 4). The text is imported encyclopedia text; the protocol section next to it says "any collective, non-person identity".
- **C2 — "Group of groups" is settled, and group's own research does not settle it.** [organization.md](organization.md#organization-vs-group-conceptual-level) calls it "clear and settled"; the group research (its contradiction 4) records that organization and group are sibling loops on a user, so neither contains the other as a type.
- **C3 — The protocol means the scope, the document means the participant.** The only code is `Field_OrganizationUUID` ("the organization the record belongs to") and `Field_OrganizationOwnerID`; the document describes a collective party (finding 1, meanings 1 and 2).
- **C4 — The promised plural is absent.** [inventory.handoff.md](../../../modules-plugable/economics/inventory/inventory.handoff.md) (answered 2026-10-08): "`identity/organization`'s protocol wave gains `Field_OrganizationsUUID` with its array type"; [protocol/dt-uuid.kh](protocol/dt-uuid.kh) has none. The uri handoff also notes the file declares no `OrganizationUUID` type and its accessor returns `UserUUID`.
- **C5 — Edge words filed as code.** The handoff already says `edges/organization.kh` is filed under the wrong bucket and that neither label file has a document; the labels are a classification and a relation vocabulary kept as Go `const` blocks.
- **C6 — "Government" names two things.** [organization.md](organization.md#naming-rationale-resolved) reserves **Government** for a future hosting layer; [modules-plugable/identity/government](../../../modules-plugable/identity/government/government.md) is "a government-issued identifier on a user", not "a state-as-organization model"; and [membership-and-identity](../../../bundles/government/modules/membership-and-identity/membership-and-identity.md) uses "governing organisation" for the society's platform and its forks.
- **C7 — Citizenship is stored in two opposite directions in two homes.** [citizen](edges/citizen/citizen.md): `(user)-[:citizen]->(organization)`; [society national-of](../../../modules-plugable/identity/society/edges/national-of/national-of.md): `(society)-[:national_of]->(person)`. The same fact, inverse arrows, two modules, and a society is an organization.
- **C8 — Who may register an organization is stated three ways.** The comment in [register-org.kh](../../management/organization/_import/organization/register-org.kh): only an active, identified, verified real person; the code in [service-register.kh](../../management/organization/_import/organization/service-register.kh): refuses anyone but `adminUserID`, with an authorization declared as `UserType_Person`; the update service: `UserType_Org | UserType_Person` with a TODO to remove person "when register org free by everyone".
- **C9 — `Blocked` differs from the shared block.** The Go `OrganizationStatusBlocked` is set by "just justice MS" on the record; the shared [block](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/block/block.md) status is "by justice (admins) or fraud detection"; the user and group researches report the same stem with a different claim (user C8, group contradiction 8). Here the block is also society-relative (scenario 8).
- **C10 — A society that "does not work".** The Go constants `Society_Persia` and `Society_Iran` carry the note "SocietyID not work"; Persia contains Iran in the Go's own ordering; [society](../../../modules-plugable/identity/society/society.md) is a one-line document. The record `SocietyID` and the recognition proposal both depend on what a society is, and no document says.
- **C11 — The module is split across two folders.** [modules.handoff.md](../../modules.handoff.md) records that `management/organization` has only Go residue and `identity/organization` is the concept home, and asks the owner whether the services belong here or in a management pluggable; unresolved.
- **C12 — No authentication token against the user sentence.** The Go media type: an organization "doesn't have any authenticate token"; [user.md](../user/user.md): "each user must have a valid public key" (the user research's C1). Consistent with the user research's refinement; recorded so that the organization case is cited when the sentence is amended.
