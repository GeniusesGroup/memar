# Khayyam Language Gaps
## Abstract
This document is what three modelling sessions measured about Khayyam while compiling probes under [`bootstrap/`](./bootstrap/), and what each measurement would need in the language documents before the thing it measured can be written. A probe is a measurement held as a document: it compiles, or it is refused with a named label, and the label is a finding about the language rather than an opinion about it. Fourteen interventions are recorded here, each carrying the document and anchor it belongs in, replacement-ready text in that document's own voice and terminology, what the text deliberately does not claim, what it releases, and every other artifact it would leave inconsistent. One further finding needs no text at all and says so. Twelve further questions are recorded separately, because what they need is a choice among alternatives and only an owner can make it; a drafter who phrases them has not answered them. Three findings are on the critical path and they are three different kinds of gap: a sentence that never says whose name a body-less method carries, a spelling on which the documents disagree with each other and with the parser, and a rule that forbids what two models need (an abstraction's signature admits neither a Scope nor a concrete capsule). Two of the fourteen are rules of this toolchain's own level rather than claims about the language — the display contract's role for a provision, and the Rule that records which provision supplies which body-less declaration — and each is written for the document that owns that level rather than for `docs/khayyam/`.

## Introduction
### Motivation
Three models were written against this target's probes and each stopped. [`modules/process/control-flow/`](../../../process/control-flow/) has no declaration it can write, because the type that would own a conditional pair is a category the language has not admitted to a signature. [`modules/process/construction/`](../../../process/construction/) has no declaration it can write, because the same rule forbids the one shape a shared construction contract could take. [`modules/computer/host-interface/`](../../../computer/host-interface/) cannot write its first capsule, because nothing says whether a body-less method's name belongs to the program or to whoever supplies the body — and until that is said, the separation the owner requires between a host's vocabulary and the language's own has no rule that can cite it. None of the three is blocked by a hard problem. Each is blocked by a sentence that is not written, and each session reported the silence rather than filling it, which is the correct behaviour and leaves the silence in place.

The measurements are not all of the same kind, and treating them as one is the failure this document exists to prevent. Some are claims about the grammar: what may be written where a signature writes a type, whether a body-less method and an empty body are one declaration or two. Some are claims the framework owns: whether deciding to run a body is an operation that can fail, which class of error a halt carries. Some are rules of this toolchain: how a name a body may reach is decided by the tool, not by the language, and the language documents are silent about it correctly. Writing any of the third kind into `docs/khayyam/` would put a claim in a document that has no standing to make it, and that is the most expensive error available here.

### Methodology
Every claim below was verified by reading the file it names at the line it names, in this tree. Where a count is given it came from a scan over every `.kh` file outside `build/`, not from a sample, and the scan's result is stated so a reader can repeat it. Two scans produced findings the models did not have: one over the corpus for methods whose declaration ends at the third group with nothing after it, and one over [`docs/khayyam/`](../../../../docs/khayyam/) for the spelling of a body-less method. Both are reported under [What this target measured](#what-this-target-measured-and-what-the-measurements-found), and both change what the interventions say.

Three standing rules shape what follows. **A gap is reported as a gap.** Where no document states something, this document names the document, the section, the line, and the sentence that would have to be written; it does not propose what that sentence ought to conclude where the evidence cannot settle it, and it says so where it cannot. **An intervention is drafted, not applied.** Every file named as a target of an intervention is a file this document does not edit, and each intervention is written so that adopting it needs no further drafting — but adoption is the owner's, and a text that presupposes a ruling says which ruling it presupposes and what must be written instead if the ruling goes the other way. **A level is named before a claim is written.** Each intervention states whether its subject is the language, a protocol, or this toolchain, and a toolchain-level finding is written for the toolchain's own documents rather than for the language's.

## Explanation
### What this target measured, and what the measurements found
The probes and what each shows are in [`target.md`](./target.md#what-a-compiler-written-in-khayyam-would-need) and the paired [handoff](./target.handoff.md); this section records only what bears on the interventions below.

The seven probes under [`bootstrap/`](./bootstrap/) compile as the handoff records, and two of them fail on the label they were written to find. [`behaviour.kh:15`](./bootstrap/behaviour.kh) writes `(body Method)` and is refused `unbound-type-name`, because the language admits a method in an argument position ([`method.md:71`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#type-level-arguments-for-sc-and-mt)) and no unit can declare the type that holds one. [`write-field.kh:31`](./bootstrap/write-field.kh) is refused because a call writes into a name the body binds and a field is not one, which is a rule of this toolchain and not a claim any document makes — [`target.handoff.md:88`](./target.handoff.md) records it as such and is right. The remaining five compile, and three of the five leave a question the language has not answered: what fills a slot a body declared ([`construct.kh:26`](./bootstrap/construct.kh)), how a scope is driven ([`repeat.kh:25`](./bootstrap/repeat.kh), where the probe declares a scope and has nothing that can drive it), and what a declaration without a body means.

Two scans over the tree add what the probes do not.

**The documents disagree with each other about the spelling of a body-less method.** [`khayyam.md:89`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) and [`method.md:77`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) both write it as `{}` — "A method can be defined without a body (`{}`)". [`abstraction.md:60`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md) and [`khayyam.practice.md:63`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.practice.md) both write it with nothing after the third group — `tp Read mt (self Reader) (data Element) (err Error)`. The parser sides with the second: [`core/src/parse.ts`](../../core/src/parse.ts) reads a method's body through `parseBlock()`, which returns `null` when no `{` follows the third group, and [`targets/js/src/program.ts`](./src/program.ts) reads that as the whole difference between `implemented`, `contract`, and `supplied`. Nothing in `docs/khayyam/` states which of the two spellings is the body-less one.

**The corpus holds 59 body-less methods on concrete capsules, not three.** [`modules/computer/host-interface/model.md:196`](../../../computer/host-interface/model.md) reports "exactly three body-less methods on a concrete capsule exist" and that all three are recorded as cases where the body was not meant to be external. Under the parser's reading those three — [`modules/gui/initialize.kh:6`](../../../gui/initialize.kh), [`modules/gui/navigator.kh:13`](../../../gui/navigator.kh) and `:18` — carry `{}` and are therefore *not* body-less at all; they are the three methods the corpus itself flagged as doubtful. A scan for methods whose declaration ends at the third group with nothing after it, whose owner is a `cp` declared in the same file, finds **59**: eight under this folder's `bootstrap/` and `demo/`, and fifty-one in [`modules/math/`](../../../math) and [`modules/time/duration/`](../../../time/duration) — every `ToString`, `FromString`, `R32_Equal`, `W8_FromString_Base10`, `Second_FromNanoSecond`, and the rest, on capsules such as [`W32`](../../../math/integer/unsigned-32.kh). None of those fifty-one was meant to be supplied by a host; they are the port's arithmetic and text conversions, and under the parser's reading they are `supplied` and wait at run time for an implementation this repository does not write. The same scan finds **728** body-less methods in total and 664 of them on abstractions, which is the contract form and is what it should be.

This is why the spelling question is on the critical path and not a detail. Whichever way the owner rules, fifty declarations on numeric and duration capsules change what they are, and today nothing says which way they are read.

### How the interventions are ordered
Fourteen interventions, in the order a reviewer should take them.

The critical path is three, and they are three different kinds of failure. **I-B** is a missing sentence: the language states that a body-less method's implementation is supplied externally and never says whose name the declaration carries, so the host-interface model's central claim has no document behind it and no rule may cite one. It is first because it is two sentences, it releases an entire model, and it is the owner's own stated requirement. **I-A** is a conflict rather than a silence: two documents give the body-less method a body and two give it none, and the parser sides with the second, so the corpus's fifty arithmetic conversions and three doubtful declarations are classified in opposite directions from what the documents say. It is second because it is the same two sections as I-B, it decides what a Rule and a display role must look for, and it is the one intervention with a measured consequence already in the tree. **I-C** is a rule in the way: `abstraction.md:101` forbids what two of the three models need, and one edit to one sentence releases the conditional pair's declaration site, the construction library's shared contract, and the protocol's own registered conditional-pair topic together. It is third because it is the only one where a *weaker* text would be a decision made by omission.

Then, in dependency order: **I-E** (the spelling of a scope parameter) and **I-F** (what a call writes) each release one model's first signature and neither blocks the other; **I-D** makes the construction model's per-Type answer legible as the current rule rather than an omission; **I-H**, **I-N**, **I-J**, and **I-I** are protocol-level sentences that cost one paragraph each and unblock the fallibility and halting questions by giving them something to apply; **I-G** removes one unreferenced word from one sentence; **I-L** is conditional on a ruling about a corpus file. **I-M** and **I-K** are hygiene, and **I-K** cannot be written before I-A and I-B.

What can wait: nothing here is optional, but **I-K** can wait for a second need, **I-L** waits for the owner of [`modules/math/logic/`](../../../math/logic), and the whole cluster of fallibility questions waits behind **I-N**, which defines the predicate rather than applying it.

### The interventions
#### I-A — Which spelling declares a method body-less
**The gap.** [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:89`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) and [`docs/khayyam/method.md:77`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) write the body-less form as `{}`, while [`abstraction.md:60`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md), [`khayyam.practice.md:63`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.practice.md) and the parser at [`core/src/parse.ts`](../../core/src/parse.ts) write it as a declaration that ends at its third group, so the language does not say which of the two a body-less method is and the corpus's fifty arithmetic conversions on numeric capsules are classified one way while its three doubtful declarations are classified the other.

**Document and anchor.** [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md), section **Method** (`#### Method`, under *Type*, anchored at the `Body-less Methods (FFI & Contracts)` bullet) — replacing that bullet's first sentence; and [`docs/khayyam/method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md), section **Body-less Methods (FFI and Contracts)** — replacing its opening sentence. The same sentence appears twice, once in each document's own section for the form, which is why one sentence in each is the whole of the edit.

**Proposed text.** For [`khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md):

```
- **Body-less Methods (FFI & Contracts):** A method is declared body-less when its line ends at the third group. A method whose line ends with `{}` is declared with a body, and an empty body is a body carrying no command. The two are different declarations, and a tool that reads one as the other is wrong rather than lenient. This is legitimately used in two scenarios:
```

For [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md):

```
A method is declared without a body when nothing follows its third group; `{}` at the end of a declaration is a body, and an empty body is a body carrying no command. Nothing in the grammar makes the two one declaration, and what a body-less declaration asks for is not what an empty one asks for. This is legally used in two scenarios:
```

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** It makes one claim: a body-less declaration and an empty body are distinguishable, and which spelling is which. It deliberately does not say what a body-less declaration *means* — that is I-B's sentence, and keeping them apart means either can be ruled on without the other. It does not say an empty body is an error, which would be a governance claim [Minimal Legislation](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#minimal-legislation-a-principle) sends to a rule, nor that an empty body is ever what an author meant. It does not claim the parser is right; it states the language's answer and leaves the parser to follow. The alternative text — ruling that `{}` *is* the body-less form — leaves a declaration with nothing in it unspellable, which is the state [`modules/gui/initialize.kh:6`](../../../gui/initialize.kh) and [`navigator.kh:13,18`](../../../gui/navigator.kh) are in today and which three corpus comments already record as doubtful. A weaker text that merely says "the two are different" would leave the fifty conversions unclassified.

**What it would unblock.** [`modules/computer/host-interface/model.md`](../../../computer/host-interface/model.md)'s finding that a body-less method is indistinguishable from an empty one, which its handoff carries as its second open question and which it calls the concept's "sharpest tooling finding"; the Rule that I-B's enforcement needs, since a rule cannot say what to look for while two spellings are one; and I-K, which is server-only rather than grammar-scope unless this sentence is written first.

**What it would change elsewhere.** A follow-up edit, not a design change, in four places: [`modules/gui/initialize.kh:8`](../../../gui/initialize.kh) and [`navigator.kh:21,23`](../../../gui/navigator.kh) carry three `TODO(go-migrate)` comments asking this question and would be retired; [`target.md:55`](./target.md#the-three-kinds-of-body-less-method) and [`program.ts`](./src/program.ts) already implement this reading and need no change, but [`target.md`](./target.md) would gain a sentence citing it as the language's rather than the target's; and the fifty declarations in [`modules/math/`](../../../math) and [`modules/time/duration/`](../../../time/duration) become a real design question — under this ruling each is a `supplied` method whose host supplies nothing, which is a corpus problem for those folders' owners and not for this document.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language**. Which spelling a declaration uses is what a program's well-formedness turns on, which is [Minimal Legislation](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#minimal-legislation-a-principle)'s own test.

#### I-B — Whose name a body-less method carries
**The gap.** [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:91`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) and [`docs/khayyam/method.md:80`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) say that a body-less method on a capsule signals that "the implementation will be provided externally during the linking phase" and stop there, so nothing states whether the *declaration's* name belongs to the program or to whoever supplies the body.

**Document and anchor.** The same two as I-A — [`khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md) section **Method**, appended to the `Foreign Function Interface (FFI)` bullet, and [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md) section **Body-less Methods (FFI and Contracts)**, appended to item 2. The enforcement is a second artifact: a new Rule at `modules/khayyam/rules/provision-record/provision-record.md` in the shape of [`scope-placement`](../../../khayyam/rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.md), whose normative claim is the sentence drafted here — which is what [`Linter → A rule's home is its subject's document`](../../../../docs/protocols/computer/linter.md#a-rules-home-is-its-subjects-document) requires.

**Proposed text.** For [`khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md), appended to the FFI bullet:

```
  The declaration is a provision: its name, its signature, and its meaning belong to the program that declares it, and only its execution is supplied. A name the supplying environment uses for the same capability is not a name this method may carry. Where the correspondence between a provision and a facility is to be recorded, it is recorded as a checkable declaration and not as a coincidence of spelling; where a rule requires that record, the rule is this repository's own.
```

For [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md), appended to item 2:

```
   A body-less method on a capsule is a provision, and the provision's name is the program's. A name the supplying environment uses for the same capability is not a name this method may carry: a name with two owners has no owner for a rule to point at, which [Khayyam → Import Mechanism (`in`)](./khayyam.md#import-mechanism-in) names as an architecture error.
```

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** It makes one claim — the name is the program's, and only the execution is supplied — which is the minimum the host-interface model's central claim needs. It deliberately does not name the term "provision" as a new Memar concept with a definition of its own; it uses the word in its ordinary sense of an arrangement made in advance, which is what [Terminology → The Default Meaning of an Unreferenced Term](../../../../docs/terminology.md#the-default-meaning-of-an-unreferenced-term) already licenses, and which is what makes it safe in a language document that must not adopt a protocol's definitions. It does not say a provision *may* not be implemented in Khayyam — a body-less method whose implementation a later target supplies is still a provision, and closing that would forbid the toolchain's own path. It does not say how the correspondence is recorded; it says a rule requiring the record is the repository's own, which leaves the Rule's content to its author. A stronger text would name the host's mechanism, which is a toolchain fact and would put a claim in the language that I-A's neighbour cannot keep.

**What it would unblock.** [`modules/computer/host-interface/model.md`](../../../computer/host-interface/model.md)'s first open question, which its handoff records as "Blocks continuation: yes. No facility interface can be declared without it, and no rule may cite the separation until a document states it"; the Rule that its handoff lists as anticipated work; and the owner's requirement that a host's interface and the language's own future method never share one name, which until now is a modeling decision no rule can cite.

**What it would change elsewhere.** A follow-up edit in [`modules/computer/host-interface/model.md`](../../../computer/host-interface/model.md) and its handoff, which would move from carrying the separation as an unbacked decision to citing it; a new Rule folder with the accept/report shape of the existing catalog; and a real design change for the corpus, since [`modules/gui/protocol/element-status.kh:86`](../../../gui/protocol/element-status.kh), [`element.kh:166,214,338,386,470,632`](../../../gui/protocol/element.kh) and [`modules/process/error/protocol/error.kh:116,311`](../../../process/error/protocol/error.kh) carry nine commented occurrences of `HTMLAudioElement.Beep` that this sentence makes inadmissible as method names — they are archived source, so no edit is forced, but the owner's own example appears in two live protocol files under a comment.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language** states the position, because the separation between a host's vocabulary and the program's is the condition under which reaching a facility is consumption rather than structure acquisition ([Runtime → A runtime does not establish structure](../../../../docs/protocols/computer/runtime.md#a-runtime-does-not-establish-structure)). Its *enforcement* is a **toolchain** decision, by [Minimal Legislation](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#minimal-legislation-a-principle): whether a name is the program's or the host's changes what a reader and a rule may assume, not what any program means. The two halves belong in different documents and are drafted separately.

#### I-C — Whether an abstraction's signature may name a Scope
**The gap.** A conditional pair's members must name a scope ([`control-flow.md:59`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md)), a code scope is the `sc` subtype ([`khayyam.md:128`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope)), a Scope is a fourth Type category that is not an Abstraction ([`type.md:181`](../../../../docs/type.md#scope--type-as-semantic-boundary)), and an abstraction's method signature may name only other abstractions ([`abstraction.md:101`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do)) — so the type that would own a conditional pair, which is the only kind under which the protocol's replaceability claim ([`control-flow.md:130`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#mechanism-summary)) means anything, cannot name the scope its members receive.

**Document and anchor.** [`docs/khayyam/abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md), section **What You Cannot Do** — replacing the second bullet; and, as its counterpart, [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md), section **Scope** — one bullet added. The second is not optional: a bullet in *What You Cannot Do* that admits `sc` without *Scope* saying the grammar writes it leaves the two documents contradicting each other, which is what [`documentation.md → Content Rule`](../../../../docs/documentation.md#content-rule-no-fabricated-or-redundant-provenance) exists to prevent.

**Proposed text.** For [`abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md), replacing the second bullet of *What You Cannot Do*:

```
- You **cannot** use a capsule type as an argument or return type in an abstraction's method signature — only other abstractions, and the `sc` subtype, are allowed. The specification stays implementation-agnostic by naming what it requires rather than how it is built: an abstraction names concepts, and a `sc` names a boundary, which [Type → Scope — Type as Semantic Boundary](../type.md#scope--type-as-semantic-boundary) counts at several levels — a namespace, a package's visibility, a local algorithmic block — none of which settles what an implementer is. What it may not name is a capsule, because a capsule is one realization.
```

For [`khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md), added to *Scope* after the first bullet:

```
- A `sc` may be written where a signature writes a type: `tp Branch mt (self Flow) (branch sc) ()` names any code scope rather than an instance of a declared type. This is what lets an abstraction-owned method receive a scope it cannot name as a capsule — see [Abstraction in Khayyam → What You Cannot Do](./abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do).
```

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** It makes one claim: the Scope category is admissible in an abstraction's signature, and the reason is the same implementation-agnosticism the existing bullet gives, re-derived for a boundary rather than asserted. It deliberately does not admit a capsule, and it deliberately does not say what a `sc` *parameter* may be paired with — that is I-E, and writing both here would make one edit carry two rulings. It does not claim the Scope category is realized the same way everywhere; [`type.md:186`](../../../../docs/type.md#scope--type-as-semantic-boundary) says placement is the realization's to choose, and the text leans on that rather than overriding it. A weaker text — admitting the Scope without giving the reason — would leave a reader unable to tell whether the exception was considered, and a stronger one — admitting capsule and Scope together — would settle I-D by omission, which is the decision this document must not make for the owner.

**What it would unblock.** The declaration site of the conditional pair, which [`modules/process/control-flow/model.handoff.md:75`](../../../process/control-flow/model.handoff.md) already calls "one decision wearing two hats"; the protocol's own conditional-pair topic that [`control-flow.md:150`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#topics-to-be-developed-here) registers and does not hold; and the generic `IF`/`ELSE` form whose realization [`control-flow.md:37`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#the-generic-form-ifelse) describes as "a generic conditional abstraction that receives an explicit branch scope".

**What it would change elsewhere.** A real design change for one model and a follow-up edit everywhere else. [`modules/process/control-flow/answers.md:159`](../../../process/control-flow/answers.md) records that the only measured realization of a two-branch contract puts the pair inside a concrete capsule to satisfy this rule, and that this gives up the replaceability [`control-flow.md:130`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#mechanism-summary) claims; this sentence lets that trade be declined rather than taken. [`docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md:61`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#library-defined-control-flow) writes its examples without an owner and says why; those examples change once the owner is decided, not once this is. [`modules/khayyam/rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md:15`](../../../khayyam/rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md) states that type-level arguments "that the signature explicitly allows remain valid" and would gain the scope spelling from I-E. And [`docs/type.md:191`](../../../../docs/type.md) would gain a sentence: it currently says the four categories are "equally direct realizations of Type; none is nested inside another as a subtype", which stays true, but a reader of that table alone would not learn that one of them is admissible where a signature writes a type.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language**. What a signature may name is ontology — whether a relationship may appear at all — which [`khayyam.md:164`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#separation-of-syntax-and-governance-a-principle) assigns to the compiler.

#### I-D — What follows from the capsule rule for obtaining an instance
**The gap.** An abstraction's signature may not name a capsule ([`abstraction.md:101`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do)) while a construction's result is a concrete instance, so no shared construction contract can exist and organizational replaceability is foreclosed — and the corpus names the responsibility thirty-three times (`tp From* mt` declarations) and discharges it zero times, none of the thirty-three carrying an influenced variable of the type it produces.

**Document and anchor.** [`docs/khayyam/abstraction.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md), section **What You Cannot Do** — a bullet added after the second, stating the consequence the rule has. Not [`abstraction.md` → A Concept Is an Abstraction; a Concrete Instance Is a Capsule](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#a-concept-is-an-abstraction-a-concrete-instance-is-a-capsule), which at `:108`–`:110` already draws the line between a capsule-owned signature and an abstraction-owned one; this sentence belongs with the rule it follows from, so a reader who meets the rule meets what it costs.

**Proposed text.**

```
- An abstraction therefore cannot be a contract over the production of a capsule's instances. A program that needs an instance it does not hold reaches it through a method of the capsule itself, called on the type, and no contract may stand between the caller and that method — which is why [`variable.md`](./variable.md#overview)'s statement that "creation and initialization remain responsibilities of capsule behavior" names a capsule and not a contract.
```

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** It makes one claim: under the rule as it stands, obtaining an instance is per-Type and no abstraction may mediate it. That is a true consequence of the existing rule, it is what the thirty-three corpus declarations already assume, and stating it makes the current state legible rather than leaving it to be inferred as an omission. It deliberately does not say this is the right arrangement — that is the owner's choice, carried as a question. It deliberately does not restate the rule or explain it, because *What You Cannot Do* is where the rule lives and this sentence is its consequence. A stronger text would either weaken the rule or forbid per-Type constructions outright, and both are decisions. A weaker text — nothing at all — leaves the construction model's central finding resting on an inference the language documents never license in writing.

**What it would unblock.** [`modules/process/construction/model.md`](../../../process/construction/model.md)'s handoff question "May a construction be declared as a shared contract, and if not, what replaces it?", whose answer under the rule as it stands is now written down rather than derived; and the file layout decision that follows, since per-Type means one declaration per Type rather than one file.

**What it would change elsewhere.** Nothing becomes inconsistent — the sentence asserts what the documents already imply. What it changes is the *cost* the owner is choosing between: admitting a capsule in an abstraction's signature would replace this bullet with new text in the rule above it and would make [`docs/khayyam/polymorphism.md:202`](../../../../docs/khayyam/polymorphism.md)'s `Factory` — which returns an abstraction — the wrong example for a construction, since its result is a capsule. That is a real design change and is the question, not this text.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language**. This is a claim about what the grammar admits, stated as a consequence of an existing grammar rule.

#### I-E — What a scope parameter is written as, and what an `sc` position carries
**The gap.** [`docs/khayyam/method.md:71`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#type-level-arguments-for-sc-and-mt) admits a `vr`, an `sc`, or an `mt` in an influencing group and says nothing about how the parameter's type is written, while [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md:132`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope) says the position is satisfied "by the type itself passed as a type-level argument" — a different mechanism from the only measured declaration, which writes a capsule named `Scope` ([`emit.test.ts:15,23`](./test/emit.test.ts)) — so the two documents that describe the position disagree about what fills it.

**Document and anchor.** [`docs/khayyam/method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md), section **Type-level arguments for `sc` and `mt`** — the paragraph extended; and [`docs/khayyam/khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md), section **Scope** — the fifth bullet (`An argument position may be satisfied by…`) replaced, because it is the sentence that currently describes the other mechanism.

**Proposed text.** For [`method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md), extending the paragraph at `:71`:

```
 A method's influencing group may receive a `vr`, an `sc`, or an `mt`. The compiler distinguishes the three from the callee's signature and performs the behavior that position requires. A `vr` is an instance of a capsule or an abstraction and is written with the type it has: `(value SomeType)`. An `sc` is a code scope: a library method that drives a scope receives that scope here ([Khayyam → Scope](./khayyam.md#scope)), and it is written by naming the category rather than a declared type: `(branch sc)`. An `mt` is a method and is written the same way: `(handler mt)`. Naming the category is what lets one position carry every value of it, including a scope a body declared and no declaration names; naming a capsule instead declares a type the grammar already has and loses every scope that is not an instance of it.
```

For [`khayyam.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md), replacing the bullet at `:132`:

```
- An argument position is satisfied by a `vr` of the declared type, or — where the signature writes the category `sc` or `mt` — by the scope or the method itself, with no declared type in the position. See [Method → Type-level arguments for `sc` and `mt`](./method.md#type-level-arguments-for-sc-and-mt).
```

**A term this text needs, and the reason.** *Category Name* — a name of one of the four Type categories written where a signature writes a type, admitting every value of that category rather than the instances of one declared type. The repository already has the pieces and no word for the position: [`khayyam.md:211`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#type-principles-realized) says the subtype keywords "specif[y] its category", [`type.md:191`](../../../../docs/type.md) names the four categories as "equally direct realizations of Type", and [`modules/khayyam/rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md:9`](../../../khayyam/rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md) is a rule about the same subject under a name that does not fit. The term is needed because the alternative — "the type-level argument" — is already used at [`khayyam.md:132`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#scope) for something else, and two names for one position would be worse than one name for a position nothing has named.

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** It makes one claim: an `sc` and an `mt` position are written as a category and carry no declared type, and that is what "type-level argument" means. It deliberately does not say which values of the Scope category a position admits beyond code scopes, because [`type.md:184`](../../../../docs/type.md#scope--type-as-semantic-boundary) counts several realizations and this language's `sc` is one of them. It does not settle the recorded conflict between the documents and the one measurement — that is I-M. A weaker text would restate `:132` and leave the disagreement. A stronger one would forbid a declared type in an `sc` position outright, which forecloses the alternative spelling before the owner has seen it.

**What it would unblock.** Every signature in [`modules/process/control-flow/`](../../../process/control-flow/), which its answers document calls "the sharpest single gap" for construction and the critical path for control flow; the corpus's only witness to the question, the comment at [`modules/computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh:3,6`](../../../computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh) — `// OnAbsent(call scope) (err Error)` — which becomes a declaration rather than a comment; and I-C's counterpart bullet in *Scope*, which this makes a spelling rather than a promise.

**What it would change elsewhere.** A real change to this toolchain, not a follow-up edit: the frontend must accept a category keyword where it now reads a name, which means a new accept row and possibly a new refusal label in [`core/`](../../core/), and the parser currently treats every position entry as a name plus an optional type. [`modules/khayyam/rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md:15`](../../../khayyam/rules/type-as-argument/type-as-argument.md) extends rather than contradicts. The 728 body-less methods in the corpus are unaffected. And the removed `targets/js/lib/` sources, recorded at [`target.changelog.md:57`](./target.changelog.md), are gone, so the one measurement that exists — [`emit.test.ts:15`](./test/emit.test.ts)'s `tp Scope cp` — would become wrong rather than merely superseded, and the fixture would need rewriting with the module that owns it.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language**, and a grammar decision rather than a principle: whether a program's well-formedness turns on what a position names. That makes it the largest single change in this document and the one with the most toolchain consequence.

#### I-F — What a call writes, and where a produced instance arrives
**The gap.** [`docs/khayyam/method.md:39`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md#key-rules) states that every variable of a call is passed strictly by reference, `:89` defines an influenced variable as one "changed by the method as a side effect of the call", and `:41` admits a call on the parent type with no receiver — but no sentence says the influenced group is the only place a result arrives, so nothing in the language documents answers whether a construction's instance can arrive at all, and the thirty-three `From*` declarations are consistent with an instance that appears nowhere.

**Document and anchor.** [`docs/khayyam/method.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/method.md), section **Key Rules** — a bullet added after the by-reference rule it depends on.

**Proposed text.**

```
- **What a call writes.** The influenced group is where a call's result arrives. Every variable in it is a name the caller declared, handed over by reference, and written by the callee and by nothing else; no call returns a value, and `return` carries nothing on its line ([Khayyam → Scope](./khayyam.md#scope)). A method whose influenced group names a variable of its own owner's type is therefore how a program obtains an instance it does not hold, and a method called on its parent type writes the same group with no receiver to write anything else into ([Method Invocation Rules](./method.md#method-invocation-rules)).
```

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** It makes one claim: the influenced group is where a result arrives, and a method may write a variable of its own owner's type there. It deliberately does not say an influenced variable is written by that call *alone*; exclusivity is a definite-assignment question, and [`khayyam.md:146`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#how-khayyam-realizes-memory) already names definite-assignment analysis as where that is obtained. It does not say what producing an instance *may* fail at — that is I-N's predicate and the owner's application of it. A weaker sentence would restate `:89`; a stronger one would make the influenced group the only way to obtain a value and so would close the file-level-variable question [`variable.handoff.md`](../../../../docs/khayyam/variable.handoff.md) already holds open.

**What it would unblock.** [`modules/process/construction/model.md:150`](../../../process/construction/model.md)'s delivery question — which its handoff calls the gap that "decides every signature in a construction library" — reduces from a gap to a stated fact, and the thirty-three corpus declarations become legible as *incomplete* rather than as *contradicting* the language.

**What it would change elsewhere.** A follow-up edit: the thirty-three `From*` declarations in [`modules/math/`](../../../math), [`modules/modules/`](../../../modules) and [`modules/time/duration/`](../../../time/duration) each need an influenced variable of the type they produce, which is a corpus change for those folders' owners; [`docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md:62`](../../../../docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md#sovereign-encapsulation) already says obtaining a new instance requires "a method of that type" once I-G is applied, so the two sentences agree. **Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **language**. It is a claim about what a call does to the variables it is handed.

#### I-K — A display role for a provision
**The gap.** [`modules/khayyam/display/display.json`](../../../khayyam/display/display.json) declares twenty-one roles, each naming what an identifier *is*, and none distinguishes a declaration whose execution the environment supplies from one the repository implements — so a reader cannot be told which declarations are provisions, because the vocabulary that would let them be told does not exist.

**Document and anchor.** [`modules/khayyam/display/display.json`](../../../khayyam/display/display.json), in `roles` — one role object added, its two attributes and its scope drawn from the `method` role; and one sentence added to `classification` alongside the `variable`/`method-local` pair, which is the contract's existing precedent for two roles sharing one kind.

**Proposed text.** The role object:

```json
{
  "name": "provision",
  "kind": {
    "standardType": "method",
    "statement": "A Method whose declaration ends at its third group, so nothing in the source says what its execution is and what arrives instead. It is a kind of Method and not a Method of its own: the name is the program's and only the execution is supplied (docs/khayyam/method.md, Body-less Methods)."
  },
  "origin": "proposed (the language distinguishes a body-less declaration from an empty body; docs/khayyam/khayyam.md, Method)",
  "identity": "A Method: a callable Type whose execution a provision supplies.",
  "appliesTo": "The name in `tp {name} mt (...)` where nothing follows the third group. The name is the same text a `method` role takes, and the two are told apart by what follows the third group, not by where the name is valid.",
  "implementedBy": "grammar-scope",
  "scopes": ["entity.name.function.khayyam"],
  "attributes": {
    "color": "khayyam.method.color",
    "fontStyle": "khayyam.method.fontStyle"
  }
}
```

And, beside the sentence that describes `variable` against `method-local` in `classification`:

> Two roles may share a kind and be told apart by what the syntax establishes about the declaration beyond where the name is valid: `variable` and `method-local` by validity, `method` and `provision` by whether the declaration carries a body. A consumer that can only read identity from the shape of one line answers what it can and leaves the rest; the residue is named here so that a consumer knows what it is not answering.

**Why this text and not a weaker or stronger one.** It makes one claim: a provision is a Method distinguishable from another Method by what its declaration carries. It deliberately reuses `method`'s colour and font, because a reader should not see the same name in two colours and infer two things — which is the reason the `method` role itself exists ([`display.json`](../../../khayyam/display/display.json)'s note on the owner listing a declaration and a call site as two items). It deliberately does not give the role its own colour, because nothing in the language says a provision is a different *kind* of thing. It cannot be `server-only`, and that is the point: it is only `grammar-scope` because I-A has said that an empty body and a body-less declaration are different texts, and a reader who takes this role before I-A has nothing to distinguish. A stronger role — one with its own colour — would assert a distinction the language has not drawn.

**What it would unblock.** [`modules/computer/host-interface/model.md:200`](../../../computer/host-interface/model.md)'s finding that "the vocabulary that would let them be told does not exist yet", and the three corpus comments at [`modules/gui/initialize.kh:8`](../../../gui/initialize.kh) and [`navigator.kh:21,23`](../../../gui/navigator.kh) being answerable in an editor rather than only in a compiler's refusal.

**What it would change elsewhere.** A follow-up edit to every realization of the display contract — the TextMate grammar and the language server each need to emit the role — and a bump of the contract's `version`, which is `3.0.0` and whose shape changes with a new role. Nothing in `docs/khayyam/` changes; the contract's own `scope` states that it "does not govern the language's syntax, semantics, or governance - it governs only the reading of what the language already states", so this role is downstream of I-A and I-B and never a source for them.

**Language, protocol, or toolchain.** The **toolchain**, and specifically the display contract, which is a consumer of the language rather than a statement about it. A role can only name what the language has stated, which is why this is last.

### Questions that need an owner, not a drafter
Twelve questions, each with its alternatives and what each forecloses. A drafter who chooses among these has made a decision wearing the costume of a sentence.

**Q1. Does an abstraction's signature admit a `sc`?** I-A… I-C drafts the "yes" branch, in two sentences, and the reasoning behind it. The alternative is to keep the rule as it stands and let the control-flow library's owner be a concrete capsule, which satisfies [`abstraction.md:101`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do) today and costs the replaceability [`control-flow.md:130`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#mechanism-summary) states an organization can have — because a capsule fixed in this repository is not a library an organization may mandate. Choosing the second does not foreclose the first; it forecloses a *shared, replaceable* control-flow contract, which is the thing [`control-flow.md:130`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md) is about.

**Q2. Does an abstraction's signature admit a concrete capsule as a produced instance?** I-D drafts only the consequence of answering "no", because the alternative — admitting it — requires rewriting [`abstraction.md:101`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do) and every sentence that leans on it, including [`qualified-names.md:24`](../../../khayyam/rules/qualified-names/qualified-names.md) and [`polymorphism.md:202`](../../../../docs/khayyam/polymorphism.md)'s `Factory` example. Answering "yes" forecloses the implementation-agnostic argument the rule is built on and makes every specification that names a width or a duration a specification of one realization; answering "no" forecloses a shared construction contract, so a construction library is thirty-three per-Type declarations and cannot be mandated by an organization.

**Q3. Which spelling declares a method body-less?** I-A drafts the ruling that a declaration ending at its third group is body-less and `{}` is an empty body. The alternative is that `{}` is the body-less form, which leaves a declaration with nothing in it unspellable and would make the fifty arithmetic conversions in [`modules/math/`](../../../math) and [`modules/time/duration/`](../../../time/duration) contract methods rather than provisions. Choosing the alternative forecloses nothing the language has; choosing the recommended one makes those fifty declarations a corpus problem those folders' owners must answer.

**Q4. Is a branch decision a fallible operation?** I-N gives the predicate and deliberately does not apply it. The alternatives are that it is fallible — which puts an influenced `err` on every conditional method, makes [`modules/process/control-flow/protocol/if.kh:6`](../../../process/control-flow/protocol/if.kh) right and the protocol's own two examples incomplete — or that it is not, which makes [`modules/computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh:3,6`](../../../computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh)'s commented `(err Error)` wrong as written. Answering "not" forecloses the possibility that evaluating a condition can itself fail, which forecloses the conditions under which it does: a condition read that raises, a scope that cannot be entered.

**Q5. Can obtaining an instance fail, and does the construction's error path follow?** I-N gives the predicate; the application is this question, and [`modules/process/construction/model.handoff.md:30`](../../../process/construction/model.handoff.md) says the corpus already answers it both ways — thirty-three `From*` declarations carry `(err Error)` while the one measured construction carries none. Answering "yes" makes those thirty-three right on the error and still defective on delivery; answering "no" leaves them right on the error and defective on delivery. Either way, if the answer is yes, [`docs/protocols/process/error.md`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/error.md#multi-cause-returns) must say which class a failed construction returns, since it currently distinguishes only the one-cause case from the many-cause case.

**Q6. Can a host call fail, and which `Error` does it return?** The same predicate, applied to a call that leaves the program. Its handoff says the answer should be given together with Q5, and the reason is structural: both are operations whose failure originates outside the thing being called, and separating them produces two rules that say the same thing in two places. Answering "no" forecloses any error path a host call could carry and makes an absent or refusing host an undefined program rather than a failed one — which is the same question as Q7.

**Q7. What does a program do about a facility its host does not offer?** I-J locates the rule in [`docs/protocols/computer/runtime.md`](../../../../docs/protocols/computer/runtime.md) and declines the answer. The alternatives are a build-time refusal, a run-time failure through an `Error`, and undefined behaviour. Each puts the rule in a different document and binds a different party: the first is the compiler's check, the second makes Q6's answer yes by construction, and the third means no document can promise anything about reaching a host facility.

**Q8. A halt's error class, and whether Recovery exists.** [`control-flow.md:152`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.md#topics-to-be-developed-here) registers the topic and does not hold it, and the prior question is prior to it: nothing says whether a halt carries an `Error` at all. The alternatives for the class are `Temporary`, `Internal`, and `Timeout` from [`error.md:57`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/error.md#optional-capability-interfaces), or none — and each conditions a different retry policy in the infrastructure that reads it. `Recovery` is a separate question with a separate cost: [`modules/computer/runtime/thread/protocol/thread.kh:31`](../../../computer/runtime/thread/protocol/thread.kh) carries it commented with the author's own "Why not just Errors??", and answering yes gives halting semantics a second path around [`error.md`'s](../../../../docs/protocols/process/error.md) visibility rule that the protocol would then have to govern.

**Q9. Where the success/failure pair lives and what it is called.** [`control-flow.handoff.md:16`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md) records the placement question as prior to the naming one, and three pairs are declared in three modules at once: [`listeners.kh:4,7`](../../../computer/adt/protocol/listeners.kh) declares `OnAbsent`/`OnPresent`, [`on-failure.kh:3`](../../../process/operation/protocol/on-failure.kh) declares `OnFailure`, and [`on-success.kh:3`](../../../process/operation/protocol/on-success.kh) declares `OnSuccess`. [`khayyam.md:62`](../../../../docs/khayyam/khayyam.md#import-mechanism-in) means one survives and the others move or are deleted, so this is a change to files three modules own. Choosing a shared abstraction rather than the `Error` abstraction costs an explicit delegation line per implementer, which [`control-flow.handoff.md:24`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md) already records against [`abstraction.md`'s](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#rejection-of-default-implementations) rejection of default implementations.

**Q10. Is `Conditional` a Flow Decision?** I-L's text is correct only if it is. The alternatives are that it enumerates branch outcomes and belongs under [`modules/process/`](../../../process/) — in which case `docs/protocols/math/math.md` gains the bullet and two files move — or that it is an unrelated logic value, in which case its own enumeration naming `Continue`, `Break`, and `Return` is a coincidence no document accounts for and someone has to explain it. The owner of [`modules/math/logic/`](../../../math/logic) answers, not a drafter.

**Q11. Which type owns the control-flow forms?** [`control-flow.handoff.md:9`](../../../../docs/protocols/process/control-flow.handoff.md) already puts three options to the owner, and the models have since attached a cost to two of them: `tp CF sc` is refused by [`modules/khayyam/rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.md:12`](../../../khayyam/rules/scope-placement/scope-placement.md), whose handoff says no document supplies the meaning a file-level `sc` would need; and an `ab` owner is constrained by [`abstraction.md:101`](../../../../docs/khayyam/abstraction.md#what-you-cannot-do) until Q1 is answered. This question does not become draftable text — it is a choice among where the forms live — but it is the one that gates the first declaration in [`modules/process/control-flow/`](../../../process/control-flow/), and it cannot be answered before Q1.

**Q12. Which of the two handoffs is wrong about the scope parameter's type?** I-M drafts the edit that stops this file asserting a resolution, but which document misstates the state of the model is the owner's: the toolchain's handoff says the model resolved it, the model's handoff says the question is open, and the language answers neither. Choosing that the model resolved it would require writing the answer into the language documents first, which is Q-adjacent to I-E; choosing that the toolchain misreported it is what I-M's text assumes.

## Implications
Three interventions touch documents other interventions depend on, in this order: I-A changes what a body-less declaration *is*, I-B changes whose name it carries, and I-E changes what a signature may name where a scope goes. Applying them out of that order produces text that is individually correct and collectively inconsistent — I-K's display role before I-A has nothing to distinguish, and I-C's `sc` allowance before I-E has no spelling to write.

Two of the fourteen interventions cannot be applied without touching code rather than documents. I-E's spelling change needs the frontend to accept a category keyword where it reads a name, and I-A's ruling moves fifty declarations in [`modules/math/`](../../../math) and [`modules/time/duration/`](../../../time/duration) from one kind to another. Both are real changes, and neither is a reason to defer the document edit — the documents state what the change is *to*, and the toolchain then follows.

One measurement in this document is not in any of the three models, and it changes their conclusions rather than adding to them: the corpus holds fifty-nine body-less methods on concrete capsules, and the three the host-interface model reports are not among them under the parser's own reading. Any session that has read [`modules/computer/host-interface/model.md:196`](../../../computer/host-interface/model.md) should read it again against I-A before acting on it.

What this document does not do is decide anything it lists under [Questions that need an owner](#questions-that-need-an-owner-not-a-drafter). It drafts the text each decision would make writable, names the alternative for each, and says what choosing it forecloses — which is as far as a document written by someone who does not own the language can go, and one step short of the answer in every case.
