## Findings
### 1. What the domain is for

**Statement (agent, Tentative).** The rule domain answers one question for the rest of the system: *which stated conditions govern this thing, who stated them, from when to when, and what did they cause?* It owns the **memory of the governing conditions themselves**: each rule's identity, the statement it carries (by reference), the owner who declared it, the interval in which it is in force, the subjects and points it is attached to, and the requests it made. It deliberately decides nothing about how a condition is evaluated (the engine, Memar's mechanism), what a particular subject's rule says (the subject's own module and organization), who holds authority over the subject (ownership, delegation, authorization), or what the consequence means to the business (a fee, a freeze, a label).

**What the documents already say (source).**
- Rule is a first-class node connected by an edge to the Code element it governs, so "which entities are subject to Rule X" is answerable by traversal; the interpreter is separate ([rule.md](rule.md#rule-as-a-first-class-graph-node)).
- A rule is an extension point, not the default processing path; it may act before, after, on a status change, on a request, or in reaction to another transaction; it may ask to be re-invoked, attach an additional transaction, or produce transactions of its own; it never modifies or rolls back the triggering transaction ([rule.md](rule.md#how-rules-react-mechanics-agreed-with-consumers)).
- Events need no new types: service request/response pairs are themselves events ([rule.md](rule.md#how-rules-react-mechanics-agreed-with-consumers); [Modularity → Event as a Module Capability](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)).
- A law is typically realized as Rule nodes; the Rule set is inside the law's payload digest, so a Rule added after voting is a new law ([rule.handoff.md](rule.handoff.md#from-organization-ideas-backlog), [computer_code](../../rights/law/edges/computer_code/computer_code.md#policies-rules)).
- Memar adds that a Rule's amendment and repeal are new facts that reference what they correct, and that an edge created while a Rule was in force is read against that Rule ([Modeling → Separating Structure from Policy](../../../docs/modeling.md#separating-structure-code-from-policy-rule)); that a Rule is introduced by an owner and another organization may define a different set for the same subject ([Memar Rule → Who Owns and Introduces Rules](../../process/rule/rule.md#who-owns-and-introduces-rules)); and that a Rule belongs to the module that owns its subject ([Which Module a Rule Belongs To](../../process/rule/rule.md#which-module-a-rule-belongs-to)).

**The finding that moves the domain (agent, Tentative; owner questions 1 to 4).** The documents say a great deal about how a rule reacts and almost nothing about what a rule node *is* while it waits, so five things a rule owner must be able to say are in no document:

- **What the one attachment carries.** Ten domains reach a rule through `condition_is`, and each says only "a rule attaches here"; the three existing `conditional` edge folders model three different relations ([survey D14](../../../.agents/tasks/20261014T071336Z-27684-0/report.md)). Nothing says at which point the rule fires, whether it may refuse, or who may attach it. The rule domain's own initiator edges say "like event dispatcher we need to let rule select when called e.g. before, async or after" and leave the field empty ([initiator-event](edges/initiator-event/initiator-event.md#fields)).
- **When a rule begins and ends.** No document states that a rule has a beginning, an end, a successor, or a suspension; the only trace is `time_limit` ("not valid after a certain of times") and the three unexplained status names. Memar's temporal-validity question is open and this domain "must not silently pick an answer" ([rule.md](rule.md#unresolved-questions-not-settled-by-this-module-carried-in-memar)).
- **What happens when two rules meet.** No precedence, no ordering, no double-fire guard; a rule reachable by many edges "can become a de-facto global event listener" and the scoping convention is a Future possibility.
- **Who may declare, attach and read.** The domain has no creator, no remover and no authority statement; "authorization" appears as an edge from a rule to a thing, which is a different question.
- **What a rule's own record is.** `made_request` and `checked` record that a rule acted; nothing says whether every evaluation is kept, only outcomes, or neither.

**The completable classification sentence** ([Modeling → Classification Emerges From Rules and Relations](../../../docs/modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels)) for the proposal: *a thing may be classified as a rule because it carries a statement that an engine or a person can evaluate and was declared by an owner at a time (an acquired fact), and therefore it may be attached to a subject at a point, take effect over an interval, make requests of its own, supersede and be superseded, and be asked what it governed and what it caused.* If a label cannot complete that sentence, it is not a rule relation (finding 5).

#### Scenarios
Twelve cases, from several cultures and industries. Each lists what the obvious model (a rule is an `if` with an action, attached to a thing, evaluated when the thing changes) gets wrong or must be extended to cover. "Obvious model" below is the model the README states in one paragraph; the cases that break it are marked.

**S1. A mobile-money transfer with a tiered fee (Kenya's M-Pesa, Iran's inter-bank transfer, India's UPI; any country).** The operator charges a tiered fee; the regulator later orders the fee on one channel to be zero. The fee is a rule producing a second transaction ([Decided](rule.handoff.md#9-fees-and-charges-are-rules-producing-transactions)); the operator chooses when it applies: on attempt, on success, on failure, after settlement.
- *What breaks:* the regulator's order and the operator's rule both govern the same transfer, and neither owns the other. The fee rule must know its point (success versus attempt) as data, not as code. Past transfers keep the fee they were charged.
- *Asks of the domain:* a point per attachment; a precedence statement by an authority; the interval in which each rule was in force.

**S2. A judicial freeze waiting for money that is not there yet (any court; an enforcement officer's levy).** One order freezes 1,000 units across all of a person's accounts, including accounts opened later, and stays until the amount has actually been blocked ([restriction](../../../modules-plugable/economics/restriction/restriction.md#policies-rules)).
- *What breaks:* the rule is long-lived and **stateful** (required, satisfied, remaining); it targets a person, not a transaction; it reacts to transactions that do not exist at the time it is made; it creates its own transactions; its release is a new fact.
- *Asks of the domain:* attachment to a subject that is a person or a label rather than the event source; firings recorded as the rule's own requests; a lifetime that ends on a condition, not a date; a rule that is the declared act of an authority, not of the organization running the system.

**S3. A tax rate that changes in the middle of the year (Germany's temporary VAT cut in the second half of 2020, a 19 % to 16 % change; an Iranian budget-law change; any country).** An invoice is issued in June for goods delivered in August and paid in September.
- *What breaks:* which rule governs depends on a date that is neither the time the rule was *recorded* (it may be recorded after it takes effect) nor the time the invoice was *created*; a later correction applies the new rate retroactively to some acts and not to others.
- *Asks of the domain:* two times on a rule's validity (when it takes effect, when it was recorded), as in [account.research.001, smaller choice 2](../../economics/account/account.research.001.md); the ability to ask "what governed this act on date D"; amendment as a new fact referencing the old.

**S4. A government price cap against a merchant's promotion (a registered consumer price in Iran, face-mask price caps in many countries in 2020).** The merchant's promotion rule would take the price above the regulator's cap (a bundle surcharge, a "special price" over the registered one); the regulator's rule must win, and the merchant's must remain on record, not be deleted.
- *What breaks:* "most specific wins" is the wrong default here: the cap is general and must win. The law research reached the same finding about defeasible norms and recommends a stated precedence, not a silent specificity rule ([law research 002](../../rights/law/law.research.002.md)).
- *Asks of the domain:* a precedence relation stated by someone with authority over both, and a record of which rule lost.

**S5. A scheduled payment on the 25th (Japanese payroll moved to the previous business day when the 25th is a holiday; a week that starts on Saturday with Friday off; the Nowruz closure).** A rule fires monthly. A server is down on the 25th.
- *What breaks:* "every month" is a calendar reading, not a time; the rule's trigger is a **schedule**, not an event; after an outage the question is whether the missed run is made up (once, not twice) or skipped; two runs must never pay the same salary.
- *Asks of the domain:* a schedule as a trigger kind owned by a capability, with the due time recorded apart from the time it fired; exactly-once per period; the calendar from the calendar domain ([repeatable](edges-loop/repeatable/repeatable.md) already says "time information indicate in calendar domain").

**S6. A voucher the customer types in (a shop's discount code; a cinema coupon in Tehran; a loyalty reward in Seoul).** The rule is the merchant's. It fires only when the customer calls the service that applies it. It may be used three times, and has been used twice.
- *What breaks:* the trigger is a **person's act**, the rule's declarer and the invoker are different parties, and "partly used" is a count of firings against a limit, not a state anybody sets. Code sharing and theft are real.
- *Asks of the domain:* a manual-call trigger; usage as a value derived from firings, not stored status (`Status_PartlyUsed`, `Status_FullyUsed`); the invoker's authority checked by the rule, not by the node.

**S7. An irrigation rota with no engine (the qanat water shares of Yazd and Gonabad in Iran; the Valencian water tribunal in Spain; both run on turn-taking written down by the community).** Each farm receives the flow for a set time on a set day; a breach is judged by a respected person.
- *What breaks the obvious model:* the rule has **no machine form**. Its executor is a person; its consequence is a task ("open your gate at dawn"), not a transaction; its check is a human observation. A rule domain that assumes an engine has nothing to say about it, yet it is a rule in every sense Memar defines (a stated condition an owner introduced for a subject they govern).
- *Asks of the domain:* statement forms other than code (a document, a diagram); consequences that are tasks; evaluation by people recorded as the same firings; the possibility that the evaluator is a person or an organization.

**S8. A rule about who may make rules (a co-operative's by-laws: the board may adopt a fee rule up to 2 % without a member vote; anything above needs the general assembly; the assembly's powers come from the founding charter).** H. L. A. Hart's secondary rules in a small case.
- *What breaks the obvious model:* the subject of a rule is another rule (or the rule domain's own attachment); the authority for declaring a rule is itself a rule; the chain ends at a founding act the rule domain cannot supply ([normative-bootstrap](../../rights/normative-bootstrap.handoff.md) holds that question for law). A cycle guard is needed so that a rule about rules cannot govern itself into a loop.
- *Asks of the domain:* attachment to a rule node as a subject like any other; the declarer's authority as an ordinary condition; a stated stop.

**S9. A message-spam labeler that learns (a messaging platform in any language).** A model proposes a rule "messages with this pattern are spam"; the platform adopts it; it labels but never refuses; it is sometimes wrong; users appeal.
- *What breaks:* the declarer is a **software agent**, not a person (the status name `Status_AutoGenerated` is the only trace of this in the domain); the rule is advisory (a label, no veto); its errors need a correction, and the correction is a new fact; the accountable party is the organization that adopted it ([synthesis: acting software agents are delegates, accountability stays with the principal](../../../.agents/tasks/20261014T071207Z-28148-0/concept-synthesis.md)).
- *Asks of the domain:* a declarer that may be software with a named accountable principal; a consequence kind that is a label with no effect on the flow; an appeal as a correction of the firing, not an edit.

**S10. A law enacted as rules, read ten years later (a statute whose machine form is signed with it).** A court in 2031 asks what governed an act in 2027, after the statute was replaced.
- *What breaks:* a rule changed in place would destroy the answer. The law's freeze says a changed rule is a new law; the rule domain must therefore hold superseded rules intact and answer "what was in force on date D" ([rule.handoff.md](rule.handoff.md#from-organization-ideas-backlog), [law computer_code](../../rights/law/edges/computer_code/computer_code.md#policies-rules)).
- *Asks of the domain:* succession as a relation (the thing domain's `supersedes` proposal), never an update; the set of rules a law names as an addressable unit, or a convention for the set.

**S11. A rule that may only run on certain machines (health data that must be evaluated on in-country servers under a data-localization law; an edge-server rule for a hospital camera).** The rule's statement may be correct and still illegal to run in the wrong place ([execution-limit_to_label](edges/execution-limit_to_label/execution-limit_to_label.md#policies-rules)).
- *What breaks:* where a rule may execute is an **acquired fact stated by a legal or operational owner**, not a property of the engine; and it limits *which engine instance*, not which subject.
- *Asks of the domain:* an execution scope as its own relation; it is the declared constraint, and the engine only honours it.

**S12. A rule that must refuse (an age-restricted sale; Japan's majority age moving from 20 to 18 in 2022; a prescription medicine).** The check must run before the sale is processed and a missing engine must not let an under-age sale through; a pharmacy in an emergency may need the opposite.
- *What breaks:* "never modifies or rolls back the triggering transaction" is true of effects but a refusal *before* processing is not a modification; the existing `synchronous` loop-edge says a rule "can change the flow of the service by returning an error", which seems to contradict the Decided sentence (contradiction 2). Behaviour when the engine is unavailable is stated nowhere.
- *Asks of the domain:* a point/mode that separates observe, accompany and refuse; an engine-unavailable policy stated per attachment (fail-closed or fail-open) by the owner.

**What the twelve cases show together (agent, Tentative).** The rule domain is not mainly about the evaluation of conditions. It is about **time and authority around conditions**: when a statement began to govern, who stated it, over whom, at which point, against which competing statement, and what it left behind. Every case that breaks the obvious model (S2, S3, S7, S8, S9, S10) breaks it on one of those five and none on the expression language.

**What the domain is not for (agent, with sources).**
- It is not the rule engine. The engine is a mechanism, justified by a modeled responsibility, not by the word Rule ([Event, Rule, and Mechanism-First Design](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-rule-and-mechanism-first-design)); Memar keeps its evaluation abstractions under `modules/process/rules-engine` and `modules/process/rule`.
- It is not the home of every organization's rules. A Rule belongs to the module that owns its subject ([Memar Rule](../../process/rule/rule.md#which-module-a-rule-belongs-to)); the discount facets, the VAT value rule, the restriction constraint and the cycle guard of a task each live with their subject. This domain holds the node, the attachment and the memory, not the policies.
- It is not the law. A law is the signed instrument; the rule is how it executes ([rule.md](rule.md#rationale-and-alternatives)).
- It is not a kind taxonomy. "Access control", "checker", "manual" and "repeatable" are words on a rule, not kinds of it (finding 5).

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

**Tests applied (source).** [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement): kernel only if removal forces a foundational type to be fabricated or distorted; pluggable if an optional capability attaches through an explicit relationship and owns edge plus processing together; an edge becomes its own module only with independent identity, lifecycle, authorization, rules, or processing; no bare edges (who may create or remove it, and what it triggers); the kernel never references the rule domain directly; analytics is never kernel; behaviour binds to a module's UUID, never a name.

#### The removal test run on the rule itself (agent, Tentative)

Remove the rule domain. What is forced?
- Every consumer's `condition_is` edge must end somewhere. It can end at a `thing`, and an engine could read any thing's statement. No new foundational type is fabricated.
- But "this thing carries a statement an owner declared, attached to subjects at a point, in force over an interval, superseded by another, and the requests it made are traceable" is then a fact with no home. Either every thing carries it (a distortion of `thing`), or it is a marker on some things with rules of its own. That marker, with those rules, is the rule.

The rule therefore earns its place by four rules no other domain owns: **a statement is evaluated only while in force and only on attachments** (the interval and the attachment are the rule's); **what governed at any past moment stays answerable** (superseded rules are kept, not edited — the Memar sentence above); **a rule never edits its trigger and every consequence is a new fact traceable to it** (Decided); **the attachment is one relation with one set of fields, defined once** ([Placing a relation](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation)). The independent-lifecycle test ([Concept Existence vs. Model Existence](../../../docs/modeling.md#concept-existence-vs-model-existence)) is met by in-force, suspended and superseded having rules of their own (S3, S10). That is owner question 1, and it decides between models C and M3 below.

#### Proposed kernel (agent, Tentative)

| Kernel element | Why kernel | Note |
|---|---|---|

No second identifier type |
| **The statement, by reference** | without a statement the node is a label; the statement may be code (`code_is`), a diagram or action list (`uml_is`), or a human document (S7) | the kernel holds a reference to an object or content and says nothing about its language ([representation stays open in Memar](rule.md#unresolved-questions-not-settled-by-this-module-carried-in-memar)); the statement is never edited, a change is a successor |

**Fields of the attachment (agent, Tentative).** The attachment is an edge that carries four facts and no more: the **point** (a named occurrence of the subject's type that its event target declares: before processing, after processing, on a status change, on a given request kind, on another transaction); the **mode** (observe: no effect on the flow; accompany: may make requests after the point; refuse: may stop the subject at a before-point, a refusal being a response of the subject's own service, not an edit of it); the **declarer** and **time** of the attachment, as the events of its creation and removal; an optional **limit** of use (a count or an amount, S2, S6). The point is a property of the attachment, not of the rule, because the same rule may be attached to two subjects at different points. "Who attaches" and "what removal does" are in the table below. Everything the subject's domain needs to say about points is an event target on that domain, never a rule reference in its kernel.

### 3. Rival models

Seven rival models of the rule domain, with the status of each, then two smaller choices.

#### The models

- **A. Status quo: a rule is a node with many direct edges in both directions, no lifecycle, a per-consumer `conditional` edge (Explored; the current state).** Cheap, and enough for a first consumer. *Against:* three different relations share the name `conditional`; edges to and from the rule disagree about direction; a rule has no stated beginning, end or successor; the point is nowhere; the invariants the domain depends on (kept history, no edit of the trigger, one attachment) are enforced by nobody in particular; the three status names mix lifecycle, provenance and consumption.
- **M3. Node with a statement, an interval, one attachment and a firing trail (Tentative; recommended).** The kernel of finding 2. Keeps every Decided statement true. It adds no consumer edge: the attachment is defined once here, consumers expose only event points. *Migration cost:* low by construction; an existing `condition_is` edge is read as an attachment with the point "unspecified, per the consumer's default" until the owner states it.
- **B. The attachment is a record with its own identity (Explored; the strongest rival).** The way group membership became a record in [group](../../management/group/group.research.001.md): `(subject)-[:condition_is]->(attachment)-[:of]->(rule)`, with the attachment carrying point, mode, declarer, interval and status. *For:* the declarer and interval of an attachment differ from the rule's (a platform declares a standard fee rule and each merchant attaches it, S1); an exemption becomes a suspension of one attachment; usage limits per subject (S6) fit naturally. *Against:* one more node for every attachment, which for class-level rules (S2, "all future accounts") and for the many-subjects case is large; an exemption is also expressible as an overriding rule (the precedence capability). M3 with the attachment's creation and removal as events carries the declarer and time; B becomes the better model if an attachment must have a **status and limit of its own** independent of the rule (question 2 and 10 decide).
- **C. A rule is a classification on a content or object that carries a statement; no rule domain (Explored).** Following the [Premature Classification Check](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#premature-classification-check): "is X a rule?" is answered by whether an attachment points at it. The attachment would be a module of its own, validity a general status. *For:* fewest foundations. *Against:* it forgets that the graph lens owes the Rule an identity of its own, apart from the document it came from; and the three rules no other domain owns (finding 2) would have no home. Becomes the better model if validity, precedence and firing trail are not the rule's own (question 1).
- **D. Event-condition-action as three nodes (Explored; rejected by a Decided sentence).** The classic production-rule triple: event, condition, action, each reusable. *Against:* [rule.md](rule.md#how-rules-react-mechanics-agreed-with-consumers) and the handoff decide that events need no new types and Memar's [Event as a Module Capability](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability) says the same; condition and action are one statement with one lifecycle. Kept for the one useful idea: **point** as data on the attachment.
- **E. Decision-table or fact-based rules as the representation (Deferred).** Production-rule and business-rule literature (RETE engines, SBVR vocabularies) are named prior art in [rule.md](rule.md#prior-art); Memar records its vocabulary findings as read from sources of no standing ([Memar Rule handoff](../../process/rule/rule.handoff.md)). The representation question stays open in Memar; the kernel holds a reference to a statement and does not choose.
- **F. The rule is a Module and its identity is the module identity (Explored; the module lens).** Memar says the module lens owes a Rule a stated boundary and behaviour binds to a module's UUID ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement)). *For:* one identity under both lenses. *Against:* a rule that is only a stated condition is not always developed as a module (S7's irrigation rota); a thing may carry several kinds under one identity ([thing research, Q2](../../identity/thing/thing.research.001.md)). Compatible with M3 if "is a module" is a classification of the same `ThingUUID`.

**Not re-argued (Decided or confirmed, source):** the Rule is a node and the engine is separate; core never knows Rule; rules react at several points; a rule never modifies or rolls back its trigger; a fee is a rule that produces a transaction; events need no new types; a law is realized as Rule nodes inside the payload digest; the identifier is a `ThingUUID` and no `RuleUUID` abstraction exists.

"Fits" means no distortion; "strained" means it works only with a workaround; "fails" means it forces a wrong statement.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 fee and regulator | strained (no point, no precedence) | fits with precedence | fits | strained | strained | fits |
| S2 freeze, stateful | strained | fits (rule state is its firings) | fits | strained | fails (state of the whole) | fits |
| S3 mid-year rate change | fails (no interval) | fits | fits | strained | strained | fits |
| S4 cap versus promotion | fails (no precedence) | fits with precedence | fits | fails | fails | fits |
| S5 scheduled payment | strained | fits with schedule | fits | strained | fits | fits |

| S7 irrigation rota | strained (assumes engine) | fits (statement form open) | fits | fits | fails | fails |
| S8 rule about rules | strained | fits | fits | strained | strained | fits |

#### Smaller choice 1: what is the Code / Rule boundary (Tentative: the ontology/governance test)

Memar leaves "the precise Code/Rule boundary test" open in this domain ([rule.md](rule.md#unresolved-questions-not-settled-by-this-module-carried-in-memar)); the sibling kernels all lean on it (task's cycle guard, account's candidate kernel rules, product's kernel rules). Memar's own Rule document states a test for the *tier* of a rule: **if turning the condition off would admit something the owning specification says cannot exist, it is ontology wearing a governance label** ([Memar Rule → What a Rule Is](../../process/rule/rule.md#what-a-rule-is)). Applied here as an agent proposal, not as Memar's answer:
- **(i) the ontology test (recommended).** A condition stays Code, enforced by the owning domain's validator, if no organization could legitimately run the domain with it off (a task graph without cycles, one count unit per account, a unique identifier). A condition becomes a Rule node if organizations legitimately differ (who may join, what a discount is, when a fee applies).
- **(ii) everything conditional is a Rule.** Maximal flexibility, and the sprawl the README warns about ([Drawbacks](rule.md#drawbacks)): a cycle guard becomes removable.
- **(iii) nothing is a Rule that the kernel enforces.** Leaves organizations no way to tighten.
(i) puts the sibling researches' cycle guards in the owning domain's validator, which differs from their current wording (contradiction 5).

#### Smaller choice 2: which time orders validity (Tentative: both)

A rule has an effective time (when it is meant to govern) and a recorded time (when it was declared). A rate change recorded in August, effective from 1 July (S3), makes both necessary. Keeping only the recorded time makes retroactivity unsayable; keeping only the effective time lets a past answer change silently. Recommended: both, as in the account research's ledger choice; "what governed on date D" is discovered data over both. The alternative of a single `time_limit` edge cannot express a successor.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions

Written as questions favouring no answer where unsettled. A citation means the document already answers or constrains the question; "open" means nothing in the tree decides it.

#### Identity
- What makes two rules the same: the `ThingUUID` alone, or the statement? Is a rule identified by its statement's content (as text values are) or by its declaration? Open.
- Does a rule keep its identity when its statement changes, or is every change a new rule that supersedes the old? The law freeze says a change is a new law; Memar says an amendment is a new fact referencing what it corrects. Open (owner question 4).
- Does a rule survive a change of owner, of organization (a merger), of the engine that evaluates it? Open.
- May one rule be shared by several organizations (a published standard) and does the same UUID then mean the same statement everywhere? [Modules](../../modules.md#placement) says behaviour binds to a UUID so that two organizations may ship the same term with different rules; the converse is not stated. Open (owner question 17).
- Is a rule's identity the same as its module identity when it is developed as a module? Open (owner question 12).
- Is a **set** of rules (a law's machine form) a thing with an identity, or only the edges? Open (owner question 17).
- May an auto-generated rule be identified apart from its generator? Open.

#### Lifecycle
- What states does a rule have: declared, tested, in force, suspended, superseded, repealed, expired; which are one-way? Open (owner question 4; waits on [survey R4](../../../.agents/tasks/20261014T071404Z-26308-0/report.md)).
- May a rule take effect before it is recorded (retroactive), and may a rule's effect on already-booked acts change? Open (owner question 4).
- What happens to firings made under a rule that is repealed? Memar: they are read against that rule and stay ([Separating Structure from Policy](../../../docs/modeling.md#separating-structure-code-from-policy-rule)). Cited, not stated by this domain.
- What happens to a stateful rule that is repealed while unsatisfied (S2's freeze)? Open.
- What does the rule do when its subject is retired or tombstoned? Open.
- May rule text that names a person be erased on request, with the fact that the rule governed kept? Open; the same shape as [account question 14](../../economics/account/account.research.001.md).
- May a rule be created in draft and tried (rule-test) before it governs anything? Open.

#### Authorization
- Who may declare a rule: its future owner, an organization on its behalf, an authority? May software declare one (S9) and under which principal? Open (owner question 9).
- Who may attach a rule to a subject: the rule's owner, the subject's owner, or both? The proposal says authority over the subject; open.
- Who may suspend, supersede or repeal a rule, and who may override one? Open.
- Who may read a rule's statement and who may read only that it governs a subject? A fraud threshold known to the person it governs is defeated; a subject that cannot learn which rules govern it cannot contest them. Open (owner question 9).
- Who may create a rule about rules (S8), and what stops the regress? The founding act is outside this domain ([normative-bootstrap](../../rights/normative-bootstrap.handoff.md)). Open.
- Across organizations: may one organization's rule attach to another's subject, and under whose authority? Open.

#### Rules (about rules)
- Which conditions does the domain itself require, and which are organization policy? Candidate domain-level conditions (own proposal): a rule has a statement before it can be in force; a rule no input can satisfy is not enabled (the law's `computer_code` policy states this for laws); a rule never edits its trigger (Decided); one firing per (rule, triggering fact); a rule's consequence does not re-enter the same rule; an attachment names a point the subject's event target declares. Open.
- What is the cap on the cascade: how many rules may follow one fact, how deep may consequences trigger consequences? Open (owner question 8).
- What is the order of several rules at one point: stated precedence, then declared time? Open (owner question 7).
- Where does the Code/Rule boundary lie? Open (owner question 5).
- May a rule attach to a type, a label or an edge kind rather than an instance (the class-level binding)? [Modeling](../../../docs/modeling.md#separating-structure-code-from-policy-rule) says a Rule attaches to "node type, edge type, or specific instance". Open (owner question 10).

#### Events
- Which facts must the domain emit: declared, in force, attached, detached, suspended, superseded, fired (with outcome), refused? The kernel exposes an event target, not a new event model ([Event as a Module Capability](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)).
- Who listens: the log, a follower, analytics, the rule's owner. Is each reaction the listener's own rule, never a cascade the rule domain performs? Open.
- Ordering and exactly-once: is a firing emitted once per (rule, trigger) and in what order against the trigger's own events? Open (owner question 8).
- When the engine is unavailable at a before-point, does the subject proceed (fail-open) or stop (fail-closed)? Open (owner question 3).
- Are non-firing evaluations recorded here or in the log-listener? Open (owner question 13).

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each as a sketch, with who creates and removes it and what it triggers. None is created by this research.

**6.1 `condition_is` — the defining document (Tentative).** The folder `edges/condition_is` in this domain: `(subject)-[:condition_is]->(rule)` with the reverse traversal `is_condition_for`, and fields point, mode, limit. Created by someone with authority over the subject; removed by the same, an event, and past firings stay. Triggers `rule_attached` and the engine's subscription at the stated point. It replaces the definitions in transaction, invoice and the other consumers; they keep only an event target naming their points. This is what [Placing a relation](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation) requires for a relation ten domains need, and it is what the survey asked for ("a generic `condition_is` home doc").

**6.2 `precedence` (Explored; mirrors the law research's `law-priority`).** `(rule)-[:overrides]->(rule)` stated by someone with authority over both; the engine orders by it, records the loser, and defaults to "no silent specificity" (S4). Created by the authority; removed as a new event. Triggers the ordering and a "rule overridden" record. Open: whether a rule set carries its own internal order; whether an exception is an overriding rule scoped narrowly.

**6.3 `rule-test` (Explored).** Given/when/then scenarios attached to a rule and run by the engine without effect. A rule is enabled only when they hold; for a rule a law names, the "no input can satisfy it" check is mandatory. Created by the rule's owner; removed with the rule's supersession. Triggers a pass/fail event that gates `rule_in_force`. Echoes this domain's own test scenarios (finding 7) as data.

**6.4 `rule-set` (Explored; check against the law's `computer_code`).** A closed, named set of rules whose digest is stable and which is in force as a unit; adding a member makes a new set. The law's freeze already requires this unit; whether it needs an identity apart from the edges is question 17. Created by the declarer; never edited; removed by supersession.

**6.5 `adoption` (Explored; uses thing's `follow`).** An organization adopts a rule an authority published (a tax rate, a standard) and is told of its successors; the adopter's attachment then points at the published rule. Created by the adopter; the publisher declares successors. Triggers an event that the adopter's own rule can answer (accept the successor, hold). This is the cross-organization case (S3) without copying the rule.

**6.6 `usage` (Tentative; derived).** The count or amount of firings of a rule against an attachment's limit, read as partly used, fully used. Discovered data over the `made_request` trail; never stored status. An exhausted attachment is suspended by an event of the engine, not edited.

**6.7 `trigger-binding`, `schedule`, `execution-scope`, `checking` (Tentative; reclassification, not new).** The existing folders (finding 5) become pluggables that state, each, who creates and removes them and what they trigger; this is the work of the existing survey task T4 ("rule edge docs").

**6.8 `consequence` kinds (Explored; belongs to each subject's pluggables).** A rule's consequence is a refusal, a new request, a label, a task or a notification. A transaction-producing consequence is the economics pluggables' (fee, restriction, release); a task-producing one is the task domain's (S7: the rota's task); a label is the label domain's. The rule domain records only that the rule made the request. Naming the consequence kinds here would make the rule domain know every consumer.

### 7. Test scenarios

Given/when/then, each a check on the model above. Not run; the model has no code.

1. **Attachment is one relation.** Given a transaction and an invoice that each have a `condition_is` edge. When the edge definition is read. Then there is one definition, in the rule domain, with point, mode and limit, and neither consumer defines it.
2. **A point is required.** Given an attachment without a point. When the engine reads it. Then the attachment is read at the consumer's default point and flagged "point unspecified", never guessed.
3. **Refusal before processing.** Given a rule attached to a sale at a before-point in refuse mode. When an under-age sale is requested. Then the sale's service responds with a refusal naming the rule, the sale is not recorded, and nothing was edited (S12).
4. **After-point cannot refuse.** Given a rule attached at an after-point in accompany mode. When it evaluates false. Then it may make a request of its own and cannot undo the triggering transaction (Decided).
5. **Engine unavailable.** Given a refuse-mode attachment declared fail-closed. When the engine is down at the point. Then the subject's request is refused with the reason "engine unavailable"; a fail-open attachment lets it through and records that it did.
6. **Fee as a second transaction.** Given a fee rule attached at the success point. When the transfer succeeds. Then the fee is a new transaction made by the rule (`made_request`), and a failed transfer makes none.
7. **Regulator wins.** Given a merchant's discount and a government price cap, with `overrides` stated by the regulator. When both govern one sale. Then the cap applies, and a record shows which rule lost (S4).
8. **No silent specificity.** Given two rules on one point with no stated precedence. When they conflict. Then the engine reports a conflict and applies the owner's default order (declared time), and the report names both.
9. **Stateful freeze.** Given a judicial rule requiring 1,000 units blocked, 400 available. When 700 more arrive next month. Then the rule blocks 600, is satisfied, and ends; each block is a transaction it made.
10. **Mid-year rate change.** Given a rate rule superseded effective 1 July and recorded on 3 August. When an invoice created in June and supplied in August is evaluated on 15 August. Then the answer is read against the effective time, and the record shows the rule was recorded after it took effect.
11. **What governed on date D.** Given a repealed rule and a firing from 2027. When asked in 2031 what governed the act. Then the superseded rule is returned unchanged, with its successor.
12. **Amendment is a new node.** Given a rule in force. When its statement is changed. Then a new rule is declared that supersedes it; the old statement is not edited.
13. **Retroactive rule.** Given a rule with an effective time before its recorded time. When it is declared. Then it governs acts from the effective time forward only where its owner states retroactivity, and past firings are not rewritten, a correction request is made.
14. **Scheduled run after an outage.** Given a monthly rule due on the 25th and an outage on that day. When the engine returns on the 26th. Then the run happens once, the record shows due 25th and fired 26th, and a repeat of the 25th is refused (S5).
15. **Voucher limit.** Given a manual-call rule limited to three uses and used twice. When it is used a third and a fourth time. Then the third fires, the usage reads fully used, the attachment is suspended by an event, and the fourth is refused.
16. **Software declarer.** Given a rule declared by a learning component. When the declaration is recorded. Then the declarer is the software with a named accountable organization, and a human decision is needed before it refuses anything (S9).
17. **Appeal is a correction.** Given a labeler's wrong label. When the owner appeals. Then a correction fact references the firing; the firing is not edited.
18. **Rule about rules.** Given a by-law rule allowing fee rules up to 2 %. When the board declares a 3 % fee rule. Then the declaration is refused by the by-law rule, which is a rule attached to the rule domain's own declaration point (S8).
19. **No self-loop.** Given a rule whose consequence triggers the same rule. When it fires. Then the consequence does not re-enter that rule, and the attempt is reported.
20. **Execution scope.** Given a rule scoped to in-country nodes. When an engine on another node is asked to run it. Then the run is refused and the refusal is recorded (S11).
21. **Human-evaluated rule.** Given an irrigation rule whose statement is a document and whose consequence is a task. When dawn arrives on a turn. Then a task is created for the farm and its completion is recorded as the firing (S7).
22. **Law set freeze.** Given a law naming a rule set. When a rule is added to the set after the vote. Then it is a new set and so a new law; the old set remains the one in force until the successor is.
23. **Repealed stateful rule.** Given a freeze rule 40 % satisfied. When it is repealed. Then it makes no further block, existing blocks stay until released by a new transaction, and the repeal is an event with a declarer.
24. **A rule cannot be enabled without a statement.** Given a rule with no statement. When `rule_in_force` is requested. Then it is refused.

### Contradictions found

Reported, not resolved, as asked. Each cites both sides.

1. **Direction of the governing relation.** The Memar sentence is that a Rule is connected "by an edge to the Code element it governs" and rule.md repeats it; the three consumers record `(subject)-[:condition_is]->(rule)` ([transaction](../../economics/transaction/edges/conditional/conditional.md), [invoice](../../economics/invoice/edges/conditional/conditional.md)); but this domain's own edges run the other way: `(rule)-[:authorization_for]->(thing)`, `(rule)-[:execution__limit_to_node]->(node)`, `(rule)-[:initiator_by_event]->(event)`, `(rule)-[:restricted]->(user)` in restriction, `(rule)-[:discount]->(---)` in discount. Both directions exist for one idea ("this rule governs that"). The reverse traversal is cheap; one stored direction is the question (owner question 2).
2. **`synchronous` against "never modifies or rolls back".** [synchronous](edges-loop/synchronous/synchronous.md#policies-rules): a synchronous rule "will part of process and can change the flow of the desire service (process) e.g. by return an error". [rule.md](rule.md#how-rules-react-mechanics-agreed-with-consumers): a rule "never modifies or rolls back the triggering transaction". They coexist only if a refusal before processing is not a modification; no document says so. The proposal's modes (observe, accompany, refuse) make it explicit (owner question 3).
3. **Three `conditional` folders, three relations, four "rule allowed" statements.** [transaction conditional](../../economics/transaction/edges/conditional/conditional.md) and [invoice conditional](../../economics/invoice/edges/conditional/conditional.md) are `condition_is`; [request conditional](../request/edges/conditional/conditional.md) is `authorized_by`, a different relation; this domain's [authorization](edges/authorization/authorization.md) is `authorization_by` and `authorization_for`; [management/authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.handoff.md) holds `group_rule_authorization`. Five statements that a rule has something to do with permission. The survey counts the three `conditional` folders (D14) and recommends the merge (Q3, B4); the fourth and fifth are not counted there.
4. **`uml.md` names a notation and describes actions.** Its title and first paragraph define UML; its Policies describe "the service called in the process of rule, or action service", with `NotifyUser` and `UserEarnCoin` as examples. Two concepts under one name: the diagram form of the statement and the consequence.
5. **Sibling kernels count the attachment as their own element and also name invariants that are policy.** Product, task, content, group, account, transaction and invoice each list the generic `condition_is` as a kernel element (reconciliation). [Modules](../../modules.md#placement) says the kernel does not reference the rule domain; a kernel element that **is** the attachment to the rule domain does. Task puts the cycle guard of `part_of` in a rule ([task research](../../management/task/task.research.001.md): a cycle is refused by a rule attached through `condition_is`) and group the same ([group research](../../management/group/group.research.001.md)), while the account research lists "one count unit" and "gapless index" as candidate kernel rules. Which of these is Code and which Rule is the open Code/Rule boundary.
6. **The kernel documents name the rule domain.** [task.md](../../management/task/task.md) has a table row linking `computer/rule`; [transaction conditional](../../economics/transaction/edges/conditional/conditional.md) names `contract`, a pluggable; [invoice conditional](../../economics/invoice/edges/conditional/conditional.md) names discount, VAT and restriction; [request conditional](../request/edges/conditional/conditional.md) names `rule_p`. The same finding as the account, invoice and product researches (their contradictions 8, 12 and 5): the references explain why a thing is not in the kernel and should point from the pluggable to the kernel only.
7. **Two homes for rules: Memar's by-subject home and this domain's storage.** [Memar Rule → Which Module a Rule Belongs To](../../process/rule/rule.md#which-module-a-rule-belongs-to) says a Rule belongs to the module that owns its subject, with executable rules in that module's `rules/` folder; this domain's idea backlog asks for a "rule storage" service that gets rules by field and label, and says the module "stores Rule nodes only" ([rule.md](rule.md#rationale-and-alternatives)). Not necessarily a conflict (node and attachment here, statement and normative claim with the subject), but nobody has said so; it is owner question 6.
8. **Overlap with Memar's `modules/process/rule` and `rules-engine`.** The computer survey records that Memar's process/rule overlaps the engine side of this domain. This domain says the engine is another component; Memar's document says the engine abstractions live under process; neither says whether this domain's README is the graph lens only.
9. **Status names without a classification.** `Status_AutoGenerated`, `Status_PartlyUsed`, `Status_FullyUsed` are provenance and consumption, not lifecycle; the lifecycle (declared, in force, superseded) has no vocabulary at all. The conversion handoff asked the question; the survey (B6) recommends the split.
10. **Withdrawn (reviewer 2026-10-19): not a contradiction.** [rule.handoff.md → Status](rule.handoff.md#status) says the mechanics are "already partly in `README.md`"; `README.md` is a git symlink to rule.md (commit 39542476), so it shows the same text. The earlier reading of it as a one-word file was a misreading of the symlink.
11. **Edge folders with no Placement or Open questions.** All seventeen edge documents lack the sections [Modules → Edge folder](../../modules.md#edge-folder) requires. `execution-limit_to_label` spells its Model edge with a double underscore (`execution__limit_to_label`). Housekeeping, already survey task T4.

They were read by structure and by search for the rule mentions (Methodology); the passages relied on are cited.

**What each assumes of the rule domain.**

**Agreements.**
1. **The same attachment, never the rule's name in the kernel.** All ten agree that the consumer names no rule and the rule domain decides semantics. This research agrees and adds that the attachment's **definition** should also live once, here (owner question 2). They differ on whether to count it as a kernel element of the consumer (they do) or as the rule domain's (this research proposes): the consumer keeps an event target naming its points, which is a smaller commitment than owning an edge.
2. **"Where does the rule attach: the subject, the relation, the proposal or the module?" is asked by four researches (task, content, group, product) and answered by none.** Proposed answer: **on the subject instance or on a class of subjects** (a label or a type), at a **point** the subject's event target declares; never on the relation as an edge (an edge is not a thing with events here), and never on the module that owns a capability (the module owns the *consequence*, the subject owns the *point*). Tentative.
3. **A correction is a new fact.** Task (supersession), content (immutable revisions), text (immutable values), account (a reversal is a new record) and thing (tombstone, `supersedes`) all say it; Memar says it of a Rule's amendment. Rule amendment as a successor is the same move (owner question 4).
4. **Authority over the subject, not over the rule, decides who attaches.** Thing's authority statement (question 8) and group's "authority over the group" borrowed from ownership and delegation say the same.
5. **Status as events on the shared vocabulary, waiting on ruling R4.** Product, task, content, account and now the rule's validity.
6. **A software declarer is a delegate.** The synthesis rule about acting software agents is what S9 needs; the user research's "verification pluggable" and the account research's "instruction authority" lean on delegation too.
7. **Fee-like consequences are new requests.** Account (restriction, credit), transaction (conditional-fulfilment) and invoice (payment-schedule) all create a transaction per event; the rule never edits the trigger.

**Contradictions and tensions across the researches.**
1. **Guards as Rules versus guards as Code.** Task and group put the cycle guard in a rule attached through `condition_is`; account lists "one count unit" and "gapless index" among *candidate kernel rules*; product the same. The Code/Rule boundary (smaller choice 1) would send acyclicity and the single count unit to the owning domain's validator and leave "who may join" to a rule. The researches speak of both as "rules"; the word is carrying two jobs (the same style as the synthesis's "definition that carried two jobs").
2. **A rule that cannot run in the wrong place.** None of the ten researches mentions where a rule may execute; the data-residency case (S11) is a requirement the account and user researches (erasure, ledger never erased) would meet at the first jurisdiction.
3. **Stateful rules versus the stateless README.** Transaction's conditional pledges and the account's restriction need a rule with state (required, satisfied, remaining); content's interaction limits keep a per-user count. The README describes a rule as a stateless extension point. The proposal derives the state from the firing trail (usage), which the sibling researches did not consider.
4. **Who owns a published rule.** Thing's `same_as` and organization-per-product mapping (product question 4) say each organization owns its own node; the adoption of a published rule (S3, S10) wants one node shared across organizations. Not a contradiction of a Decided statement, but the same fork as product question 4 and invoice exchange.
5. **Time of validity.** The account research separated recording order from effective time for the ledger; this research asks the same of a rule's validity; the transaction research asks "which time orders an effect" for a booking. One convention across the three would avoid three answers.

**On the sibling kernels (read, not edited).** If question 2 is accepted, each sibling's kernel list drops the attachment as its own element and gains an event target naming its points. The product kernel goes from four elements to three, task from five to four, group from four to three, account from five to four, transaction from nine to eight, invoice from seven to six; content and text lose one each. This is a consequence for their lists, not a claim that their researches were wrong; their owners decide.

## Recommendation (unratified)
The owner has not reviewed this, and nothing in it is decided.

1. **Say what the rule domain is for (Tentative, medium confidence).** The rule domain owns the memory of governing conditions: identity, statement by reference, owner, interval, attachment and the trail of what a rule caused. It does not evaluate, and it does not own any organization's policies. This changes the opening of [rule.md](rule.md) and nothing else visible.
2. **Keep a six-element kernel (Tentative):** identity, the statement by reference, the one `condition_is` attachment (point, mode, limit), validity as events, consequence provenance (`made_request`), and an event target. Everything else is a capability, a rule or an existing domain.
3. **Choose M3 over A, and keep B open until the attachment-status question is answered (Tentative).** M3 costs almost nothing to adopt; B wins only if an attachment needs a status and a limit of its own.
4. **Define `condition_is` once, here (Tentative).** One relation, one document, one direction (subject to rule), with the reverse by traversal; consumers keep an event target and drop their copies.
5. **Separate three modes: observe, accompany, refuse (Tentative).** A refusal before processing is the only veto; effects after are new requests; fail-open or fail-closed is stated per attachment.
6. **Amendment is a successor, and validity has two times (Tentative).** A changed rule is a new rule that supersedes; effective and recorded times are both kept; "what governed on date D" is discovered.
7. **Precedence is stated, never silent (Tentative).** By an authority over both rules; no specificity default.
8. **Split the status names (Tentative).** Provenance, consumption (discovered), lifecycle.
9. **Reclassify the edge folders as in finding 5, after the owner answers.** Do not move, delete or convert anything on this evidence. `RuleModel` stays as it is.
10. **Ask the questions that change what the work is first:** question 1 (what the domain is responsible for), question 2 (who owns `condition_is`), question 3 (what a rule may do at a before-point) and question 4 (amendment and the two times).
