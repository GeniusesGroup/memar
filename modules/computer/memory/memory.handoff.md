## Decisions

### Owner rulings of 2026-10-24 on the shape of the memory module (decided)
Given by the owner after reading the design of task `20261023-T074641Z-5420`; they supersede the Tentative entry below wherever the two differ, and the tasks queued from them settle the rest. The order is the owner's.
- **The adapters are independent modules.** `git` and `files` are modules of their own and do not stand under `modules-plugable/computer/memory/record/`: putting them there is like putting `record` under every domain because every domain's storage uses it. The model must be more exact: there is one common abstraction of retention that `record` itself uses down to the `block` layer, the base layer. The present categories of `modules/computer/memory/` were not accidental, and they are open to review.
- **File and Directory move to the filesystem module** (agreed). Whether the filesystem module itself should then move under `modules-plugable/computer/memory/` is the owner's own question, and the modelling task answers it.
- **`services/` is distributed.** The files of `modules/computer/memory/services/` are spread among the modules they belong to, or become a module of their own; a category that holds several modules does not keep the internal folders of a module (`services`, `error`).
- **The category rule changes.** The categories of `modules/` and of `modules-plugable/` correspond, and a flat pluggable side is a defect as the categories grow; the old categories are open to review too. `modules/storage` is wrong as a category and its modules belong under `memory`, since that word was chosen for the whole family.
- **A file that is developed is upgraded with care.** `record/protocol/model.kh` is the first example: `Record_Model_Lock` and `Record_Model_Unlock` become `Lock` and `Unlock`, since a capsule that realizes the abstraction realizes no other and needs no prefix on a method; `Storage_UUID` is a bad name for the key of the record module and is `RecordUUID`.
- **A key-value store is not a record store.** Its key is not a fixed-length id and it lists keys, which a record store cannot and should not; storing it in records would cost a hash of the key and the whole pair in the record's data. It is developed as a module of its own on `page` or even `block`.
- **A category holds no contract** (owner, 2026-10-26, task `20261024-T052937Z-22844`). `memory` is a category, so its `protocol/` is divided into independent modules and the capsules stand at the mirror of the module whose contract each realizes; the division is in [The division of the memory contract into modules](#the-division-of-the-memory-contract-into-modules-2026-10-26-tentative) (task `20261026-T120259Z-16244`), and the paths in this entry that put capsules directly under `modules-plugable/computer/memory/` read with `store/` inserted.
- **Answers are given in the layers above this chat.** The owner will answer in the tasks queued below, not in the conversation that produced the design.

### The division of the memory contract into modules (2026-10-26, Tentative)
Designed by task `20261026-T120259Z-16244` from the owner's ruling that a category holds no contract; reviewed by a sub-agent whose findings are applied. Tentative until the owner reads it; the moves and build tasks use its paths.
- **`memory` is a category** and holds no `protocol/`. The contract is divided into kernel modules: [`address`](address/address.md) (what names a retained thing: equality, total order, byte string, the kind and length fixed by the instantiator), [`store`](store/store.md) (the operations, token, guarantees, parameters, capabilities, limits), [`volatile`](volatile/volatile.md) (the reference adapter) and [`conformance`](conformance/conformance.md) (the suite and its runner). Dependence runs `address` <- `store` <- `volatile` <- `conformance`; the suite cannot be the store's `tests/` because it needs `volatile`, which needs the store. `reference` stays a module of its own.
- **The pointer is not the address.** `MemoryAddress` and `Memory_Pointer` (equivalence only, "the most primitive type of reference") and the address-sized integer ruled to memory on 2026-09-28 move to the `reference` module; `address` holds the ordered contract address only. The build of the contract moves the declarations.
- **Pluggable paths.** The capsules and optional operations of the store stand at `modules-plugable/computer/memory/store/<capability>/`: `lockable`, `nonvolatile`, `block`, `page`, `key_value`, `record`, `index`, `graph`, `git-store`, `files`, `cluster`, and `view` beside `record`. Directly at `modules-plugable/computer/memory/`, because they realize no one store: `filesystem` (the guest surface, taking `File` and `Directory` from `file/` and `directory/`), `sql` (to `store/sql` if it proves a surface over one store) and `allocator` (its home depends on the second division below). Every path this ruling gave as `modules-plugable/computer/memory/<capability>/` is read with `store/` inserted for the capsules above.
- **Task, timeline and Request** depend on `address` where they only name, and on `store` where they read or write (the Request's atomic batch and Conflict, the Task's `changes`), and on nothing of `record`. This keeps the owner's ruling and states which module "the memory contract" is.
- **Groups of the suite** belong to the module that declares the capability: `fallback` and `working-tree` to the adapters that declare them, `lockable` to its module. The token's SHA-256 is the subject, not a choice (a token recorded outside a store must be equal in every adapter).
- **`protocol/version.kh`** gets no home here: three kernel files import it, and moving it to a pluggable module is a back edge. The open entry below decides it before any move. `protocol/nonvolatile.kh` goes to `store/nonvolatile`; `key_value` rests on it as its barrier.
- **Anchors.** [Memory → Retention is reached through one contract](memory.md#retention-is-reached-through-one-contract) stays as a short section pointing at the modules, so the citations of Task, timeline, Request, key-value and graph keep their anchor; the moves task repoints each to the module that owns the statement.

### The model of the memory layer (2026-10-24, decided by the owner in task `20261024-T052936Z-22844`)
Written in [Memory → Retention is reached through one contract](memory.md#retention-is-reached-through-one-contract); the build tasks queued from it carry it out. The owner's answers:
- One contract, parametric in the kind of address (position, fixed id, variable key, path); block, page, record, key-value and the file system are capsules that realize it and add no abstraction.
- The contract is the memory module's own protocol and not a module of its own. The kernel keeps the definition, the contract with its suite and `volatile`, `address` and `reference`. Everything else is pluggable under `modules-plugable/computer/memory/`.
- Task, timeline and Request refer to an address through the memory contract and depend on nothing of `record` or `file`; the critique's finding that they depend on `record` is answered by repointing their documents, not by keeping `record` in the kernel.
- `git` and `files` realize the contract and know no id or label; `record` is a capsule over any store. The file system module, with File and Directory, moves under `modules-plugable/computer/memory/`. `key_value` is a module of its own on `page`. `cluster` is a module of its own.
- Adopted from the critique, without a separate ruling: the contract gains `state` and `changes`; its parameters are dense or sparse, fixed or any value length, and whether one address can be compared and written; held ranges are ranges of addresses; `git` shards by a prefix of the address.

### Should storage capabilities be named as independent capabilities, against this document's rejection of named kinds?
- State: recovered 2026-09-30 from an audit of Omid Hekayati's Go-community Telegram-group messages. The owner's position is that a storage concern's needs are independent axes rather than one bundled kind — durable key/value retention, time-series retention, full-text search, vector search, transactions, and a faster derived tier are separately adoptable, and a framework that requires a system to carry a local store, a cache, and a distributed store together has turned one requirement into a package. The tension is with this document's own position, which is not a gap but a designed refusal: [Volatility: the real classifier](./memory.md#volatility-the-real-classifier) keeps retention as a *property* precisely so the field is not split into named kinds, and [The memory/storage dichotomy rejected](./memory.md#the-memorystorage-dichotomy-rejected) records that reifying a tier is what produces the shadow-tier pathology.
- Question for the owner: are these capabilities to be stated as properties a contract may require (in the spirit of [Copy and ownership semantics are contract members](./memory.md#copy-and-ownership-semantics-are-contract-members)), which preserves the refusal, or as a named capability vocabulary, which reifies the field a second time? No document enumerates them today; the closest existing statement is [OS](../os/os.md), where whether blocks become records, tables, logs, journals, or object stores is explicitly left as meaning owned by the system above the boundary.
- Next: answer this before any storage design chat opens, because a profile list adopted by default would be exactly the reification the two sections above reject, and the [Filesystem](../../../modules-plugable/computer/memory/filesystem/filesystem.md) protocol's "a high-level library whose inclusion is a decision" stance is the precedent for the property-shaped answer.

### What is the retention and erasure rule for preserved history, and where do a record's versions live?
- State: the Modeling document fixes the modeling half — a concern's actual history of state changes is reality, a fix or reversal is a new fact referencing what it corrects, and no storage engine is prescribed ([Modeling → Modeling State Change as Events, Not Destructive Updates](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates)) — and the owner has stated the same position directly and repeatedly in the Go-group discussions recovered 2026-09-30: there is no logical reason to update or delete data, and a decision recorded at one time must not be erased by a later decision to change it. The same discussions carry the qualifications that keep this from being an absolute rule, and those qualifications are stated nowhere in `docs/`: secret erasure, legal purge, and bounded retention are not the same act as a state change, and "never delete" makes all three impossible.
- Question for the owner: what is the erasure rule that sits beside history preservation — is a removed value a tombstone that records the removal as a fact, a gap in the history, or an exception to the whole rule — and who states it, this protocol or the Modeling document? Related and equally unstated: whether the versions of one record must be co-located. The source argues both ways inside a single message — that the storage layer should not be made to understand the nature of the data, and that in a distributed store the temporal versions of a record must be kept on one node rather than spread across nodes.
- The record contract ([port](../../../modules-plugable/computer/memory/store/record/record.md#what-every-adapter-guarantees)) offers no removal until this is ruled, so no verb erases, and contents labelled `temp` are kept; the entry in [Memory Record Handoff](../../../modules-plugable/computer/memory/store/record/record.handoff.md#what-is-the-retention-and-erasure-rule-now-that-the-port-offers-none) carries the options.
- Next: owner ruling on the erasure rule first; the placement question then belongs to whichever storage design consumes the capability question above, and to [Time Handoff → Distributed ordering versus the Instant contract](../../time/time.handoff.md#distributed-ordering-versus-the-instant-contract) for what "co-located" would cost.

### Must teardown be idempotent, and which lifecycle names belong to which resource family?
- State: recovered 2026-09-30 from an audit of Omid Hekayati's Go-community Telegram-group messages, where the owner's stated house practice was three lifecycle members for most structures — initialize, re-initialize, de-initialize — with two disciplines attached: re-initialization is worth its cost only after being measured against release, and must not let information from the previous cycle leak into the new one; and initialization performed inside an external factory function was rejected outright, because it moves the capsule's own behavior outside its encapsulation. What survives of that practice in this repository is narrower than the source: `modules/computer/capsule/protocol/life_cycle_contract.kh` states that `Init` and `Reinit` are not members of the protocol, and this document gives `Deinit` only to a capsule whose acquisition requires a release ([Teardown is explicit, and automation writes source](./memory.md#teardown-is-explicit-and-automation-writes-source)). No document states whether releasing twice is defined, permitted, or an error — a rule the question immediately above this one needs before the composition in `service.kh` can be ruled on either way.
