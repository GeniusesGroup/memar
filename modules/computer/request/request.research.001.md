## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The words, defined before they are used.**

- **Request, in this domain's own document.** [request.md](request.md) opens with a dictionary sense: "a formal message requesting something that is submitted to an authority or an act of asking politely or formally", and then, two sections later, gives its usages: store requests "to prove how other records created as a chain to other records or standalone data", store user requests as drafts to send later, and prove how a request was created in the chain of other requests. The first is a *petition*; the usages are about *provenance of a call*. See contradiction 1.
- **Request, in Memar.** An interaction between participants that may initiate, advance, observe, modify, retry or terminate a process and is not the process ([Process](../../process/process.md)); the thing a capability addressed to a known participant is ([Event → What an Event Is Not](../../process/event/event.md)); the type `Process_Request`, which composes a `Type` and a `Codec` (`memar/modules/process/request/protocol/request.kh`). Memar's `Request_UUID` is documented as "RecordID to prove how domain objects created in the chain of other objects" (`uuid.kh`), which is this repository's usage, not the dictionary's.
- **Request record (agent's term).** The organization's *durable memory of one asking*: not the call in flight (Memar's request, a runtime occurrence) and not the work it asked for (a process, a transaction, a task).
- **Connection.** A persistent, stateful association between two components so that a series of exchanges can share context; its state is owned by the component that runs the protocol logic and exposed read-only ([Networking Connection](../../net/connection/connection.md)). The organization's `connection` domain is the *record* of such an association (`ConnectionID`, peer address, token, cipher suite, and the request that created it), not the association's engine state.
- **Software, instance, node.** *Software* is "written programs or procedures or rules and associated documentation" ([software.md](../software/software.md)); an *instance* (the folder `instance_of`, whose document is titled "Node") is "running version of applications. Any application CAN have many running instance" ([instance_of.md](../instance_of/instance_of.md)).

**The proposal (agent, Tentative).** The request domain gives an organization one answer to a question every other domain keeps asking and none can answer for itself: *which asking caused this fact, who asked, through what, for what, when, with what proof, and what answer did it get?* It owns the **memory of an asking** — an acquired fact that is cited by the registration fact of nearly every record in the organization (125 `.kh` files in this repository mention a request identity, converted or in Go residue; the synthesis's twelve-domain model lists "requester, time, request id" in the registration fact of thing, user, content, text, account, group and rule). It does not own the work asked for, the right to ask, the wire, or the program that answered.

The decomposition follows the four identities the handoff already records as Decided: the **request identity** is this domain's; the **service identity** is the `service` edge; the **operation identity** has no home yet (question 17); the **routing identity** is the connection's far end, on the instance. That the request is a fourth, separate identity is what lets one request be relayed, retried or recorded after the connection is gone.

**Where the chain runs (the runtime side).**

| Question | Answer | Home today | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who asked? | an acting user; possibly acting for a principal; a software agent is a user of type app (the node-details service authorizes `user_p.Type_App`) | no folder; implied by `connection` (user and delegate user) and the retired `UserID` | **missing** (finding 6) |
| Over which pipe? | the connection, plus the stream or time coordinate inside it (sRPC: stream id, or the frame's `Time` for a call without a stream) | `request/edges/connection` and `connection/edges/request`, the same relation twice | duplicate (contradiction 2) |
| To what? | a service of a software, selected by service identity; the operation inside the service | `request/edges/service` | operation has no home |
| Running which software? | the software of the instance on each end of the connection; "official GUI app or SDK or API call" is the app kind and run target | `software/edges/app` (kinds and run targets, candidate) | derivable, not stored on the request |
| On which node? | the instance at the far end of the connection (`connection_to`: instance to instance); the client's active user is `instance_of/edges/user_active` | `connection/edges/connection_to`, `instance_of/edges/user-active` | derivable |
| With what proof? | a signature by a key over the payload; the key is the token's | `request/edges/signature` (far end unnamed) | far end missing |
| Permitted by what? | a rule that allowed it; a delegation it acted under | `request/edges/conditional` (rule only) | delegation missing |
| What came of it? | the call's outcome: refused, answered, failed, dropped | the retired `user-request-metric` statuses | **missing** as events |

So the request is the *pivot* between four domains that each know one end: `user` and `token` (who), `connection` (the pipe), `service` and `software` (what and where), `instance_of` (the node). It needs no direct edge to software or node: both are reached through the connection, and a direct edge would store a derivable fact.

**Scenarios.** Ten, from several cultures and industries; each states what it breaks in the obvious model (obvious model: a request is a live network call from a signed-in user to a server, recorded as a log row).

1. **A community health worker with a tablet in rural Kenya or Bihar (offline first).** She records ten household visits over three days with no network, each one a signed request queued on the device; at the district road junction the tablet syncs through a stranger's phone hotspot, then again two days later through the clinic's network, and one batch is sent twice because the first acknowledgement was lost. The server's clock and the tablet's differ by a day. *Breaks:* a request exists before any connection; one request may be delivered over several connections and several times; "the request time" is two instants (when she asked and when the organization received it); a connection edge must be optional. *Says:* identity is minted where the request is first recorded, the signature covers the asked-at instant, a retry is a new request that refers back to the earlier one, and the draft is a shared status, not a module ([Modeling → A Relation Several Domains Need Is Defined Once](../../../docs/modeling.md#a-relation-several-domains-need-is-defined-once) names draft as a marker defined once with the other statuses).
2. **A company bank transfer with maker and checker (Germany, Iran, Brazil).** An accountant requests a transfer; a second person approves; the engine then books a withdrawal and a deposit; settlement with another bank completes the next day, and fails for one of three payments. *Breaks:* one business act is many requests with different requesters (the maker, the checker, the engine); the call "register the transfer" completed while the *work* failed a day later. *Says:* the request's outcome is the call's (answered), not the work's (the transaction's status events); the approver is the requester of another request, and the engine's bookkeeping request has the engine as requester.
3. **A permit petition at a counter (an Iranian municipality, an Indian block office, a Saudi visa window).** The applicant fills a paper form, a clerk keys it in on her behalf, a stamped receipt number is handed back, and the petition passes through three agencies over six weeks, each issuing its own internal request. *Breaks:* the dictionary sense of "request" (a formal petition to an authority) is a real thing in the world and is not a call; the requester is the applicant but the typist is the clerk; no signature in the cryptographic sense; no connection (paper); the receipt number is an identity the applicant holds. *Says:* the petition is a domain act with its own lifecycle and decision (a case or task kind), the keystroke call that registers it is a request whose requester is the clerk acting for the applicant, and the domain must not swallow petitions into its kernel (question 14).
4. **A scheduled fee at 02:00 and a sensor reading (any bank, any smart building).** A rule charges a monthly fee; a thermostat reports a temperature every minute. Nobody is signed in. *Breaks:* the requester is not a person; the rule's `made_request` ([rule](../rule/edges/request/request.md)) and the sensor are agents. *Says:* the requester is an identity answerable through an accountable principal (the declaring organization, the sensor's owner), the recorded volume of readings is a rule's choice, and not every call is worth a record (question 3).
5. **A telecom support ticket (Sweden, Nigeria).** A customer reports a fault; the ticket depends on three internal tasks; a week later she reopens it. *Breaks:* "request" as the customer's wish: that is a task (the [task research](../../management/task/task.research.001.md) scenario 7). *Says:* the call that creates the ticket is a request; the ticket is a task; the reopen is a new task that supersedes the first and the *call* that reopens is a new request. Two different "again"s, see contradiction 11.
6. **A QR payment demand (Brazil's Pix, India's UPI, Sweden's Swish).** A shopkeeper shows a code that *asks the customer for money*; the customer's phone then sends a transfer. *Breaks:* a "payment request" is a claim from payee to payer (it looks like an invoice, [invoice](../../economics/invoice/invoice.md)) that rides on no connection from payee to payer; the call that follows goes the other way. *Says:* the word request names at least four different things in the corpus (a call, a transaction, a join, a payment demand); this domain is only the first.
7. **An invoice sent from one organization to another (the open question of 2026-10-10 in [request.handoff.md](request.handoff.md#open-questions)).** A purchase's invoice must reach every organization it involves; the receiver's rule decides which senders it accepts, the sender is authenticated, each delivery is metered, a price mismatch is rejected. *Breaks:* the receiver did not follow the sender, so [follow](../../identity/thing/edges/follow/follow.md) cannot carry it; two organizations each keep their own memory of the same asking. *Says:* delivery is a request to the receiver's service surface; the sender's record and the receiver's record are two counterparts, neither a replica of the other ([Modeling → One Authoritative Location per Concern's Data](../../../docs/modeling.md#one-authoritative-location-per-concerns-data)).
8. **A person asks that what she wrote be erased (the EU, Iran's data-protection bill, anywhere with a deletion duty).** Her original request to post contained her words; her erasure request is itself a request. *Breaks:* "nothing is ever deleted" against "the payload must go"; the erasure request is a fact that must itself persist. *Says:* the fact of the asking, its time, requester, target, digest and outcome stay; the payload becomes unreadable; the signature still proves a request with that digest existed — which only works if the signature covers a digest, not the stored bytes (question 7).
9. **An emergency call to a dispatcher (112, 115, 911).** The caller is unauthenticated, perhaps unidentified; the dispatcher keys the call in, adds notes, dispatches an ambulance; later the caller is identified by a call-back. *Breaks:* every assumption about a recognisable user, a signature, a software-to-software connection; the "channel" is a telephone line. *Says:* the model must record an anonymous requester explicitly and let the recording party be the dispatcher; the connection domain's assumption that both ends are software instances ([connection_to](../connection/edges/connection_to/connection_to.md)) does not hold for it (question 11).
10. **A signed request relayed across a mesh (store-and-forward over several hops, Chapar or a mail-in).** One signed request travels over three connections, each between two different instances; the last hop is a gateway that forwards to the application. *Breaks:* `request → connection` is not one-to-one; a gateway's forwarded call looks like a new request with a new requester. *Says:* one request, many hops, the hops belong to the connection layer; a gateway that re-issues the call under its own authority makes a *sub-request* whose requester is the gateway and whose cause is the first.

**What the cases agree on.** The domain is the memory of the *asking*, never of the *work*: in 2, 3, 5 and 6 the work has its own domain; in 1, 8, 9 and 10 the asking outlives the pipe; in 4 and 9 the asker is not an ordinary signed-in user; in 7 the asking is the unit that crosses an organization boundary. Four cases (3, 5, 6, 9) show the word is overloaded and must be fenced.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

**The removal test, run on the request itself (agent, Tentative).** [What Earns Foundational Status](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status): remove the candidate and try to express the part of reality it covers through the remaining abstractions; if that forces fabricating or bending a foundation, it is foundational. Remove the request: each of the dozens of domains whose records cite a request would have to carry its own requester, time, proof and answer. The registration event of thing ("the time, the request that caused it, the domain that asked", [thing research TH2](../../identity/thing/thing.research.001.md)) would hold an identity that points nowhere. That *is* a distortion of thing: it would acquire the fields of an asking. Nothing in the remaining set (thing, rule, user, token) owns an asking; the independent-lifecycle test of [Concept Existence vs. Model Existence](../../../docs/modeling.md#concept-existence-vs-model-existence) also passes: a request begins (is recorded, possibly as a draft), is answered, may be retried, may have its payload erased, and has rules about who may begin it (authentication, authorization, quota), about when it is valid (the asked-at window, replay), and about its proof (signature). It stays a domain.

**Proposed kernel (agent, Tentative): seven elements and an event target.**

| # | Element | Who may create / remove | What it triggers | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RK1 | **Identity** — one opaque identifier per asking, never reused, citable by any registration fact; Memar's `Request_UUID`; minted by the first party that records the asking (the requester's device for a draft, the receiving instance for a live call) | any admitted caller or an agent; never removed | the request exists and can be cited; its own registration is its receipt (finding 4) | `memar/.../uuid.kh`; [request.md](request.md#identity) |
| RK2 | **Requester** — the thing that asked, as an identity (not typed `user` in the kernel, see Reconciliation, layering), with the thing it acted for when different | the party recording the asking states it; for a live call the instance derives it from the authenticated connection; never removed (erasure detaches readability, X1) | answerability: every later fact can be attributed | [user research K2](../../identity/user/user.research.001.md); [content research Q5](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md); retired `UserID` in `_import/user-request` |
| RK3 | **Target** — the service asked (the service identity), with the operation inside it | the recording party; fixed once recorded | which rules and authorizations apply; which handler | [request/edges/service](edges/service/service.md); Decided four-identity decomposition |
| RK4 | **Time** — two instants: *asked-at* (as the requester states and signs it) and *received-at* (by the organization's clock when it first recorded it) | the recording party; never edited | ordering, replay window, the effective time other records may cite (X4) | `Observer_Time_Request` and its two-layer comment, `memar/.../time/protocol/request.observers.kh` |
| RK5 | **Payload proof** — a digest of the payload and its codec; the payload by reference; the signature over the digest, the target and the asked-at, with the key's identity | the requester; the payload alone may become unreadable (erasure) while the digest, codec and signature stay | proof that this asking existed; verification | [request/edges/data](edges/data/data.md), [signature](edges/signature/signature.md) |
| RK6 | **Outcome events** — received, refused (naming the rule), answered, failed, dropped; each a new event with its time, none overwriting | the instance that handled it; never removed | the answer's availability to the requester; retries; metering | retired `user-request-metric` statuses (unset, completed, failed, pending, drop), minus `pending` (question 8) |
| RK7 | **Cause** — a request that caused this one, or a rule that did (`caused_by`); the retry link `retry_of` is its sibling | the handling party, when it makes a further request; never removed | provenance chain across domains | [sub-request](edges/sub-request/sub-request.md); [rule/edges/request](../rule/edges/request/request.md) |
| — | **EventTarget** naming the points rules may attach to: before authorization, after the answer, on refusal | — | rule firing; listeners | [Modularity → Event as a Module Capability](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability); [rule research kernel](../rule/rule.research.001.md) |

The kernel does not name the rule domain (the generic `condition_is` is the rule research's, Tentative: [RU.2](../rule/rule.research.001.md)); if RU.2 is accepted the attachment disappears as an element and the table loses nothing.

**What the kernel never owns.** The work asked for (a transaction, a task, an admission); the right to ask (delegate, ownership, authorization); the key and its rotation (token); the wire and its engine state (connection, with Networking Connection); the program and the node (software, instance_of); the name of the service (service/text); payment, price, quota values; retention law; the volume policy.

**Pluggable capabilities, each owning a relation and its processing together** ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement): an edge is a module only with independent identity, lifecycle, authorization, rules or processing; no bare edges).

| Pluggable | Relation it owns | Processing | Who may create / remove | What it triggers | Mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **outbox** (new) | `(request)-[:retry_of]->(request)`; the idempotency key | queueing on the device; ordering; deduplication on receipt (same key returns the earlier outcome); retry limits | create: the requester's client; remove: none (a retry link is a fact) | a repeated call returns the first answer instead of acting twice | Tentative |
| **quota** (new; merges the `time` edge's "minute window" and connection and node limits) | none of its own: a rule attached at the before-point with a limit; `usage` discovered from the requests | per requester, per connection, per instance windows | create/remove: the organization's rule owner | refusal at the before-point, with the rule named | Tentative; merge candidate with interaction-limits of the [synthesis](../../../.agents/tasks/20261018-T195830Z-17032/base-model.md) |
| **acknowledgement** (new) | `(request)-[:acknowledged_by]->(receipt)`; a receipt identity the requester holds | issues a receipt (a counter slip, a ticket number, a signed response) | create: the receiving party; remove: none | the requester can later cite the asking | Explored (scenario 3) |
| **payload-erasure** (an application of the shared erasure of [thing](../../identity/thing/thing.research.001.md)) | none new | makes the payload unreadable, keeps digest, signature, fact | the requester or an authority under a rule | tombstone of readability; notification | Explored; not a module, a use of X1 |
| **relay** (new) | `(request)-[:relayed_over]->(connection)` in order | records hops of store-and-forward | the forwarding instance | none beyond provenance | Explored (scenario 10); may stay in the connection domain |
| **inbound-gateway** (new) | none new; an authenticated-organization rule at the before-point | accepts requests from another organization; per-organization authentication, acceptance rule, metering | the receiving organization | the receiver's own request record; a refusal reaches the sender | Explored (scenario 7) |
| **sensitive-read record** (a rule, not a module) | a rule attached to a read service in accompany mode that makes a request record | records chosen reads (a medical record, a bank statement) | the rule's owner | the read leaves a record | Tentative |
| **analytics** | none | latency, success rate, volume; replaces the retired metric record | `modules-plugable/computer/analytic*` | derived only | Tentative |
| **verification** (home: token) | none new | checks the signature against the key valid at the asked-at | token domain | an outcome event | Tentative |

**Placement tests.** (1) *Independent identity/lifecycle/rules/processing*: outbox, quota and acknowledgement each have all four; analytics and verification are derived/other-owned. (2) *No bare edge*: each row states creator, remover and trigger; `retry_of` and `acknowledged_by` have a trigger. (3) *Kernel does not reference rule*: the kernel names points, not rules. (4) *Cross-domain analytics live under the category's analytics namespace*: the request analytics join `modules-plugable/computer/analytic*`. (5) *Behaviour binds to a UUID*: the service identity is a UUID; the label "retry" or "refused" is data.

### 3. Rival models

Marks: Decided, Tentative, Explored, Deferred.

- **M1 — a log row per call (the status quo reading; Explored).** The retired `user-request` family: id, user, connection, service, time, payload, signature, past request, duration and status, written for every call. *For:* simple; audit complete; legacy exists. *Against:* records reads and noise as facts; mixes the call's outcome with the work's (`pending`); stores payload and proof together so erasure destroys the proof; treats the connection as always present.
- **M2 — the memory of an asking, cited by registration facts (Tentative, recommended).** The kernel of finding 2: identity, requester, target, two times, payload proof, outcome events, cause. Recorded for every call that changes recorded state or crosses an organization boundary, for every refused attempt at such a call, and for any call a rule chooses (sensitive reads). *For:* gives the dozens of citing domains a real target; keeps the request out of the work; survives erasure; fits draft, relay and agent cases; agrees with Memar (request is an interaction, not the process). *Against:* a new kernel domain to hold; a choice of what to record that needs a rule.
- **M3 — no domain; the request is Memar's runtime event (Explored).** Event is "a runtime occurrence and is not a stored record" ([Event](../../process/event/event.md)); each domain's registration fact carries requester and time itself; a log listener keeps the stream. *For:* fewest concepts; Memar's own position that "request, response, event, command" names do not each need a model. *Against:* the same requester, time, signature and answer would be restated in every citing domain (the failure [One Authoritative Location per Concern's Data](../../../docs/modeling.md#one-authoritative-location-per-concerns-data) names); no home for a refused attempt, a retry or a relayed request; the chain across domains cannot be walked. M3 is right that the *occurrence* is Memar's; it does not answer where the organization keeps its *memory* of one.
- **M4 — a request is a kind of task or case (Explored; not recommended).** Every ask is the intent of some work, so it is a [task](../../management/task/task.md). *For:* one concept for tickets and petitions. *Against:* a call is not work; task would own participants and outcomes it does not need (scenario 4: a sensor reading is not a task); and it dissolves the boundary scenarios 3, 5, 6 need.
- **M5 — fold into thing's registration event (Explored).** The registration event of thing *is* the request record; there is no separate domain ([thing research M4](../../identity/thing/thing.research.001.md): "identity + audit, request and time", Explored). *For:* one fewer domain; matches the shape of the registration fact. *Against:* a request that creates nothing (a refused attempt, a retry, a read a rule records) has no thing to register; a request that changes an *existing* thing is not a registration; so the audit would need a second home for everything except births. Regress (finding 4): if the request were a thing its own registration would need a request.
- **M6 — fold into the process (Deferred).** Model the asking as a step of the process it initiates. *Against:* Process states a request "is not a process"; a process may be initiated by something that is not a request (a condition, a schedule), and one process has many requests.

**How each meets the cases** (agent judgment): S1 offline: M2 yes (draft, retry), M1 no (connection required), M3 partial, M5 no. S2 maker-checker: M2 yes, M1 mixes outcomes. S3 petition: M2 only with the fence, M4 yes but wrongly absorbs calls. S4 agent: M2, M3 yes. S5 ticket: M2 + task. S6 payment demand: none; it is an invoice-like claim. S7 cross-organization: M2 (counterpart records), M3 no. S8 erasure: M2 only with digest-signed proof. S9 anonymous: M2 with explicit anonymous requester. S10 relay: M2 with the relay pluggable.

**Smaller choice 1 — what is recorded (Tentative: state-changing, boundary-crossing and refused, plus rule-chosen).** Options: every call (M1's volume, and a read-heavy system records itself into the ground); state-changing only (misses an attempted but refused change, which is a security fact, and a sensitive read); state-changing, refused attempts and boundary-crossing, plus anything a rule chooses. The last keeps reads out by default and puts the decision in a rule, where Memar puts policy.

**Smaller choice 2 — where the signature's far end lives (Tentative: the token's key).** `edges/signature` today is `(request)-[:signature]->(---)`, a bare edge. The user research puts credential binding in the token domain and says rotation keeps old keys so old signatures verify ([user research](../../identity/user/user.research.001.md)). The request should name the key (the token identity) that signed it; the byte sequence of the signature is a value on the edge.

**Smaller choice 3 — is the request identity a thing (Tentative: no).** See finding 4, Identity.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions

Written to favour no answer where unsettled. A citation means a document already constrains the question; "open" means nothing in the tree decides it.

#### Identity
- What is a request's identity, who mints it, and when? (Constrained: Memar declares `Request_UUID` and the domain imports it, [request.md](request.md#identity). Open: the requester's device for a draft, the receiving instance for a live call, or one rule for both?)
- Is a request a thing? The synthesis's A1 ruling makes "all thing-derived ids return `ThingUUID`" ([owner-questions A1](../../../.agents/tasks/20261014T071207Z-28148-0/owner-questions.md)), and the Go source of the retired file declared `type UUID thing_p.UUID` beside the comment `// type UUID request_p.UUID`. If the request is a thing, then thing's registration event ("the request that caused it", TH2) requires a request, and the request's own registration requires a request: an infinite regress, unless the request's own receipt *is* the registration. If it is not a thing, its identity stays Memar's `Request_UUID` and the A1 rule needs one stated exception. (Open.)
- Is the identity unique across organizations? A request delivered to another organization (scenario 7) is, for the receiver, a different asking with its own identity; does the receiver's record cite the sender's id as `same_as` or as `caused_by`? (Open; X7.)
- The handoff asks what distinguishes `Request_Storage_Key_Primary` from `Request_Storage_Key_Cluster`, which both require only the request identity ([request.handoff.md](request.handoff.md#open-questions)). (Open, existing.)
- The wire coordinate (a stream id, or the call frame's `Time`) identifies a request only inside one connection ([sRPC → Stream ID](../../net/srpc/srpc.md)). Is it a field on the connection edge, or does a request without a connection (a draft) have none? (Open.)

#### Lifecycle
- What are the events, and is each a new fact? Candidates: drafted, recorded, received, refused (with the rule), answered, failed, dropped, erased (payload only). (Constrained by [Modeling → Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates).)
- Is "pending" a state of the request or of the work? The retired metric had five statuses (unset, completed, failed, pending, drop). "Pending" in a transfer is the transaction's status (partially processed, deferred), not the call's. (Open.)
- May a recorded request ever be removed? The retired `user-request-data` has `Delete(requestID)`; request.md says the signature proves the request "was not invented after the fact". (Constrained by X1: erasure detaches readability, keeps the fact; open for the payload.)
- Is a retry the same request, a new request that refers back, or a replacement? Memar: "a new interaction that happens to refer back to an earlier one" ([Process](../../process/process.md)). (Constrained; the relation name and X2's two kinds are open, contradiction 11.)
- When the connection that carried a request is revoked or erased, what survives on the request? (Open.)

#### Authorization
- Who may *make* a request: any caller, only an admitted user, only a user with a key, a guest (`NetworkInfo` splits owner types 0 guest and 1 registered, [instance_of.md](../instance_of/instance_of.md))? (Open.)
- Who may *read* a request record, the payload separately from the fact? (Open; the same overlap of [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md) and [access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md) the task research flags.)
- Who may record a request *for* another (the clerk in scenario 3, the dispatcher in 9)? That is acting-for, one capability with delegate, ownership and authorization (X3 of the synthesis). (Open; blocked by the unresearched delegate.)
- Which rule allowed a request, and which delegation did it rely on? Three places now hold a version of this: `request/edges/conditional` (`authorized_by`), `connection/edges/conditional` (`authorize_by`, "exact rule that allows the request will store in request domain"), `rule/edges/authorization` ([rule research](../rule/rule.research.001.md) contradiction 3). (Open.)

#### Rules
- What rules attach at the request's points: authentication strength (`software/edges-loop/authentication-*` are software-level policies that apply to requests at the before-point), quota, replay window, signature requirement, accepted-organizations, captcha? (Open; the kernel names no rule.)
- Which rule decides what is recorded (question 3)? (Open.)
- Which rule decides the retention and payload erasure of a request? (Open; law domain not read.)
- What invariants must hold: requester present or explicitly anonymous; target present; asked-at not after received-at by more than a stated tolerance; an outcome event only after a record; a request is never edited. (Own proposal.)

#### Events
- Which occurrences does the request expose as an EventTarget? Candidates: recorded, refused, answered, failed, dropped, erased. The service request/response pair is itself the event ([Modularity](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)); which of these already exists as a request or response and needs no event type of its own? (Open.)
- Which events may a subscriber learn without learning the requester's identity (the same question user research asks)? (Open.)

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each is a one-paragraph sketch; none is designed.

- **`requested_by` (edge; request kernel RK2).** `(request)-[:requested_by]->(thing)`, with an optional `acted_for` far end. The retired record carried `UserID`; the current `protocol/storage.kh` still exposes `Service_ListRequestIDsByUserID_*`; no edge folder carries it. Created when the asking is recorded, never removed; erasure detaches readability only. It triggers answerability. It is the keystone: the domain's central question has no folder.
- **`permitted_by` (edge; reshape of `conditional`).** The exercised permission: the far end is a rule (a verdict) or a delegation (a right relied on). Created by the handling instance at authorization; never removed. It triggers the audit answer "why was this allowed", and, with the refused case, "why was this refused". One relation for two kinds of far end, if the owner accepts one evidence relation (question 13).
- **`retry_of` (edge; outbox).** A new request refers back to the earlier one with the same intent. Created by the requester's client or the outbox; never removed. Triggers deduplication by idempotency key. Not `supersedes` (a failed attempt had no effect to replace) and not `corrects` (nothing to offset); the existing two kinds of correction (X2) do not cover it (contradiction 11).
- **Outcome events (kernel; RK6).** A status loop is not enough: each outcome is a new fact with a time, a cause (the rule that refused) and an answer reference. Belongs with the shared status vocabulary (R4).
- **`quota` (pluggable).** A rule at the before-point with a limit and `usage` discovered from requests; it replaces the `time` edge's "dedicate time category per user" and merges with connection firewall counters' policy half and `node-metric`'s rate limits. Created and removed by the rule owner; triggers refusal naming the rule.
- **`outbox` (pluggable).** The device-side queue of signed drafts, ordering and deduplication; its processing is what scenario 1 needs. Draft stays the shared status, not owned here.
- **`acknowledgement` (pluggable).** The receipt the requester holds; the counter slip, ticket number or signed response. Explored.
- **`inbound-gateway` (pluggable).** Cross-organization acceptance: authentication per organization, the receiver's acceptance rule, metering. Answers the invoice-delivery question; may be a use of `quota` plus a rule rather than a module.
- **`relay` (possibly in connection).** The ordered list of connections one request crossed; Explored.
- **`operation` (field or label on the service edge).** The operation identity of the Decided decomposition has no home.
- **A request-to-request relation record that is not named `widget`.** The relation record for `caused_by` and `retry_of`, replacing the `widget` folder.

### 7. Test scenarios

Given/when/then. A mark in brackets says which proposal the test depends on.

1. **Offline draft keeps its identity and its two times (RK1, RK4).** Given a signed request drafted on a device with no network. When it is delivered three days later over a different connection. Then the request keeps the identity minted at drafting, asked-at is the signed instant, received-at is the delivery instant, and the delivering connection is recorded as that request's connection.
2. **A double delivery acts once (outbox).** Given a request delivered twice with the same idempotency key. When the second arrives. Then no second effect occurs, the second is recorded as `retry_of` the first with the first's answer, and the requester sees one outcome.
3. **A refused attempt is a request (RK6, smaller choice 1).** Given a delete of a product with history ([product research](../../economics/product/product.research.001.md) test 4). When the service refuses it. Then a request exists with an outcome event `refused` naming the rule, the product is unchanged, and the requester can see why.
4. **Call outcome is not work outcome (RK6, finding 2).** Given a transfer registered by a call that was answered. When settlement fails the next day. Then the request's outcome stays `answered`, the transaction has its own failed status event, and no request status changes.
5. **Maker and checker are two requests (RK2).** Given a maker's request to register a transfer and a checker's request to approve it. When the transfer is approved. Then its approval event cites the checker's request, its registration cites the maker's, and neither request names the other's requester.
6. **An engine is a requester (RK2, X3).** Given a rule that charges a fee. When it fires. Then the fee transaction's registration cites a request whose requester is the rule's engine with the declaring organization as the thing acted for, and `caused_by` the triggering request.
7. **Erasure keeps the proof (RK5, X1).** Given a request whose payload held personal data and whose signature covers its digest. When the person's erasure is accepted. Then the payload is unreadable, the digest, codec, signature, target, requester (as an identity link per X1), times and outcome remain, and a verifier can still check that a request with that digest was signed.
8. **A signature that no longer matches fails verification (RK5).** Given a stored request whose payload bytes are altered. When the digest is recomputed. Then it differs from the signed digest and verification reports a mismatch without changing the request.
9. **The handshake request precedes its connection (RK1, connection).** Given a connection registered by a request. When the request is recorded. Then it has no connection edge; the connection record cites the request; later requests cite the connection.
10. **A relayed request stays one request (relay).** Given a request carried over three connections. When it arrives. Then one request exists, its requester and signature are unchanged, and the hops are recorded in order; a gateway that re-issues the call creates a second request `caused_by` the first with the gateway as requester.
11. **An anonymous caller is recorded as anonymous (RK2, question 5).** Given an emergency call keyed in by a dispatcher. When the request is recorded. Then the requester is the dispatcher acting for an unidentified caller, stated explicitly, and a later call-back adds the identification as a new fact.
12. **A request needs a target (invariant).** Given a request with no service. When it is recorded. Then it is refused; an empty request is not stored as a fact (Khayyam rule 6 in spirit, [Khayyam practice](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md)).
13. **A reply to a different organization is two memories (X7).** Given an invoice delivered to another organization. When both record it. Then each has its own request record, the receiver's cites the sender's as `same_as` or `caused_by` (question 15), and neither record is edited when the other changes.
14. **Peer frames are not requests (question 3).** Given a ping and a time-sync frame between nodes. When they are exchanged. Then no request record is made; node health records them.
15. **A sensitive read is recorded by a rule (smaller choice 1).** Given a rule attached to the medical-record read service in accompany mode. When a doctor reads a record. Then a request record exists for the read; an ordinary read has none.
16. **Rate limit refuses at the before-point (quota).** Given a quota rule of ten requests per minute per requester. When the eleventh arrives. Then it is refused with the rule named, recorded as a refused request, and the usage figure is derived from the earlier requests.
17. **Request time never edited (RK4).** Given a request recorded with a received-at. When a later correction of the clock is applied. Then the request keeps its instant; a corrected view is a new fact or a derived reading (X4).
18. **A chain across domains can be walked (RK7, `last-chain`).** Given a text changed three times by three requests. When the history is asked. Then the shortcut lists the requests in order and its source is the registration facts of the text's values, not a stored list.

### Contradictions found

Reported, not resolved. Items 1 to 3 and 12 to 15 concern this domain's own documents; the others the neighbours.

1. **The domain document defines two things.** [request.md](request.md) opens with a petition to an authority and then describes provenance of a call. Memar's own definition is the second (an interaction), so the first sentence reads as the dictionary; "Definitions outrank terminology" ([Cognition](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)).
2. **One relation, two homes, the word `called` used twice.** [connection/edges/request](../connection/edges/request/request.md) says `(connection)-[:called]->(request)`; [request/edges/connection](edges/connection/connection.md) says `(request)-[:called_by]->(connection)`; [request/edges/service](edges/service/service.md) says `(request)-[:called]->(service)`. A relation several domains need is defined once; here the same relation lives in two and a name is reused.
3. **The handoff and request.md cite Memar files that do not exist.** request.md and the handoff name `memar/modules/process/request/protocol/dt-uuid.kh`, `dt-uuid.observers.kh` and `memar/modules/process/time/protocol/dt-request.observers.kh`. The files in Memar are `uuid.kh`, `uuid.observers.kh` and `request.observers.kh` (`ls` of both folders in this chat). Nine imports elsewhere, among them this domain's converted `request/protocol/storage.kh`, use the right path; **four `.kh` files in other domains import the wrong ones**: `economics/account/edges/bookkeeping/protocol/data_model.kh`, `knowledge/text/edges/duration/protocol/data_model.kh`, `knowledge/text/edges-loop/query/protocol/data_model.kh`, `management/group/edges/membership-term-member_limit/protocol/data_model.kh` (each imports `.../dt-uuid.observers.kh`, and the time observer from `dt-request.observers.kh`). Not fixed here (outside the four folders); recorded in the handoff.
4. **`logic-rollback` against the no-rollback rule.** [transaction.handoff.md](../../economics/transaction/transaction.handoff.md) §6 (Decided) and [transaction.md](../../economics/transaction/transaction.md): cancelling after a debit creates a compensating record; [Process](../../process/process.md) says rollback is one response among several, not an intrinsic requirement. A `logic__rollback` label on a sub-request names a mechanism the corpus rejected.
5. **`Delete` against the proof.** `_import/user-request-data` carries `Delete(requestID)`; request.md says the signature proves the request "was not invented after the fact"; X1 says erasure detaches readability and keeps the fact.
6. **Five statuses mixing two things.** The retired metric's `pending` is a work state, the others call outcomes; shared `block` / task `Blocking` / group `Blocked` collisions of the synthesis (C8) apply to any request status vocabulary.
7. **`time` as an edge, an observer, and a policy.** The folder `edges/time` is `(request)-[:request_time]->(time)` with a rate-limit policy; Memar declares `Observer_Time_Request` with a two-layer comment ("in storage layer, it is the save time of relation records; in business layer, it is the time user call related service"); the retired record's own comment says "request time or save time of the request not the created record". One name, two instants.
8. **Identity: thing-derived or memar's.** The Go `type UUID thing_p.UUID` and the A1 ruling against the handoff's decision that memar owns `Request_UUID` (itself an `ab` with no requirement, `uuid.kh`); finding 4 states the regress.
9. **Primary and cluster keys that require the same thing** (existing open question).
10. **The kernel of the request chain vs the synthesis' layering.** The synthesis `base-model.md` lists "request chain" in the registration facts but has no request domain in its twelve; every layer L2 to L5 cites a request, while the request names user (L2), token and service. See Reconciliation.
11. **Two different "again"s and a third.** A reopened ticket is a task that `supersedes` the first; a corrected invoice `corrects`; a failed call that is repeated is neither. The synthesis' X2 has two kinds (replaces, offsets).
12. **Every edge document lacks Placement and Open questions** (the required shape).
13. **Two audit mechanisms.** Memar's `uuid.observers.kh` comment calls the request "Field audit request (logging)"; [instance_of/edges/audit-log](../instance_of/edges/audit-log/audit-log.md) and [log-listener](../../../modules-plugable/computer/log-listener/log-listener.md) are logs too; the instance_of policy ("if any mode is disabled the logger must not save that log type … but must still dispatch it") is a log rule, while a request record must not be selectively dropped.
14. **The connection domain assumes software at both ends.** `connection_to` is instance to instance; scenarios 3 and 9 have a counter and a telephone.
15. **`last-chain` has no source edges** though the bucket rule requires them.

### Reconciliation with the sibling researches

**Agreements.** (a) Every sibling puts a requester, a time and a request identity in its registration fact or its revision: thing TH2 ("time, the request that caused it, the domain that asked"), user K2 (admission records "who requested it"), content K3 and text TK3 ("every revision carries who requested and who approved"), account (the bookkeeping record keeps the request and its time), group (`RequestID` in `joined`, provenance of a membership), rule (`made_request`, a kernel element), delegate (the data model carries request id and time). This is the strongest agreement across domains and it names the thing this domain must own. (b) Each separates the requester from the speaker or answerer ([content smaller choice 2](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md), [user research](../../identity/user/user.research.001.md) "the actor is not the speaker or the answerer"): the request records the requester only. (c) Reference, never embed: the sibling records cite a request by identity and do not copy its fields. (d) Erasure keeps the fact (X1), correction is a new fact (X2), record order and effective time are both kept (X4): all apply to the request unchanged.

**What the request must own, and what stays with each sibling.**

| Part of the "registration fact" | Owner | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| request identity | request | the thing every sibling cites |
| requester (who asked) | request (RK2) | siblings cite the request, not the user |
| acted-for principal | request records it; the right to act is delegate's | X3 |
| asked-at / received-at | request (RK4) | Memar's `Observer_Time_Request` |
| effective time of the *fact* (occurred, in force from, placed at) | the sibling | X4: separate from the asking |
| approver | the sibling, as a second request (the approver's own) | approval is another asking, cited by the event |
| reason, rationale | the sibling or a rule's rationale | not the asking |
| the domain that asked | derived from the service edge | not stored twice |
| payload, signature | request | proof of the asking |
| the work's status | the sibling | scenario 2 |

**Contradictions with a sibling.**
- **Thing (M4, "request and time" as a candidate `ThingModel` requirement).** The thing research keeps them as attributes of the registration *event*, Explored; the request research says the same thing differently (the registration event cites a request). Agreement in substance. The open knot is whether a request is itself a thing (finding 4); thing's TH.1 (the identity accessor) is independent of it.
- **Rule (kernel `made_request`).** The rule research keeps `(rule)-[:made_request]->(request)` as kernel element 5 and says nothing of a request's cause being a rule; this research's `caused_by` (RK7) is the same fact from the request's side. Two folders for one relation (`rule/edges/request`, `request/edges/sub-request`) unless the owner decides one direction is stored. Also the three `conditional` homes (contradiction in [rule research](../rule/rule.research.001.md)): this research's reading is *standing* (`condition_is` on the connection or service) versus *exercised* (`permitted_by` on the request).
- **Task.** The ticket (scenario 7 of the task research) is a task; a reopen is a task that `supersedes`. The request is the *call* that creates or reopens. No conflict, but the word must be fenced (question 14). The task research's second part (agent runs, claim and WIP) makes agents requesters in volume, which sharpens question 3.
- **Content.** Content's kernel "keeps the request chain" (`ChangeSenderUser`, the ownership participant, [synthesis CO.5](../../../.agents/tasks/20261018-T195830Z-17032/decision-graph.md)) while content K3 says each revision carries "who requested and who approved". Consistent with the table above if the revision cites two requests.
- **Text.** `edges-loop/query` is "a request someone made, not a relation between texts" (text research mapping): a *search* is a request in the loose sense and a read; whether it is recorded is question 3. Text's erasure on request (scenario 12, tests 29 and 30) means the erasure request is itself a request that must persist while the words go (this research's scenario 8).
- **Product.** A refused delete is "refused or recorded as withdrawal" (product research test 4): a refused attempt is a request with a refusal outcome (test 3 here).
- **Group.** `request_to_join` and the joined edge's `RequestID` use "request" in two senses (a petition to join, and the provenance id). The group research's admission pluggable (proposal either side, acceptance) is a domain act; the call that records it is a request. Fence, not conflict.
- **Account / transaction.** The bookkeeping record's request is the request that *booked* it (the engine's), not the customer's; the transaction (a "request, not an operation", [transaction.md](../../economics/transaction/transaction.md)) is a domain petition. Three causes coexist in one booking: the customer's asking (cited by the transaction), the transaction (the business cause), and the engine's booking asking (cited by the ledger record). The account research's ledger row lists "request" without saying which; this research says the booking one.
- **Layering.** The synthesis [layering](../../../.agents/tasks/20261018-T195830Z-17032/base-model.md) says "a lower layer never names a higher one". Every layer cites a request, so the request sits beside thing at the bottom, but its requester is a user (L2) and its signing key a token. Resolution proposed: the kernel's requester is an *identity* (a thing's), and that it is also a user is a fact other rules check (admission, standing); the request therefore names no layer above L0. If the owner rules the requester must be typed `user`, the request moves to L2.5 and the registration facts of thing, user and the rest cite upward, which the layering forbids. This is a conflict to rule on, not to resolve here.

**Position in the decision graph** ([decision-graph.md](../../../.agents/tasks/20261018-T195830Z-17032/decision-graph.md)). This research adds no new foundational joint question; it asks that the existing ones be applied to the request: question 2 depends on TH.1/TH.2/A1 and X0; question 7 on X1; question 4 on X4; question 9 on X2/X5; question 5, 13 on X3 and RU.2 (and the unresearched delegate); question 15 on X7; question 8 on R4 (status home). Best placed in the sitting that rules rule and authority (sitting 2), because the request is where rule, delegate and token meet; the lifecycle questions then follow in the sitting of the first domain that needs them.

## Recommendation (unratified)
The owner has not reviewed this, and nothing in it is decided.

1. **Keep the request as a base domain whose responsibility is the durable memory of one asking** (M2; Tentative), and define it so in the opening of [request.md](request.md), fencing the petition meaning to the domains that own it (transaction, task, group admission, search).
2. **A seven-element kernel** (identity, requester, target, two times, payload proof, outcome events, cause) and an event target. No element names the rule domain.
3. **The request is the root of provenance.** Its identity stays Memar's and is not minted by thing's registration, to avoid the regress; it is citable by every registration fact.
4. **Record what changes state, what crosses an organization boundary and every refused attempt at either; reads and peer frames only when a rule says so.**
5. **Sign a digest, name the key; the payload alone may be erased.**
6. **One home per relation:** the request–connection relation lives in the request domain; standing permission lives in `condition_is` on the connection and service, exercised permission as evidence on the request.
7. **The call's outcome is the request's; the work's outcome is its domain's.**
8. **Fold the three `logic-*` loops and the `widget` record into one request-to-request relation (`caused_by`, `retry_of`);** keep `last-chain` as a shortcut with its sources named.
9. **Add `requested_by`, `permitted_by`, `retry_of`, outcome events;** pluggables `outbox`, `quota`, `acknowledgement`, `inbound-gateway`, `relay`.
10. **Distinguish the request memory from the node's operational log** (audit-log, log-listener), and the connection record from the connection engine state.

## Open Questions
This is the research's eighth section. Only the owner can decide these. Ordered by what they change: those that change what the domain is first. Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. They are also entered in the [handoff](request.handoff.md#from-request-research-001-2026-10-19-agent-proposals-nothing-ratified).

1. **Is the request domain the durable memory of one asking (an acquired fact cited by the registration facts of other domains), or only Memar's runtime request with each domain restating requester and time?** Default: the memory of an asking (M2). Blocks: the rewrite of the opening of [request.md](request.md), thing's registration event shape (TH2), the 125 files that mention a request id, model M2 versus M3.
2. **Is a request a thing (so `ThingUUID`), or the root of provenance whose identity stays Memar's `Request_UUID`?** Default: not a thing; Memar's `Request_UUID`, with one stated exception to the "all ids return ThingUUID" rule (A1), because thing's registration cites a request and a request that is a thing would need one. Blocks: the retirement of `protocol/dt-uuid.kh` and `uuid.kh`, the A1 sweep, thing's TH2.
3. **What is recorded: every call, every state-changing call, or state-changing, refused and boundary-crossing calls plus any a rule chooses?** Default: the last; peer frames (ping, time sync) and ordinary reads are not requests. Blocks: volume, the connection stream counters, quota, the audit of sensitive reads, scenario 4's sensor readings.
4. **Is the request time one instant or two (asked-at as signed, received-at by the organization)?** Default: both (X4). Blocks: `Observer_Time_Request`'s two-layer meaning, offline sync, replay protection, tolerance rule.
5. **Must a request name its requester; may an anonymous caller be recorded, and how?** Default: required, as an identity; an anonymous caller is stated explicitly (the recording party acting for an unidentified caller), only where a rule admits it. Blocks: the missing `requested_by` edge, `Service_ListRequestIDsByUserID`, guest limits, the user research's "acting user is recognisable or acted for".
6. **What does a signature cover, and which key signs?** Default: a digest of the payload with the target and asked-at, by a key named from the token domain, required for state-changing requests of an admitted user. Blocks: erasure, the admission bootstrap (a first key signs its own admission request), relay.
7. **May a request's payload be erased while the fact, digest, signature and outcome stay?** Default: yes, by the shared erasure (X1). Blocks: `user-request-data` `Delete`, retention rules, regulated sectors.
8. **Are the outcomes only the call's (refused, answered, failed, dropped), and is the work's status the sibling's?** Default: yes; drop `pending` from the request. Blocks: the status vocabulary (R4), the retired metric, test 4.
9. **Is the chain one relation `caused_by` (request to request or to a rule) with a role field, and `retry_of` a separate relation; are the three `logic-*` folders and `widget` retired?** Default: yes; `last-chain` stays a shortcut with sources named. Blocks: the edges-loop folders, the widget record, `ChainNumber`, the rule's `made_request`.
10. **Does the request–connection relation live only in the request domain?** Default: yes; retire `connection/edges/request`. Blocks: the connection edge cleanup, `Field_ConnectionID` conversion of `edges/connection`.
11. **Is the connection edge optional, does it carry the stream coordinate, and does the connection domain accept a non-software channel (counter, telephone, paper)?** Default: optional; carries the coordinate; the channel generalisation is a separate question for connection. Blocks: drafts, relay, scenarios 3 and 9, `connection_to`.
12. **Is a retry a new request that refers back (`retry_of`), and is that a third kind beside X2's replaces and offsets?** Default: yes, a third relation. Blocks: the outbox, idempotency, X2's wording.
13. **Is exercised permission one evidence relation (`permitted_by`, far end a rule or a delegation), kept apart from standing permission (`condition_is`)?** Default: yes. Blocks: the three `conditional` homes, connection's `authorize_by`, X3, the unresearched delegate.
14. **Is this domain only the call occurrence, with petitions (formal asks to an authority, a join request, a payment demand, a search) owned by their domains?** Default: yes; the dictionary sentence leaves the opening. Blocks: the opening of request.md, the naming of group's `request_to_join`, transaction's wording "a request, not an operation".
15. **When a request crosses to another organization, are the two records counterparts, and does the receiver's cite the sender's as `same_as` or `caused_by`?** Default: counterparts (X7); the receiver's cites the sender's as `caused_by`. Blocks: the invoice-exchange question of 2026-10-10, follow delivery, `inbound-gateway`.
16. **Are the request memory and the node's operational audit log two things sharing a listener?** Default: yes. Blocks: the instance_of `audit-log` Khayyam shape, Memar's `Observer_RequestID` comment.
17. **Where is the operation identity (the method inside a service) recorded?** Default: on the service edge beside the service identity; the routing identity stays on the instance. Blocks: the `edges/service` data model, the four-identity decomposition's implementation.
