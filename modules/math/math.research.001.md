## Question and Goals

### Questions

The owner's framing, in his own words and his own doubt, which this record treats as the reason the inquiry was opened rather than as a claim to be evaluated:

> «به نظرم خود مفهوم math یک مفهوم چتری هست، نمیشه برای یک پروتکل و یک انتزاع که نوشت. مثلا مفهوم اعداد هم خودش یا یک انتزاع واحد شاید نشه، نیاز به بررسی داره، یک جواب بله و خیر من نیست واقعا.»

1. **Given that math is an umbrella — several protocols, several abstractions, several modules — how should Memar hold an umbrella subject at all, when "one document per concept" and "one abstraction per concept" are both rules this project states? What does the record already answer, and what does it leave open?** — Answered as to what the record holds and what it does not; the choice of holding is not this research's to make.
2. **Is there one abstraction over all numeric kinds, or one per kind, and which methods does each require?** — Answered as to what the record holds, what constrains the answer, and what would falsify each candidate. The choice between the candidates is not this research's to make.

### Goals

- Establish the exact constraint that bounds any answer to question 2, and check it against the declarations the modules actually carry, rather than against the widths as the protocol describes them — Met
- Surface every place where two documents of this repository disagree about the numeric tower or about the kinds beside it, and leave the disagreement standing rather than resolving it — Met

- Preserve the owner's doubt — that even "numbers" may not be one abstraction — as a live candidate rather than as a rhetorical doubt — Met

The source-standing rules ([`documentation.md` → Citations](../knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#citations)) were therefore never put in play, and no citation outside this repository appears below.

### Scope

It does not decide the umbrella's decomposition, does not name concepts, does not draft declarations, does not rule on whether complex numbers or platform-sized integers belong to the tower — those are ruled in [`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md#does-the-tower-hold-widths-the-reference-does-not-declare) — and does not examine any language's design of numeric abstraction except where this repository's own documents report one.

## Findings

### The record already holds one umbrella, and it holds it as a folder with a main document

Math is not an unheld case. [`docs/protocols/math/math.md`](./math.md) is the folder's main document, and the folder's `README.md` is a symbolic link to it — `modules/math/README.md` is recorded by git at mode `120000` with content `../../modules/math/math.md` (it read `../../docs/protocols/math/math.md` before the folder's rename) — which is exactly the shape [Folder READMEs](../knowledge/documentation/documentation.practice.md#folder-readmes) prescribes: "A folder that has a main document beside it takes its `README.md` as a symbolic link to that document." So the repository's answer to "how does Memar hold an umbrella" is already in use for this very subject: **the umbrella is the folder, and the folder's main document is the statement of what the folder is for and of the criterion for what belongs in it.**

That criterion is stated, in the umbrella's own main document, which is what the practice requires of a folder's main document — "move into the main document everything the README says that belongs to it — what the folder is for, and the criterion for what belongs in the folder". [`math.md` → Realization membership](./math.md#realization-membership) holds it: "A declaration belongs under `modules/math/` when its subject is a number or a truth value: a magnitude, an operation over magnitudes, a truth value, or the logic over truth values," followed by four exclusions — time spans and instants, positions among elements, memory addresses, characters and octets. [`modules/README.md`](../README.md) states the criterion one level up, for the folder itself rather than for what lives under it, and says the folder listing is the index and that the README states only the folder's role and criterion, which do not change when documents are added.

**Evidence:** `modules/math/README.md` at mode `120000`; [`documentation.practice.md`](../knowledge/documentation/documentation.practice.md) → Folder READMEs steps 1–2; [`math.md`](./math.md) → Realization membership; [`modules/README.md`](../README.md) → Membership criterion.

### The two rules the owner's doubt presses against are rules about naming and addressing, not about how much one document may hold

"One document per model" is stated in [`modeling.md`](../../docs/modeling.md) → Expected Output of a Modeling Session — a session "ends by writing the documents that understanding produced … one document per model, each placed and named under [Documentation → File Naming]" — and the naming rule itself says a document is "named after the concept the model is about," that "a folder holding that document is named for the responsibility whose model it is, and the document takes that same name beside it," and that this rule "is for addressing." It also records that 41 of 115 base documents already carry their own folder's name. Read as written, the rule says a document's *name* is its concept; it does not say a document may hold one concept and no other, and the record does not anywhere state a one-concept-per-document limit.

The naming rule's separator table already encodes the umbrella-to-part edge the owner asks about. Under [File Naming](../knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#file-naming), "**Folder, or hyphen (`-`)** — the structural boundary between category and topic. The folder carries it where a category has enough documents to own a directory … the hyphen carries it where documents sit beside one another in one directory … The left side names the namespace, the right side the subject." `math/` is the namespace and `math.md` is the subject beside it, which is the same sentence that would place a document on one part of the umbrella as a sibling of `math.md` in the same folder.

**Evidence:** [`modeling.md`](../../docs/modeling.md) → Expected Output of a Modeling Session; [`documentation.md`](../knowledge/documentation/documentation.md) → File Naming.

### The record holds umbrella shapes of both sizes and states no rule that discriminates between them

A scan of `docs/protocols/*/` for base documents (excluding `README.md` and any companion matching `\.(handoff|changelog|practice|research)[0-9]*\.md`) returns: `codec` 2, `computer` 8, `gui` 1, `identifier` 1, `math` 1, `memory` 2, `modules` 2, `net` 8, `process` 4, `runtime` 1, `time` 1. Five of the eleven folders hold exactly one base document and four of them hold more than one. A one-document folder and an eight-document folder are therefore both shapes this repository uses, and **no document read in this inquiry states any criterion at which a folder's parts deserve documents of their own** — [`modules/README.md`](../README.md)'s criterion is per-document ("specifies, or is expected to grow into the specification of, one of Memar's own protocols"), and [`math.md`](./math.md)'s is per-declaration under `modules/`. Neither is a rule about when one document must become several, and that silence is the record's answer to half of question 1: it is an absence.

**Evidence:** the scan stated above; [`modules/README.md`](../README.md) → Membership criterion; [`math.md`](./math.md) → Realization membership.

### Modularity's tests apply to the umbrella question and are not applied anywhere in the record

Two tests in [`modularity.md`](../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md) bear directly on question 1, and neither is applied to `math` anywhere in this repository.

[Foundational Concepts Should Remain Few](../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#foundational-concepts-should-remain-few) states that "the first hypothesis for an apparently new domain should therefore be that it names a capability — a relationship, Rule, or independently evolving Module — over Concepts that already exist," and that only when a requirement demonstrably cannot be expressed that way does a candidate foundational Concept earn examination. [What Earns Foundational Status](../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status) gives the test: attempt to express the candidate through the remaining abstractions; "If the result forces the fabrication of a new foundational abstraction, or a distortion of an existing one, the candidate has earned consideration for foundational status." The test runs "against requirements that actually exist, not anticipated ones," and its own text concedes that "borderline candidates … can be argued either way by motivated reasoning" and that it "does not replace the judgment at its center."

Applied to this inquiry's two candidates, the test is well-formed and unrun: a candidate `Number` is testable against the four recorded exception signatures ([below](#the-four-recorded-exceptions-are-three-hash-widths-and-one-decimal-width-and-they-are-not-the-same-case)), and a candidate decomposition of the `math` umbrella is testable against the exclusions in [Realization membership](./math.md#realization-membership). [Capability Completeness](../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#capability-completeness) cuts the other way and is worth stating because it is a warning against the obvious move: a Module owning a relationship "owns the complete capability that makes that relationship meaningful," completeness "pulls against minimality," and it "can be misused to justify swallowing neighboring responsibilities wholesale." Splitting the numeric tower away from the truth-value and logic side of `math.md` would divide one Responsibility across two boundaries *if* truth values belong to numeric magnitude — and the record has already decided that they do, by putting `Boolean` (`modules/math/boolean/protocol/boolean.kh`) and the logic protocols inside `modules/math/` and by [`math.md`'s own sentence](./math.md#the-number-tower) that "Truth values follow the same split." So the umbrella's outer boundary is adjudicated in one direction, and what is not adjudicated is whether `math` is one subject or several inside it.

**Evidence:** [`modularity.md`](../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md) → Foundational Concepts Should Remain Few, → What Earns Foundational Status, → Capability Completeness; [`math.md`](./math.md) → The number tower, → Realization membership.

### The umbrella is addressed and bounded, but it is not a named thing — and the record knows the gap by name

A System's [Boundary](../../docs/system.md#key-properties) "is the point at which the criteria for system membership change," and "the boundary is an analytical choice made by the observer, not an inherent property of the system's material." That is exactly what `math.md` → Realization membership does: it states the criterion at which membership changes, in the umbrella's own document. What the umbrella is *not* is a concept this repository holds. [`system.md`](../../docs/system.md) → Structure Is Not the Arrangement of Parts says so in its own words: "Memar does not yet have a dedicated, formalized term for 'the arrangement of a system's parts' as a first-class concept (candidate names, and whether it warrants a concept of its own, are left open)," and it records that no count of parts decides anything and that a description of parts is not a description of capability or constraint. An umbrella subject is precisely an arrangement of parts — a namespace whose members are peers under one membership criterion — and the repository holds that arrangement as a **convention** (folder, main document, membership criterion, symbolic-link README) while holding no concept for it.

There is a second boundary the umbrella must respect and the record states it: [Citation direction](../README.md) runs "from the protocol document *down* to the base document — never back up," and a base document that must name a concern whose working-out is owned by the protocol layer "names that ownership without a link." So an umbrella that splits into several protocol documents cannot become an authority a base concept document links to, and whichever document holds the umbrella's own statement is not the one a base document may cite for a principle. This constrains every decomposition of the umbrella equally, and it is not a constraint the owner's framing mentions.

**Evidence:** [`system.md`](../../docs/system.md) → Key Properties (Boundary), → Structure Is Not the Arrangement of Parts; [`modules/README.md`](../README.md) → Citation direction.

### Nothing in the tree declares a numeric concept but one, and the one that exists requires string conversion

Of the numeric concept names in this repository, exactly one is realized as a Khayyam declaration: `Signed` (`modules/math/integer/protocol/signed.kh`) at `:8`, an abstraction carrying three methods — `Signed_Equal (self Signed) (with Signed) (b Boolean)`, `ToString (self Signed) () (str String, err Error)`, and `FromString (self Signed) (str String) (err Error)`. Every other candidate is residue inside a commented migration block and carries no declaration at all: `Number` (`modules/math/protocol/number.kh:9-11`, `Integer | Float`), `Integer` (`modules/math/integer/protocol/integer.kh:9-11`, `Signed | Unsigned`), `Unsigned` (`modules/math/integer/protocol/unsigned.kh:10`), `Float` (`modules/math/protocol/float.kh`), `Complex` (`modules/math/protocol/complex.kh`), `Ordered` (`modules/math/protocol/ordered.kh:9-11`, `Integer | Float | ~string`). **These are not a candidate position.** `bundles/khayyam/targets/js/category-parameter.md` records that the owner has ruled the code under `modules/**` neither evidence nor a premise, and that the monomorphised naming around those comments is likewise not evidence about what this repository needs; the paragraph carrying that inference is marked withdrawn. What remains is a description of the tree: those files carry a Go constraint union and nothing else.

The realized one carries a method set that no document states any numeric concept must have. `ToString` and `FromString` are **serialization**, not numeric properties — `Signed` requires that a signed integer of any width be convertible to and from a `String`, which is a capability chosen for this one abstraction and required of nothing else. It is the only precedent the record holds for "which methods does a numeric concept require," and on its face it is a poor template: a concept whose method set includes text conversion cannot be satisfied by a numeric magnitude that declines to parse text. This is the sharpest thing the record says against copying `Signed`'s shape to `Whole` and `Real`, and it is the inquiry's own reading of the declaration rather than a claim any document makes.

**Evidence:** `modules/math/integer/protocol/signed.kh` at `:8`, `:10`, `:13`, `:15`; the six residue files at the lines given; `bundles/khayyam/targets/js/category-parameter.md` → What the sources already assume, and what they work around.

### The binding constraint is exact, and it is the constraint the handoff already names

`docs/khayyam/abstraction.md` → What You Cannot Do states: "You **cannot** use a capsule type as an argument or return type in an abstraction's method signature — only other abstractions are allowed, ensuring the specification remains implementation-agnostic." → A Concept Is an Abstraction; a Concrete Instance Is a Capsule makes the same rule decide which of the two a signature names: "A signature owned by an abstraction names the concept … because it may name abstractions only," while "A signature owned by a capsule may name a capsule, including the capsule itself."

Stated as the handoff states it — "A shared method set can hold only methods whose types are abstractions, so equivalence cannot be among them as the widths state it" ([`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md#is-there-a-common-abstraction-over-all-numeric-kinds)) — the consequence is that a shared numeric method set may hold only methods every one of whose influencing and influenced variables is an abstraction. `Signed` demonstrates the shape such a set would have, and demonstrates its cost at the same time: `Signed_Equal` takes `(with Signed)` and returns `Boolean`, because `Bool` is a capsule and a signature owned by an abstraction may not name one. Every width's own equality is capsule-typed on both sides — `W8_Equal (self W8) (with W8) (b Bool)` at `modules/math/integer/unsigned-8.kh:10-11`, `I16_Equal (self I16) (with I16) (b Bool)` at `modules/math/integer/signed-16.kh:10`, `R32_Equal` at `modules/math/float/float-32.kh:11`, `R64_Equal` at `modules/math/float/float-64.kh:11` — so none of them can be lifted into a shared set unchanged, and a shared set that includes equality at all must include a method no width declares today.

**Evidence:** `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md` → What You Cannot Do, → A Concept Is an Abstraction; a Concrete Instance Is a Capsule; the four width declarations at the lines given; [`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md#is-there-a-common-abstraction-over-all-numeric-kinds).

### The tree's de facto answer is already per-kind, and contracts outside `math` have been written on it

A scan of `modules/**/*.kh` for the token `Signed` returns contracts in three other domains that name the concept rather than a width: `GUI_Screen_ID`, `Height`, `Width`, `AvailHeight`, `AvailWidth`, `UpdateRate`, `PixelDepth`, `ColorDepth` at `modules-plugable/computer/ui/gui/protocol/screen.kh` `:11`–`:30`; `ScrollWidth`, `ScrollHeight`, `ScrollTop`, `ScrollLeft` at `modules-plugable/computer/ui/gui/protocol/scroll.kh` `:17`–`:26`; `GUI_Element_NthChild` at `modules-plugable/computer/ui/gui/protocol/element.kh` `:52`; and `BlockSize` at `modules/computer/crypto/protocol/cipher.kh` `:21`.

This is the concrete cost the handoff names, made visible: **a screen's width and height are declared as `Signed`**, and so is a scroll offset and a hash block size. Each of those magnitudes is numeric and none of them is stated to be an integer. So the port already answered question 2 in practice — one concept per kind, and where a contract wanted "a number" it named the kind it meant — and it did so without a document saying that was the rule. Whether a width or a scroll offset *is* a signed integer, or a magnitude whose kind the contract should have left open, is exactly the owner's question, and it has been answered in the sources by default rather than by ruling.

Two qualifications keep this from being a finding that `Number` is unnecessary. First, the same scan shows the constraint biting elsewhere: `modules-plugable/computer/ui/gui/protocol/scroll.kh` `:46` carries an unresolved TODO recording that an abstraction's methods "use abstractions, not capsules, as arguments" and asking whether a Go struct parameter "becomes an `ab` or the signature changes" — the same rule, in a document that is not about math, unresolved. Second, `docs/khayyam/polymorphism.md` states the documented way to be generic, and it is not a union: "Khayyam rejects generic **syntax**, not generic **capability**," because "a method accepting an abstraction type is, in effect, a universally quantified function over all types satisfying that abstraction," and under → Domain-Specific Containers, "Where a method needs to accept any implementer of a shared behavior, it refers to the abstraction directly (e.g. `Index(el Element)` rather than `Index[T](el T)`)." **The base layer provides a spelling for "any implementer of a shared behavior" and none for "any of these kinds"** — which is the whole of question 2's shape, stated as the record states it.

**Evidence:** the scan stated above; `modules-plugable/computer/ui/gui/protocol/screen.kh`, `modules-plugable/computer/ui/gui/protocol/scroll.kh`, `modules-plugable/computer/ui/gui/protocol/element.kh`, `modules/computer/crypto/protocol/cipher.kh` at the lines given; `docs/khayyam/polymorphism.md` → Generic Syntax vs Generic Capability, → Domain-Specific Containers.

### A category or union parameter is not merely undocumented — it is measured as unspeakable today

If a candidate answer to question 2 is "one abstraction, and let its methods take a category rather than a type," then the answer needs a spelling the language does not have. `bundles/khayyam/targets/js/category-parameter.md` measured this with programs written for the purpose and reports the result: a signature naming `sc`, `ab`, `mt`, `cp`, `in`, or `tp` in a type position is refused by the frontend with the label `keyword-as-identifier` — the same label a mistyped keyword earns, so "a category is unspeakable rather than merely unstated" — and a parameter's type is carried as a bare `string` in the semantic representation, so admitting one "is a change to the representation and not a parser fix." Its own summary is that a signature "may name nothing but a declared type's name today, in every position, and not by omission."

Two things follow for question 2, and they are this inquiry's own inference over that measurement. A `Number`-shaped abstraction whose methods quantify over *kinds* is blocked by the language and the toolchain, not by an unwritten document — so it is not a candidate the owner can adopt by ruling alone; it needs the change that document costed in three files under `core/` plus the representation change. And a `Number`-shaped abstraction whose methods quantify over *the abstraction itself* — the polymorphism.md shape — is expressible today at no cost, which is the option the same document records as the owner's recorded preference for the neighbouring `sc` case. This research does not choose between them; it records that one is blocked and one is not.

**Evidence:** `bundles/khayyam/targets/js/category-parameter.md` → Every place a signature may name something, → What the grammar and the frontend do with what a signature names, → The options (α), → Implications. Not re-measured here.

### The four recorded exceptions are three hash widths and one decimal width, and they are not the same case

[`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md#is-there-a-common-abstraction-over-all-numeric-kinds) records four abstraction-owned signatures that still name a capsule, as "the recorded exception." Three are confirmed and are one case: `Hash16_Sum16 () (w W16)`, `Hash32_Sum32 () (w W32)`, and `Hash64_Sum64 () (w W64)` in `modules/computer/crypto/hash/protocol/hash.kh` `:42`, `:47`, `:52`, owned by the empty abstractions `Hash16`, `Hash32`, `Hash64` at `:40`, `:45`, `:50`. The fourth is a different shape: `Decimal mt (self Math_Decimal) () (w W64)` at `modules/math/decimal/protocol/decimal.kh` `:14`, where the abstraction `Math_Decimal` also composes `Operations_Math_Decimal` and returns `Signed` from `Integer` at `:12` — so one abstraction already mixes a concept-typed and a width-typed return, and its own arithmetic requirement is stated through the per-type pair `Operations_Math_Decimal` (`modules/math/protocol/operations.kh` `:13`–`:22`), not through a numeric concept.

The distinction matters to the decision, because the two cases want different fixes. A hash sum's width is part of *what that hash is* — a `Hash16` produces sixteen bits and a `Hash32` produces thirty-two — so retargeting `Sum16` to a `Whole` would state that any whole satisfies the position, which may be true and is not obviously what the contract means. `Decimal`'s `(w W64)` carries no such meaning: `W64` there is a *representation* of a decimal's fractional part, and the concept that would replace it is a decimal, not a whole. One case is a width that is the answer; the other is a width that is an accident of representation. The handoff lists them as four instances of one thing, and **they are not four instances of one thing** — which is a disagreement between the handoff's framing and the declarations it cites, reported rather than resolved.

**Evidence:** `modules/computer/crypto/hash/protocol/hash.kh` at `:40`–`:52`; `modules/math/decimal/protocol/decimal.kh` at `:9`–`:14`; `modules/math/protocol/operations.kh` at `:13`–`:22`; [`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md#is-there-a-common-abstraction-over-all-numeric-kinds).

### The record contradicts itself about whether a width's `Equal` realizes the logic module's `Equivalence`

This disagreement is between an owner ruling and the tree, and it bears on question 2 because a shared numeric method set must decide what comparison means.

`docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md` → Primitive Capsule Specification states it in one sentence: "Value equality and logical equivalence are two operations and keep two names. A width's `Equal` is value equality." [`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md#does-bool-satisfy-boolean) records the owner's ruling of 2026-09-28 to the same effect — "equal is not equivalence … The widths' `Equal` is value equality, a different operation, and keeps its name; **the answer does not reach the widths**."

The tree has not been updated to that. `W8_Equal` carries `//memar:impl memar/math/logic/protocol.Equivalence` at `modules/math/integer/unsigned-8.kh` `:10`; so do `R32_Equal` at `modules/math/float/float-32.kh` `:10` and `R64_Equal` at `modules/math/float/float-64.kh` `:10`; and `Signed_Equal` carries it at `modules/math/integer/protocol/signed.kh` `:9`, where it sits on an abstraction-owned method whose parameter is `Signed` and whose result is `Boolean` — a signature that could not satisfy the logic protocol's method even if the marker were meant. `I16_Equal` at `modules/math/integer/signed-16.kh` `:10` carries no marker at all, so the tree is also inconsistent with itself.

The target of the marker cannot check anything today either: `Logic_Equivalence` is declared at `modules/math/logic/protocol/equivalence.kh` `:6` as an abstraction with **no methods**, so an `Equal` claiming to realize it satisfies a requirement set that requires nothing. So the marker is, at present, unenforceable in both directions, and three widths plus one abstraction assert a relationship the base layer's own sentence denies. This record does not resolve it — it is the owner's ruling that stands against the tree, and the tree is what a later session would build from.

**Evidence:** `docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md` → Primitive Capsule Specification; [`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md#does-bool-satisfy-boolean); the five declarations cited above; `modules/math/logic/protocol/equivalence.kh` `:6`; `docs/khayyam/abstraction.md` → Abstraction Realization (Implicit Satisfaction), on exact signature matching.

### A shared method set would require behavior no document has settled, and the record says so twice

The handoff's second sub-question is confirmed and is not merely a dependency — it is a dependency on a question that is itself open. `docs/khayyam/encapsulation.handoff.md#what-behavioral-guarantees-do-primitive-capsules-provide` carries three unanswered items: "What behavioral guarantees does each primitive capsule provide? Is there a formal specification for `W32`, `W64`, `R32`, `R64`, and other primitive capsules?"; "Should primitive capsules be defined by the language itself, by the standard library, or by the Memar framework?"; and "How do primitive capsule guarantees interact with the compiler's optimization strategy — can a `W32` with wrapping semantics be optimized differently from a `W32` with saturating semantics?"

`docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md` adds the statement that closes the door on assuming otherwise: "none are guaranteed by this document — they are illustrative ('possible'), not mandatory," and "this is also why 'zero-cost' cannot be asserted for `I32` as a category." So any method a shared numeric abstraction would require — ordering, arithmetic, conversion between kinds — would have to state behavior that no document states and that the base layer explicitly declines to state. A shared method set is therefore not only a naming decision; under the record as it stands it is a decision to specify behavior first.

**Evidence:** `docs/khayyam/encapsulation.handoff.md#what-behavioral-guarantees-do-primitive-capsules-provide`; `docs/khayyam/encapsulation.md` → Primitive Capsule Specification.

### Two values whose bits are numeric are excluded from the tower by concept, which bears on whether "numbers" is one abstraction

The tower excludes complex numbers outright — [`math.md`](./math.md) → The number tower: "A complex number is an algebraic concept, not a width; no primitive capsule is a complex width" — and [`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md#does-the-tower-hold-widths-the-reference-does-not-declare) records the owner's ruling (b): "Complex numbers are an algebraic concept, not a width. A module for them may come later; the tower gains no complex width." An address-sized integer is excluded by domain: [`memory.md`](../computer/memory/memory.md#address-sized-integers-are-not-numeric-widths) — "An address-sized integer belongs to the memory domain, not to the numeric tower. No math capsule is declared for it." An octet and a character are excluded by concept: [`characters-codec.md`](../computer/codec/characters-codec/characters-codec.md#characters-and-octets-are-not-numeric-widths) — "an octet — an encoding unit — is a different concept from an unsigned 8-bit width even where the bits are the same, because different meanings are different types," and the character concept is `String_Character` with each encoding declaring its own capsule.

Three cases, one shape: **a value can be numeric in its representation and not a number in its concept, and the record answers each of them the same way — by not putting it under the numeric concepts at all.** That is the record's own precedent for the owner's doubt, applied twice already. It is not evidence that `Number` should not exist, and this record does not read it as such: an octet is excluded because a *different* concept claims it, which is a claim about ownership, whereas a magnitude claimed by no other concept is in a different position. But it does establish that "numeric in its bits" and "a number" are already two different things in this repository, and that the tower's boundary is drawn on the concept rather than on the representation — which any `Number` abstraction would have to honour.

**Evidence:** the four documents at the sections named.

### The analogous umbrella question in the durations was answered the other way, and no document says why

[`time.handoff.md`](../time/time.handoff.md#is-there-a-common-concept-over-the-duration-units) is the handoff's own named counterpart: "`Duration` is the concept the unit instances share (`Second`, `NanoSecond`, and `NanoInSecond` under `modules/time/duration/`), and it is what every contract names today, so nothing is blocked by the absence of a narrower one; what is missing is a way for a contract to state a *resolution*." So over a family of units this repository holds **one** abstraction with many instances, where over numeric kinds it holds **one abstraction per kind** with no umbrella. The record states no reason for the difference, and the two cases are not symmetric: units of one quantity are instances of one concept, whereas widths of different number systems are, by [`modularity.md`](../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md) → One Term, One Identity, arguably different concepts with different Responsibilities. Whether that argument extends to numbers is the decision, and it is the owner's.

**Evidence:** [`time/time.handoff.md`](../time/time.handoff.md#is-there-a-common-concept-over-the-duration-units); [`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md#is-there-a-common-abstraction-over-all-numeric-kinds) names it as the counterpart; [`modularity.md`](../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md) → One Term, One Identity.

### The folder's membership rule and the folder's contents disagree, in three places

[`math.md`](./math.md) → Realization membership excludes "a position among elements, which is a container's concept," pointing at `modules/computer/adt/`. The folder holds one anyway: `modules/math/integer/adt-container-index.kh` declares `tp ElementIndex ab` at `:5`, commented "ElementIndex can refer to any location of memory blocks in byte or 8bit number" — an index, under `math`, excluded by the rule that names indexes as another domain's.

Two further observations, which this record states as its own reading of declarations rather than as a claim any document makes. The `per` family declares ratios as concepts — `Billion ab` at `modules/math/per/billion.kh` `:4`, and the same shape for `ten`, `million`, `billion`, `trillion`, `quadrillion`, `myriad`, `mille`, `cent-mille`, `cent` — which fits "a magnitude" in the membership rule and does not fit "a numeric width is named by its number system and its width in bits" in the tower; the two sections of the same document are not obviously in agreement about whether a per-million is a number. And `modules/math/logic/conditional.kh` declares `tp Conditional ab` at `:5` while the Go residue beneath it declares `type Conditional uint64` — a declaration and its own residue disagreeing about the category of the same name.

None of the three changes what `math` is. All three are instances of the owner's first question inside the umbrella they are asking about: **the membership criterion exists and the folder does not conform to it in every particular, and nothing in the record says which of the two governs when they differ.**

**Evidence:** [`math.md`](./math.md) → Realization membership, → The number tower; `modules/math/integer/adt-container-index.kh` `:3`–`:5`; `modules/math/per/billion.kh` `:4`; `modules/math/logic/conditional.kh` `:5`.

## Open Questions

Each of these is the owner's, and none is answered here. While this record is `Active` they live here; when it completes they belong in [`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md), which this session is not to edit.

- **Is `math` one subject or several, and if several, where does the boundary fall** — between the tower and the truth-value/logic side the record has already joined, or between the numeric widths and their operations, or between `decimal`, `per`, `quantum`, and `errors` and the tower? What settles it: applying [What Earns Foundational Status](../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status) to each candidate and observing whether a part can be expressed without fabricating or bending a foundational concept. What would falsify the *decomposition* candidate: a part that cannot be stated without naming the whole — and [`ElementIndex`](#the-folders-membership-rule-and-the-folders-contents-disagree-in-three-places) is already a candidate, since its comment ties it to "byte or 8bit number" while its kind is a container's.
- **Does an umbrella document have a defined relation to documents on its parts** — parent, peer, or index — and does the record need to say so at all? What settles it: whether a reader arriving at `math.md` needs to be told that other documents in the folder exist, which [Folder READMEs](../knowledge/documentation/documentation.practice.md#folder-readmes) step 2 already treats as what a main document owns ("where a reader goes next") without saying whether a *part's* document is "next" or a peer.
- **Is one abstraction over all numeric kinds wanted, one per kind, or neither** — and is "numbers" one concept at all? What settles it: the first contract that must genuinely span kinds and cannot be written either way. The record supplies four candidates and no winner: the three hash sums whose width is the answer, `Decimal`'s `(w W64)` whose width is a representation, `GUI_Screen_Width () (s Signed)` whose kind may be wrong, and `scroll.kh`'s unresolved parameter.
- **Which methods a numeric concept requires**, given that the record's one precedent requires text conversion. What settles it: the owner's ruling on whether a concept may require a capability a legitimate magnitude declines to have — and if it may not, `Signed` itself is a candidate for revision, which no document asks for.
- **Whether `Equal` on a width realizes the logic module's `Equivalence`** — the tree says yes for four declarations and the base layer's own sentence says no. What settles it: whether `Logic_Equivalence` is to carry methods; while it carries none, the marker asserts nothing and the disagreement stays invisible to every check this repository has.
- **Whether a width's behavioral guarantees are settled enough to hang a shared method set on** — open in `docs/khayyam/encapsulation.handoff.md#what-behavioral-guarantees-do-primitive-capsules-provide`, and the precondition of the numeric answer rather than a side note to it.
- **Whether the membership criterion governs the folder or the folder governs the criterion**, given the three disagreements above. What settles it: an owner ruling on precedence; nothing in the record ranks them.

## Conclusion

**Question 1 is answered as to the record, and the record's answer is a convention rather than a concept.** Memar already holds this umbrella: `modules/math/` is the namespace (it was `docs/modules/math/` when the record was written), `math.md` is its main document and the folder's `README.md` is a symbolic link to it, the criterion for what belongs is stated in that main document, and `documentation.md` → File Naming already provides the separator that puts a document on one part beside it. What the record does **not** hold is a named concept of an umbrella, a rule for when a folder's parts deserve documents of their own — five of eleven folders held one base document and four held more, at the scan, with no criterion stated — or any defined relation between an umbrella document and documents on its parts. Modularity's two tests are well-formed for this question and applied nowhere; System's own text records that "the arrangement of a system's parts" has no formalized term in this repository yet. The citation-direction rule constrains every decomposition equally and is a constraint the owner's framing does not mention. **Nothing changed in the repository as a result of this research, and no changelog entry is owed to [`math.md`](./math.md).**

**Question 2 is answered as to the record, and what the record holds is a constraint, a de facto practice, and three disagreements — not an answer.** The binding constraint is exact: a signature owned by an abstraction may name abstractions only, so a shared method set can hold only abstraction-typed methods, and every width's equality is capsule-typed on both sides. The base layer supplies a spelling for "any implementer of a shared behavior" — name the abstraction — and none for "any of these kinds"; a category or union parameter was measured as unspeakable today, needing a semantic-representation change and not merely a ruling. The tree has already answered in practice: one concept per kind, with `Signed` the only realized numeric abstraction, and contracts in three other domains naming `Signed` for magnitudes they never said were integers — a screen's width and height among them. What the record does **not** hold is a decision, and this research does not supply one.

**The owner's doubt survives the record as a live candidate and this research does not argue it down.** Three values whose bits are numeric — a complex number, an address-sized integer, an octet — are already excluded from the tower by concept rather than by representation, which establishes that "numeric in its bits" and "a number" are two different things here. That is not the same as showing that a magnitude claimed by no other concept is not one concept, and the difference is where the decision lives.

**Three disagreements are left standing** rather than resolved: the handoff's four recorded exceptions are two cases and not one; four declarations claim a width's `Equal` realizes a logic method the base layer's own sentence separates from it, while a fifth declares the same operation with no such claim; and the folder's membership criterion excludes indexes and does not hold. Each is recorded above with both sides quoted.

**Nothing graduated.** No document was changed by this research, so there is no revision and no changelog entry for [`math.md`](./math.md) or [`math.handoff.md`](./math.handoff.md) to carry. The seven open questions above are the handoff's to receive when this record closes, and this session did not edit the handoff.
