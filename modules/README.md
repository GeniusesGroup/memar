# Modules (`modules/`)

## What this layer is
Memar's modules: each a bounded responsibility of the systems Memar builds, in the sense of [Modularity → Module as a System](../docs/modularity.md#module-as-a-system). A module's folder holds the module's specification and its realization together. The main document, named for the module, states chiefly the module's protocol — the declarative rules Memar owns for the process the module is responsible for, and expects systems built with it to conform to — and binds every implementation, whatever language it is written in; its companions (changelog, handoff, practice, research) sit beside it, and a folder's `README.md` is a link to the main document where there is one. The realizations are the protocol implementations and the Khayyam library sources (`.kh`) beside it. Every module here is reusable, and every module here is placed as kernel: the optional capabilities that attach to these modules stand in [`modules-plugable/`](../modules-plugable/README.md), and [Classification](knowledge/classification/classification.md) states both placements; what runs or aggregates modules — a server, an executable, a language's toolchain — is a bundle and lives under [`bundles/`](../bundles/README.md). A module's document answers "what does *Memar's* module for X require?", not "what is X?": the general concepts are the documents of [`docs/`](../docs/README.md), [Protocol](../docs/protocol.md) among them. Within this layer, placement follows the responsibility a folder names, and each module states its own membership criterion in its own main document.

## Membership criterion
A document belongs here when its subject is one of Memar's own modules and it specifies, or is expected to grow into the specification of, that module's protocol — requirements whose conformance a party that did not write them can decide from a stated input ([Protocol → Protocol Properties](../docs/protocol.md#protocol-properties)), whether they take the form of a contract, a wire or encoding format, or a rule set any language or stack can realize. Documents enter at whatever [Status](knowledge/documentation/explanation/explanation.md#status) they carry, including Draft, and are developed in place toward their full protocol form: a Draft that currently records the *why* and the open questions is expected to mature until it states the protocol itself. What an organization may hold a different view on, and what the OS leaves to the system above it, are stated in the documents themselves as parts of the protocols.

A document stating **Memar's position on something whose authority lies outside Memar** — a protocol surface Memar conforms to and may realize, or a design name that circulates with senses Memar does not inherit — belongs in the module of that surface, which is a [pluggable](./knowledge/classification/primitive/pluggable.md) module under [`modules-plugable/`](../modules-plugable/README.md) carrying the label [`guest`](./knowledge/classification/primitive/guest.md).

A citation runs down the layers of the written content, and a link that runs up is a debt kept until its text is upgraded, never deleted to comply, as [Documentation → Citation Direction](knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#citation-direction) states once for every layer.

## Where implementations live
A module's specification is the source of truth for the module and is not executable code; the declarations and sources that realize it sit beside it in the module's folder, and an agent that needs running code goes to the bundles that run or aggregate modules (`bundles/`, [`bundles/README.md`](../bundles/README.md)) or to the realization folder, and opens a specification only when the contract itself is the subject.

Implementing Memar's modules is in no way limited to Khayyam. Khayyam is Memar's official language, and a specification does not retype its module's code: it refers to its module's Khayyam declarations, and keeps only illustrative examples ([Documentation → A Fact in Code Is Written Once](knowledge/documentation/documentation.md#a-fact-in-code-is-written-once)), and every module can be carried into any language, though without Khayyam's efficiency ([Framework → Khayyam and Other Languages](../docs/framework.md#khayyam-and-other-languages)). A specification binds every realization whatever its language; inside one module folder, its role folders show which file binds every realization and which is one language's. The repository hosts the current Khayyam toolchain plan and module material as one local realization, alongside any implementation modules a project needs for its own realization. The language used by a realization is chosen where that system is implemented; the module's meaning remains stable across realizations.

## Why a folder may exist before it holds code
A folder here is a carrier of thinking, not a container waiting for code. Much of this project's design reasoning lived in folder names long before it lived in documents; where the folders were absent, those ideas went unevaluated and the work fell into the ecosystem's general patterns by default — the outcome this project's own principles reject. A folder created ahead of its content keeps a decided direction visible and gives each idea a place to be argued about.

Three rules follow, and they are decisions rather than preferences:
- **An empty folder is a decision made visible.** Do not delete one for being empty; restructure it only with an argument.
- **No folder here is final.** Names may be merged, split, moved, or retired whenever the responsibility they name is better served another way.
- **A folder without a README carries its intent in its name** until one is written; a README is what makes the membership criterion inspectable.

## The kinds of folder here
- **By responsibility** — the categories under `modules/` and under [`modules-plugable/`](../modules-plugable/README.md), one level each, named for the subject they hold and not for a mechanism ([Classification → Category](knowledge/classification/primitive/category.md)). `knowledge/` holds the modules of knowledge: content, documentation and its facets, labels, and how modules are classified. A category is itself a module: the modularity principle accepts one concept, or several concepts that need grouping, as a module, so a category is not held to a stricter shape than the modules inside it. A module may carry a second level that is itself a set of ideas, its own facets or parts (for example `process/rule/` and `process/rules-engine/` — a rule, and the general mechanism that executes rules by their stated conditions). Where a module stands is decided by [Classification](knowledge/classification/classification.md): kernel here, pluggable under `modules-plugable/`, a composition under `bundles/`, and labels in the module's manifest.
- **A record that runs nothing** — the Go target's [research record](../bundles/khayyam/targets/go/target.research.001.md) holds the port's residue and where two archives disagree; it is a record, not a category.
- **By mechanism, language-independent** — `computer/lexer/` and the general rule engine are shared: they must not import a language's knowledge, and a language keeps only its own model beside its toolchain ([khayyam → Membership criterion](../bundles/khayyam/README.md)).
- **By role inside a module** — a module that carries more than one kind of work groups them, so that the module's root stays a list of kinds rather than a list of instances. The roles in use are `protocol/`, `primitive/`, `rules/`, `errors/`, `tests/`, `services/`, `docs/`, and `temp/`; what each holds, and what it does not, is declared once in [`Role`](./computer/module/services/folders.py), which `memar module folders --help` prints. The list is not closed: a kind of work that recurs across modules becomes a role here once it is named. A role folder is named for the kind it holds rather than for the instance inside it: one service is `services/<name>/`, and a module with a single service still groups it, because the root is read as a list of kinds and an instance standing in a kind's place is what makes a root stop being one. A role folder carries the role's name exactly, with no prefix or suffix — not `test/` for `tests/`, not `services____/` for `services/` — because the name is how a reader and a tool find the role, and a padded name hides it from both; a folder a tool must not read is kept from it by that tool's own configuration, not by renaming. A folder that shares a role's name is not always that role: [`process/test/`](./process/test/) and [`process/error/`](./process/error/) are modules that develop the Test and Error concepts, each with role folders of its own, and a folder is read as a role only where it sits inside the module whose work it groups.

## The sources, and what they are not
This repository's Khayyam source files — the `.kh` tree under `modules/**`, and the port's files the Memar server still carries under [`bundles/memar/`](../bundles/memar/memar.md) — are called **the sources**. They were produced by porting an existing Go implementation into Khayyam ([the Go target's research record](../bundles/khayyam/targets/go/target.research.001.md) records that work and what it left behind), and they were never written in the language's final syntax. They are a record of that conversion, kept as record: the owner has ruled them neither evidence nor a premise, so no claim in this repository rests on what they happen to contain, and a measurement over them describes the sources rather than the language. The word the sources replaced — *corpus* — was left to its ordinary sense, which is a body of written text.

The name says what the files are and nothing more. What the sources are *for* is a separate question, and it is not answered here or anywhere by their name.

## What does not belong here
- **Concept documents and general claims** — those live in the repository's [`docs/`](../docs/); a module's document applies them and does not restate them. A concept document stands beside the module, or the category, of its subject: [Content](knowledge/content/content.md), [Knowledge](knowledge/knowledge.md), [Process](process/process.md) and [Rule](process/rule/rule.md) have moved, and a document whose subject has no module yet, such as [Type](../docs/type.md), stays in `docs/` until it has one. The owner expects most of the base documents to move this way, and a question about whether `docs/` should hold any is open in [Classification's handoff](knowledge/classification/classification.handoff.md#do-the-base-documents-of-docs-move-to-their-modules). The Khayyam language's own design and specification lives in [`docs/khayyam/`](../docs/khayyam/README.md); the modules are what Khayyam conforms to, not the language itself.
- **What runs or aggregates modules** — servers, executables, and toolchains, the Khayyam toolchain and the Memar server among them, are bundles ([`bundles/`](../bundles/README.md)).
- **Generated output** — compiled artifacts and caches live in git-ignored `build/` folders.
- **A language-specific dependency inside a shared mechanism** — a language-agnostic implementation that acquires Khayyam knowledge stops being language-agnostic.
