   - *Edge / loop-edge / shortcut* — [Modules → Edge buckets](../../modules.md#edge-buckets) and [Modeling → Edge Types](../../../docs/modeling.md#edge-types-and-their-traditional-counterparts): an edge joins two distinct nodes; a loop-edge names "a candidate or established classification of the node it is attached to"; a shortcut is a derived cache and "never the source of truth".
   - *No bare edge* — [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement).
   - *Acquired and discovered* — [Acquired Data vs. Discovered Data](../../../docs/modeling.md#acquired-data-vs-discovered-data): acquired is stated by an external source and cannot be recomputed; discovered is derived and "should not automatically become a stored concept".
   - *Event* — Memar's [Event](../../process/event/event.md#what-an-event-is): an abstraction declaring that an occurred fact of a kind exists; "a runtime occurrence and is not a stored record"; the capability to subscribe is `EventTarget`, owned by the Type or Module exposing it ([Modularity → Event as a Module Capability](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)). **In this document "event" always means that.** What the timeline domain stores is called a *placement* (below).
   - *Instant and ordering* — [Time → The four separable concerns](../../time/time.md#the-four-separable-concerns): an instant is a point on a shared reference timeline; its contract is comparison and ordering; "a term naming causality, simultaneity across distributed components, or a monotonicity guarantee is not a term this contract carries".
   - *Timeline* — **no Memar definition** (above). The working definition this research tests, and which is the agent's own proposal (Tentative): **a timeline is an ordered sequence of placements, where a placement says that one thing appears in the sequence, in a position the sequence decides, for a reason, put there by an actor, and where who may see and add to the sequence is a matter of its audience**. It deliberately says nothing about people, chat, feeds, notifications, an owner, a clock or deletion.

## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The timeline domain gives an organization one answer to four questions about anything that is *shown to someone, in an order, because of something*: *what appears in the sequence* (any thing, by reference: a comment, a form, a product, an invoice, a case reason, a system notice), *in what order and by whose decision*, *why it appears here* (the reason, and who put it there), and *for whom* (who may see it, add to it, and where each viewer has got to). Everything the industry sells separately as chat, feed, wall, inbox, notification centre, activity log, case file, channel, thread view, diary, or "recently viewed" list asks these four questions of a different sequence. This restates what the Decided material already says in plain words: content is an independent unit that is *attached* to a timeline ([handoff](timeline.handoff.md#decisions)), which makes the timeline the place of attachment and not the thing attached.

**The word hides at least four things (agent, Tentative; the evidence is in the corpus).** Read across the repository, "timeline" is asked to do jobs that do not share a lifecycle:

1. **A sequence of placements** — `(timeline)-[:event_is]->(thing)` and its reverse `event_of`, `pin`, `limit_max_event` ([event](edges/event/event.md), [pin](edges/pin/pin.md), [limit_max_event](edges/limit_max_event/limit_max_event.md)).
2. **A channel identified with a group** — `(group)-[:---]->(timeline)` "CAN use as ChannelID" ([edges-loop/timeline](edges-loop/timeline/timeline.md)), the group-to-group chat channels ([chat](edges/chat/chat.md)), and the case premise "the case UUID is a group, so it can be a timeline" ([case.research.001.md](../../rights/case/case.research.001.md)). Here the timeline is not a thing; it is a use of a group.
3. **A kind of feed per user** — `PrivateTimeLine` (home feed), `PublicTimeLine` (what others see), `LifeTimeLine`, `UserNotificationTimeline` ([user](edges/user/user.md)). Here the timeline is a derived view of what a user follows, or a stream addressed to one person.
4. **Per-viewer state on a sequence** — `read_until` (how far a user has read), `mute` (no notification for this), and possibly `pin` ([read_until](edges/read_until/read_until.md), [mute](edges/mute/mute.md), [pin](edges/pin/pin.md)). Here the timeline is only the thing a person's preference is about.

Under the [Single Responsibility Check](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#single-responsibility-check) (different discussions of the node focus on unrelated aspects) and the [Premature Classification Check](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#premature-classification-check) (the session argues about the word while agreeing about the behaviour), the proposal the scenarios test is that **the domain's concept is the placement, the sequence is what placements form, and "is a channel of a group", "is a feed", "is an inbox" and "holds a viewer's position" are separate capabilities or derived views**. The handoff's question "is Timeline an independent entity?" then splits: the *sequence* needs an identity because limits, audience, order authority, cursors and pins attach to it (finding 3, T2); the *feed* does not (T3).

**Scenarios.** Thirteen, from several industries and cultures. Each states what is being done, who is in it, what breaks the obvious model, and what it says. "The obvious model" is *a timeline is a chronological list of posts that its owner publishes and followers read*.

1. **A family chat group (any messenger, any country).** A grandmother sends a voice note; a cousin replies with a photo; the note is forwarded to another group and shown as "forwarded"; a sibling "deletes for me", the cousin "deletes for everyone"; a new aunt joins and may or may not see what was said before; ticks show delivered and read; one member mutes the group for a week and pins the note for herself. *Breaks:* owner-publishes-followers-read; delete as one act; one position per timeline; visibility as a property of the timeline alone. *Says:* the placement has a placer and a provenance (forward); "delete for me" is a viewer's preference, "for everyone" is an end of the placement; what a newcomer sees depends on the membership interval (group research scenario 1), which is data the timeline's rule reads; mute and read position are per viewer.
2. **A broadcast feed (Twitter or Weibo; Instagram; Telegram channels).** An author publishes; followers' home timelines show it; a follower's friend replies and the reply appears in her home timeline "because a friend replied"; the platform inserts "you might like" items; the author pins one post to the top of her page; a moderator hides a post. *Breaks:* the idea that a timeline is stored per reader (the home timeline is assembled from other timelines and from rules), and that every item is there for one reason. *Says:* the home timeline is derived (discovered, in Memar's sense) from acquired placements in other timelines; the reasons in [dt-reason.kh](edges/event/protocol/dt-reason.kh) are exactly the labels of that derivation, and the recommendation reasons are analytics; a pin by the author is a curation fact for every reader, not a viewer preference.
3. **A public consultation on a draft law (participatory legislation; the origin of `limit_event_per_user`).** Citizens may each place one comment per article; officials may place any number; the count per article matters more than reading every reply; the consultation closes on a date and the record is kept as published. *Breaks:* unlimited placement by any viewer; a timeline that stays open. *Says:* the limit is set by the owner of what is replied to, evaluated when the placement would be created, with a count that is state ([limit_event_per_user](edges/limit_event_per_user/limit_event_per_user.md): "it will not limit user to reply to other comment"); closing is a state of the timeline; the same shape as content's `reply-limit_per_user` ([content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md) scenario 12).
4. **A court file (any judiciary).** Judges' reasons are "timeline texts on the case" (Decided, [case.handoff.md](../../rights/case/case.handoff.md)); no party sees any judge's vote or reasons until every required judge has voted (Decided, same file, "a requirement to note for the content and timeline domains"); on a final ruling, an apology the defendant is ordered to give is written on *the defendant's own* timeline, addressed to the claimant, by a compelled service ([case.md](../../rights/case/case.md)). *Breaks:* the owner of a timeline controls what appears on it; placement is visible when made. *Says:* a placement can be made by an authority against the timeline owner's will (compelled placement); a placement can exist and be invisible until a condition holds (conditional visibility); and these are rules on the placement, not facts about the case.
5. **A hospital record and a nursing handover (any health system).** An entry is made at 07:40 about a dose given at 06:10; a wrong dose is corrected by a new entry that references the first, never by editing it; entries by a night nurse are visible to the care team and not to the patient's employer; a patient may request that one entry be hidden from non-clinical readers. *Breaks:* the instant of placement equals the instant the thing occurred; delete or edit as correction; visibility by timeline. *Says:* two instants are acquired and differ (placed, occurred); a correction is a new placement referencing the earlier ([Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates)); visibility is per placement; sensitivity labels ([thing-labels](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/thing-labels.md)) must reach a placement, not only a content.
6. **Voice notes recorded offline in a low-connectivity region (rural health workers; the content research's scenario 10).** Two people create notes for the same group while offline; on reconnection the order must be agreed; device clocks disagree by minutes. *Breaks:* order by timestamp. *Says:* Memar's time contract carries comparison and ordering of instants, not simultaneity across distributed parts ([Time](../../time/time.md#the-definitional-question-kept-open)); the Decided direction (a unique changer per entity, [content.handoff.md](../../knowledge/content/content.handoff.md)) answers it if the *sequence* is the entity with one changer. The content's own creation is local; its position is the timeline's decision.
7. **An immutable register (a ship's logbook, a land-registry journal, an account ledger).** Entries are numbered, never removed; an error is corrected by a later entry; inspectors read it years later; auto-trimming would be a crime. *Breaks:* `limit_max_event`'s first mode ("auto delete old event", which the document itself doubts: "delete data?? really??"). *Says:* a retention window is a way of *reading* (show the last N) or a rule with legal consequences, never a property of the sequence; the sequence of the [account](../../economics/account/account.research.001.md) ledger (ordered, append-only, a correction is a new fact) is the same shape (finding 3, T6).
8. **A notification inbox (mobile app, email digest, government SMS).** The system, not a person, places an item for one user ("your order shipped"); the inbox keeps the last 200; the user mutes a type; an emergency alert must appear even if she muted notifications. *Breaks:* the placer is a person; mute as absolute. *Says:* the placer may be the system acting by a rule; mute is a viewer's preference that a rule may override for a class of notice ("Communicate direction: timeline/event broadcast", [leadership.md](../../../bundles/leadership/leadership.md)); delivery channels (app, web app, email, SMS: the `signal_preference` of [notification](../../computer/notification/notification.md)) are the notification domain's, not the sequence's.
9. **A task or project discussion (this organization's own model).** "Each Task has one timeline of its own ... that all its participants use" ([task.md](../task/task.md)); an RFC lives in its author's timeline and in a project's (Decided); a repository's timeline admits only designated members. *Breaks:* a timeline per person; a timeline that exists before its audience (a task gains its group "when people join it"). *Says:* the timeline is born with the task and its audience is acquired later, so the timeline cannot be the group (contradiction 3); one content may sit in two timelines with two placers.
10. **A person's life and its end (a life timeline, a memorial page, a biography).** Items are placed at occurred instants decades before they were placed; a person dies and the heirs may read, add or close the sequence; some platforms memorialize. *Breaks:* order by placement instant; an owner who is always alive. *Says:* a sequence may be ordered by *occurred* instant, not placed instant; the owner of a timeline can cease, and ownership passes by the [heir](../../rights/ownership/edges/heir/heir.md) mechanism (user research scenario 5, [user.research.001.md](../../identity/user/user.research.001.md)); closing a timeline is not deleting it.
11. **A public bulletin of a ministry or a municipality (Iran's ministries, a city's notice board, a gazette).** The notice must reach everyone in a defined audience; compliance is shown by who has read it; some recipients cannot refuse it; corrections are published, not edited. *Breaks:* mute and unfollow; follower-driven reach. *Says:* the audience is a defined set, not a follower set; read position becomes an obligation record; and "unfollow" is a rule the bulletin can switch off.
12. **A support group whose existence is secret (addiction recovery, a banned union, a religious minority circle; group research scenario 13).** The group's timeline must not be found by search, a non-member's request must not reveal that it exists, and a leaked read list endangers people. *Breaks:* a timeline as a discoverable object; read receipts as harmless. *Says:* sensitivity on the timeline and on its read positions; the cursor of a viewer is itself sensitive; refusing a request must say nothing about existence.
13. **A "recently viewed" list (the one use [timeline.md](timeline.md) names).** The system places each product or film the user opens on her private timeline; the list holds a few dozen; she clears it; the company analyses it. *Breaks:* every item placed by a person's deliberate act; permanence. *Says:* placement by the system on a person's behalf with her implicit consent; here, and almost only here, removal by the owner is the point (a right to clear), and the history it leaves in analytics is a separate privacy question; it is the contrast case to scenario 7.

**What the scenarios say together (agent, Tentative).** A timeline does not always have a human owner (8, 10, 13), is not always chronological (6, 10), is not always readable by whoever may write (4, 5, 12), is not always writable by its owner (4, 11), does not always keep what was placed (13) and must not lose it in other cases (5, 7), is not always stored per reader (2), is not always a thing before its audience exists (9), and is not always one reason per item (2). The kernel therefore cannot assume an owner, a human placer, a clock, an audience, a retention, a single reason or a reader-specific copy. Each is a relation with rules or a pluggable capability.

**The second layer: the underlying problem the answers assume.** The first layer answers what was asked. The reading this research assumes beneath it is that the owner's actual problem is not "how do we model a feed" but "how do content, task, case, group, notification and calendar each get an ordered, audience-limited, reason-bearing place to show things, without each inventing its own list, its own pin and its own read marker". Evidence: content's `widgets/group`, `widgets/pin` and `widgets/log-activity` duplicate timeline relations ([content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md), contradictions 4 and 7); four domains say their UUID "is a timeline or a group" for that reason ([case.research.001.md](../../rights/case/case.research.001.md), [modules.md](../../modules.md#top-or-thing-levels-labels): a product UUID "as group for a product as default chat timeline"); the task states "one timeline" in its own table ([task.md](../task/task.md)). A reader who holds a different reading of the problem can set the proposals aside without discarding the scenarios.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

#### The removal test run on the timeline itself (agent, Tentative)
[What Earns Foundational Status](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status): express the part of reality the candidate covers through the remaining abstractions; if that forces the fabrication or the distortion of a foundational abstraction, the candidate earns consideration. Without a timeline (meaning the placement relation and the thing that bears it):

- Content would have to hold the list of places it appears in, which contradicts the Decided independent unit "attachable to one or more Timelines"; the content research's kernel excludes "placement in timelines" for exactly that reason ([content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md), finding 2). That is distortion of content.
- Group would have to hold order, which is the reverse of what the group research finds (membership and sequence have different lifecycles: the sequence outlives members, [group.research.001.md](../group/group.research.001.md) M5).
- Task, case and contract would each hold their own discussion list; the task document already says it holds none ([task.md](../task/task.md): "A Task never carries the text of a plan, a report or a timeline").

The guard in the other direction also applies: the placement may be only a *relation* any thing can take, with no domain called timeline at all. That is not refuted. The test shows the *capability* needs a home; it does not show the home must be a node. Whether the sequence needs a node is finding 3. The kernel below is therefore small whichever way that goes.

### 3. Rival models

Marks: **Tentative** (leaning), **Explored** (explored, unresolved), **Deferred**.

#### The models
- **T1 — a timeline is a group used as a channel (the status quo reading; Explored).** `(group)-[:---]->(timeline)` is a loop-edge: a group is classified as a timeline, and its UUID is "ChannelID" ([edges-loop/timeline](edges-loop/timeline/timeline.md)); [dt-uuid.kh](protocol/dt-uuid.kh) types the timeline UUID as the group's; the per-user timelines are "a group node typed by kind labels" ([user](edges/user/user.md)); the case premise says a case UUID is a group "so it can be a timeline". *For:* the most common experience (a chat group has a timeline); one identity for audience and channel; no second node to create. *Against:* a task's timeline exists before the task has a group (task.md); a public author timeline has no members; a feed has no group; a sequence that outlives its group (dissolved, merged) has no identity; audience and sequence have different lifecycles; it is the reading the group research refuses in M5 and Q13.
- **T2 — a timeline is a thing of its own; a placement is a record; the audience is a group, a rule or a user attached through authorization (Explored, leaning; recommended).** The kernel of finding 2. *For:* meets scenarios 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12 without bending any node; gives limits, pins, order, read cursors, closing and sensitivity something to attach to; removes the pressure that made domains say "my UUID is a timeline"; one content in many timelines with one placement each (the Decided Twitter model). *Against:* it asks the owner to accept three unratified moves (placement is a record, order has a single decider, audience is attached rather than identical with a group); a task or case then has *a* timeline rather than *being* one, which is a change to the case premise; the timeline is only as meaningful as its placement rules.
- **T3 — a timeline is only a derived view: a rule over placements and replies, no node (Explored; right for feeds and threads only).** `thread` is "the set of contents reachable through `reply_to` from a root" and is discovered ([content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md), finding 2); the home timeline is assembled from followed sources. *For:* matches [Acquired vs. Discovered](../../../docs/modeling.md#acquired-data-vs-discovered-data); no stored copies. *Against:* it has nothing for acquired facts that are not recomputable: who put this here, in what order, with what reason, when it ended; forward and pin are acts; the court file and register cannot be a query. Kept as the **feed** pluggable.
- **T4 — a timeline is the history of one thing (Explored).** Every thing has a history of facts about it ([Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates)); `(timeline)-[:event_is]->(thing)` with "note: LogActivity" reads like that, and the log-activity widget duplicates it. *For:* no new concept; history is reality. *Against:* history is each domain's own (the account ledger, supersession, tombstones); a timeline places many things from many domains for an audience, which no single thing's history does; a "history of a thing" would be the same sequence seen from the thing, and that view is derivable from placements of its change records.
- **T5 — a timeline is a collection (an ordered, curated set of contents; Explored).** Content's `collection` pluggable is `(collection)-[:contains {position}]->(content)` with the curator deciding position ([content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md), finding 2). *For:* one ordered-membership capability instead of two; a pinned list, an album and a timeline are the same shape. *Against:* a collection contains contents only; a timeline places any thing, including a form, an invoice, a case reason or a system notice; the collection's order is a curator's choice, the timeline's order is by sequence with time as presentation; the timeline carries audience, reasons, limits and per-viewer state which a collection does not. It may be that a collection is a timeline with a curator as its only placer; that identification is Q22's question.
- **T6 — a timeline is one instance of a general ordered append-only sequence shared with the account ledger (Explored).** The account research finds "the ordered, append-only sequence of one account's bookkeeping records" ([account.research.001.md](../../economics/account/account.research.001.md), term *Ledger*); scenario 7 here is the same shape. *For:* a correction as a new fact, an order decided once, and an unbroken append rule would be written once ([A Relation Several Domains Need Is Defined Once](../../../docs/modeling.md#a-relation-several-domains-need-is-defined-once)). *Against:* the ledger carries a quantity, a unit and a running total, the timeline carries a reason and an audience; [Modules](../../modules.md#placement) says "a difference in processing semantics may be a distinct module". Not recommended; the shared parts (correction, order authority) are asked as a question.

#### Smaller choice 1: is a placement a record with an identity, or a plain edge (Tentative: a record)
A plain edge `(timeline)-[:event_is]->(thing)` cannot be the far end of another edge. Yet the corpus already hangs things on it: a reason and a reason user ([service-set_event.kh](edges/event/protocol/service-set_event.kh)), a forward's provenance (content's `forward`), a pin ("any quiddity like comment, product"), a read cursor that points at "the last placement read" ([data_model.kh](edges/read_until/protocol/data_model.kh): `ReadUntil() CommentID`), a limit's count, an end (delete for everyone), a correction. These are the "further properties or relationships of its own" that justify reification in [Classification Emerges From Rules and Relations](../../../docs/modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels). The counter-argument is *unnecessary reification* ([Modeling Practice](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#common-modeling-anti-patterns)); the answer is that a placement has its own lifecycle (made, hidden, pinned, ended, corrected) and its own authorization. Same move the group research makes for membership, and for the same reason; the two records are siblings, not the same record. Kept Tentative because every consumer must now name the placement.

#### Smaller choice 2: what "reason" is (Tentative: a reference plus the placer; the social-graph and recommendation reasons are derived)
The carried vocabulary mixes three things: *why the placement exists* (`Reason_UserMentioned`, `Reason_FriendForward`, `Reason_FriendReplied`), *why it reached this viewer's home timeline* (the 1st to 6th friendship cycles, `FollowByFriend`, `UserFollow`), and *why a recommender suggests it* (`BaseOnUser*`, `UserMightLike`). The first is acquired and belongs to the placement. The second is a derivation from the social graph owned by [social_connections](../../identity/person/edges/social_connections/social_connections.md) (the event document says so itself) and so belongs to the feed. The third is analytics and belongs under the analytics namespace. `ReasonUserID` ("it can be the owner of comment or any other user base on reason", with "TODO::: node details??") is the *other party* the reason is about, not the placer, and a placement may have several. A reason is a reference to a label (data), so a new reason is a data addition, not code (the Code/Rule boundary the event document asks about).

#### Smaller choice 3: pin, mute and read position are three different things (Tentative)
- **Pin** appears in `timeline/pin` ("any quiddity like comment, product"), in content's `widgets/pin` and in the pin GUI widget. Two meanings sit under one word: a pin by an authority that every reader sees (a pinned announcement), and a pin by one viewer for herself (a pinned chat). The first is curation on the timeline; the second is the viewer's preference.
- **Mute** is a viewer's preference: "user will not get notification for this comment for any reason" ([mute](edges/mute/mute.md)). It affects fan-out, not the sequence. Its far end varies (`thing`, `user`, `comment`), so it is a preference on a relation, not a timeline relation.
- **Read position** is the viewer's acquired cursor. It is not a shortcut unless a source walk is named ([Modules → Edge buckets](../../modules.md#edge-buckets)): "read until here" cannot be recomputed from per-item facts that were never recorded, and content's ladder (delivered, seen, read) is the per-item version of the same fact ([content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md), contradiction 8). So the cursor is acquired, the counts are discovered.

#### Smaller choice 4: the timeline kinds are not kinds (Tentative)
`PrivateTimeLine` (the user's own feed), `PublicTimeLine` (what others see), `LifeTimeLine`, and `UserNotificationTimeline` differ by *audience* (owner only; everyone; owner and designated), by *who places* (the owner; the system), and by *assembly* (stored or derived). Those three facts, not a label, are what the kinds mean. A label may stay as an optional classification loop-edge, with the completable sentence of [Classification Emerges From Rules](../../../docs/modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels): *this timeline may be classified as a notification timeline because its only placer is the system and its audience is one user, and therefore receives channel delivery*.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions

The domain, once decided, has to answer each of these. Questions are written to favour no answer where unsettled; a settled item says so and cites its decision.

#### Identity
- *Settled:* content is an independent unit attachable to one or more timelines ([timeline.handoff.md](timeline.handoff.md#decisions), Decided).
- Is a timeline's identity the thing domain's, a group's (as [dt-uuid.kh](protocol/dt-uuid.kh) says), or the identity of whatever it belongs to (a task, a case, a user)? Can one identity be a task, a group and a timeline, and which operations of each may reach it? (The group, task and case researches ask the same.)
- Is a placement an identity of its own, or a fact about two other identities? What would each answer let forward, pin, read position and a correction do?
- Does the timeline's identity stay the same when its audience is replaced, when its group is dissolved or merged, when its task is completed, when its owner dies, when it is closed and reopened? Which is a new identity and which a `supersedes` ([supersedes](../../identity/thing/edges/supersedes/supersedes.md))?
- May one thing be placed twice in the same timeline (a repost, a re-announcement)? Is that two placements or one?
- Who may mint a timeline: any user, only the owner of a thing, the system at birth of a task or case? Is there a per-user limit?
- Is the identifier ordered? The thing domain asks whether `ThingUUID` is ordered or opaque; a timeline must not depend on identifier order for sequence.

#### Lifecycle
- What states does a timeline have: open, closed (no new placements, readable), archived, ended? Which are positions in its life and which are rules (a consultation closes on a date)? Is closing set or derived?
- What states does a placement have: made, hidden, pinned, ended, corrected, compelled? Are hidden and ended set by the shared `hidden` and `block` statuses ([hidden](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/hidden/hidden.md), [block](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/block/block.md)) or by the timeline?
- Does an ended placement resume (re-placed) or does a return create a new placement referencing the earlier? (The group and task researches ask the same of membership and reopening.)
- When a thing is withdrawn or tombstoned, what happens to its placements: they follow it, stay with a marker, or go? (Content asks the same from the other side, question on withdrawal.)
- What does "delete" mean: end of a placement (for everyone), end of the viewer's view (for me), erasure of a person's data? Which does the domain own?
- Does a timeline survive the end of its group, task, case or owner? Who may read it afterwards?
- May a timeline be deleted at all, given [Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates) and the ledger shape?

#### Authorization
- Who may create a timeline, place, end another's placement, hide, pin, close, reopen, change the order decider, attach a rule, add an audience? Each is a question of who and of what rule; none is the kernel's.
- Where does authority over a timeline come from: [ownership](../../rights/ownership/edges/owner/owner.md), membership of the audience group, a role, a delegation ([delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md)), a ruling (a court placing on a defendant's timeline)? [Agency → Authority](../../../docs/agency.md#authority) separates authority from responsibility; which does the placement record?
- May an authority place on a timeline against its owner's will, and may the owner end that placement? How is the compulsion recorded so that "the owner posted this" is never false?
- Who may see a timeline exists, list its placements, see a placement before a condition holds, see history from before they joined, see another's read position? Which module owns these, [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md) or [access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md)?
- May the system place on a person's timeline without her act (notifications, recently viewed), and under what consent?
- Across organizations: may a follower in another organization receive placements ([follow](../../identity/thing/edges/follow/follow.md))? Who owns the placement record then?

#### Rules
- Which rules does the domain itself need, and which belong to organizations: per-user count; capacity and what happens when full (refuse, trim the view, trim the record); window; closing date; admission of a placer; blocked texts; conditional visibility; override of mute; newcomer history?
- Where each rule attaches: through `condition_is` on the timeline, on a placement, on the audience group, or on the module that owns the capability?
- Is the rule that limits an interaction the same capability for content and timeline (content asks the same about reply, quote and forward)?
- Who may attach or remove each rule, and what does removal leave of what it already produced?

#### Events
- Which occurrences are events the timeline exposes as an `EventTarget` ([Event](../../process/event/event.md#the-eventtarget-capability)): timeline created, closed, reopened, audience changed, order decider changed; placement made, refused, hidden, pinned, unpinned, ended, corrected; limit reached; a viewer's cursor moved (an event, or only a record)?
- Who consumes them: notification (fan-out to listeners and the `signal_preference` channel), feed assembly, task (activity), content (reply notice), calendar, analytics, rules, follow (cross-organization delivery)?
- Which of these already exist as a request or response and need no type of their own? (`Service_SetEvent_Request` in [service-set_event.kh](edges/event/protocol/service-set_event.kh) is a request, not an event.)
- A correction (a placement made by mistake) is a new fact referencing the one it corrects, never a rewrite. Does the domain accept that as stated?
- Memar's Event is not a stored record. Does the domain rename its stored record (the placement) so that "event" keeps Memar's meaning (contradiction 1)?

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each is one paragraph, as a sketch: not a design, and none is to be built before the owner decides.

1. **`placement` (kernel record; Tentative).** A placement is a thing with an identity of its own, tied to one timeline and one placed thing, with a placer, a reason reference, an optional provenance, a placed instant, an occurred instant where different, a position decided by the sequence's changer, and an end. It is created by the placer, an authority, a ruling or a rule; it is ended, never deleted; it triggers `placement_made` and `placement_ended`. Other domains attach to it instead of keying their own record by the pair (timeline, thing): a pin, a read cursor's anchor, a hide, a correction. Replaces `event_is` / `event_of`. Not built before Q2.
2. **`order authority` (kernel rule; Tentative, mechanism Deferred).** One decider of position per timeline, the Decided direction for the group's order; time is a presentation of it. The mechanism (consistent-hash routing, single writer) is the content handoff's open detail. The timeline's part is that the position is a fact the changer states, and that the placed and occurred instants are kept beside it.
3. **`audience` (pluggable, probably authorization's; Explored).** `(timeline)` to groups or users with a level (may see, may place) and a history rule for newcomers: from joining, from the start, or from a stated instant. It reads the membership interval of the [group](../group/group.research.001.md) and is the answer to "who may see". Home is open because [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md) and [access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md) both claim group-scoped access and neither owns it (the group research's contradiction 7).
4. **`feed` (pluggable; Tentative).** Assembles a viewer's home timeline from followed timelines, friends' replies and rules, labelling each item with a derived reason. Stores nothing it can recompute. Created by the viewer or the organization. Its reasons are the friendship-cycle and recommendation vocabulary now in `dt-reason.kh`. Does not own ranking, which is analytics.
5. **`curation` (pluggable; Tentative).** Pinned and highlighted placements, ordered, with a maximum, created and removed by an authority over the timeline. Triggers `placement_pinned`. Retires content's pin widget.
6. **`viewer-preferences` (pluggable; Tentative).** Mute, personal pin and personal hide, created and removed only by the viewer; a rule may override a mute for a class of notice (scenarios 8 and 11). It holds no sequence; it shapes fan-out and the viewer's own view. May belong with the notification domain, whose `signal_preference` is the channel half of the same preference (Q7).
7. **`closing` (a state and a rule; Explored).** A timeline may be closed so that no placement is accepted, while it stays readable: a consultation ends, a case reaches a final ruling, a task is completed. Closing is set by an authority or by a rule on a date or a status; reopening is an event. Listed so scenarios 3, 4 and 9 have a stated home.
8. **`compelled placement` (a use of delegate, not a module; Explored).** A ruling or an authority places on a timeline whose owner did not consent. The placer is recorded as the system acting by a delegation that cites the ruling ([delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md)); the owner may not end that placement; the case's own rule says the write is "another compelled service". Listed because no document says who owns it.
9. **`conditional visibility` (a rule vocabulary; Explored).** A placement exists but cannot be seen until a condition holds (all required judges have voted; an embargo date; a status). The case's decision says it is "a requirement to note for the content and timeline domains". The timeline's part is a place to evaluate the condition at read time, through `condition_is`; the condition itself is the organization's.
10. **`retention` (a rule, not a module; Explored).** The distinction between a window on what is shown, a limit on what is accepted, and an erasure of a person's data. The domain states that a retention rule never deletes the fact of a placement without an erasure rule that says so, and that erasure detaches the actor and the thing, not the existence of the position.
11. **`system placement` (a use of K2; Tentative).** Notifications, "recently viewed", "Omid changed the group photo" and the log-activity record are placements made by the system by a rule, with no content and no author ([content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md) scenario 11 and Q16). Listed so the placer is never assumed to be a person.

### 7. Test scenarios

Given/when/then in prose, so another session can write tests once the owner decides. Each is marked with what it depends on: **kept** if it follows from settled text, **T2** if it assumes the recommended model, **Q n** if it assumes the default of open question n.

1. **A content in two timelines (kept: Decided).** Given a content C in its author's timeline. When it is placed in a project's timeline. Then there are two placements, one content, and ending one leaves the other.
2. **Placement is not authorship (T2, Q2).** Given a member places an existing content in a timeline. When the placement is read. Then the placer is the member, the content's author is unchanged, and a "who" is never inferred from the timeline's owner.
3. **A forward is a placement with provenance (T2, Q19).** Given content A in timeline G1. When a member of G2 forwards it. Then A appears in G2 with a provenance to the first placement, no new content exists, and the owner of A is notified.
4. **A forward the owner forbade is refused (Q19, Q20).** Given the owner's restriction attached to A. When a silent forward is attempted. Then no placement is made and a `placement_refused` with the reason is recorded.
5. **Order is the decider's, not the clock's (kept: concurrency direction; Q3).** Given two devices that placed items offline in one timeline. When both reconnect. Then the timeline's single decider orders them, both exist, and the order does not depend on device clocks.
6. **Two instants are kept (Q3).** Given an entry placed at 07:40 about a dose given at 06:10. When the timeline is read by occurred time and by placed time. Then both orders are answerable and neither instant was overwritten.
7. **A correction is a new placement (kept: Modeling State Change).** Given a placement made by mistake. When it is corrected. Then a new placement references it, the earlier remains unchanged, and "what was visible on date D" is still answerable.
8. **Ending is not deleting (T2, Q4).** Given a placer ends her placement "for everyone". When asked who placed what on a past date. Then the answer includes it, and readers after the end do not see it.
9. **Delete for me is the viewer's (Q4, Q7).** Given a viewer hides a placement for herself. When another viewer reads. Then she sees it, and no event of the sequence was recorded.
10. **A per-user limit holds, an official is exempt (kept: limit_event_per_user policy; Q20).** Given a consultation timeline limited to one placement per citizen per article. When a citizen places a second. Then it is refused with a reason; when an official places a second, it is accepted.
11. **A full timeline refuses, or the view trims (Q4).** Given a capacity of 200 and 200 placements. When a 201st is attempted under the "refuse" rule. Then it is refused and nothing is removed; under the "window" rule it is accepted and the view shows the last 200, and all 201 remain.
12. **A closed timeline is readable (Q15).** Given a task completed. When a participant tries to place. Then it is refused as closed, reading still works, and reopening is an event by an authority.
13. **A compelled placement stands (Q12).** Given a final ruling that orders an apology on the defendant's timeline. When the service runs. Then a placement exists whose placer is the system acting by the ruling, the owner's attempt to end it is refused, and the placement is not attributed to the owner.
14. **A placement can be invisible until a condition holds (Q13).** Given judges' reasons placed on a case's timeline before all have voted. When a party reads. Then she sees none of them; when the last judge votes, all become visible at once.
15. **A newcomer sees from joining (Q14).** Given a group timeline with a history rule "from joining". When a new member joins on day 10. Then she sees placements from day 10, not earlier, and the earlier ones are not altered.
16. **The home timeline is not stored (T2, Q9).** Given user U follows authors A and B. When U's home timeline is read. Then it is assembled from A's and B's timelines with derived reasons, and no placement belongs to U's home timeline.
17. **Mute does not hide the sequence (Q7).** Given a viewer mutes a timeline. When a placement is made. Then no notification reaches her, the placement is in the sequence, and her read cursor is unaffected.
18. **A mandatory notice overrides mute (Q7).** Given a municipal emergency notice marked mandatory by rule. When it is placed. Then it reaches an audience member who muted the timeline.
19. **A cursor is acquired, counts are discovered (Q8).** Given 40 placements and a viewer who has read to the 25th. When unread is asked. Then it is computed as 15 from the cursor and the sequence, and no stored count can drift.
20. **A secret timeline reveals nothing (Q25).** Given a timeline carrying a sensitivity label. When a non-member asks for it. Then the refusal says nothing about whether it exists, and its cursors are not readable by others.
21. **A task's timeline predates its group (T2, Q1).** Given a task with no participants. When three people join. Then its timeline already existed, none of them created a second one, and the audience is now the group they form.
22. **A timeline outlives its owner (Q16).** Given a user's life timeline. When the user dies. Then heirs by the ownership mechanism may read or close it, nothing was deleted, and the timeline stays addressable.
23. **A system placement has no author (Q17).** Given a change of a group photo. When the notice appears in the timeline. Then it is a placement by the system pointing at the record and its version, and no content with an author was created.
24. **Detaching a rule leaves placements (T2).** Given a limit rule detached. When the timeline continues. Then existing placements stay, new ones fall back to the default, and refusals made earlier stay in history.
