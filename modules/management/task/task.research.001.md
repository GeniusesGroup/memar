## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The task domain gives an organization one answer to four questions about any piece of work: *what is it* (an intent a human can read and discuss), *who is on it* (and under whose authority), *how does it relate to other work* (part of, blocked by, produced a change in), and *what came of it* (an outcome, with the history that led there). Everything the industry sells separately as project, ticket, issue, reminder, job order, leave request or workflow step is the same question asked of different work. This restates, in plain words, what the domain documents already say: Task is "the primary organizational unit (replacement for commit messages)" hosting "discussions, questions, objections, alternatives, evidence, decisions, outcomes, and the content revisions it produced" (Decided, [task.handoff.md](task.handoff.md#from-organization-ideas-backlog--task)); a project, a production run, an issue, a ticket and a leave request are each a Task ([task-concept.md → Guide-level explanation](edges/task-concept.md#guide-level-explanation)).

**The word hides two things (agent, Tentative; the evidence is in the domain's own definition).** [task.md](task.md) gives the dictionary definition in two clauses: work "undertaken or attempted", and work "required to be done as a duty or for a specific fee". They are not the same thing. A piece of work undertaken exists only as work happens; a piece of work required exists before any work and may never be done. A todo item never started is required and not undertaken. A change recorded after the fact is undertaken and was never required. The seed RFC's unresolved question 7 offers two candidate definitions and leaves the choice open ([task-concept.md](edges/task-concept.md#unresolved-questions)). Under [Challenging a Proposed Concept](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#challenging-a-proposed-concept), the first sign of a mixed node is a definition that needs two unrelated clauses. The proposal that follows, and that the scenarios test, is that **the domain's node is the declared intent of a unit of work** (what is to be achieved, readable, discussable, identifiable), and that *being required of someone* (assignment, duty, fee) and *being undertaken* (enactment, attempts, outcome) are relations and events around it, not parts of its definition. This is the reading of Memar's [Process](../../process/process.md#intent) closest to the domain: intent is the basis of a process's boundary, "the participant that expresses an intent does not necessarily execute every activity", and purpose "does not imply successful completion".

**Scenarios.** Eleven, from several industries and cultures. Each states what is being done, who is on it, what breaks the obvious model, and what it says.

1. **A solo shopkeeper's reminder (any bazaar, any country).** The owner of a one-person business notes "collect the post-dated payment on the due date". No team, no assigner other than himself, a deadline, and a notification. *Breaks:* a model in which every task has an assigner and an assignee distinct from each other, or in which a task needs a team before it exists. *Says:* the seed's claim that the industry's "reminder" is a task plus a notifying policy ([task-concept.md](edges/task-concept.md#summary)) holds only if participation is optional and the deadline and the notification are attached, not intrinsic. It also gives the first worked example for capacity reasoning ([organizational-reasoning](../organizational-reasoning.handoff.md#decisions)).
2. **Building a headquarters (construction, any country).** The project is one task; it splits into thousands. A concrete-pour inspection is needed by both the electrical and the plumbing phases, so one sub-task serves two parents. Electrical rough-in cannot start before the pour, which is a dependency, not containment. Two contractors, each seeing only its own tasks. *Breaks:* a single-parent tree, and a model with only a "parent" label that must also express "blocked". *Says:* the seed's "Graph, not tree" (unresolved 10) is a requirement, and containment and dependence are different relations ([Process Composition](../../process/process.md#process-composition): composition "does not require the composed processes to lose their independent identity"; it is "explicitly not a tree").
3. **A furniture order (manufacturing).** An order spawns a tree of tasks (framing, finishing) derived from a separate task that defines the manufacturing process ([task-concept.md](edges/task-concept.md#guide-level-explanation)). The customer accepts nine of ten chairs; one stage is optional (extra lacquer); a rejected finish returns the item to rework. A by-product is produced. *Breaks:* "a parent is done when its children are done" as a rule, and a binary success/failure. *Says:* completion needs a policy (all required, quorum, optional children), outcomes are plural ([Process → Outcomes](../../process/process.md#outcomes)), and "Rework" is a loop in the process, not a status of the parent. The task that "defines the process" is a template whose instances are tasks, the shape the domain's own [repetitive](edges-loop/repetitive/repetitive.md) policy and thing's [template](../../identity/thing/edges/template/template.md) edge already describe.
4. **A hospital operation and its shift handover.** A surgical operation contains induction, incision, repair and recovery monitoring, each with sub-tasks ([Process Composition](../../process/process.md#process-composition) uses this very example). The nurse owning "monitor recovery" hands over to the next shift mid-task. A dose already given cannot be un-given when the order is cancelled. Only the care team may read the patient's tasks. A skill requirement is a legal condition, not a preference. *Breaks:* an assignee fixed at creation, cancellation meaning rollback, and visibility equal for every viewer. *Says:* reassignment is an event on a living task, cancellation is a decision whose effects may stand ([Process → Cancellation](../../process/process.md#cancellation)), and the `Mandatory` level of [requires](edges/requires/requires.md) may have to block assignment, which is enforcement, hence a rule.
5. **A Japanese ringi circulation (corporate Japan).** A proposal is circulated for the approval of every affected manager; each adds an approval; one objection sends it back for revision; the end of the circulation is a decision. *Breaks:* a model with one approver label, and the idea that "approval" is a status. *Says:* the decision is an outcome of a task (Decided: "Decision is an outcome of Task, not a root concept", [task.handoff.md](task.handoff.md#from-organization-ideas-backlog--task)); approval is an act by authorized participants recorded as events, which is what the shared [approved](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/approved/approved.md) and [approval_needed](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/approval_needed/approval_needed.md) loops already name (and the first one already lists `task` among the records it marks). What is missing is the consequence: whether the task may close while approvals are outstanding.
6. **A communal harvest (hashar in Central Asia, gotong royong in Indonesia, harambee in Kenya, boneh-style cooperation in rural Iran).** A neighbour proposes the work; people join over days; nobody assigns, nobody is paid, and obligation is by reciprocity. The group does not exist before the work; it forms around it and dissolves after. *Breaks:* the second clause of the dictionary definition (nobody is "required"), assignment by skill, and a model in which the team pre-exists the task. *Says:* a task need not be assigned or paid and still be a task; the team is acquired by participation, not given. [task.md](task.md) already says so for teams ("team up some user just for that task and break it at end of task"), which supports reading "additive over Group" (Decided) as *a task gains a group when people join*, not *every task is born a group*.
7. **A support ticket (software or telecom).** A customer's request depends on several internal tasks the customer never sees. When they close, the ticket shows only the outcome. The customer reopens it a week later; the support team asks whether this is the same ticket or a new one. *Breaks:* uniform visibility, and "reopen" as a free state toggle. *Says:* the seed's ticket example ([task-concept.md](edges/task-concept.md#guide-level-explanation)) needs per-participant visibility and relayed outcomes; a reopen is a retry, and Memar says a retry "is a new interaction that happens to refer back to an earlier one" ([Process → Continuation and Retry](../../process/process.md#continuation-and-retry)), which in this graph is the shape of thing's [supersedes](../../identity/thing/edges/supersedes/supersedes.md) relation.
8. **A court appoints an expert (civil-law jurisdictions, including Iran).** A judge, under authority the law gives, assigns an expert to assess a disputed building. The expert may decline for a conflict of interest. The fee is set by the court, not negotiated. The result is a report added to the case. This is the premise that a case UUID is also a task "so a judge can assign an expert" ([case.research.001.md](../../rights/case/case.research.001.md#findings)). *Breaks:* assignment as one user's will, consent assumed, and a fee as a private contract. *Says:* who may assign comes from an authority (a law, a case), the assignee's acceptance is its own act, and the task's output lands in another domain's record. [Agency → Delegation](../../../docs/agency.md#delegation) models exactly this: a principal delegates responsibility and authority to an agent.
9. **Recurring and event-triggered work (finance and manufacturing).** "Monthly financial report" produces "Financial report - 2024/09", approved by the CFO role ([repetitive](edges-loop/repetitive/repetitive.md)). The organization keeps books in the Solar Hijri calendar, whose year begins at the spring equinox, so "monthly" and "fiscal year" are not the Gregorian ones, and public holidays shift the due date. A kanban card for an empty bin creates a replenishment task with no schedule and no human assigner. *Breaks:* repetition as a time-only property (`time_based`), and "made by rule, not by a human" as the only trigger. *Says:* instances are created by a rule on an event; time is one event among others; the calendar is an input to the rule, not part of the task.
10. **A task as the commit message (this repository's own practice).** Decision-42 changed three RFCs; nobody was "assigned"; the task was written after the work and holds the discussion, the rejected alternatives and links to three content revisions ([task.handoff.md](task.handoff.md#from-organization-ideas-backlog--task): Change Set). *Breaks:* tasks as future work, and any model where a task needs an assignee or a deadline to exist. *Says:* the retroactive task is a task, which is the strongest evidence for defining the node by intent and not by duty.
11. **A software agent or machine as the worker.** A billing agent is delegated "reconcile the day's transactions"; a CNC machine is delegated a cutting job. A person remains answerable. *Breaks:* skill matching that assumes a human skill register, and accountability that follows the doer. *Says:* [delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md) already notes persons "can delegate to things (as a user type)"; [Agency → Responsibility and Authority](../../../docs/agency.md#responsibility) separates responsibility, authority and capability, so the principal's accountability does not move to the doer.

**What the scenarios say together (agent, Tentative).** A task is not always assigned (6, 10), not always required (6, 10), not always future (10), not a tree (2), not complete because its children are (3, 4), not performed once (7, 9), not held by one worker throughout (4), not obligatory for the assignee (8), not visible equally to all (4, 7), not recurring by a clock alone (9), and not always done by a human (11). The kernel therefore cannot assume an assigner, an assignee, a deadline, a single parent, a completion rule, or a human. Each of those is either a relation with rules or a pluggable capability.

**The second layer: the underlying problem the answers assume.** The first layer above answers what was asked. The reading this research assumes beneath it is that the owner's actual problem is not "how do we model a task" but "how do we keep a task model from filling up with the vocabulary of whichever tool (Scrum, a helpdesk, an HR system, a factory system) contributed the next label". Evidence: most of the ten edge folders are label vocabularies of a particular practice (leave types, Scrum columns, production approvals, campaign kinds), and each ends in the same unanswered question, who may set this and what does it trigger. A reader who holds a different reading of the problem can set the proposals aside without discarding the scenarios.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

#### The removal test run on the task itself (agent, Tentative)
[What Earns Foundational Status](../../../docs/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status): express the part of reality the candidate covers through the remaining abstractions; if that forces the fabrication or the distortion of a foundational abstraction, the candidate earns consideration. Without task:

- The Decided "Decision is a label on a Task" has no host, so a root Decision concept would be fabricated, which the domain documents reject.
- The Decided replacement of the commit message needs a thing that holds discussion, outcome and linked revisions together; without it the central Snapshot, which was removed, is fabricated.
- "A project is a task" would split into a Project entity, an Issue entity and a Ticket entity, which is the category proliferation the seed RFC exists to prevent ([Foundational Concepts Should Remain Few](../../../docs/modularity.md#foundational-concepts-should-remain-few) says the usual error is proliferation).

The guard in the other direction also applies: a plausible alternative is that a task is only a content in a timeline plus a group, with no node of its own ("does Task decompose into Group + Content + Relation + State + Permission?", [task.handoff.md](task.handoff.md#open-questions)). That is not refuted here; the removal test shows the *work* a task does needs a home, not that the home must be a separate type. Marked Tentative for that reason.

#### Proposed kernel (agent, Tentative)
Five elements. Each is stated with its who-may-create-or-remove and what-it-triggers, because [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement) forbids a bare edge.

| # | Element | Who may create or remove | What it triggers | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | **Identity** — a task is a thing; its identity is the thing domain's | any participant with authority in the organization creates; removal is never a delete (see finding 4) | the task exists; a `task_created` event | Decided ([task.handoff.md → Khayyam conversion](task.handoff.md#khayyam-conversion-2026-10-03): identity is `ThingUUID`; `Field_TaskID`, `Field_TasksID`) |
| K2 | **Intent as content** — the task is also a content: text, images, audio, with a timeline for its discussion | the creator writes; edits are events on the same identity | nothing beyond events; discussion lives on the timeline | Decided (task is also a Content; content identity across edits is a decided direction, [content.handoff.md](../../knowledge/content/content.handoff.md)) |
| K3 | **`part_of`** `(task)-[:part_of]->(task)` — containment, many parents allowed, acyclic | the owner (or an authority) of the child and of the parent; removal is an event, never a rewrite | a `task_reparented` event; a cycle guard (a rule) refuses a loop | Agent proposal; replaces the `Parent` label and the `Move` label of [edges/task](edges/task/task.md) |
| K4 | **`blocks`** `(task)-[:blocks]->(task)` — the first must reach its end before the second may start | an authority over the blocked task; removal an event | a `task_blocked` / `task_unblocked` event; enforcement is a rule attached through `condition_is` | Agent proposal; replaces the `Blocking` label |
| K5 | **`changes`** `(task)-[:changes]->(content version)` — the revisions the task produced, which is the Change Set | the task's participants when they save a revision | the revision is reachable from the task; "all states related to Task-42" is a walk | Decided as a shape ("the association lives as the Task's linked content revisions", [task.handoff.md](task.handoff.md#from-organization-ideas-backlog--task)); the edge's name is working: content's handoff lists `produces` and `changes` as working verbs ([content.handoff.md](../../knowledge/content/content.handoff.md)) |

Not in the kernel by this proposal: assigner, assignee, reviewer, approver, deadline, priority, recurrence, completion, outcome vocabulary, skill requirement, place, budget, campaign. Participation is Group's ("participant/member management belongs to Group, not Task", Decided); the kernel also exposes the generic `condition_is` attachment through which a rule is connected ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement): "Kernel does not reference the rule domain directly").

The task's own lifecycle is not a kernel table of states. It is the shared [status](../../knowledge/status/status.md) mechanism applied to tasks, with the vocabulary question left to the owner (finding 4, Lifecycle; owner question 9).

#### Pluggable: the capability each would own (agent, Tentative)
Each row: the relationship and its processing together; who may create or remove it; what it triggers. A pluggable carries a capability name, not a domain name ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement)).

| Capability | Relationship | Processing | Who may create/remove | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **completion** | `(task)-[:completion_policy]->(rule)` | derives a parent's closing proposal or outcome from its children under a policy: all required, quorum, any, with `optional` children ([optional](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/optional/optional.md)) | the parent's owner attaches or detaches | on a child's `task_closed` event it evaluates the parent; emits `task_closure_proposed`, never silently closes |
| **recurrence** | `(task)-[:recurs_by]->(rule)` plus thing's `template` for instance-to-template | creates instances from a template task on an event (a calendar moment, a signal, a closure) in the organization's calendar; owns the missed-instance policy | the template's owner | each firing creates a task and links it with `template`; the shift is its staffed, time-windowed case |
| **skill-matching** | `(task)-[:requires]->(skill)` with a level (the MUST/SHOULD/MAY set) | suggests assignees whose skills match ([task.md](task.md#assign-task-rules)); with `Mandatory`, refuses an assignment that lacks the skill | the task's owner sets requirements; a rule engine suggests | `assignment_suggested`; `assignment_refused` |
| **campaign** | `(task)-[:campaign]->(---)` loop with attached product set, label set, audience group and budget reference | limits and selects products and audiences, sends to the joined group, honours un-joining | the campaign task's owner | sends and budget draws; join and leave events |
| **negotiation** | a task of the negotiation kind about an invoice or an offer | offers, counter-offers, acceptance; accepted result recorded as a decision that changes the price fact it concerns | the parties; authority per price rules | an accepted decision; the effect on the invoice or price is owned on that side |
| **leave** (Deferred) | task kinds for absence (the vocabulary in [ownership staff](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/staff/staff.md)) | balances and entitlements, approval consequences | staff and approvers | balance changes; shift effects |

Two things are deliberately *not* proposed as new modules because the graph already holds them: approval (shared `approval_needed` and `approved` loops on thing exist; what is missing is only the closure consequence, which is a completion rule), and capacity diagnosis (a pluggable analytics under [management analytics](../../../modules-plugable/management/analytics/), per [organizational reasoning](../organizational-reasoning.handoff.md#open-questions); analytics is never kernel, [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement)).

### 3. Rival models

Marks: **Tentative** (leaning), **Explored** (explored, unresolved), **Deferred**.

#### The models
- **M1 — one node, many labels (the status quo reading; Explored).** The task node (content and group) carries label sets: task-to-user labels (`Participant`, `Assigner`, `Reviewer`, `Assigned`, `Approver`, `WaitList`, `Invited`), task-to-task labels (`Blocking`, `Move`, `Parent`), kinds (`Project`, `Meeting`, `Event`), workflow states (Scrum, XP, production approvals), rejection labels. Source: [edges/task](edges/task/task.md), [task-labels](edges-loop/task-labels/task-labels.md), [ownership user](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/user/user.md). *For:* matches every file; no restructuring. *Against:* each label set is a bare edge as recorded (no who, no trigger); it mixes at least four axes (position, stage, outcome, approval) in one vocabulary; it freezes one practice's words into the model ([Premature Classification Check](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#premature-classification-check): "the label arrived before the rules").
- **M2 — lean kernel plus reuse (Tentative, recommended).** The five-element kernel of finding 2. Participation is Group membership with a Role (assignment is a delegation, its provenance is who created the membership); statuses come from the shared status mechanism; stage vocabularies (Scrum, XP, Eisenhower), kinds, and every capability in the finding 2 table are pluggable. *For:* every label set either collapses into an existing domain or acquires a rule and an owner; the task stops absorbing practices. *Against:* it asks Group to carry roles-in-a-task, which the group domain has not ratified (membership plus role as separate edges is its "resolved direction", [group.md](../group/group.md#resolved-via-source-inspection)); the lifecycle remains thin until the status home is decided.
- **M3 — Task and Attempt as two node kinds (Explored).** A task is the declared unit; every enactment is an Attempt with its own participants, timing and outcome, mirroring [Process Definition and Process Instance](../../process/process.md#process-definition-and-process-instance). *For:* it answers reopen, retry, shift handover, and recurrence cleanly. *Against:* it doubles the nodes in the common case; an attempt that has its own intent, participants and outcome is by Memar's own test ([Process Composition](../../process/process.md#process-composition): a sub-process is separately nameable when it has "a sufficiently independent intent, boundary, responsibility, outcome") a child task, so M3 reduces to M2 with a convention (an attempt is a `part_of` child, a retry is a `supersedes`). The difference left is only whether the kernel *requires* the split. Not recommended as a requirement; kept open as a convention.
- **M4 — task as commitment (Deferred).** A task exists only as an obligation of an agent to a principal; work with no obligation is not a task. This is the seed's unresolved 8 ("Task may reduce to ... Commitment, Responsibility, or Agreement"). *For:* clean assignment, accountability and fee semantics (scenarios 4, 8, 11); it would link the domain to contract and delegate. *Against:* scenarios 6 and 10 are tasks with no obligation; the definition then excludes work the Decided material calls tasks (the chat records of sessions, the commit-message replacement). Deferred until the Agreement and delegate domains can say what a commitment is.

#### How each model meets the scenarios (agent judgment)
Ok = handled without distortion; strain = handled with a convention or an unratified assumption; fails = needs a fabricated or bent node.

| Scenario | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 solo reminder | strain (group of one) | ok | ok | strain (agent = principal) |
| 2 headquarters graph | fails (one `Parent` label) | ok (`part_of` + `blocks`) | ok | ok |
| 3 partial completion, rework | fails | ok (completion rule, outcomes) | ok | strain |
| 4 handover, cancel, visibility | strain | ok (roles, events, authorization) | ok | ok |
| 5 ringi | strain | ok (approval loops + completion) | ok | strain |
| 6 communal harvest | strain (assigner label with no assigner) | ok | ok | fails |
| 7 ticket, reopen | strain | ok (supersedes, relayed outcome) | ok | strain |
| 8 expert by court | strain | ok (delegation + acceptance) | ok | ok |
| 9 recurrence, kanban | strain (`repetitive` only) | ok (recurrence rule) | ok | strain |
| 10 task as commit message | ok | ok | strain (attempt after the fact) | fails |
| 11 software agent | strain | ok (delegation to a thing) | ok | ok |

Reading: M1 survives only the cases that look like a tracker. M4 fails exactly the two cases where the domain's own Decided material is a task without a duty. M3 matches M2 everywhere once an attempt is allowed to be a child task. M2 meets every case at the price of two unratified reuses (Group roles, status home).

#### Smaller choice 1: containment and dependence, one relation or two (Tentative: two)
The existing file puts `Blocking`, `Move` and `Parent` on one candidate edge `(task)-[:relation]->(task)` ([edges/task](edges/task/task.md)). Containment says "this is a part of that"; dependence says "this must end before that begins". They differ in cycle rules, in what removal means, and in what they trigger (a re-parenting versus a freeze). [Group](../group/group.md#resolved-via-source-inspection) already resolves the same question the same way for membership ("decomposes relationships into several small, single-purpose edges"). `Move` is not a relation: it is the event of changing `part_of`, whose history ([Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates)) already answers "where did this come from". Counter-argument: a generic edge with labels keeps one place to look; against it, [Modules](../../modules.md#placement) calls an apparently simple `related_to` "either a real capability with rules or a shortcut over a deeper path".

#### Smaller choice 2: participation labels versus Group, Role and delegation (Tentative: reuse)
The five task-relation files filed under ownership ([user](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/user/user.md), [group](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/group/group.md), [assigner](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/assigner/assigner.md), [staff](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/staff/staff.md)) each say "strip" and point back to the task. Read in turn: `Participant`, `Reviewer`, `Approver`, `Assigned` are membership with a role in the task's team; `Assigner` is the creator of that membership (an edge can record "which user set it" and when, [status.md](../../knowledge/status/status.md#policies-rules)); `WaitList` and `Invited` are an invitation that is not yet accepted, which the [invite](../../../modules-plugable/management/invite/invite.md) capability and the assignee's acceptance already cover; the leave labels (`TakeLeave`, `Sick`, `Maternity`) are kinds of task or of a staff capability, not edges. If this holds, the task owns no participant vocabulary at all. It depends on a Group answer ([group.md unresolved 1](../group/group.md#unresolved-questions)): whether a task is a group by embedding or by acquiring a type.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions

The domain, once decided, has to answer each of these. Questions are written to favour no answer where unsettled; a settled item says so and cites its decision.

#### Identity
- *Settled:* a task's identity is the thing domain's identity ([task.handoff.md](task.handoff.md#khayyam-conversion-2026-10-03), Decided). The Go source's comment that it was "base on group to support some group rules e.g. invite" is the argument for the reverse and is reported below.
- Does the identity stay the same when the intent is edited, when the task moves under another parent, when it becomes a member-bearing group, and when it is reopened? Which of these is a new identity?
- Is a template task and each instance it creates one identity or two? Is a retry a new identity that refers back, or the same identity in a new state?
- If one identity is at once a task, a group and a case ([case.research.001.md](../../rights/case/case.research.001.md)), which operations of each may reach it, and what removes one role without ending the others?
- Who may mint a task: any participant in an organization, only a role, a rule engine, an authority acting under law?

#### Lifecycle
- What states does a task have, and which are positions in the lifecycle (declared, underway, ended) as against stages of a method (backlog, doing, done), outcomes (success, partial, rejected, cancelled) or approvals (approved, approval needed)? The vocabulary in [task-status.kh](edges-loop/status/task-status.kh) (`ReadyForDevelopment`, `InProgress`, `Close`, `Success`) mixes at least three of those.
- Is a state set or derived? A parent's progress derived from its children is discovered data and must not be stored as acquired ([Acquired vs. Discovered](../../../docs/modeling.md#acquired-data-vs-discovered-data)); the `Progress` criterion type in [criterion](../../identity/thing/edges/criterion/criterion.md) is stored as a value.
- May an ended task return to underway, or does reopening create a new task? What does each say for the history the task is meant to preserve?
- Is cancellation distinct from failure and from rejection, and what happens to effects already produced ([Process → Cancellation](../../process/process.md#cancellation))?
- May a task with open children end? What does each child then carry?
- May a task be deleted? Decided material says history is the point ("nothing lost"); the question is whether any removal is allowed.
- When the only participant leaves the organization, what is the task?

#### Authorization
- Who may create a task, and who may create one *for* another? Does the receiver have to accept, and does silence mean acceptance? (Scenario 8 says an expert may decline.)
- Who may change the intent text, the dependencies, the containment, the participants, the outcome?
- Who may read a task, a task's children, its discussion? Does a participant in a parent see the children? Does an outside requester see only the outcome (scenario 7)? Which module owns this, [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md) or [access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md), whose own documents flag the overlap?
- Across organizations: may a customer's ticket reference a task of the supplier, and who owns the shared record?
- Where does authority to assign come from when it is a law or a case, not a person (scenario 8)? That is [delegate's](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md) open question on authority versus permission.

#### Rules
- Which rules does the domain itself need, and which belong to organizations: cycle guard on `part_of` and `blocks`; the effect of `blocks` on start and on assignment; completion policy; skill requirement levels; recurrence and missed-instance policy; deadline and escalation; approval consequences; capacity warnings?
- Where each rule attaches: through `condition_is` from the task, or on the relation, or on the module that owns the capability?
- Is the MUST/SHOULD/MAY set generic constraint vocabulary shared with other domains (rights law, rule conditions), configured per use, or the task's own? ([requires](edges/requires/requires.md#open-questions) asks the same.)
- Who may attach or remove each rule, and what does removal leave of what the rule already produced? (The same question the [pricing](../../../modules-plugable/economics/pricing/edges/pricing/pricing.md) lessons put to rule-stated prices.)

#### Events
- Which occurrences are events the task exposes as an EventTarget ([Modularity → Event as a Module Capability](../../../docs/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)): created, intent revised, participant added or removed, accepted or declined, started, blocked or unblocked, reparented, child ended, ended with outcome, reopened, deadline passed?
- Who consumes them: calendar, notification, budget, analytics for capacity, the rules engine, content (Change Set)?
- Which of these is a request or response that already exists and needs no event type of its own?
- A correction (a wrongly closed task) is a new fact referencing the one it corrects, never a rewrite ([Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates)). Does the task domain accept that as stated?

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each is one paragraph, as a sketch: not a design, and none is to be built before the owner decides.

1. **`part_of` (kernel edge; Tentative).** `(task)-[:part_of]->(task)`. A task may have several parents (a shared inspection serves two phases); the graph is acyclic and a cycle is refused by a rule attached through `condition_is`, since the group domain has already flagged cycles as a risk for its own hierarchy ([group.md unresolved 4](../group/group.md#unresolved-questions)). Created and removed by someone with authority over both ends; removal and re-creation are events, which is how "Move from another project" is recorded without a label. Triggers `task_reparented`. Does not itself close a parent.
2. **`blocks` (kernel edge; Tentative).** `(task)-[:blocks]->(task)`. A fact that one task must end before another starts, created by an authority over the blocked task. The edge does not freeze anything; a rule decides what a blocked task may do (start, be assigned, be closed). Triggers `task_blocked` and `task_unblocked`. It is not the same thing as the shared [block](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/block/block.md) status, which marks a record blocked by justice or fraud detection (reported below).
3. **`changes` (kernel edge; Tentative on name, Decided on shape).** `(task)-[:changes]->(content version)`. The Change Set: the revisions a task produced. Created by the participants who save a revision under the task; never removed. Triggers nothing except that the revision becomes reachable from the task. Replaces the central snapshot. Its relation to the decision a rule came from ([task_reason](../../computer/rule/edges/task_reason/task_reason.md)) is the same shape and should be one vocabulary.
4. **completion (pluggable, Tentative).** Attaches a policy to a parent: all required, quorum, or "optional" children via the shared [optional](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/optional/optional.md) marker. On each child's end it evaluates the parent and emits a closure proposal; the owner or a rule may then close with an outcome. It computes progress as discovered data, so no one stores a percentage. Removing the module leaves every task closeable by hand.
5. **recurrence (pluggable, Tentative).** A template task, a trigger rule over the organization's calendar or an event, and a missed-instance policy (skip, catch up once, catch up all). Each firing creates a task and records `template`. It owns calendar systems and holiday shifts through the calendar domain, never through the task. The shift is the case where instances carry a staff member and a time window; [shift.handoff](../shift/shift.handoff.md#open-questions) holds the far-end question this module would answer.
6. **skill-matching (pluggable, Tentative).** `(task)-[:requires]->(skill)` with a level, plus the matching processing implied by [task.md](task.md#assign-task-rules): suggest, rank, and (for `Mandatory`) refuse. Suggestion is discovered data and must be removable without losing the requirement. Non-human assignees (scenario 11) need a capability register, which `knowledge/skill` may or may not offer for things; unverified.
7. **acceptance (an extension of delegate, Explored).** The assignee's own act of accepting or declining a delegated task, with a timeout policy and a record of who delegated. It is probably [delegate's](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md) capability scoped to a task, not a task module; listed because no document says who owns it.
8. **per-participant visibility and relayed outcomes (Explored).** The ticket case needs an external requester to see the outcome without the graph beneath it. This is an authorization rule plus an outcome relay, and probably belongs to [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md); the question is which module.
9. **campaign (pluggable merge, Tentative).** One capability for marketing, advertisement and campaign tasks: product set and label set that limit what is promoted, an audience group with join and un-join, and a budget reference (one with `budget`, not a second `settlement-reference`). Home: either `modules-plugable/management/campaign` (instantiated through a task) or an economics home; the filing rule ("filed under the domain through which it is instantiated") points to management; owner question 12.
10. **outcome vocabulary (a status mechanism question, Explored).** A small set of outcomes (completed, partial, rejected, cancelled, abandoned) separate from lifecycle position. [failed](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/failed/failed.md) is the only existing shared one and speaks of transactions; the task's outcomes may reuse the shared status home or live under `task/status/`; owner questions 8 and 9.

### 7. Test scenarios

Given/when/then in prose, so another session can write tests once the owner decides. Each is marked with what it depends on: **kept** if it follows from settled text, **M2** if it assumes the recommended model, **Q n** if it assumes the default of open question n.

1. **A task without an assignee exists (kept in spirit; Q 1).** Given a participant records, after the work, a task holding a discussion and links to three content revisions. When it is saved. Then it exists with no assignee, no deadline and no team, and its three revisions are reachable from it (scenario 10).
2. **The retroactive task is a task (Q 1).** Given the task of test 1. When someone asks for "all states related to this task". Then the answer is the walk over `changes`, with no snapshot entity.
3. **A group is acquired by joining (M2, Q 2).** Given a task created by one person with no members. When three neighbours join. Then the task has a team of three, each with a role, and before the first join it had none (scenario 6).
4. **One sub-task, two parents (M2, Q 4).** Given inspection I and phases Electrical and Plumbing. When I is made `part_of` both. Then I appears under both, is one task, and closing it is one event seen by both.
5. **A containment cycle is refused (M2).** Given A `part_of` B and B `part_of` C. When someone makes C `part_of` A. Then the attempt is refused by the cycle guard and nothing changes.
6. **Dependence does not imply containment (M2).** Given task P `blocks` task Q. When P is not ended. Then Q is not part of P, and what Q may do is decided only by the rule attached to the block, not by the edge.
7. **Re-parenting keeps the story (M2).** Given a task under project X. When it is made `part_of` project Y. Then the old containment remains in the history as an event, and the task has the same identity.
8. **Completion is a proposal, not a silent close (M2, Q 3).** Given a parent with a completion policy "all required" and two required children and one optional. When both required children end and the optional does not. Then a closure is proposed to the parent's owner, the parent does not end by itself, and no stored percentage changes.
9. **Partial outcome (Q 3, Q 8).** Given a production order for ten chairs and an accepted delivery of nine. When the order is closed. Then its outcome is partial, recorded as such, not as success and not as failure.
10. **Cancellation keeps effects (kept in spirit: Process → Cancellation).** Given a task whose first step already produced an effect. When the task is cancelled. Then its outcome is cancelled, the effect stands, and no rollback is implied.
11. **A mandatory skill gates assignment (M2).** Given a task requiring the skill "surgeon" at level `Mandatory`. When someone without it is proposed as assignee. Then the assignment is refused and a `assignment_refused` event records who, when, and why.
12. **An assignment can be declined (Q 6).** Given a court delegates an expert's task. When the expert declines for a conflict of interest. Then the task has no assignee, the declination is a recorded fact, and the delegating authority is told.
13. **Handover mid-task (M2).** Given a task owned by nurse A. When the shift ends and nurse B is made responsible. Then the task is the same identity, A's participation is ended by an event, B's begins, and the history shows both.
14. **An external requester sees only the outcome (Q 11).** Given a ticket depending on three internal tasks. When they end. Then the requester sees the outcome relayed to the ticket and cannot read any internal task or its discussion.
15. **A reopen is a retry (Q 5).** Given an ended ticket. When the requester asks again. Then a new task is created that `supersedes` the first, the first stays in the graph, and neither is rewritten.
16. **An instance comes from a template and a trigger (M2).** Given a template "Monthly financial report" and a calendar in which the month ends on a holiday. When the trigger rule fires. Then an instance is created with the template link, a due date moved by the calendar, and the CFO role as the required approver.
17. **A trigger need not be time (M2).** Given a template "replenish bin" and a signal "bin empty". When the signal arrives. Then an instance is created with no schedule and no human assigner.
18. **A discovered progress is not stored (kept: Acquired vs Discovered).** Given a parent with four children, two ended. When progress is asked. Then it is computed from the children at the moment of asking, and nothing was stored that could drift.
19. **A software agent is delegated (Q 10).** Given a billing agent delegated "reconcile the day's transactions" by a role. When it fails. Then accountability stays with the delegating principal, the agent's failure is an outcome of the task, and the task is not reassigned silently.
20. **A removed rule leaves the task intact (M2).** Given a task whose completion policy rule is detached. When children end. Then no closure is proposed and the task stays closeable by hand; the earlier proposals stay in history.

### Contradictions found

Reported, not resolved, as asked. Each cites both sides.

1. **"Additive over Group" versus the question it is meant to answer.** Decided: "Task is not primitive: modeled as an additive entity over **Group**" ([task.handoff.md](task.handoff.md#from-organization-ideas-backlog--task); [group.md](../group/group.md#decisions-from-organization-ideas-backlog-2026-09-16)). Open in the same handoff: "does Task decompose into Group + Content + Relation + State + Permission?" ([Open Questions](task.handoff.md#open-questions), 1). The mechanism is open in [group.md unresolved 1](../group/group.md#unresolved-questions) (embedding versus a runtime type upgrade).
2. **The type upgrade the group says the task implements is not in the task.** [group.md unresolved 1](../group/group.md#unresolved-questions) says "the `task` module implements type-upgrade via a runtime graph `edges-loop`". No such loop exists under [edges-loop/](edges-loop/): the six folders are `process_based`, `repetitive`, `status`, `table`, `task-labels`, `time_based`. The Go identity declaration `type UUID group_p.UUID` ("It is base on group to support some group rules e.g. invite") is the only trace of the claim, and the Khayyam conversion reads it as an argument for the opposite of what it was attached to ([task.handoff.md](task.handoff.md#decisions)).
3. **The definition has two clauses and the domain's own cases need one.** [task.md](task.md) defines a task as work undertaken or attempted, and also as work required as a duty or for a fee. Decided material includes tasks with no duty (the chat records of sessions, the commit-message replacement). The seed lists the two readings as unresolved 7. Not resolved here; finding 1 proposes a split.
4. **`Blocking` collides with the shared `block` status.** [edges/task](edges/task/task.md) uses `Blocking` as "stuck" (a dependency); [block](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/block/block.md) is "by justice(admins) or fraud detection mechanism". Same stem, two meanings.
5. **The three marketing edges disagree on direction.** [advertisement](edges/advertisement/advertisement.md) is `(product)-[:task]->(advertisement)`; [marketing](edges/marketing/marketing.md) is `(task)-[:product]->(marketing)`; [campaign](edges/campaign/campaign.md) is `(task)-[:product]->(campaign)`. They are also filed under `edges/` though each reads as a classification of one node plus attachments, which [Modules → Edge buckets](../../modules.md#edge-buckets) places in `edges-loop/`.
6. **One relation in two homes.** [settlement-reference](edges/settlement-reference/settlement-reference.md) `(task)-[:account]->(reason)` and [budget](../../../modules-plugable/economics/budget/budget.md) `(account)-[:task]->(budget)` state the same budget-source relation from two ends. [One Authoritative Location](../../../docs/modeling.md#one-authoritative-location-per-concerns-data) names the defect.
7. **Task relations filed under ownership.** Five files in [rights/ownership/edges-loop](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/user/user.md) describe task relations (`user`, `group`, `assigner`, `staff`) and say "strip"; the task domain has no `edges/user`, `edges/group` or `edges/staff` to receive them (survey D8, catalogue P4).
8. **A seed that says it is kept outside git.** [task-concept.md](edges/task-concept.md) opens with a note that the owner "asked for this to be kept outside of git (not committed) as a placeholder", is tracked, sits in `edges/` though it is not an edge, and carries `RFC Number: 000000` (survey D18; [modules.handoff](../../modules.handoff.md)).
9. **Two kernel-status-style statements about `project`.** [modules.md](../../modules.md) says "`project` is just a label for `task` and MUST NOT add as thing level label"; [management/project](../project/project.md) exists as a sibling domain with the model `(task)-[:---]->(project)` (survey D9). Its own far end is `---`, which is the loop-edge spelling.
10. **Decision as a label versus decision as an edge target.** Decided: Decision is "a label/state on a Task (or sub-task)". [content.handoff.md](../../knowledge/content/content.handoff.md) lists `produces (Task→Decision)` and `changes (Decision→Content Version)` as working edge verbs, which make a Decision a node. The handoff marks the verbs "direction", not decided, so this is a tension, not a conflict; it decides whether K5 reads `(task)-[:changes]->` or goes through a decision node.
11. **A criterion type that stores a discoverable value.** [criterion](../../identity/thing/edges/criterion/criterion.md) lists `Progress` beside `StoryPoint`; progress of a parent is derivable from its children ([Acquired vs. Discovered](../../../docs/modeling.md#acquired-data-vs-discovered-data)).
12. **The handoff's state.** [task.handoff.md](task.handoff.md#status) is "Active — Task / Change Set decisions decided; canonical decomposition and full Task edge taxonomy still open", and its numbered open list skips 3.

## Recommendation (unratified)
The owner has not reviewed this, and nothing in it is decided.

1. **Define the node by intent, not by duty (Tentative, medium confidence).** A task is the declared intent of a unit of work; being required of someone and being undertaken are relations and events around it. This lets the retroactive task and the communal task stay tasks, and makes assignment a delegation. It changes the first paragraph of [task.md](task.md) and nothing else visible.
2. **Keep a five-element kernel (Tentative):** identity, intent as content, `part_of`, `blocks`, `changes`; plus the generic `condition_is` attachment. Everything else is a capability, a rule, or an existing domain.
3. **Choose M2; keep M3 as a convention and M4 deferred (Tentative).** M3's Attempt is a child task and a retry is `supersedes`; M4 waits on the agreement and delegate domains.
4. **Split containment from dependence and retire `Move` into an event (Tentative).**
5. **Let participation be Group, Role and delegation, and stop minting participant labels (Tentative, depends on the Group answer).**
6. **Treat completion, recurrence and skill-matching as pluggables, each with a stated who and trigger (Tentative).** Do not let the kernel compute a parent's state.
7. **Merge campaign, marketing and advertisement into one pluggable and give budget the one copy of the budget relation (Tentative).**
8. **Do not move, delete or convert anything on this evidence.** The mapping in finding 5 is a reading list for the sessions that will, after the owner answers. `TaskModel` stays unwritten ([Khayyam rule 6](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md#governing-rules): an empty abstraction is a design smell).
9. **Ask the three questions that change what the work is first:** question 1 (what is the node), question 2 (does a task have a team from birth), and question 3 (is completion kernel or pluggable).

## Open Questions
This is the research's eighth section. Only the owner can decide these. Ordered by what they change: those that change what the domain is first, then those that change how a part is done ([Ask rather than assume](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. They are also entered in the [handoff](task.handoff.md#research-001-questions-and-next-steps-2026-10-15).

1. **Is a task the declared intent of a unit of work, or a unit of work required of someone?** Default: the declared intent; duty and assignment are relations. Blocks: the rewrite of the first paragraph of [task.md](task.md), M4, the meaning of "required" in the Assign task rules, any `TaskModel`.
2. **Does a task have a team from birth (every task is a group), or does it acquire one when people join?** Default: acquired by first membership. Blocks: [group.md unresolved 1](../group/group.md#unresolved-questions), `TaskModel` ("over `GroupModel`"), the case premise ([case.research.001.md](../../rights/case/case.research.001.md)), the `edges-loop` the group says the task implements.
3. **Is completion propagation kernel or pluggable, and may a parent end with open children?** Default: pluggable; a parent may end only with an explicit outcome for each open child (cancelled, abandoned, or carried on). Blocks: the completion module, the "ticket closes automatically" example in the seed, test 8.
4. **May a task have several parents, and is `blocks` a separate relation from containment?** Default: several parents, acyclic, two relations. Blocks: the edge/task split, `Move`, the cycle-guard rule, test 4.
5. **Is a reopen a state change or a new task that supersedes the first?** Default: a new task that `supersedes`; "reopen" is the GUI word. Blocks: the lifecycle, test 15, the ticket model.
6. **Must an assignee accept, and does silence mean accept?** Default: accept is required; silence is not acceptance; the module is delegate's. Blocks: the assignment model, scenario 8, the acceptance extension.
7. **Do participant labels dissolve into Group plus Role plus delegation, with the assigner as the creator of the membership edge?** Default: yes. Blocks: retiring the four ownership task-relation files, the `Invited` and `WaitList` homes, the Group answer on roles.
8. **What is the outcome vocabulary, and does it live with the shared status mechanism?** Default: a small set (completed, partial, rejected, cancelled, abandoned) separate from lifecycle position, under the shared status home. Blocks: the `Success` and `Close` constants in [task-status.kh](edges-loop/status/task-status.kh), partial completion, tests 9 and 10.
9. **Where do statuses live for the task: `edges-loop/` or `task/status/<name>/`?** This is catalogue R4. Default: `status/<name>/` for lifecycle, `edges-loop/` for classification. Blocks: the folder of task-status.kh and the Scrum/XP vocabulary.
10. **Is the delegate to a thing (a software agent or machine) a first-class case, and does accountability stay with the principal?** Default: yes and yes. Blocks: skill-matching for non-humans, the user-type question in [delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md).
11. **Who may see which task?** Default: a participant sees the tasks they are on; an outsider sees only relayed outcomes. Blocks: scenario 7, the authorization versus access-control overlap, the support-ticket model.
12. **Where does the merged campaign capability live, and does `settlement-reference` yield to budget?** Default: `modules-plugable/management/campaign`; budget holds the one budget relation. Blocks: moving or deleting four edge folders, the economics home.
13. **Does `Progress` leave criterion?** Default: yes, as discovered data; estimates stay. Blocks: [criterion](../../identity/thing/edges/criterion/criterion.md) conversion, the completion module's progress.
14. **Do `project`, `shift` and the stage vocabularies (Scrum, XP, Eisenhower) become classifications or pluggable vocabularies of a task, not sibling domains?** Default: yes (catalogue Q3 default "demote project/department/shift"). Blocks: the project and shift folders, the stage mechanism.
15. **Is the decision a label on the task, or a node the task produces?** Default: a label on the task, with `changes` read directly from the task; a decision node is the later graduation if a decision needs its own lifecycle (votes, challenges). Blocks: the name and shape of K5, the content working verbs.
16. **May a task be created in another organization's space (a customer's ticket into a supplier's)?** Default: no; creation is always inside the creator's organization, and cross-organization work is participation. Blocks: ticket and exchange models, the authorization rules.
