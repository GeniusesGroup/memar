7. **Definitions used (verified against the governing document, not assumed).**

A pluggable owns "the relationship and its processing together" ([Capability Completeness](../../../docs/modularity.md#capability-completeness)).
   - *Role* — the domain document's own definition ([role.md](role.md)): "a set of connected behaviors, rights, obligations, beliefs, and norms as conceptualized by people in a social situation", which "may have a given individual social status or social position", is "occupied by individuals, who are called actors" (item 3 of its list), and may be "outdated or illegitimate" when conditions change (item 5). Three things are bundled in that sentence: rights, obligations, and expected behaviour. [Agency](../../../docs/agency.md#capability) says they are three things: "Capability ≠ Authority ≠ Responsibility ... should not be merged".
   - *Agent as a position in a relationship* — [Agency → Agent as a Relational Concept](../../../docs/agency.md#agent-as-a-relational-concept): the agent is "a role emerging from a relationship rather than ... a separate node"; and [Agency and Roles](../../../docs/agency.md#agency-and-roles): "modeling every named role as a node can obscure the actual domain structure"; roles are to be told apart from "who something is" versus "what position it currently occupies".
   - *Independent lifecycle* — [Concept Existence vs. Model Existence](../../../docs/modeling.md#concept-existence-vs-model-existence): an abstraction is justified by "an independent responsibility, behavioral boundary, or lifecycle", not by the existence of data or of a name.
   - *Acquired and discovered* — [Acquired Data vs. Discovered Data](../../../docs/modeling.md#acquired-data-vs-discovered-data), as the group research uses it.
   - *Role, the working vocabulary of this research (agent, Tentative; the proposal under test).* **Position**: a place in the structure of a context (an organization, a unit, a task, a case) that exists whether or not anyone holds it, and that is held for intervals. **Occupancy**: one holder's tenure of one position, for an interval. **Occupant**: the thing named by an occupancy (a person, an organization, a software agent). **Exerciser**: the thing that actually acts for an occupant that cannot act for itself. **Capacity**: a part played in one relation (plaintiff, assigner, buyer). The word *seat* is avoided: [economics/service](../../../modules-plugable/economics/service/edges/item-seat/item-seat.md) already uses `seat` for a place that is sold (a theatre or flight seat), and the two would collide.

## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**What the corpus already uses the word for.** A search of the repository finds the word doing at least seven different jobs. The first column is the use; the last says which job it is, by the vocabulary just defined.

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The role domain gives an organization one answer to four questions about its structure of places: *which places exist* (in which context, under which unit, answering to which other place), *who holds each one now*, *who held it before and by what act*, and *what is expected of whoever holds it*, the last by reference only. The domain's concept is the **position and its occupancy**. It is deliberately not the capacity in one relation (that is the relation's own edge), not the bundle of authority an occupant exercises (that is delegate's and authorization's, attached by rule), and not a job title (a text, a convention). The sentence of the dictionary entry is read as the *expectation* part and stays a reference: the position carries a statement of what is expected, and rules attached to it carry what is required.

**Why a domain at all, when Memar says not to model every role as a node.** The test is the independent lifecycle of [Concept Existence vs. Model Existence](../../../docs/modeling.md#concept-existence-vs-model-existence). A *capacity* has none: the plaintiff of one case exists exactly as long as the case names her, and [plaintiff.md](../../rights/case/edges/plaintiff/plaintiff.md) rightly rejects a role node for it. A *position* has one that no relation of its holder gives it: it can be declared before anyone is found, can be vacant, can be filled by one person or several in turn, can be moved to another unit, can be abolished while its last holder is still there, and has its own rules for who may begin and end it. Scenarios 1 to 5 each depend on that. So the two readings of Memar's caution are both kept: where the "role" is a position in one relationship (an agent for a principal), it stays an edge; where it is a place that outlives its holders, it is a node.

**Scenarios.** Eleven, from several cultures and industries. Each states what it breaks and what it says.

1. **A hospital ward round the clock (any country).** The charge nurse of a ward must always be someone; three nurses share the post over three shifts; at 07:00 one hands over to the next, and an operation under way continues under the new nurse ([task.research.001.md](../task/task.research.001.md) scenario 4 and test 13). A nurse on sick leave stays employed but does not hold the post that day. *Breaks:* "a role is a job title of a person"; "a role has one holder"; "leave ends employment or nothing"; "vacancy is a flag someone sets". *Says:* the position persists; the holder at an instant is a fact of its own; being employed, being on duty and holding the post are three different facts; coverage is a requirement of the position.
2. **An Iranian joint-stock company (the board, the managing director, the signatories).** The company's gazette lists who may sign and the rule "two of the three, with the seal"; a new gazette notice replaces a manager while open contracts continue ([organization.research.001.md](../../identity/organization/organization.research.001.md) scenario 1; [user.research.001.md](../../identity/user/user.research.001.md) scenario 3). In several jurisdictions a legal person may be elected to a board and must name a natural person to act for it (illustrative; the exact provision was not checked). *Breaks:* authority as a flag on a user; the occupant is a person; the one who is appointed is the one who acts; the gazette date is the day it was recorded. *Says:* the position (signatory) is held by an occupant that may be an organization; an exerciser acts for it; the threshold is a rule of the position's context; effective date and recorded date differ.
3. **A Japanese company (concurrent posts, April transfers, titles without line authority).** One person is head of one department and director of another at once (concurrent posting); on one day in spring the organization reassigns hundreds of people; an adviser keeps a senior title with no line responsibility (illustrative). *Breaks:* one person, one post; a change of post is a change of person; a title always carries authority. *Says:* a user holds several occupancies in one organization at once; a mass reassignment is many ended and begun occupancies with one effective instant; title is a classification, authority is a different attachment.
4. **A village council with reserved and rotating places (India's local councils, illustrative).** A fixed share of the places in a council is reserved for women and for scheduled categories, and which wards carry the reservation rotates between elections by lot; the term is five years. *Breaks:* "eligibility is a property of the person"; "a role is the same in every election". *Says:* eligibility belongs to the position and changes between terms by a rule that is not the person's; the same position (the ward's seat in the council) is declared again each term or is carried with a changed rule; the term is a bounded interval with its own end.
5. **Succession and regency (a hereditary office; any chain of command that names an acting commander).** The holder dies and the next in a fixed line holds the office at the same instant, with no vacancy; where the successor is a minor, a regent exercises the office in the minor's name; when a commander is absent the next in rank acts without becoming the commander. *Breaks:* "vacancy is the state between holders"; "the holder is the one who acts". *Says:* occupancy has modes (holder, acting, exerciser for); succession is a rule that fires on the end of an occupancy; the exerciser is a different thing from the occupant.
6. **A franchise or bank with four hundred branches (any country).** "Branch manager" is a job description written once at headquarters (duties, required skills, authority limits); each branch has its own branch-manager position, with its own holder, vacancy and local variation; headquarters changes the description in spring. *Breaks:* "a role is either a type or an instance"; "changing the description changes every holder at once". *Says:* three levels are needed and the corpus names one: the description (a template), the position (an instance in a context), the occupancy (a tenure). The template reaches the instances by the rule of [thing's `template`](../../identity/thing/edges/template/template.md): instantiation copies, the copy is a new thing, and the maker's lineage is `derived_from` ([thing.research.001.md](../../identity/thing/thing.research.001.md) findings 2 and 6).
7. **A one-person organization (a sole proprietor with no employee).** The owner holds the post of director, accountant, buyer and cashier, all at once; the system's job is to notice that the load is too much and to suggest a hiring task and a delegation ([organizational-reasoning.handoff.md](../organizational-reasoning.handoff.md), Ambiguities Resolved; its own Assumption says the example may be too degenerate to generalize from). *Breaks:* "a position needs a distinct person"; "a role count above a number means hire" (rejected there as a static rule). *Says:* one user may hold many positions; load is derived from occupancies and tasks, never stored; the diagnosis is not a rule.
8. **An AI-agent fleet (this organization's own).** A "Database Agent" position is held by a software agent whose behaviour changes when its model is replaced; a human reviewer holds the review position for the same task; accountability stays with the principal who set the agent to the post ([role.handoff.md](role.handoff.md) Decisions; [task.research.001.md](../task/task.research.001.md) test 19). The decided direction says a role is a focus area and never a knowledge boundary. *Breaks:* "an occupant is a human"; "a role lists the tools it knows"; "the occupant is the same thing for the life of the post". *Says:* the occupant is any user-model thing; replacing the underlying model is a question of the occupant's identity (user and thing researches), not of the position; the focus of a position is a classification, and what it requires is a separate list.
9. **A court (a standing judge, a per-case seat, a juror drawn by lot).** A state has a post of judge in a court, held for years; a case seats particular judges for that case ([judge-assignment](../../../modules-plugable/rights/judge-assignment/judge-assignment.md)); a juror is summoned by lot, may not decline, and serves for one trial. The decided judge edge points at the user, never at a role ([judge.md](../../rights/case/edges/judge/judge.md)); whether a standing office exists with no case is the open Q1 of [judicial-office](../../../modules-plugable/rights/judicial-office/judicial-office.handoff.md). *Breaks:* "occupying is always consented to"; "a position belongs to an organization". *Says:* the standing post and the per-case seat are two positions in two contexts (the court, the case), which is consistent with the decided edge; compelled occupancy exists; and the case's own capacities (plaintiff) stay relations.
10. **A volunteer rota with no employment (a mosque committee's duty week, a village fire brigade on call, the host of the month in a rotating savings circle).** Nobody is paid, nobody is hired, a rota names who holds the duty each week, and members swap; the duty is a real post with a count and a window ([group.research.001.md](../group/group.research.001.md) scenario 2). *Breaks:* "shifts belong to staff"; "occupancy implies a contract". *Says:* occupancy and rota are independent of employment; the staff domain's lifecycle (hire, fire, end) is one way to come to hold a post, not the only one.
11. **An open class: "seller" on a marketplace, "member" of a community, "driver" on a platform.** Anyone who passes a check may act as a seller; there is no limit, no vacancy, no succession, no reporting line, and no one is ever "the" seller. *Breaks:* "every role is a position". *Says:* a name that has no count, no vacancy and no term is not a position but a classification of users that a rule grants (a loop-edge and a rule); it is the authority-bundle job (finding 3, model M4) and not this domain's concept. The test that separates it from a position is stated in finding 2: a position can be *full* or *vacant*.

**The second layer: the underlying problem the answers assume.** The first layer answers "what is a role". The reading assumed beneath it is that the owner's problem is not to model a word but to give three consumers a stable thing to point at: the organization-reasoning pipeline, which needs a structure of places and a load on people to diagnose capacity ([organizational-reasoning.handoff.md](../organizational-reasoning.handoff.md)); task assignment, which names "Group plus Role plus delegation" and finds no Role to point at ([task.research.001.md](../task/task.research.001.md) Q7); and rules and selectors, which today name a role by edge, by group or by a rule identity ([pos group](../../../modules-plugable/economics/pos/edges/group/group.md), [delegate](../../rights/delegate/protocol/data_model.kh), [reaction](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/reaction/reaction.handoff.md)). The proposal below is built to give those three one referent. It is guarded in the other direction: the position may be too thin to justify a domain, which is the rival M5 of finding 3, and that rival is not refuted here.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

#### The removal test run on the role itself (agent, Tentative)
[What Earns Foundational Status](../../../docs/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status) asks: express the part of reality the candidate covers through the remaining abstractions; if that forces the fabrication or distortion of a foundational one, the candidate is a candidate for the foundation. Run for the position: with no role domain, **group** would have to hold the place that outlives its holder (a group of zero members is neither dissolved nor a place), the seat limit, the reporting line, the vacancy and the succession, which [group.md](../group/group.md) and [group.research.001.md](../group/group.research.001.md) both refuse ("Not in the kernel ... role or position (role's and staff's)"); **task** would have to hold assignment to "whoever is the approver now", which it cannot do without a participant list the Decided material forbids; and **delegate** would have to delegate to a person where the organization means to delegate to a post. Each distortion is in a foundational domain, which is the pressure the test counts. The guard cuts the other way too: the whole of it could be expressed by a group that is classified as a position (model M2), so the position must show that the distortion is real. It does so only where a position has its own rules (count, term, eligibility, succession), which is why the kernel is small and the rest is rule.

The test keeps plaintiff, assigner, buyer and "seller" out of the kernel and keeps judge, charge nurse and managing director in.

### 3. Rival models

#### The models
- **M1 — a role is a label on a membership or participation (the status-quo reading; Explored).** Task participant labels (`Reviewer`, `Approver`, `Assigned`), the `joined` labels of group, and the case participant list all carry the role as a word on the relation. *For:* it needs no node; it is right for capacities. *Against:* the place cannot outlive its holder, there is no vacancy, and the same word means different things on different edges.
- **M2 — a role is a group, classified by the loop `(group)-[:role]->(---)` (the document's own resolved direction; Explored).** The judge is a group titled "judge" ([data/judge](data/judge/judge.md)); its members hold the role. *For:* no new edge; the seat count is the member limit; occupants are memberships; it is what the files say today. *Against:* the group research's own caution that this is the anti-pattern *unnecessary reification* unless the seat has a life of its own; it gives no place for eligibility, succession, exerciser, term or reporting line; and the group hierarchy would compute effective membership upward, which is wrong for positions (the director is not an occupant of a clerk's post).
- **M3 — a position is a thing with its own identity and context, and occupancy is the membership of that thing; capacities stay relations; authority, template, coverage and the rest are pluggable (agent, Tentative, recommended).** The kernel of finding 2. M2 survives *inside* it as the realization of occupancy, and loses only the claim that the position is nothing but a group.
- **M4 — a role is an authority bundle: a named set of permissions assigned to users (the access-control reading; Explored).** The legacy delegate, `GetRoleAccess`, `PrivateInRole`, `app_role` and POS "limit to roles" all read it so. *For:* it is what most software means; selectors become trivial. *Against:* it merges Capability, Authority and Responsibility, which [Agency](../../../docs/agency.md#capability) says should not be merged; it has no vacancy, no succession and no place in a structure; and its identity is a bundle, so two posts with the same permissions would be one role.
- **M5 — there is no role node: a role is only a position in a relationship, as Memar's agency reads it (Explored).** `agent_for`, plaintiff, assigner are edges; the organization's structure is groups, units and delegations. *For:* it is Memar's stated caution and the decided direction "Agent is a position, not an entity type"; it is exactly right for capacities and open classes. *Against:* it has nothing to say about a post that is vacant, about succession, about an acting holder, or about "the CFO" as a thing a task can be assigned to.
- **M6 — three levels: job (a template), position (an instance in a context), assignment (a tenure) (Tentative, as an addition to M3).** *For:* it is the only model that states the franchise case; the template reaches instances by thing's existing `template` rule. *Against:* it needs thing's `derived_from`, which does not exist; an organization of one branch does not need the top level, which is why it is a pluggable and not kernel.

Reading: **no single model meets all eleven**, and the failures are complementary. M3 fails only scenario 11 (the open class), where M4 and M5 are right; M5 and M1 are right wherever the thing is a capacity; M6 alone meets scenario 6. So the recommendation is a composition: M3 for the place, M5 and M1 for capacities and open classes (stated as a test, finding 2), M4 as a pluggable attached by rule (not the concept), M6 as the template pluggable. This is not a compromise for its own sake: each model is right for a different job of the word, which is the claim of finding 1.

#### Smaller choice 1: is occupancy its own record or the membership of the position (Tentative: the membership)
If a position is a member-bearing thing (group Q2) and a membership has an identity (group Q3), then the occupancy is that membership with the kind `occupant`, and the group's interval, kind and provenance are exactly what it needs; the events `member_joined` and `member_left` are `occupancy_began` and `occupancy_ended` under another name. A separate record would give two sources for one fact. *Against:* the group's membership assumes consent as the usual way in; compelled and inherited occupancy (scenarios 5, 9) are the other ways and the group research already allows them (Q6). *Dependency:* it holds only if group Q2 and Q3 are answered as their defaults; otherwise the role domain keeps its own occupancy record with the same fields. Either way the fields are the same, so the choice does not change the model, only where the record is stored.

#### Smaller choice 2: employment and occupancy are two relations (Tentative)
[staff.md](../staff/staff.md) says "Staff relates users to organizations as personnel"; its status loop is Hire, Fire, End. A person can be employed with no post (hired, post not yet assigned: `Reserved`, `Designation` in the hiring pipeline), can hold a post with no employment (a board director, a volunteer, a juror, scenario 10), and can hold two posts for one employment (scenario 3). So neither contains the other. *Proposal (agent):* employment is staff's record (user to organization, with a lifecycle); occupancy is role's; ending employment ends that organization's occupancies of that person **by a rule**, not by merging the records. *Against:* in many organizations the two are always created together; the cost of two records is a second write. *This leans on the staff identity question:* the Decided "a staff member's identity is the user domain's identity" cannot by itself name two concurrent or successive employments of one user (contradiction 2).

#### Smaller choice 3: reporting line and unit containment are two relations (Tentative)
The legacy `Role.ParentID` ("Exist if it isn't top of the organization") is a reporting tree of positions; the department loop `(group)-[:department]->(---)` is meant "to show organizational chart" of units. They are two charts: a unit contains its members and a company's members include its departments' members (group's `parent_is`, effective membership upward); a position reports to another and the upper occupant is not an occupant of the lower post. Matrix organizations (group scenario 7) and dotted lines need many parents for reporting. *Proposal (agent):* two relations with two rules, the cycle guard attached to each through `condition_is`. *Against:* the synthesis's X5 asks whether containment is defined once on thing; reporting is not containment (nothing is part of anything), so X5 does not reach it.

#### Smaller choice 4: does authority travel with the position or stay with the person? (Explored)
(a) Authority is delegated to the position (the delegated end is the position); the occupant at an instant exercises it; a new occupant exercises it from the effective instant, the old one stops, and acts done earlier stay valid. (b) Authority is delegated to the person at appointment; ending the occupancy must revoke it. (a) matches the organization's wording ("the CFO signs") and avoids the revocation that is forgotten; (b) matches delegate's present record (given user, gotten user). Neither is settled, delegate has no research, and the legacy record names a role or a rule in the delegated end (contradiction 3). Recorded as a question with a default (Q10).

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions

The domain, once decided, has to answer each of these. Questions are written to favour no answer where unsettled; a settled item says so and cites its decision.

#### Identity
- Does a position keep its identity when its title changes, when it moves to another unit, when its grade changes, when it is renamed after a reorganization, when two positions merge, when one splits into two (a job share), and when it is abolished and re-created with the same title years later?
- Is a position's identity scoped to one organization (two organizations' "manager" are two posts, the modules rule that behaviour binds to a UUID and not a name) or can a post be shared (the notary of a district whose holder serves several parties)?
- May the occupant be a person, an organization, a group, a software agent? When the occupant's own identity changes (an agent's model is replaced, an organization merges), is it the same occupancy?
- Is the position's identity the thing's alone, and is its accessor `RoleID` or `PositionID`? (The staff import already says `PositionID`.)
- Is the identity of an employment that of the user, or is there one per employment (contradiction 2)? This is the only identity question that is already marked Decided in a neighbour, and it is stated as a question because its consequence reaches this domain.

#### Lifecycle
- What states does a position have, and which are positions in its life (planned, approved, open, filled, frozen, abolished) as against classifications (grade, family) or terms (fixed-term)? Is a headcount approval (a budget line) a state of the position or a separate fact?
- What states does an occupancy have: offered, accepted, active, suspended (leave, secondment), acting, ended? Is a leave an occupancy suspension, a task, or nothing at all (staff's leave vocabulary is task kinds in [ownership staff](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/staff/staff.md))?
- Is an end announced (notice period) and an end effective two facts?
- What happens to the occupants of a position when it is abolished: refused, or ended with the cause "abolished"? What happens to the position when its context is dissolved?
- Is vacancy a stored state or a derived one, and which events fire when a count falls below its minimum or rises above its maximum (a handover overlap)?
- When may a former occupant occupy again (cooling-off periods, term limits)?

#### Authorization
- Who may declare a position, change it, move it, abolish it? Who may appoint, end, suspend, or designate an acting holder? May an occupant appoint their own successor? May a person appoint themselves?
- Which positions may not be held together by one occupant (separation of duties: the same person as chief executive and auditor; a judge and a party)? Where is that stated, and who may waive it?
- Who may read the occupants of a position and its history (a public office versus an internal one; a protected identity)? The synthesis's X1 (erasure) applies to the identity link of a former occupant; whether a public record of a post's holders is erasable is the question.
- When authority attaches to the position (smaller choice 4), who may attach it, and does a delegation survive a change of occupant?
- Where authority is exercised through an exerciser, how is the act attributed: to the occupant, to the exerciser, to both?

#### Rules
- Eligibility (age, skill, citizenship, reserved category), count (minimum, maximum, per window), term (length, renewals), incompatibility, rotation, order of succession, quorum and threshold ("two of three"): which are the position's, which the context's, which the template's? Which are invariants (Code) and which are Rule (the synthesis's RU.5)?
- Where a rule attaches to the position through `condition_is`, which points does the position declare: before an occupancy begins, before it ends, on the end of an occupancy, at term end, before an act is attributed?
- Does a template's rule bind its instances, or is it offered?

#### Events
- Which events does the position emit and which does the occupancy emit, and in which domain do they live if the occupancy is the group's membership (a `member_left` that is also `occupancy_ended`)?
- Which consumers need the event rather than the state: task (reassign), shift (re-roster), authorization (refresh), staff (hire task for a vacancy), notification, analytics?
- Are record order and effective time both kept (the synthesis's X4)? An appointment is typically effective before or after it is recorded (the gazette date), and the question "who held post P on date D" and "what did the organization know on date E" are different queries.
- Is an effective date stated in a named calendar system (a Persian-year term, a Gregorian fiscal year), and where is that system recorded (finding 5, calendar)?

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each is one paragraph, as a sketch: not a design, and none is to be built before the owner decides.

1. **`position` (kernel thing; Tentative).** The place in a context. Identity is the thing's; its context is `position_in`; it has an optional title (a text), an optional focus classification, and is an event target. Created by an authority over the context (the organization, a unit's head, a rule on an event such as a new branch opening, a template instantiation); abolished by the same authority, never deleted. Triggers `position_declared`, `position_changed`, `position_abolished`. Not built before Q1, Q3.
2. **`occupancy` (kernel record, or the group's membership; Tentative).** The tenure of one occupant in one position: interval, kind (holder, acting, exerciser for), provenance. Created by appointment (or a rule, or compulsion); ended by the occupant, an authority, the term, a death, a succession, an abolition; never deleted. Triggers `occupancy_began` and `occupancy_ended` with cause. Not built before Q4.
3. **`appointment` (pluggable; Tentative).** The ways an occupancy begins: a proposal from either side with an expiry, an acceptance, an election, a lot, a succession, a compulsion; the provenance each leaves. The same offer-and-acceptance shape as group admission and task acceptance (synthesis X6), so it is built on that capability if it is defined once. Home open until X6.
4. **`succession-and-acting` (pluggable; Tentative).** An ordered line of succession read by a rule when an occupancy ends (the successor begins at the same instant); an acting occupancy for an absence (the holder stays the holder); an exerciser for an occupant that cannot act (a regent; a representative of a legal person). Absorbs the staff `PresentedID`. Created by the organization's rule or the holder's delegation of an absence; ends on return or on a new order. Triggers a new occupancy and the notices. The exerciser is a delegation in disguise and should use delegate (group.research.001.md missing-piece 5, `representation`); the two should not be built twice.
5. **`reporting-line` (pluggable; Tentative).** `(position)-[:reports_to]->(position)`, many parents, acyclic, the cycle guard attached through `condition_is`; the position-level organizational chart; the authority an upper position has over a lower. Created by an authority over the lower; reparenting is an event. Does not make an upper occupant an occupant of a lower post. Not the group hierarchy (smaller choice 3).
6. **`position-template` (pluggable; Tentative).** A job description as a template: a charter (content by reference), a requirement list, a focus, default rules. A position is instantiated from it (copy, new thing, `derived_from`); a change to the template is offered to the instances; a local variation is a rule on the instance. Needs thing's `template` and the missing `derived_from`. The decided direction "role is a focus area and never a knowledge boundary" is satisfied by keeping the focus a classification and the requirements a separate list.
7. **`coverage` (pluggable; Tentative; what `shift` becomes).** The position's requirement that someone hold it in stated windows: how many, which windows, strictly or flexibly (`Type_Strict`, `Type_Float`), on which calendar. It generates dated duty instances as tasks from a repetition rule (the survey default P12), assigns occupants, and handles swap and handover; the person's preference (staff's `StaffShift`) is an input, not the requirement. Created by the position's authority; the swap by the occupant; triggers a task per window and an alert for an uncovered one. The organization's working calendar and the calendar system (finding 5, calendar) are inputs it reads; it does not own them.
8. **`employment` (staff's kernel record; Explored).** The user-to-organization personnel relation with its own identity, so that two concurrent employments, a rehire after a dismissal, and a secondment each have a record (parallel to the group's membership with identity, GR.3). The Decided staff identity (the user's) stays the identity of the person as personnel. Created by hiring; ended by Fire or End; triggers the end of that organization's occupancies by rule. Built only if the owner answers Q6 that the user's identity is not enough.
9. **`availability` (a person's claim; Tentative, small).** What hours and days a person says they can work: stated by the person, revisable, with the organization's calendar as its frame. A different asserter from the coverage requirement, so a different record. Today `StaffShift`. Home: staff or the calendar's work periods.
10. **`holds` (a derived query; Tentative).** "Does user U hold position P (or a subordinate of it) at instant T?" and "who holds P at T?". Offered by the role domain's protocol, composed by task assignment, pos, reaction and authorization; never stored. Replaces the role-as-edge in `reactionable_by`, the pos group limit and `PrivateInRole`, which then reference a position by identity (the reference-not-embed rule they already cite). Resolves a coordinate-style lookup ("resolve RoleID to related UserIDs") as a query.
11. **`compensation attachment` (a rule attach point; Explored).** The salary pluggables attach to `(org)`, `(group)` and `(staff)` ([salary-earth-hourly](../../../modules-plugable/management/salary-earth-hourly/salary-earth-hourly.md)); a pay band is usually a property of a post, not of a person. Whether the position is a fourth attach point is not decided here and costs nothing to ask.
12. **Separation of duties (a rule vocabulary, not a module; Explored).** Pairs of positions one occupant may not hold together, and who may waive. A rule at the point before an occupancy begins; the vocabulary of what makes two positions incompatible is the organization's.
13. **A changelog for each of the five domains (missing; hygiene).** Created with this research as one line each.

### 7. Test scenarios

Each is a given/when/then that another session can write as a test. Tags: [K] kernel, [P] pluggable, [C] cluster domain, [R] rule.

1. **A post outlives its holder [K].** Given a position held by A. When A's occupancy ends. Then the position still exists with the same identity, no occupancy is current, and A's occupancy remains readable.
2. **Vacancy is discovered [K].** Given a position whose minimum is one. When its last occupancy ends. Then no "vacant" flag is written; a query reports vacancy at that instant, and the event `occupancy_ended` is the trigger consumers use.
3. **Count enforced by rule [R].** Given a two-holder position with two occupants. When a third appointment is proposed. Then the rule at "before occupancy begins" refuses it with its reason, and the position is unchanged.
4. **Concurrent posts [K].** Given a user. When she holds department head of A and director of B in one organization. Then there are two current occupancies, neither affects the other, and ending one leaves the other.
5. **Two organizations [C].** Given a user employed by organizations X and Y. When the employment with X ends. Then X's occupancies of her end by rule and Y's are untouched.
6. **Employment ends, post remains [C].** Given an employed holder of a post. When the employment ends with cause "end of contract". Then the occupancy ends with the same cause by rule and the position is vacant; the ending was not written into the position.
7. **Occupancy without employment [C].** Given a board director who is not an employee. When she holds the post for a term. Then no staff record is required, and the occupancy has the term.
8. **Retroactive appointment [K].** Given an appointment effective 1 April, recorded on 15 April. When asked who held the post on 10 April, and what was known on 12 April. Then the first answer names the appointee, the second does not.
9. **An organization as occupant [K].** Given a legal person elected director and a named natural person acting for it. When the director signs. Then the act is attributed to the occupant, the exerciser is recorded, and replacing the exerciser does not change the occupancy.
10. **A threshold [R].** Given a signatory position set requiring two of three. When one signs. Then the act is incomplete and attributed to nobody yet; when a second signs it completes (organization research test 3, Explored).
11. **Succession without a gap [P].** Given an ordered line and a holder who dies. When the occupancy ends with cause death. Then the next in line is the holder from the same instant and no vacancy is recorded.
12. **Regency [P].** Given a minor holder and a regent. When the regent acts. Then the act is in the holder's name, the holder stays the holder, and the regent's authority has its own end.
13. **Acting for an absence [P].** Given a holder on leave and a person presented as alternate. When the leave begins. Then an acting occupancy begins for the alternate, the holder remains the holder, and the acting ends on the holder's return.
14. **Reserved eligibility [R].** Given a position reserved for a category for this term. When a candidate outside it is proposed. Then the proposal is refused with the reason; when the reservation rotates next term, the rule on the position changes by a recorded event, not by an edit.
15. **Incompatible posts [R].** Given a rule that one user may not hold the post of chief executive and of auditor. When a user holds one and is proposed for the other. Then the second proposal is refused, and a waiver, if the rule allows one, is a recorded act of an authority.
16. **Term and reappointment limit [P].** Given a term of five years and a limit of two terms. When the second term ends. Then the occupancy ends with cause term end, and a third appointment is refused by the limit.
17. **Reporting line [P].** Given position A reporting to B and to C. When B is proposed to report to A. Then the cycle is refused; and B's occupant is not thereby an occupant of A.
18. **A move [K].** Given a position under unit X. When it moves to unit Y. Then its identity is unchanged, `position_in` has two periods, and the old context stays readable.
19. **Template change offered [P].** Given a branch-manager template and four hundred derived positions. When headquarters revises the charter. Then each instance is offered the new version; none changes silently; an instance with a local variation keeps it.
20. **Assignment to a position [C].** Given a task assigned to the position "approver". When the task is claimed. Then the current occupant is resolved and recorded; if the occupant changes mid-task, a rule decides whether the task follows the position or stays with the person.
21. **Selector [P].** Given reaction allowed to the position "moderator". When a user ceases to hold it. Then the permission ends from the effective instant, and reactions made earlier stay valid.
22. **Coverage window [C].** Given a ward post that must have three holders on duty round the clock. When a window has two on duty. Then an alert is raised for the uncovered window and a task to fill it exists; nothing silently reassigns anyone.
23. **Pattern, instance, assignment [C].** Given a morning pattern 06:00 to 14:00 strict. When the week is generated. Then seven duty instances exist as tasks derived from the pattern, each assigned to an occupancy; a swap changes the assignment and not the pattern.
24. **Flexible hours [C].** Given a `Float` pattern requiring eight hours within a window. When the occupant works seven. Then the shortfall is recorded against the instance and the window is not marked covered.
25. **Requirement versus availability [C].** Given a person's stated availability that excludes a window the position must cover. When the roster is built. Then the conflict is shown; the requirement is not overridden by the preference nor the preference silently ignored.
26. **Calendar system [C].** Given a term "until the end of Esfand" and an organization whose week runs Saturday to Thursday. When the occupancy's end is computed. Then it is computed in the Persian calendar, the weekday semantics are the organization's calendar's, and the stored end is an instant that can be shown in either system.
27. **Authority with the post (Explored) [P].** Given a delegation to the position "chief financial officer". When the occupant changes at an effective instant. Then the new occupant exercises it from that instant, the old one stops, and earlier acts stay valid and attributed to the old occupant.
28. **Erasure of a public office record (X1) [K].** Given an occupant who asks for erasure. When an authority orders the identity link detached. Then the post, the interval and the fact that someone held it remain; the link to the person is removed only where the order says so.
29. **Abolished with an occupant [K].** Given a position with a current occupant. When the position is abolished. Then either the abolition is refused or the occupancy ends with cause "abolished"; nothing is deleted.
30. **One-person organization [C].** Given an owner holding six positions. When the load query runs. Then it reports six occupancies and the tasks assigned to them, and a hiring task is suggested as a diagnosis citing those observations, not fired by a count.
31. **An open class is not a position [K].** Given the name "seller" with no count, term or vacancy. When it is granted to a user. Then a classification is set by a rule and no position or occupancy is created.
32. **Capacities stay relations [K].** Given a plaintiff on a case. When she is named. Then no position is created; the case's own edge states it, and the graduation test (finding 2) decides if an office such as judge is a position.
33. **Mentor, coach, consultant [K].** Given a mentor of a person. When the relation is recorded. Then it is a relation between two persons with no position; a consultant's obligations are a contract.
34. **Role is not tool knowledge [P].** Given a position "Database Agent" whose requirement list says a database skill is SHOULD. When an agent without it is proposed. Then the proposal is allowed with a warning; with MUST it is refused. The position's focus never lists the tools.

[calendar-reason.md](../../../modules-plugable/management/calendar-reason/calendar-reason.md) and [attendance.md](../staff/edges/attendance/attendance.md) each ask whether the other owns it.
9. **`recruit` names two facts.** [recruit.md](../department/edges-loop/recruit/recruit.md): "Recruitment in progress" and "Active membership recruitment" are, respectively, hiring into vacancies and a group's open admission; the department handoff asks whether it belongs to the hiring pipeline; neither is an edge of a department.
10. **The member limit is on the group in the document and on a job position in the Go.** [group.research.001.md](../group/group.research.001.md) Q20 already records it. Under this proposal a limit per position belongs to the position; a limit on a group's total stays the group's. The two defaults are compatible if the group's default is read as "the group's total".
11. **Task assumes a Role that does not exist.** [task.research.001.md](../task/task.research.001.md) Q7 default and the synthesis TA.7 dissolve the participant labels into "Group plus Role plus delegation"; the role side of that sum is a one-line loop and a title.
12. **"Position" and "seat" collide.** [timeline](../timeline/timeline.research.001.md) uses "position" for a placement's place in a sequence; [economics/service](../../../modules-plugable/economics/service/edges/item-seat/item-seat.md) uses `seat` for a sold place; this research uses "position" for a place in a structure and avoids "seat". Three uses of two words. The owner may rename; the Khayyam practice (rule 3, full domain-qualified names) already requires `RolePosition` or `PositionID`-style qualification once converted.
13. **The synthesis's omission.** See Reconciliation with the synthesis: the organization research exists; the synthesis was written as if it did not.

## Recommendation (unratified)
1. **Name the concept the position and its occupancy; keep the folder `role` (Tentative, medium confidence).** The dictionary sense (expectation) stays as the entry; the domain statement is the four questions of finding 1. Separate the jobs the word does: the place (this domain), the capacity in one relation (the relation's own edge), the authority bundle (delegate and authorization by rule), the job description (a template), the title (a convention). The graduation test: a thing is a position if it can be full or vacant.
2. **Adopt the five-element kernel of finding 2** (identity, context, occupancy, events, `condition_is`), with occupancy realized as the membership of a member-bearing position if group Q2 and Q3 are answered by their defaults. The count, eligibility, term, incompatibility and succession order are rules.
3. **Make employment and occupancy two relations** (staff and role); ending an employment ends the organization's occupancies by rule. Ask whether the Decided staff identity covers employment (Q6).
4. **Keep reporting and unit containment apart.** Reporting is a role pluggable; containment is the group hierarchy.
5. **Let tasks, rules and selectors name a position by identity and resolve the occupant at a stated instant** (the `holds` query and late-bound assignment), so the handover case and the reaction, pos and authorization selectors stop each carrying a role edge.
6. **Treat `shift` as the pattern of a `coverage` pluggable** whose instances are tasks and whose assignments are occupancies, with the person's availability a separate record; per the survey default (P12) rather than as a new idea.
7. **Treat the calendar as a reading the cluster uses** (a named calendar system, an organization's working calendar, work periods), not as an owner of terms or shifts; blocked by the time domain's missing instant and span (the calendar handoff).
8. **Record the contradictions in the five handoffs, ask the questions below in sitting 3, and write nothing in the Model or Policies sections** until the owner answers Q1, Q3, Q4 and Q6.

## Open Questions
Only the owner can decide these. Ordered by what they change: those that change what the domain is first, then those that change how a part is done ([Ask rather than assume](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. They are also entered in [role.handoff.md](role.handoff.md).

1. **Is the domain's concept the position and its occupancy, with the capacity in one relation, the authority bundle, the job description and the title as separate things?** Default: yes, with the graduation test "can be full or vacant". Blocks: the statement in [role.md](role.md), the meaning of `edges-loop/role`, GR.8, TA.7, every `RoleID`.
2. **Does the folder and the accessor keep the name `role` (`RoleID`), or become `position` (`PositionID`, as the staff import already writes)?** Default: keep `role` for the folder and the dictionary entry, `RoleID` for the accessor, and call the concept "position" in the first paragraph; avoid `seat`. Blocks: [protocol/dt-uuid.kh](protocol/dt-uuid.kh), staff's `PositionID`, delegate's `RoleID`.
3. **Is a position a thing with its own identity that can take members, or only a group classified as a position?** Default: its own thing identity, which also takes members (group Q2), marked by the loop. Blocks: `edges-loop/role`, [edges/group](edges/group/group.md), [data/judge](data/judge/judge.md), group Q2 and Q3.
4. **Is an occupancy the group's membership record or its own?** Default: the membership of the position, with the kind `occupant`, `acting` or `exerciser for`; the role domain adds no second record. Blocks: a `MembershipDataModel`, the events, staff's person-position, group Q3.
5. **Are employment and occupancy two relations?** Default: yes; employment is staff's, occupancy is role's, and ending employment ends that organization's occupancies by a rule. Blocks: staff-status, person-position, hiring-pipeline, the volunteer and board cases.
6. **Does the Decided staff identity (the user's) cover each employment, or is there one identity per employment?** Default: the user's identity stays the identity of the person as personnel and each employment gets its own identity (like the group membership, GR.3). Blocks: staff-status per employment, attendance (which employment), the shift preference, the `employment` record of finding 6.
7. **Does a position belong to exactly one context at a time, and can any thing be a context?** Default: one at a time, changed by an event with history; any thing (organization, unit, group, task, case). Blocks: `position_in`, the legacy `departmentID`, template positions in tasks, case judge positions.
8. **Can a task, a rule or a selector name a position and have the occupant resolved at a stated instant (late binding)?** Default: yes; the occupant is resolved and recorded when the task is claimed; a change of occupant mid-task is decided by a rule. Blocks: task assign rules, TA.7, handover, the `holds` query.
9. **Are reporting and unit containment two relations?** Default: yes; reporting has many parents and no effective occupancy upward; containment is the group hierarchy. Blocks: the harvest of `ParentID`, the organizational chart, authority along the line, X5.
10. **Does authority travel with the position or stay with the person?** Default: with the position, through delegate with the position as the delegated end, and acts carry "occupant X acting as position P" (Explored; delegate has no research). Blocks: the delegate record (role, rule or bundle), `GetRoleAccess`, `PrivateInRole`, X3, TR.6, GR.17.
11. **May an occupant be an organization, a group or a software agent, and who exercises for it?** Default: any user-model thing; an exerciser is separate and is a delegation; accountability stays with the principal. Blocks: the legal-person director, the agent fleet, US.9.
12. **Are the count, eligibility, term, incompatibility and succession order rules at named points rather than fields?** Default: yes, rules attached through `condition_is` at the points the position declares; the identity invariants stay Code. Blocks: group Q20 (the limit's far end), the term pluggable, reserved-place rules.
13. **Are the organization's required hours and a person's preference two records?** Default: yes, different asserters; the requirement belongs to the coverage of the position, the preference to the person. Blocks: the conversion of `StaffTimingUTC` and `StaffShift`.
14. **Is "shift" demoted to the pattern of a coverage pluggable, with duty instances as tasks and assignments as occupancies?** Default: yes, as the survey default P12. Blocks: the fate of the shift folder, `ShiftID`, `Type_Strict`/`Type_Float`, the staff status shift states.
15. **Does a leave suspend an occupancy, or is it a task, or neither?** Default: a leave is a task kind (as the task and ownership documents say); whether it suspends the occupancy is a rule of the organization, not a status. Blocks: the acting occupancy, the ward case, task's Deferred `leave`.
16. **Is the design/engineering distinction a rule over a position's focus (who decides requirements) and never a barrier to taking part in reasoning?** Default: yes, guarding the false-taxonomy caution. Blocks: how the decided direction is applied in the staffing model and the agent briefs.
17. **Are mentor, coach and consultant relations between persons and not positions?** Default: yes; mentor and coach in `person`'s social connections, consultant as a contract with obligations; a school's official "mentor post" would be a position. Blocks: applying the decided distinction.
18. **Is the hiring pipeline an annotation on a hiring task whose subject is a vacant position, created by a rule?** Default: yes. Blocks: the pipeline's unconfirmed target node, the department `recruit` edge.
19. **Does a position's job description live as a template (thing's `template` and `derived_from`) with changes offered to instances?** Default: yes. Blocks: the franchise case, `derived_from`, `requires` level vocabulary (GR.19).
20. **Does the calendar supply a named calendar system for terms, an organization's working calendar and work periods as intervals?** Default: yes as a reading the cluster uses; the stored value is an instant with an optional civil expression. Blocks: calendar `type`, term ends, weekday semantics, shift windows; depends on the time domain's missing instant and span.
21. **Does a public-office history survive an occupant's erasure request?** Default: the post, interval and the fact stay; the identity link is detached only where an authority orders it (X1). Blocks: X1's application, judge transparency in [judicial-office](../../../modules-plugable/rights/judicial-office/judicial-office.md).
22. **Is the standing judge post and the per-case seat two positions in two contexts?** Default: yes, consistent with the Decided judge edge pointing at the user; whether a standing office exists with no case is the judicial-office Q1 and stays the owner's. Blocks: whether judicial-office is copied as a standing office.
23. **Does a capacity (plaintiff, assigner, buyer, credited author) stay a relation?** Default: yes, unless it passes the graduation test. Blocks: the case participant vocabulary, the task participant labels, content `credited`.
24. **Is compensation a rule attached to the position (a pay band), or to the person?** Default: ask; costs nothing. Blocks: the salary pluggables' attach points.
