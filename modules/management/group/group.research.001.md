Memar's own position on the word is a starting fact, and it is a negative one. *Group* is not a term Memar defines. [Terminology → A Term Deliberately Left Open](../../../docs/terminology.md#a-term-deliberately-left-open) lists only *Task* in that class, and names groups, departments and staff as domains the Organization project develops alongside it. Under [The Default Meaning of an Unreferenced Term](../../../docs/terminology.md#the-default-meaning-of-an-unreferenced-term) the word therefore carries its ordinary general meaning (a number of persons or things regarded as a unit), and the structural questions (what a group is here, what states it has, how it is identified) are owed by this project. Memar does hold two adjacent definitions that this research uses: a *system* is a collection of interacting elements whose boundary "may be defined by membership, role, or contractual relationship" ([System → Key Properties](../../../docs/system.md#key-properties)), and an *organization* can exhibit agency, so that "the boundary of an Agent does not necessarily coincide with the boundary of a biological individual" ([Agency → Organizational Agency](../../../docs/agency.md#organizational-agency)).

It deliberately says nothing about registration, size, purpose, a hierarchy, a conversation, or a legal personality.

## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The group domain gives an organization one answer to four questions about any collective: *who is in it* (with since when, until when, and how they came to be), *who may change that* (admit, remove, refuse), *how it sits among other collectives* (inside one, beside one, across several), and *on what standing it is addressed as one* (as an audience, a team, a party that owns or is notified). Everything the industry sells separately as team, squad, department, committee, family, club, class, audience, mailing list, guild, cohort or consortium asks these four questions of a different collective. This restates what the domain documents already say: Group is where "participant/member management" lives ([group.md](group.md#decisions-from-organization-ideas-backlog-2026-09-16), Decided), and membership is decomposed into "several small, single-purpose edges" ([group.md](group.md#resolved-via-source-inspection), resolved direction).

**The word hides two things (agent, Tentative; the evidence is in the corpus).** Read across the repository, "group" is asked to do at least five jobs that do not share a lifecycle:

1. **A thing that has members.** `(group)-[:joined]->(user)`, `invite`, `request_to_join`, `blocked`, the member limit and the skill term in this domain's own edges.
2. **An actor.** `GroupModel` composes `UserModel` ([model.kh](protocol/model.kh)); `(user)-[:group]->(---)` classifies a user as a group ([edges-loop/group](edges-loop/group/group.md)); the ownership and status edges accept a group where they accept a user (`owner_is`, `heir_is`, `confirmation_by`, `approval_needed_by`, `request_by`, `suggest_by`, notification listener: [owner](../../rights/ownership/edges/owner/owner.md), [heir](../../rights/ownership/edges/heir/heir.md), [confirmation](../../rights/ownership/edges/confirmation/confirmation.md), [approval_needed](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/approval_needed/approval_needed.md), [request](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/request/request.md), [suggest](../../knowledge/status/edges-loop/suggest/suggest.md), [listener](../../computer/notification/edges/listener/listener.md)).
3. **A place where contents are exchanged.** The content widgets `group` and `group-user` index comments by group ([widgets/group data model](../../knowledge/content/ui/group/protocol/data_model.kh)); `(group)-[:---]->(timeline)` is used "as ChannelID" ([timeline](../timeline/edges-loop/timeline/timeline.md)); the case premise says a case UUID is a group "so it can be a timeline" ([case.research.001.md](../../rights/case/case.research.001.md)); [modules.md](../../modules.md#top-or-thing-levels-labels) says a product UUID is used "as group for a product as default chat timeline".
4. **An audience or selector.** A form is linked to a group, a register is limited to role-groups, a product's sale is limited to groups, a discount and a voucher are limited "to this group", a product has a "buyers" group ([form](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/form/edges/group/group.md), [pos](../../../modules-plugable/economics/pos/edges/group/group.md), [sell-limit](../../economics/product/edges/sell-limit/sell-limit.md), [buyers](../../economics/product/edges/buyers/buyers.md), [discount](../../../modules-plugable/economics/discount/protocol/group.kh), [voucher](../../../modules-plugable/economics/voucher/protocol/group.kh)). Here nobody joins; a rule selects.
5. **A position or organizational unit.** `(group)-[:role]->(---)` makes a group a role, a `judge` is a group with a title ([role](../role/edges-loop/role/role.md), [judge](../role/data/judge/judge.md)), a department is a group ([department](../department/edges-loop/department/department.md)), staff are hired "for a group" ([staff edge](../staff/edges/group/group.md)).

Under the [Single Responsibility Check](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#single-responsibility-check) ("different discussions of the node focus on different, unrelated aspects of it") and the [Node Classification Audit](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#node-classification-audit) (a node that is "mixed" is a warning sign), the proposal that the scenarios test is that **the domain's concept is the membership, and that "being an actor", "being a place", "being an audience" and "being a position" are separate capabilities that a thing with members may also have**. The word *group* then names a thing whose main nature is having members; it does not name a type of user, a conversation, a query result or a job. The same split reads Memar's agency model: whether a collective acts as one is a matter of agency and delegation ("the boundary of an Agent does not necessarily coincide with the boundary of a biological individual", [Organizational Agency](../../../docs/agency.md#organizational-agency)), not of how many members it has.

**The domain's own eight test cases.** [group.md](group.md#test-cases-any-resolved-criterion-must-pass) lists eight cases any resolved criterion must pass. Seven are scenarios below (sole proprietorship: 12; unregistered street vendor collective: 8; DAO: 5; informal friend group chat: 1; family unit: 3; government agency: 6; university committee with an external oversight member: 4). The eighth, a registered NGO, is the easy case and breaks nothing: it is a group classified as an organization (`(user)-[:organization]`) whose registration is an identifier issued by law ([citizen](../../identity/organization/edges/citizen/citizen.md) shows the same split for voting rights), not a fact about membership.

**Scenarios.** Thirteen, from several industries and cultures. Each states what is being done, who is in it, what breaks the obvious model, and what it says. "The obvious model" is *a group is a named list of users with an administrator*.

1. **A family or friends chat group (any messenger, any country).** A person creates a group, adds her mother and cousins without asking (the messenger shows "added by"), an administrator leaves and nobody holds the role, a member leaves and returns a year later, a new member may or may not see the history before the day she joined. *Breaks:* consent as a precondition, administrator as a permanent fact, membership as a flag instead of a stretch of time. *Says:* membership has a beginning, an end and a provenance (who added, by which way); an authority may add without consent; history visibility depends on the membership interval, which is data the timeline's rule reads; and what happens when no authority remains is a question the model must own (finding 4, Authorization).
2. **A rotating savings circle (dowreh in Iran, tanda or cundina in Latin America, susu in West Africa, a chit fund in India, a tontine).** Twelve people each pay monthly and one takes the whole sum each month in an order drawn by lot. A member cannot leave in month four without finding a replacement who inherits the obligations; the circle has no registration; it ends when the cycle ends. *Breaks:* a group is a set of people (here the group is twelve *seats*, and people fill them), a member limit as a ceiling (here minimum equals maximum equals twelve), a group that lives for a fixed term and then dissolves, and membership as freely revocable. *Says:* the seat (a position) outlives its holder; the member limit is a property of the group (twelve seats), not of a position (as the Go comment said, [handoff](group.handoff.md#open-questions)); replacement is a rule of this group, not of groups; and the money is the account and transaction domains', with the group only as the party that keeps the book.
3. **A family or household (any culture).** Membership comes by birth, adoption or marriage and ends by death or divorce; nobody consents at birth. A child has two parents who each belong to a different extended family, so the child's household sits inside two larger groups at once. The same person is a daughter in one group, a spouse in another and an aunt in a third. *Breaks:* a tree (a household has two parents), consent, and one role vocabulary per group. *Says:* membership may be created by an event the member did not choose; containment is a graph, not a tree (the Task research found the same for task containment, [task.research.001.md](../task/task.research.001.md#1-what-the-domain-is-for) scenario 2); and the role a person holds in each group is that group's own data.
4. **A university committee with an external member and an oversight body (given in the domain).** An ethics committee includes the Ministry of Health, an organization, as an external member; the Ministry sends a named official, who is replaced after two years; separately an accreditation agency may inspect the committee's records without sitting on it. The Ministry's own advisory board has a seat held by the university. *Breaks:* members are persons, representation by a named person, oversight as the same thing as membership, and a membership structure with no cycle (here, university sits on the Ministry's board and the Ministry sits on the university's committee). *Says:* a member can be an organization; who acts for it is a separate, changing fact (a delegation); a seat held without voting and an inspection right with no seat are different things; and cycles through "member of" are real and harmless to the fact itself, harmful only to a derivation that assumes membership flows one way ([group.md](group.md#unresolved-questions), unresolved 4).
5. **A DAO (decentralized, pseudonymous; given in the domain).** Members are holders of a token; nobody applies; a holder who sells loses membership in the same block; members are pseudonymous keys, some of them other DAOs or smart contracts; the group owns a treasury and votes weighted by holdings; there is no legal registration. *Breaks:* membership as an act of joining (here it is derived from a ledger the group does not control), identity as a person, equal votes, and a group that needs a registry to be persistent. *Says:* some memberships are discovered from an external fact and should not be stored as acquired ([Acquired vs. Discovered](../../../docs/modeling.md#acquired-data-vs-discovered-data)); the group has an identity without registration, and acts as one (a treasury) without being a person; weight is a rule.
6. **A ministry reorganization (Iran's ministries, France's ministères, Japan's central-government reform, any merger of agencies).** A directorate is moved from one ministry to another, two directorates merge into one, a department is split in two and one half is renamed. Staff stay; the history of who belonged where must stay answerable. *Breaks:* a static tree, "a group is the same group while it has the same name", and "the parent is a field". *Says:* the parent is a relation that changes over time and a change is an event; identity across merge and split is a question the model must answer (Q14); and nothing is deleted because auditors ask who sat where on a given date.
7. **A matrix organization (the Spotify model named in [group.md](group.md#prior-art); a federation of cooperatives).** A person belongs to a squad (a cross-functional team) and to a chapter (people with the same skill across squads); squads sit in a tribe; a guild crosses everything. None of the four is the parent of another in the sense of containing it. *Breaks:* a tree, "the person's group", and a fixed vocabulary of group kinds. *Says:* a person is in several groups at once with different roles; the kinds (squad, chapter, tribe, guild) are an organization's data, not code ([group.md](group.md#unresolved-questions), unresolved 5); and "inside" and "across" are different relations.
8. **An unregistered street-vendor collective or bazaar guild (اصناف in Iran, a sellers' association in Latin America or South Asia).** There is no registry and no founder document. A new vendor is admitted when three existing members vouch for her; the elected spokesman collects dues in cash and speaks to the municipality for all; when the spokesman is replaced the municipality's counter-party does not change. *Breaks:* admission by an administrator, authority from a registry, and legitimacy from a document. *Says:* admission can be a rule over existing members' acts (a vote or vouching), the group can act through a representative it chooses, and the group's identity does not depend on any registry.
9. **A school class or university cohort.** "Class 5B" exists every year with different pupils; "the 2025 graduates" are a fixed set that no longer meets; a course section is open for registration for two weeks, then closed, then ends. Access control's own example is "answer exam just when they are in the school" ([access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md)). *Breaks:* group identity as the identity of its members, and membership without an end. *Says:* a membership has a term and an end; whether Class 5B of 2026 is the same group as Class 5B of 2025 is an owner decision (Q14); and some "groups" are fixed by a rule of intake (everyone enrolled in 2025) and are derived.
10. **A surgical team, or the neighbours at a harvest (the Task research's scenarios 4 and 6).** The team forms around the work, has roles (surgeon, anaesthetist, nurse) and exists for that work; nobody created "a group" first. When the operation ends the team dissolves and the roles stop. *Breaks:* a group that exists before its work, and a group as a separate thing from the task. *Says:* membership is something a task (or a case, or a contract) can take directly; whether the team is the task or a second thing the task has is the central question for the task/group boundary (finding 3, Q2).
11. **A marketing segment (an online shop's "customers who bought twice this year").** The shop uses it to send a voucher, to limit a sale and to restrict a form. Nobody joined; the customers do not know the segment exists; the set changes every night. *Breaks:* every property of acquired membership: consent, interval, provenance, awareness. *Says:* it is a rule's result, which Memar calls discovered data and says should not automatically be stored; it is used by the same consumers that use real groups (form, pos, sell-limit, discount, voucher), so they must be able to refer to it by the same kind of identifier; and being in it must not be confused with being a member.
12. **A sole proprietorship (given in the domain).** A person trades under her own name. The tax office treats her as an organization, the bank opens an account for the business, and for years she has no employee. Later she hires one. *Breaks:* "a group has at least two members", and "a group and a user are different nodes". [group.md](group.md#test-cases-any-resolved-criterion-must-pass) and the [handoff](group.handoff.md#open-questions) both name this as the case the composition of `UserModel` in `GroupModel` cannot express. *Says:* the same identity may be classified as person and as organization; the day she hires, the thing that already exists gains a second member; no migration of identity is required.
13. **A group whose membership is itself sensitive (a support group for addiction or illness, a union in a state that bans unions, a minority religious circle, a political party under a restrictive regime).** The list of members is the secret; so, in some cases, is the existence of the group; a leaked list endangers people. *Breaks:* membership as public, "list the members" as a harmless read, and any model where membership is stored in one place readable by anyone who can read the group. *Says:* who may list members, and who may know the group exists, are rules of the first importance; sensitivity is already answered by [thing-labels](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/thing-labels.md) (synthesis agreement 7) for contents, and membership needs the same treatment; deleting a membership must not be the only way to protect a person, because the history that protects the group also records the person.

**What the scenarios say together (agent, Tentative).** A group does not always have an administrator (1, 8), does not always have a registry (5, 8), is not always a set of persons (4, 5), can have a fixed number of seats (2), can be born with its members (3), can be formed by a task (10) or by a rule (5, 9, 11), is not a tree (3, 7), does not keep one identity across a reorganization (6, 9), is not always something one joins (3, 11), and is not always safe to list (13). The kernel therefore cannot assume an administrator, a consent step, a hierarchy, a size, a registration, a person, a conversation or a visible member list. Each of those is either a relation with rules or a pluggable capability.

**The second layer: the underlying problem the answers assume.** The first layer above answers what was asked. The reading this research assumes beneath it is that the owner's actual problem is not "how do we model a group" but "how do we let task, case, contract, product, form and department each have members, invitations and a chat, without every one of them becoming a kind of group or inventing its own member list". Evidence: four domains record in their own words that their identity "is group" for exactly that reason ([task](../task/task.research.001.md): "base on group to support some group rules e.g. invite"; [contract](../../rights/contract/contract.handoff.md): "UUID is group to support many functionality like chats, expert opinion"; [case](../../rights/case/case.research.001.md): one UUID as case, group and task; [modules.md](../../modules.md#top-or-thing-levels-labels): a product UUID as group for a default chat), and three domains independently keyed their own record by the pair (group, user) because the pair has no identity of its own ([content widgets/group-user](../../knowledge/content/ui/group-user/protocol/data_model.kh), [group-user-display-name](../../identity/user/protocol/group-user-display-name.kh), [access-control group-user](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/protocol/group-user-access-control.kh)). A reader who holds a different reading of the problem can set the proposals aside without discarding the scenarios.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

#### The removal test run on the group itself (agent, Tentative)
[What Earns Foundational Status](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status): express the part of reality the candidate covers through the remaining abstractions; if that forces the fabrication or the distortion of a foundational abstraction, the candidate earns consideration. Without group (meaning, here, the membership relation and the thing that bears it):

- Task would have to hold its own participants, which the Decided material forbids ("participant/member management belongs to Group, not Task", [group.md](group.md#decisions-from-organization-ideas-backlog-2026-09-16)); so would case (parties), contract (parties), timeline (who may add: "only designated members", [timeline.handoff.md](../timeline/timeline.handoff.md#decisions)), form (audience), department and staff. That is the fabrication of several member lists, one per domain.
- User would have to absorb collectives as users with no members, which is the distortion the sole-proprietorship case already shows from the other side.

The guard in the other direction also applies: membership may be only a *relation between users* that any domain can attach to its own identity, with no domain called group at all. That is not refuted here; it is the position the proposed kernel comes close to. The removal test shows the *capability* needs a home, and that the home is smaller than "a type of user".

#### Proposed kernel (agent, Tentative)
Four elements, each with who may create or remove and what it triggers, because [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement) forbids a bare edge. "Authority over the group" is not defined here; it is borrowed from [ownership](../../rights/ownership/edges/owner/owner.md) and [delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md), and what counts as authority is a rule attached through `condition_is`, since the kernel does not reference rules directly.

| # | Element | Who may create or remove | What it triggers | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | **Identity** — a group is a thing; its identity is the thing domain's | any user creates (a limit is a rule); removal is never a delete (finding 4) | the group exists; a `group_created` event | Decided ([group.handoff.md](group.handoff.md#decisions): `ThingUUID`, no `GroupUUID`; `Field_GroupID`, `Field_GroupsID`) |
| K2 | **Membership** `(group)-[:joined]->(user)` — a user (person, organization, another group, or a thing acting for a principal) is in the group from one instant until another, or until now. A membership has an identity of its own (Q3), a provenance (who or what created it, by which way), and a kind drawn from the organization's vocabulary (member, honorary member, supporter) | the member (by joining), an authority over the group (by adding or removing), or a rule or event (by law, by birth, by token holding, by term expiry). Ending a membership sets its end; it is never deleted | `member_joined`, `member_left`; the counts and the effective membership are discovered, not stored | Agent proposal; replaces the labelled `joined` edge ([joined](edges/joined/joined.md)); the labels `Member`, `Honorary Members`, `Supporter` stay as the kind vocabulary, and `JoinWay`, `RequestID` and `Accepter User ID` become the provenance |
| K3 | **Events** — the group is an event target for the occurrences above and below, so consumers (task, timeline, notification, staff, analytics, rules) listen and do not poll | the group emits; the consumers subscribe | see finding 4, Events | Decided as a shape (`GroupModel` composes memar's `EventTarget`, [group.handoff.md](group.handoff.md#decisions)); the event list is the agent's |

A fifth element could be argued: the **classification loop** that marks a thing as a group of a kind (department, squad, tribe, guild, committee). It is not listed in the kernel because [Modeling → Edge Types](../../../docs/modeling.md#edge-types-and-their-traditional-counterparts) already gives the mechanism ("a `Department` is a `Group` because it satisfies the rules that say so — and modeling states that claim once, as a loop-edge"), and the vocabulary of kinds is an organization's data. It lives in `edges-loop/` and needs the completable sentence of [Classification Emerges From Rules](../../../docs/modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels) for each kind: *this thing may be classified as a department because it satisfies rules […], and therefore participates in relations […]*.

Not in the kernel by this proposal: administrator or owner (ownership's), role or position (role's and staff's, Q8), hierarchy, invitation, request, refusal, member limit, skill term, audience or cohort, representation of an organization-member, visibility of the member list, conversation, treasury, dues, a size or a name. A group's name, description and photo are references to text and object, not fields ([The Attribute-or-Edge Test](../../../docs/modeling.md#the-attribute-or-edge-test)); an edit is an event on the same identity.

#### Pluggable: the capability each would own (agent, Tentative)
Each row: the relationship and its processing together; who may create or remove it; what it triggers. A pluggable carries a capability name, not a domain name ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement)). Where an existing folder already holds part of the capability, the mapping is in finding 5.

| Capability | Relationship | Processing | Who may create/remove | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **admission** | a *proposal* `(group)↔(user)` initiated from either side (invitation or request), its acceptance or refusal, an expiry; a standing *refusal* `(group)-[:blocked]->(user)`; the *terms* of admission (member limit, skill requirement with its MUST/SHOULD/MAY level) | makes the offer, records the response, applies the rule that accepts without a person ("accept if the requester is a first-cycle friend of any member"), enforces the maximum and mandatory skills, honours the refusal | a proposal: either side; an acceptance: the other side or a rule; a refusal and the terms: an authority over the group | `proposal_made`, `proposal_accepted`, `proposal_declined`, `proposal_expired`, `admission_refused` with a reason; on acceptance the kernel creates the membership |
| **hierarchy** | `(group)-[:parent_is]->(group)`, many parents allowed, acyclic | the cycle guard; the effective membership of a group (its own members plus those of its descendants, computed when asked); the organizational-chart reading; authority of an ancestor over a descendant (rename, move, dissolve) | an authority over both the child and the parent | `group_reparented`; nothing is stored about effective membership |
| **cohort** (a derived group) | `(group)-[:defined_by]->(rule)` | evaluates the rule over users on demand or on a schedule; live or snapshot; no consent and no membership event; privacy of the result | the owner of the cohort | `cohort_changed` as a difference, if anyone listens; never a member event |

Two things are deliberately *not* proposed as new modules because the graph already holds them: who may see a group and its members (the "group access types" idea of [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md), where the overlap with [access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md) is already flagged by both handoffs; the group only offers K4 and the events), and what a role in a group is (a position, finding 3 small choice 3). Analytics about groups (size, churn, overlap) is a pluggable under the category's analytics namespace, never kernel ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement)).

### 3. Rival models

Marks: **Tentative** (leaning), **Explored** (explored, unresolved), **Deferred**.

#### The models
- **M1 — a group is a kind of user; membership is a labelled edge (the status quo reading; Explored).** The group is an actor: `GroupModel` composes `UserModel` ([model.kh](protocol/model.kh), Decided with a reservation), `(user)-[:group]->(---)` classifies a user as one ([edges-loop/group](edges-loop/group/group.md)), and `joined` carries `Member`, `Honorary Members`, `Supporter` as labels on a `(group)-[:joined]->(user)` edge ([joined](edges/joined/joined.md)). *For:* it matches the Go embedding and every file that accepts "a user or a group"; one actor type for owner, heir, requester and mention target ([text.research.001.md](../../knowledge/text/text.research.001.md): mention is "a span to a user (any kind: person, group, organization, bot)"); a group of one is a user with a second classification (scenario 12). *Against:* it makes a task team, a case's parties and a marketing segment into users (they do not act and have no key; [user](../../identity/user/user.md) says "each user must have a valid public key"); the labelled edge has nowhere to hold the display name, the access grant or the provenance that three domains keep by pair ([contradiction 6](#contradictions-found)); and the ownership and status edges list `(user)` and `(group)` as separate far ends, which would be redundant if a group were a user.
- **M2 — a group is its own kind of thing, never a user; membership, role and term are small separate edges; the group acts only through delegation (Explored).** This is the "resolved direction" of [group.md](group.md#resolved-via-source-inspection) taken literally and the reading the sole-proprietorship comment in the [handoff](group.handoff.md#open-questions) asks for. *For:* it separates the actor question from the member question; a group never has to authenticate; it uses [Agency → Delegation](../../../docs/agency.md#delegation) for "who acts for it". *Against:* it needs a second node for every task, case or contract that wants a team (the Go comments say those wanted to be group by identity, not to own one); it has no answer for the sole proprietor except two nodes with one person in each; it still has no home for the (group, user) records; and it needs a rule for how a collective that clearly acts (a company) becomes a party.
- **M3 — membership is a capability a thing can take; "group" names a thing whose main nature is having members; acting as one is a separate classification (Tentative, recommended).** The kernel of finding 2. Anything with an identity can take members: a task, a case, a contract, a department, a bare club. A thing is *called a group* when its members are the point (a committee, a club, a family). Whether the collective acts as one (owns, signs, is notified) is a separate fact: the `(user)-[:group]` or `(user)-[:organization]` classification is applied to the same identity when it does, not before. Membership is a relation with an identity of its own, so that display name, access grant, role and read position attach to it. Rules, invitations and limits bind to the thing's UUID and so work on a task as on a club ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement): "Behavior binds to a module's UUID"). *For:* it removes the pressure that made four domains say "my UUID is group"; a task needs no second node (scenario 10); a sole proprietor is one identity with two classifications (12); "group" no longer carries the actor, the place and the audience at once. *Against:* it asks the owner to accept three unratified moves (membership has an identity; roles are positions, not membership labels; a cohort is a separate pluggable), and it makes "is X a group?" answerable only by a completable sentence, not by a type test, which is more honest and less convenient for a reader who wants a type.
- **M4 — a group is a derived set: a rule defines it, membership is discovered (Explored; suits scenarios 5, 9 and 11 only).** `(group)-[:defined_by]->(rule)`; the members are computed. *For:* it is the right model for the audience jobs (form, pos, sell-limit, discount, voucher, buyers), and Memar says a derived value "should not automatically become a stored concept". *Against:* it has nothing for consent, provenance, an interval chosen by a person, or a member who knows; applied to a family or a chat it loses the acquired fact. Kept as a pluggable (**cohort**), not as the kernel.
- **M5 — a group is a place: the conversation, the channel, the container of contents (Deferred).** The reading in the content widgets, the timeline stub, the case premise and the product-default-chat note. *For:* a chat group is the most common experience of the word; the timeline decision says only "designated members" may add to some timelines ([timeline.handoff.md](../timeline/timeline.handoff.md#decisions)). *Against:* contents already live in the timeline, which Content decided is an attach surface for any content; a place that is also a membership list mixes two lifecycles (the conversation outlives members; a membership outlives the conversation); and it fails every group that talks nowhere (scenarios 2, 3 as a list of kin, 8's dues, 9, 11). Deferred until the timeline domain says whether the group is the timeline's audience (the reading this research prefers) or its identity.

M4 and M5 are each right for one job and wrong for the rest, which is the evidence for splitting the jobs. M3 meets every case at the price of three unratified moves and an identity question (6, 9) that no model answers by shape: it is an owner decision (Q14). Scenarios 2 and 13 strain every model for reasons outside the shape: a seat is a position (role and staff's), and visibility is a rule.

#### Smaller choice 1: is a membership a record with an identity, or a plain edge (Tentative: a record)
[group.md](group.md#resolved-via-source-inspection) resolves "several small, single-purpose edges". A plain edge between group and user cannot be the far end of another edge, so everything that belongs to *this user's presence in this group* has nowhere to attach. The corpus has already answered by inventing three pair-keyed records: the content widget `group-user` (a per-user index of comments in a group; its purpose is unstated, [content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md)), the user domain's `GroupUserDisplayName` (the name a user is known by in a group; filed under *user*), and access-control's `GroupUserAccessControl` (an access grant for a pair). Add the things this domain's own edges carry on `joined` (the way, the request, the accepter, the kind) and the position a member holds (scenario 2) and the case for reification in the sense of [Classification Emerges From Rules](../../../docs/modeling.md#classification-emerges-from-rules-and-relations-not-from-intrinsic-labels) is met: the relation "needs to carry further properties or relationships of its own". The counter-argument is the anti-pattern *unnecessary reification* (a node whose sole purpose is connecting two others); the answer is that a membership has its own lifecycle (proposed, active, suspended, ended) and its own events, which a bare edge cannot hold. It is also the same shape as the Task research's "an edge can record which user set it and when" ([status.md](../../knowledge/status/status.md#policies-rules)), pushed one step further. Kept Tentative because it adds a thing that every consumer of membership must now name.

#### Smaller choice 2: containment of groups versus a group as a member (Tentative: two relations)
[organization.md](../../identity/organization/organization.md#organization-vs-group-conceptual-level) says a group can contain "other groups, or even other organizations as members" and that departments are groups of an organization; [parent](edges/parent/parent.md) says a group has a parent. Both state "a group sits inside a group". They differ in a way the scenarios show: a **subgroup** (a department in a company) has members who *count* as members of the larger group; an **organization-member** (the Ministry on the ethics committee) is *one* member of the committee, and the Ministry's own staff are not on it. Collapsing them gives either a committee that inherits the Ministry's staff or a company whose departments' staff are not its staff. Proposed: `parent_is` for subsumption (members flow up, computed), `joined` with a group or organization as the member for unit membership, and a rule that the same pair is not related both ways. A cycle is possible only through unit membership (scenario 4), not through `parent_is`, which is acyclic; this answers [group.md](group.md#unresolved-questions) unresolved 4 for the structure, and leaves a derivation that walks unit membership obliged to be cycle-safe. Against: two relations where the document has one; the alternative is a single `contains` with a flag, which [Modeling Practice](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#common-modeling-anti-patterns) would call a god edge.

#### Smaller choice 3: where a role in a group lives (Tentative: a position owned by role and staff, not a label on the membership)
The `joined` labels mix two things: a *kind of membership* (member, honorary member, supporter: how the group counts the person) and a *role* (treasurer, shift lead, judge). A third reading in the corpus makes the role a group: `(group)-[:role]->(---)` and a `judge` is a group titled "judge" ([role](../role/edges-loop/role/role.md), [judge](../role/data/judge/judge.md)); the member of the role-group holds the role. That reading has the merit of needing no new edge (a role in a group is membership of a child group) and the demerit of the anti-pattern *unnecessary reification* (a group whose sole purpose is to connect one user to one group); it survives only if the seat has a life of its own, and scenario 2 says it does (the seat persists while holders change; a vacancy exists with no one in it), and staff already records a person's position ([staff.md](../staff/staff.md): "person-position"). So: a role in a group is a **position** that belongs to the role and staff domains; a member holds it; the membership carries only its kind. Under that reading the member limit is on the group (twelve seats) or on a position, and the open far end of [membership-term-member_limit](edges/membership-term-member_limit/membership-term-member_limit.md) is answered by the same distinction. Against: the position concept in role and staff is not yet modelled ([role.handoff.md](../role/role.handoff.md), [staff.handoff.md](../staff/staff.handoff.md)), so this choice leans on something that does not exist; hence Tentative and a question for the owner (Q8).

#### Smaller choice 4: the pending states, three edges or one proposal (Explored)
`invite`, `request_to_join` and `joined` are three edge documents. An invitation (the group offers) and a request (the user asks) are the same exchange begun from two sides, and acceptance by the other side is what makes a membership. One *proposal* with an initiator and a response would replace two edges and make "who started it" data, not a name. Against: the documents give the two different rules ([invite](edges/invite/invite.md): a direct invite versus the non-direct `invite_rule` of the [invite](../../../modules-plugable/management/invite/invite.md) pluggable; [joined](edges/joined/joined.md): "accept a join request if the user is a first-cycle friend of any member"), and merging them before the rules are placed would hide that difference. The same offer-and-acceptance shape appears in the Task research (assignment must be accepted; [task.research.001.md](../task/task.research.001.md#open-questions) question 6) and in contract signature; whether it is defined once is Q18.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions

The domain, once decided, has to answer each of these. Questions are written to favour no answer where unsettled; a settled item says so and cites its decision.

#### Identity
- *Settled:* a group's identity is the thing domain's identity ([group.handoff.md](group.handoff.md#decisions), Decided); no `GroupUUID`.
- Is a membership an identity of its own, or a fact about two other identities? What would each answer let the three pair-keyed records do?
- Does the identity stay the same when the group is renamed, when every member has been replaced, when it is moved under another parent, when it is classified as a department, when a new intake year starts, when two groups merge or one splits, and when it is reopened after dissolving? Which of these is a new identity, and which is a `supersedes` ([supersedes](../../identity/thing/edges/supersedes/supersedes.md), defined once on thing)? Does `supersedes` take several predecessors for a merge and several successors for a split?
- If one identity is at once a group, a task, a case, a contract or a product's default chat, which operations of each may reach it, and what removes one role without ending the others? (The Task and Case researches ask the same.)
- Is the same human two members when she has two user identities (personal and work)? Does the group see one person?
- Who may mint a group: any user, only a role, an organization, a rule? Is there a per-user limit (spam), and where is it stated?
- Is a group with zero members a group? With one?
- Is a group's name, description and photo a reference to text and object whose edit is an event on the same identity, so that "Omid changed the group photo" is an event and not a content ([content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md) scenario 11)?

#### Lifecycle
- What states does a group have, and which are positions in its life (forming, active, dormant, dissolved) as against classifications (department, squad) or terms (open for registration)? A minimum member count (the "Minimum" of [member_limit](edges/membership-term-member_limit/membership-term-member_limit.md)) may keep a group "forming"; is that a state, or only a warning?
- What states does a membership have (proposed, active, suspended, ended, refused), and are they set or derived? A term that has run out is derived from the end instant; a suspension is an act.
- May an ended membership resume, or does a return create a new membership that refers back (the same question the Task research asks of reopening, question 5)? What does each say for the history the model is meant to preserve?
- When a group dissolves, what happens to its memberships, its child groups (adopted, orphaned, dissolved with it), the things it owns (the ownership heir edge exists: [heir](../../rights/ownership/edges/heir/heir.md)) and the timeline it was the audience of?
- May a group be deleted at all? The domain backlog says only ancestors may delete a child ([group.md](group.md#group-deletion)); [management.md](../management.md) says organizations "can't be deleted" and take a finish date; [Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates) says overwriting discards an acquired fact. Which of these does the domain accept, and what does a person's right to erasure do to a membership list?
- When the last administrator leaves, or the only member, what is the group?
- Is a fixed-term group (scenarios 2, 9, 10) a group with an end instant, or a group with a rule that ends it?

#### Authorization
- Who may create a group, add a member without consent, accept a request, remove a member, refuse a user, end a membership on the member's behalf, reparent, classify, dissolve? Each is a question of who and of what the rule is; none is the kernel's.
- Where does authority over a group come from: ownership ([owner](../../rights/ownership/edges/owner/owner.md)), a role held in it, a delegation ([delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md)), a law (a court dissolving an association)? [Agency → Authority](../../../docs/agency.md#authority) separates authority from responsibility; the domain must say which it records.
- Who may list the members, see that the group exists, see a member's history, and see the history from before the day she joined? Which module owns these, [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md) or [access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md), whose handoffs both say neither claims group-scoped authorization?
- Who may grant a classification such as Department or Squad? Is it the same mechanism for every kind ([group.md](group.md#unresolved-questions) unresolved 5)?
- Across organizations: may a group include members of another organization, and who owns the membership record?
- When an organization is a member, who acts for it, and who may change that?
- Where authority is inherited from a parent group, does it ever follow unit membership?

#### Rules
- Which rules does the domain itself need, and which belong to organizations: a cycle guard on `parent_is`; the member maximum and its effect (refuse, queue); the minimum and its effect; mandatory and recommended skills; admission by existing members' vouching; term expiry; succession when no authority remains; visibility of the list?
- Where each rule attaches: through `condition_is` on the group, on the membership, on the proposal, or on the module that owns the capability?
- Is the MUST/SHOULD/MAY level set of [membership-term-skill](edges/membership-term-skill/membership-term-skill.md) the group's own vocabulary or the same shared constraint vocabulary the Task research asks about for `requires`?
- Who may attach or remove each rule, and what does removal leave of what it already produced? (The same question the Task research and the pricing lessons ask.)

#### Events
- Which occurrences are events the group exposes as an `EventTarget` ([Modularity → Event as a Module Capability](../../knowledge/modularity/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)): group created, described (name, photo, description), classified or declassified, reparented, dissolved, superseded; proposal made, accepted, declined, expired; member joined, left, removed, suspended, resumed; member refused or blocked, unblocked; representative changed; limit reached; minimum lost?
- Who consumes them: task (the team), timeline (who may add and read), notification, staff and department, analytics for churn and overlap, the rules engine, content (a system notice, [content.research.001.md](../../knowledge/content/content.research.001.md) scenario 11)?
- Which of these already exist as a request or response and need no event type of their own?
- The Go source typed the group's event target by the group's own data model; Khayyam has no generic syntax and that guarantee is lost ([group.handoff.md](group.handoff.md#decisions)). Does the group declare its own event-target abstraction when the event model's owner provides the shape (`Target_Race_Event` is the precedent), or stay with the unqualified one?
- A correction (a membership added by mistake) is a new fact referencing the one it corrects, never a rewrite. Does the group domain accept that as stated?

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each is one paragraph, as a sketch: not a design, and none is to be built before the owner decides.

1. **`membership` (kernel record; Tentative).** A membership is a thing with an identity of its own, tied to one group (or any member-bearing thing) and one user, with a start, an optional end, a kind, and the provenance that `joined` carries today (the way, the proposal, the accepting side). It is created by the member, by an authority, or by a rule or event; it is ended, never deleted; it triggers `member_joined` and `member_left`. Other domains attach to it instead of keying their own record by the pair: the display name (a reference to text), an access grant, a read position, a mute, a position held. Replaces the labelled `joined` edge. Not built before Q3.
2. **`admission` (pluggable; Tentative).** Owns the proposal (from either side, with an expiry), the response, the rule that accepts without a person, the standing refusal, and the terms (maximum, mandatory skills). It is where `invite`, `request_to_join`, `blocked`, `membership-term-member_limit` and `membership-term-skill` are processed together instead of being five bare edges. It does not own consent as a precondition of membership: membership by rule, by law or by birth does not pass through it (Q6). Its relation to the existing [invite](../../../modules-plugable/management/invite/invite.md) pluggable (the non-direct rule) is that one capability may widen to hold both; home open.
3. **`hierarchy` (pluggable; Tentative).** `(group)-[:parent_is]->(group)`, many parents, acyclic, with the cycle guard attached through `condition_is`; the computed effective membership; the ancestor's authority over a descendant; reparenting as an event; the organizational-chart reading for department. Created and removed by someone with authority over the child and the parent. Triggers `group_reparented`. Does not store effective membership. Whether containment is one relation defined once for task and group (`part_of`) or one per domain is Q4's sibling question; the two rules differ (a task's parent says nothing about members, a group's does).
4. **`cohort` (pluggable; Explored).** A group whose members a rule computes: `(group)-[:defined_by]->(rule)`, live or snapshot, no consent, no member event, a privacy rule for who may read the result. It is the other end of the audience links held by form, register, sale limit, discount, voucher and buyers. Marking a group as derived is a loop-edge so no consumer mistakes it for an acquired membership list. Home: either a pluggable under management, or the rule domain's output; the filing rule ("filed under the domain through which it is instantiated") does not settle it.
5. **`representation` (pluggable, probably delegate's; Explored).** `(membership)-[:represented_by]->(user)` when the member is an organization or a group: who votes, signs and receives for it, for what term, and what a replacement leaves in history. It is [delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md)'s capability scoped to a membership, and is listed because no document says who owns it. The Ministry on the ethics committee (scenario 4) is the case.
6. **Continuity under reorganization (use of an existing relation; Explored).** A merge is a new group that [supersedes](../../identity/thing/edges/supersedes/supersedes.md) two predecessors; a split is two groups that each supersede one; a rename and a change of parent are events on one identity. The relation exists on thing; whether it accepts several predecessors or successors, and whether the members' memberships are ended and begun again or carried over, is Q14. Nothing new is proposed beyond answering that.
7. **Membership sensitivity (a rule vocabulary, not a module; Explored).** Who may list members, learn that a group exists, and read a member's history before the day she joined. It is authorization's and access-control's to own; the group's part is that the member list is never readable by default and that [thing-labels](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/thing-labels.md) sensitivity may be attached to the group and to a membership. Listed so scenario 13 has a stated home.
8. **Succession of authority (a rule; Explored).** What happens when the last administrator or owner leaves: a default heir, a vote of the remaining members, a court, nothing. The group must not silently grant authority to anyone (test 22). The rule is an organization's, the event `group_without_authority` is the group's.
9. **Seats and positions (not a group module; Deferred to role and staff).** The seat that outlives its holder (scenario 2) and the vacancy are a position. Role and staff already point at it ([staff.md](../staff/staff.md): person-position; [staff edge](../staff/edges/group/group.md): `HireFor`). Listed so the group does not grow a position of its own.
10. **A shared offer-and-acceptance (Explored).** The proposal of admission, the acceptance of an assignment (Task Q6), the signature of a contract and the confirmation of a trade are the same shape: an initiator, a counterparty's response, an expiry, a record of who started it. Defining it once ([Placing a relation](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation)) would let admission, task acceptance and signature use one capability. Which domain holds it is Q18.

### 7. Test scenarios

Given/when/then in prose, so another session can write tests once the owner decides. Each is marked with what it depends on: **kept** if it follows from settled text, **M3** if it assumes the recommended model, **Q n** if it assumes the default of open question n.

1. **A group of one (M3).** Given a user creates a group and adds no one. When it is saved. Then it exists with one membership, the creator's, and no event claims it is incomplete unless a minimum has been attached.
2. **Membership without consent (Q6).** Given a municipality group with a rule "registered residents are members". When a person registers her residence. Then a membership exists with the way `rule`, no proposal exists, and the person may leave only if the rule allows.
3. **An invitation needs acceptance by default (M3, Q6).** Given a member invites user U. When U accepts. Then a membership exists whose provenance links the proposal, the inviter is visible to U, and the proposal is closed.
4. **A rule accepts a request (kept: joined policy).** Given the group rule "accept if the requester is a first-cycle friend of any member". When R, a friend of a member, asks to join. Then the request is accepted with the accepting side recorded as the rule, not a user.
5. **A refused user cannot ask (M3).** Given U is refused by the group. When U asks to join. Then no membership is created and an `admission_refused` event records the reason; U's refusal is visible to those with authority only.
6. **The maximum holds (M3, Q20).** Given a group of twelve with a maximum of twelve. When a thirteenth proposal is accepted. Then the admission is refused with "limit reached", and no partial membership exists.
7. **Leaving ends, never deletes (M3, Q7).** Given a member leaves. When asked who was a member on a past date. Then the answer is computed from the intervals and includes her.
8. **A return is a new membership (Q7).** Given an ended membership. When the same user joins again. Then a new membership refers to the earlier one, and the earlier is unchanged.
9. **A seat outlives its holder (Q8).** Given a savings circle of twelve seats. When the holder of seat 4 leaves and another takes it. Then the seat and its place in the order are unchanged, the two holders' memberships are both in the history, and the circle never has more than twelve holders.
10. **An organization is one member (Q4).** Given the Ministry is a member of a committee. When the committee votes. Then the Ministry has one membership and one representative; the Ministry's own staff are not members of the committee.
11. **A subgroup's members count upward (Q4).** Given a department whose `parent_is` is a company. When asked who is in the company. Then the department's members are included and the answer is computed at the moment of asking, never stored.
12. **A parent cycle is refused (M3).** Given A is under B and B is under C. When C is made a child of A. Then the guard refuses and nothing changes.
13. **A membership cycle is allowed but a derivation ends (Q5).** Given A is a member of B and B is a member of A. When the effective membership of A is asked. Then the answer ends, lists each once, and neither membership is refused.
14. **Reparenting keeps the story (M3).** Given a department under ministry X. When it is moved under ministry Y. Then the old parent remains in the history, the identity is unchanged, and an authority over both is recorded as the actor.
15. **A merge keeps predecessors (Q14).** Given directorates X and Y merge into Z. When the merge is recorded. Then Z supersedes X and Y, both stay in the graph, their memberships end on the merge instant and Z's begin on it, and "who sat in X on a date" still answers.
16. **A cohort needs no consent and raises no member event (Q12).** Given a cohort "customers with at least two invoices" defined by a rule. When a customer qualifies. Then she appears in the cohort when it is read, no membership exists, no member event fires, and detaching the rule leaves nothing behind.
17. **A form uses a cohort and a group the same way (Q12).** Given a form limited to a group G. When G is a cohort in one case and an acquired group in another. Then the form refers to G by identity in both, and the evaluation happens at the moment of answering.
18. **A sensitive group is not listable (Q15).** Given a group carrying a sensitivity label. When a non-member asks for its members. Then the request is refused, the refusal says nothing about whether the group exists, and no default makes the list readable.
19. **A classification is granted by an authority and recorded (Q11).** Given a group and an authority recognised by the organization's rule. When it classifies the group as a department. Then a loop-edge exists with the actor and the instant, an unauthorised user's attempt is refused, and the vocabulary of kinds is data.
20. **A task takes members directly (M3, Q2).** Given a task with no members. When three neighbours join it. Then it has three memberships and no second group was created; before the first join it had none.
21. **A sole proprietor hires (M3, Q1).** Given a person classified as an organization and working alone. When she adds an employee. Then the employee has a membership in the thing that already exists, no new identity was minted, and the organization classification and the person classification share one identity.
22. **The last authority leaves (Q17).** Given a group whose only administrator leaves. When the leave is recorded. Then a `group_without_authority` event fires, the succession rule of the organization (if any) runs, and no authority is granted by default.
23. **Detaching a rule leaves memberships (M3).** Given an admission rule is detached. When the group continues. Then the existing memberships stay, new proposals fall back to the default, and the earlier decisions stay in history.
24. **A name per membership (M3, Q3).** Given a user known as "Ali" in a family group and "Dr. Rezaei" in a committee. When either name is changed. Then only the membership it is attached to changes, and the other group sees nothing.
25. **A derived value is not stored (kept: Acquired vs Discovered).** Given a group with 40 memberships, 3 ended. When the member count is asked. Then it is computed from the intervals at the moment of asking, and no stored count can drift.

5. **Two ways to say a group sits inside a group.** [organization.md](../../identity/organization/organization.md#organization-vs-group-conceptual-level) says groups can contain "other groups, or even other organizations as members"; [parent](edges/parent/parent.md) says a group has a parent. The same pair can be written both ways, with different consequences for who counts (small choice 2).

## Recommendation (unratified)
The owner has not reviewed this, and nothing in it is decided.

1. **Separate the jobs the word carries (Tentative, medium confidence).** Name the membership as the domain's concept; treat "acts as one", "is a place", "is an audience" and "is a position" as separate capabilities. This is the main claim of finding 1 and the hinge of every other proposal.
2. **Keep a four-element kernel (Tentative):** identity (ThingUUID), membership as a record, events, and the `condition_is` attachment. Everything else is a pluggable that owns its relation and its processing.
3. **Choose M3; keep M2 and M1 as the readings it replaces, M4 as a pluggable, M5 deferred (Tentative).** Anything can take members; "group" is what a thing is called when membership is its nature; the actor classification is separate.
4. **Give the membership an identity (Tentative).** It stops three domains keying their own record by the pair, and gives display name, grant, kind and position a place to attach.
5. **Keep two relations for "inside" (Tentative):** `parent_is` for subsumption, acyclic, many parents; unit membership for a group or organization that is a member as one.
6. **Move processing to four pluggables (Tentative):** admission, hierarchy, cohort, representation; fold the five bare edges into the first.
7. **Do not own roles, conversation, authorization, business relationships or dues (Tentative).** Roles are positions (role, staff); the conversation is the timeline's; authorization and access-control keep group-scoped policy; business relations between organizations stay with the organization domain; dues are the economics domain's.
8. **Do not move, delete or convert anything on this evidence.** The mapping in finding 5 is a reading list for the sessions that will, after the owner answers. `GroupModel` is unchanged; a `MembershipDataModel` is not written ([Khayyam rule 6](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md#governing-rules)).
9. **Ask the three questions that change what the work is first:** question 1 (is a group a user), question 2 (can anything take members), question 3 (does a membership have an identity).

## Open Questions
This is the research's eighth section. Only the owner can decide these. Ordered by what they change: those that change what the domain is first, then those that change how a part is done ([Ask rather than assume](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. They are also entered in the [handoff](group.handoff.md#research-001-questions-and-next-steps-2026-10-18).

1. **Is a group a kind of user, or a thing with members that is classified as an actor only when it acts as one?** Default: the second; `(user)-[:group]` is applied to the identity when the collective owns, signs or is notified. Blocks: `GroupModel` composing `UserModel`, the `(user)`/`(group)` duplicates in the ownership and status edges, the sole-proprietorship case, the mention target of the Text research, the base of the `group` loop-edge.
2. **Can any thing take members (a task, a case, a contract, a department), or is a team always a separate group node related by an edge?** Default: any thing can; "group" names a thing whose main nature is having members. Blocks: [group.md](group.md#unresolved-questions) unresolved 1, Task question 2 and `TaskModel`, the case premise and the contract question "is a contract a group's record", the product default chat note, the `edges-loop` the group says the task implements.
3. **Does a membership have an identity of its own?** Default: yes. Blocks: the shape of `joined`, the three pair-keyed records, the display name, the position held, any `MembershipDataModel`.
4. **When a group is a member of another, do its members count?** Default: no for unit membership, yes for `parent_is` (two relations). Blocks: the committee/organization case, the department chart, effective membership, the cycle rule, and whether containment is one relation defined once with task's `part_of`.
5. **May membership cycles exist?** Default: `parent_is` is acyclic and guarded; unit membership may form a cycle, and every derivation is cycle-safe and never walks unit membership to inherit authority. Blocks: [group.md](group.md#unresolved-questions) unresolved 4, the ancestor-deletion backlog.
6. **May membership arise without the member's consent?** Default: yes, by an authority, a rule, a law or an event; consent is the default of the admission pluggable, not a kernel precondition. Blocks: the design of admission, the household and municipality cases, privacy.
7. **Does leaving or removal end the membership or delete it, and is a return a new membership?** Default: ends it; nothing is deleted; a return is a new membership that refers back. Blocks: history, erasure (Q15), the lifecycle.
8. **Where does a role in a group live: a label on the membership, a role-group, or a position owned by role and staff?** Default: a position (role and staff); the membership carries only its kind (member, honorary member, supporter). Blocks: the `joined` labels, [role/edges/group](../role/edges/group/group.md), [staff/edges/group](../staff/edges/group/group.md) `HireFor`, Task question 7, the member-limit far end.
9. **Is oversight a seat or an inspection right?** Default: a seat without a vote is a membership of a kind; an inspection right with no seat is a delegation of authority ([delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md)), not an `oversees` edge of the group. Blocks: [group.md](group.md#unresolved-questions) unresolved 2, the committee case.
10. **Do business relationships between organizations (franchise, agent, branch) belong to the group domain?** Default: no; they are organization-to-organization relations already held in [organization-relation-organization.kh](../../identity/organization/edges/organization-relation-organization.kh) (`Branch`, `Agent`, `Franchise`) and, once an agreement exists, the contract and `agent_for` ([Agency](../../../docs/agency.md#nested-agency)); a franchise *network* with members is a group of those organizations. Blocks: [group.md](group.md#unresolved-questions) unresolved 3.
11. **Who may classify a group as a department, squad, tribe or guild, and is it one mechanism for all kinds?** Default: an authority over the group (its owner, or the owning organization for a department), recorded with its actor and instant; one mechanism; the vocabulary of kinds is data. Blocks: [group.md](group.md#unresolved-questions) unresolved 5, [department/edges-loop/department](../department/edges-loop/department/department.md).
12. **Is a group defined by a rule (a cohort) a different capability from a group people joined?** Default: yes, a pluggable; the kernel membership is acquired only; consumers refer to both by identity. Blocks: the form, pos, sell-limit, discount, voucher and buyers links, scenario 11.
13. **Is a group a place for contents, or the audience of a timeline?** Default: the audience; the place is the timeline. Blocks: the content widgets `group` and `group-user`, the case premise ("so it can be a timeline"), [timeline/edges/chat](../timeline/edges/chat/chat.md), the "same group" reply rule.
14. **What is a group's identity across rename, replacement of all members, merge, split, annual intake and reopening?** Default: rename, member replacement and reparenting keep it; merge and split create new groups that supersede their predecessors; an annual intake is a new group per year unless the owner names it one. Blocks: scenarios 6 and 9, the `supersedes` shape, tests 14 and 15.
15. **Who may see a member list, and how does it meet erasure?** Default: nobody by default; members and authority only; sensitivity labels attach to a group and to a membership; erasure of a person is handled by a rule that keeps the group's history without the person's attributes. Blocks: scenario 13, authorization and access-control, the "ended, not deleted" principle.
16. **May a group be deleted, or only dissolved, and what happens to its children, its owned things and its timeline?** Default: dissolved with an end instant; children adopted by the parent or left standing; owned things pass by the ownership heir; the timeline stays readable by former members as the rule says. The ancestor-only backlog item becomes "only ancestors' authority may dissolve a child". Blocks: [group.md](group.md#group-deletion), the lifecycle.
17. **When the last authority leaves, who acts for the group, and may a collective hold authority itself?** Default: a rule of the organization decides succession; nothing is granted silently; acting for a collective is a delegation. Blocks: test 22, representation, the actor classification.
18. **Is there one shared offer-and-acceptance capability (admission proposal, task acceptance, contract signature), or one per domain?** Default: one, defined once; the home is the owner's. Blocks: admission, Task question 6, the invite pluggable.
19. **Is the MUST/SHOULD/MAY level vocabulary shared with the Task research's `requires` and the rule conditions, or the group's own?** Default: shared, defined once. Blocks: [membership-term-skill](edges/membership-term-skill/membership-term-skill.md) and the `requires` pluggable.
20. **Is a member limit a property of the group or of a position, and what do minimum and expected do?** Default: of the group (a seat count is a property of the group, a limit per position belongs to the position); maximum is enforced at admission, minimum is a warning that may hold the group "forming", expected is planning data. Blocks: the far end of [membership-term-member_limit](edges/membership-term-member_limit/membership-term-member_limit.md) and its `.kh` record.
21. **Is the org group listing (offset and limit) a modeling matter?** Default: no; a service-layer convention shared by all listings. Blocks: nothing.
