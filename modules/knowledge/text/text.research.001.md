Two facts about the word. First, Memar has no document that defines Text as a term; it uses the word as the standing example of an abstraction that earns its independence. That is Memar's support for the domain's existence and the list of five concerns this research tests the domain against: holding, validating, versioning, translating, finding. Second, Memar's Content document holds a different, finer notion that the text domain's `tokens` edge already echoes: a "Lexical Token" is a string treated "as a single, globally unique" node, with ambiguity resolved by composition and left standing when a bare token has no context ([Memar Content → Lexical Token vs. Sense](../content/content.md#lexical-token-vs-sense--handling-ambiguity)). This research keeps that notion apart from "Text" until finding 3.

The removal test is [What Earns Foundational Status](../../../docs/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status), with its two guards: the fabrication must land inside the foundational set, and the test runs against requirements that exist, not anticipated ones.

It produces discovered, removable data from acquired data; a query with its own types, events, storage, and processing is itself a pluggable module".

   - *Reference / locator / addressable entity* — [Memar Content](../content/content.md): a reference is a relation, not a property; a locator is a mechanism to find the referenced thing and must not be collapsed into it; any part of content that needs to carry an independent relation, property or rule becomes an independently addressable entity (a principle the document itself calls circular).
   - *Interned text* (agent's term, used in this research only) — a text that exists once for a given value and classification and is read-only, as the [uri loop-edge](../../identity/uri/edges-loop/uri/uri.md) says of a URI ("Just one text with desire string MUST exist"; "readonly"). The term is defined here because no Memar document names the pattern; finding 2 argues it recurs.

## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The text domain gives an organization one answer to five questions about any words a human reads or writes in it: *what exactly do the words say* (a value that stays the same however many places show it), *in which human language and writing system*, *how did they get to say that* (who wrote them, who changed them, what they said before), *how do the same words reach a reader in another language*, and *how are they found again* (by their words, by the references inside them, by their neighbours). Every other domain, instead of holding a string, says "the title of this is that text". This restates the Decided material in plain words: human-readable text may be stored only here and everything else relates to it ([text.handoff.md](text.handoff.md#from-organization-ideas-backlog--identity--text-ownership)), and it follows the five concerns the Attribute-or-Edge Test gives the abstraction (holding, validating, versioning, internationalizing, making searchable).

**What the rest of the repository already expects of text.** The domain is the place five other documents send a relation they do not want to own, which is the best evidence of what it is for:

| Who defers to text | What it expects | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Thing | `ThingUUID` is "usually used to SET/GET and show locale title by knowledge/text domain"; the thing domain's open question asks whether every thing has a name, and that name would be a text | [thing/protocol/dt-uuid.kh](../../identity/thing/protocol/dt-uuid.kh), [thing handoff](../../identity/thing/thing.handoff.md) (Open Questions, "What must a thing offer?") |
| URI | A URI "is a thing whose text is the URI string, so a URI record is a thing and is a text"; one text per URI string, read-only | [uri.md](../../identity/uri/uri.md), [uri loop-edge](../../identity/uri/edges-loop/uri/uri.md) |
| Label | The words of a label are in the text domain; the label domain carries none | [label.md → Text](../label/label.md) |
| Content | Every comment body, caption and title is a relation to a text; one content with a text per language (default) | [content.research.001.md](../content/content.research.001.md#2-minimal-kernel-and-what-must-be-pluggable) K2, open question 6 |
| Khayyam itself | Explanatory text above code "must be returnable and localizable (text domain) or live in documents" | [Khayyam practice → rule 5](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md) |
| Memar's Type | A Type's "localized names, UI labels, human-readable documentation, and localized descriptions of its behaviors and failures belong to the Type itself" | [Type → Human-facing identity](../../../docs/type.md) (see contradiction 12) |

**The word hides four things (agent, Tentative; the evidence is the documents themselves).** The documents use "text" for four different objects, and the confusion between them is the source of most of the open questions:

1. **The slot**: the place where words live for a purpose and persist through changes ("the title of this product", "the body of this comment"). Evidence: `ThingUUID` identity, the Go storage keyed by `(textID, language)` with a version offset ([widgets/text/protocol/storage.kh](ui/text/protocol/storage.kh)), the Decided shared versioning mechanism "username change included" ([content.handoff.md](../content/content.handoff.md#versioning-content-identity)).
2. **The value**: the exact characters at one moment, in one language. Evidence: "Content Addressability" kept from Git ([text.handoff.md](text.handoff.md)), the uri text "read-only ... User can create new one", the Lexical Token ([Memar Content](../content/content.md)).
3. **The interned word**: a short value that exists once and is pointed at from many places. Evidence: "A token is a hit: one token stored in one place, pointing at every text it was found in" ([tokens](edges/tokens/tokens.md)), the hashtag and mention rule "MUST store with # sign" ([edges-loop/hashtag](edges-loop/hashtag/hashtag.md)), the one-text-per-URI rule.
4. **The expression in a language**: the words as one reader in one language reads them, which a translator produces from another. Evidence: "the same text in two languages is two texts" ([text.md](text.md#model)) against `ListLanguages(textID, ...)` ([storage.kh](ui/text/protocol/storage.kh)).

The Decided statements are true of different ones: "other domains hold a relation, not a copy" is about the slot; "content-addressable" is about the value; "the same text in two languages is two texts" is about the expression; "unique is the identity, not the words" is about the slot against the value. Finding 3 tests whether one structure can keep all four true at once; its answer (rival model TM4) is the layered one.

**Scenarios.** Fourteen, from several industries and cultures. Each states what is being done, what breaks the obvious model, and what it says. "The obvious model" is: a text is a string in one language, owned by whoever refers to it.

1. **A product title in a bilingual market (a Swiss shop with German, French and Italian customers; a Canadian retailer; an Indian one with Hindi, English and a regional language).** The title exists in three languages, the French one was added last month, one customer's profile prefers Italian, another sets no preference, and the Romansh title does not exist yet. *Breaks:* one text per thing; "the title" as a single value; translation as an afterthought. *Says:* the referrer must keep one relation while the languages grow (Memar's own i18n argument), something must say which member a reader gets when theirs is missing (a fallback chain, a rule), and "no Romansh title" is a legitimate state, not an error. Which node the product points at, and what groups the variants, is the whole content of finding 3.
2. **Equally authentic versions of one law (federal law published in several languages; a regulation authentic in all of a union's official languages; a treaty with two language versions widely discussed as differing in meaning).** No version is the original; each is authoritative; in the treaty case the versions are not translations of each other and the dispute is which prevails. *Breaks:* translation as a derivation from an original; "same text in two languages" as meaning-preserving; a head-and-satellites structure. *Says:* the relation between language versions needs a symmetric reading ("these are the authentic versions of one instrument") beside the derivation reading ("this was translated from that"), and which one holds is a rule of the thing that owns the versions, not a property of the texts. The domain's own source says it: the literary-theory sense of "text" sets "translations" apart from "the original information content" ([text.md](text.md)), so a translation is a relation, never a field.
3. **Persian text typed on many keyboards (a university registry, a Persian-language shop, a chat).** The same name typed with Arabic yeh and kaf (the characters an Arabic keyboard produces) or Persian yeh and kaf looks identical and compares unequal; a half-space (zero-width non-joiner) is present or absent; digits may be Persian, Arabic-Indic or Latin; a username "Ali" with a Cyrillic letter that looks Latin passes a naive uniqueness check. *Breaks:* "equal text" as byte equality; "lowercase" as the one normalization; uniqueness of a username as a property of strings. *Says:* a text needs a declared canonical form for its identity-bearing comparison, language- and script-aware folding is a separate and removable concern (it varies by language and by purpose, and is discovered, not acquired), and the rule "this username must be unique" belongs to the domain that owns usernames, evaluated over folded text. The domain's own residue shows the entry point: a language record carries its writing systems ("Scripts: Persian script", "Latin", "Cyrillic" in [`internal/languages.kh`](edges/language/labels/internal/languages.kh)).
4. **Text with no spaces, and text in two scripts (Japanese or Chinese product reviews; a Persian post with an English brand name; a Hindi sentence in Latin letters).** The word boundary is found by a dictionary, not by whitespace; one sentence holds two languages; a search for the brand must match inside the Persian run. *Breaks:* a `word` and `phrase` as stored classifications; one language per text; a sentence offset as a given. *Says:* tokenization is an analyzer chosen per language ([tokens](edges/tokens/tokens.md) already stores which analyzer, "module+version", produced the tokens), the language of a span can differ from the language of the text (code switching), and the offsets a hit list carries are only meaningful against one exact value of one text (they break when the value changes).
5. **A username that changes hands (any platform).** A user renames from "omid" to "omid.h"; a month later another user takes "omid"; old posts mention "@omid"; a screenshot says "omid wrote this". *Breaks:* a mention as the string after `@`; text as identity; "the text of a name" as stable. *Says:* this is the example Memar's own modeling gives for the question "who held the username before, and when did it change" ([Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates)): the previous value is an acquired fact the change event keeps, the mention is a relation from the post to the *user*, resolved at the time of writing, and the string `@omid` is only how the author typed it. The mention edge already says it ("MUST store with @ sign to distinguish between `omid` and `@omid`", and points at `(user)`), but its marker rule and its resolved relation are one folder that has not decided which it is.
6. **A word with several senses (the Persian word شیر meaning lion, milk and tap; "bank" in a finance and a rivers glossary; "Architecture" bare, as a title).** One string, unrelated meanings; a search for it should offer the senses, not guess; a compound ("Software Architecture") narrows. *Breaks:* a term as a unique string with one meaning; the `Localization` idea ("Near Pizza Store" as a query-time rule). *Says:* Memar's Lexical Token already separates the interned string from its senses and says an unresolved bare token "is not an error state"; the text domain's `term` and `word` loop-edges have no rule for any of it. Whether a term opens a hub that describes it is a Decided statement of the content handoff ("a term opens a hub that describes it", content.handoff Decisions), which makes the term a text *that content describes*.
7. **The same words through different channels (an order confirmation sent as a Persian SMS, a push notification, a web page and a screen reader; a tutorial that teaches HTML and must display `<script>`).** An SMS in a non-Latin alphabet holds far fewer characters per segment than one in Latin letters; a notification truncates; some social platforms count certain scripts as two characters; a limit of 50 counted in bytes admits 50 English letters but only 25 Persian ones, since each Persian letter takes two bytes in the usual encoding. The implemented validator rejects any text containing `=` ([`widgets/text-editor/validator.kh`](ui/text-editor/validator.kh)), so "2+2=4", a URL with a query string and an HTML tutorial are all "illegal". *Breaks:* "maximum length" as a property of a text; "forbidden characters" as a property of a text; length measured by `len()` of bytes ([Memar's `ValidateTextLength`](../../process/validators/text.kh) does). *Says:* the limit and the forbidden set come from the consumer or the role (a username role, an SMS channel, an HTML render target), the same text is valid for one and not for another, and the unit of length is itself a decision. This is [Constraints Belong to the Constraining Concern](../../../docs/modeling.md#constraints-belong-to-the-constraining-concern) verbatim.
8. **A scripture with its commentaries and variant readings (a verse-numbered sacred text with readings that differ between transmissions; a divan with commentary; a statute with annotations).** The base wording must never be edited silently; people cite "chapter 2, verse 255"; commentary is anchored to a verse or a phrase; the transmissions disagree and nobody can say which is "current". *Breaks:* the current value as authoritative; in-place edit; citation as a link to a whole text. *Says:* this is the content research's scenario 4 seen from the value's side: the base is read-only with variants as separate values that name each other, a citation targets a part of an exact value, and the part's address depends on the exact value it was made against ([Memar Content → Addressability](../content/content.md#addressability), including its admitted circularity). The domain's `citations_number` folder is about this relation between texts, but stores a count.
9. **Text that comes from a recording or an image (a voice note's transcript, subtitles in two languages of a film with dubbing, alt text on a photograph, OCR of a scanned contract).** The machine's transcript is wrong in places and carries a confidence; a human corrects it; the subtitle lines are anchored to time ranges; the alt text is something a person wrote *for* the image. *Breaks:* a text as something a person authored; one text per object; "the" transcript. *Says:* a text may be *derived from* an object or another text, by a method, with a confidence, and a human correction is a new fact that references the machine one, not a rewrite. The content research reached the same conclusion for a machine-written summary ([content scenario 11](../content/content.research.001.md#1-what-the-domain-is-for)): derived text must not look like something someone said. The pluggable [ai/text](../../../modules-plugable/ai/text/protocol/dt-type.kh) already lists these producers (Translation, Summarization, Labeling, Spell and Punctuation Detection and Correction, Lemmatization, OffenseDetection).
10. **A message with holes and plurals (a notification "You have 3 new messages"; a title with dynamic data; an invoice header with the customer's name).** In Russian the noun form changes with the number in more than one way; Arabic distinguishes six plural categories; Japanese none; some languages inflect for the gender of the person named. The translator must translate a *template*, not a sentence. *Breaks:* a text as a literal value; concatenation of translated fragments; the "dynamic data in a title, text substitution, text piping" idea that [text.handoff.md](text.handoff.md#questions-this-domain-could-not-settle) records as still open. *Says:* a template is a text with named parameters whose rendering is discovered and not stored; the parameter-to-form selection rule is per language and belongs to the translation side; no model of "what a text is" solves this without a capability.
11. **A form text reused by a thousand tasks (a complaint letter template, a standard contract clause, a course's welcome message).** [thing's template edge](../../identity/thing/edges/template/template.md) says a `Task` must be duplicated "with relation label and same text (Complaint text, ...)". One instance's owner edits the clause for their case. *Breaks:* "every other domain holds a relation, not a copy" if the relation is a shared mutable node (the edit changes a thousand tasks); also "copy" if the copy is a stored string (a thousand strings that can no longer be found as the same clause). *Says:* the sharing must be of an unchanging value, and the owner's edit must create a new value for that owner only (copy on write). Who may edit is a fact about the referrer, not about the text.
12. **Erasure on request inside a thread (a person asks that what they wrote be removed; the thread of replies must keep its shape; one of the words was a hashtag other people used too).** The history of changes must stay (nothing is lost); the words must go; a shared word cannot go because one person asked. *Breaks:* append-only history as the whole story; deletion as one act; a shared text as the erased person's. *Says:* this is the content research's open question 7 at the value level: erasure of a stored value is an authority-gated event that keeps the fact it happened, it applies to the author's own values and never to an interned word others use, and a duty to erase is a rule that outranks "nothing lost" and must be written down as such. (General knowledge: such duties exist in several jurisdictions; the exact scope is not claimed.)
13. **A poem and its prose gloss (a ghazal in couplets with line breaks that carry the form; a prose explanation beside it).** Line breaks, spacing and the order of couplets are the text; reformatting changes it; the gloss is not part of it. *Breaks:* whitespace as insignificant; any normalization that "cleans" the value; the text and its paratext in one value. *Says:* the exact characters are the identity-bearing thing (so normalization for *comparison* must never rewrite the *stored* value), and the "text kind" (Poem, Scientific, Skill: the candidate values recorded in [edges-loop/text](edges-loop/text/text.md)) is a classification that tells renderers and analyzers what to preserve.
14. **A prompt and the answer an AI produced (a customer asks an assistant to draft a reply; the draft is edited and sent).** The prompt is a text with a role (an instruction); the answer is generated, may be regenerated, and a human edits it before it becomes a message; the identity of the model that produced it matters later. *Breaks:* generated text as authored; one text per exchange; regeneration as an edit. *Says:* it is scenario 9 and 10's machine side again: the generated text is a derived text with a method (the model and its version, which the existing token analyzer field already shows the shape of: "module+version"), the human edit is a new text that cites it, and [edges-loop/prompt](edges-loop/prompt/prompt.md) is a role of a text, not a kind.

**What the scenarios say together (agent, Tentative).** A text is not always written by anyone (9, 14), not one language (4, 1, 2), not one reader's equal (1, 3), not unique as words (5, 11) and not as identity (6, 3), not a literal (10), not independent of its consumer (7), not safe to normalize (3, 13), not editable in place (8, 11) and not always erasable (12). The kernel cannot assume an author, a single language, the right to edit, a length unit, a rendering target or a comparison rule. Each is either a relation with a rule, or a capability.

**The second layer: the underlying problem the answers assume.** The first layer answers what was asked. The reading beneath it is that the owner's actual problem is not "how do we store a title and a comment body" but "how do we stop the domain from becoming the place where every text-processing idea ever sketched (search engine, tokenizer, spell-checker, XSS filter, translation workflow, reading-time estimator, mention parser) lands in the kernel because it mentions a string". Evidence: of twenty-seven edge and edge-loop folders, the survey and this reading find that at least fifteen are analytics output, markers a parser finds, or normal forms (finding 5); the domain's own `internal/matn_____/` is a hand-built inverted index; and the validator in `widgets/` is a security filter. A reader who holds a different reading of the problem can set the proposals aside without discarding the scenarios.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

#### The removal test run on the text itself (agent, Tentative)
Without a text domain, what must be fabricated or bent among the remaining foundations?

- **Thing / User** would carry name and description strings: `username`, bio, titles. The thing domain's own key says "show locale title by knowledge/text domain" ([dt-uuid.kh](../../identity/thing/protocol/dt-uuid.kh)); a user domain with a string field for each is the "ProductName" duplication the Attribute-or-Edge Test rejects.
- **Label** would need the words of a label; the label domain says it carries none ([label.md](../label/label.md)).
- **URI** would need its string; it is defined as a thing that reads as text ([uri.md](../../identity/uri/uri.md)); the `TextModel` that URI composes exists because of this.
- **Content** would need a stable, language-aware, versioned body of its own; the content research's K2 is exactly "body by reference to texts".

Four of these land inside the foundational set, so the guard is met. The test shows that *a home for words* is needed, not that every text capability is: the test applied to each capability below separates them.

#### Proposed kernel (agent, Tentative)
Five elements, each with who may create or remove and what it triggers, because [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement) forbids a bare edge.

| # | Element | Who may create or remove | What it triggers | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TK1 | **Identity** — a text is a thing; its identity is the thing domain's `ThingUUID` and persists through value changes; two texts may hold equal words | any identity with authority over the referrer, in the same request that creates the referring relation; never physically removed (see TK3) | the text exists; `text_created` | Decided: [text.handoff.md](text.handoff.md) (ThingUUID); [text.md → Locale text](text.md#locale-text) (equal words allowed) |
| TK2 | **Value** — the exact characters, as written, plus one declared canonical comparison form and a length that names its unit | the creator at creation; later values under TK3 | validation against the *requirement the referrer states* (the check is the validators', see finding 4) | Decided that the text domain states the requirement, not the check ([text.md → Validation](text.md#validation)); the unit and the canonical form are proposals |
| TK3 | **Change** — a new value is an event that keeps the old one, the requester, the approver and the reason; a correction names what it corrects; a text may be *read-only* (no value change allowed, a new text is made instead); erasure of a value is a separate, authority-gated event that keeps the fact that it happened | the identity with authority over the referrer (the text does not know who that is); a read-only text is set so at creation | `text_changed`, `text_erased`; the old value stays reachable by its address | Decided direction: one cross-domain change mechanism, "username change included" ([content.handoff.md](../content/content.handoff.md#versioning-content-identity)); read-only: [uri loop-edge](../../identity/uri/edges-loop/uri/uri.md); the value address is the content-derived part of the Decided identity statement |
| TK4 | **Language** — `(text)-[:text_language]->(language_human)`, with the script when the language has several | the creator, or a language detector's discovered suggestion recorded as a different fact; changes only with a value change | re-selection of the analyzers and rules that depend on language | Decided edge ([language.md](edges/language/language.md)); the script and the detector are proposals |
| TK5 | **Referenceability** — the domain defines no role names: a referrer names its own relation (`title`, `body`, `bio`), and the text answers "who refers to me, and as what" as a discovered view | the referrer creates and removes its own relation; a text with no referrer remains (it is history) | nothing by itself; a referrer's removal may make the text unreferenced, which is a discovered fact an erasure rule may read | [Attribute-or-Edge Test](../../../docs/modeling.md#the-attribute-or-edge-test): "`Title` names the relationship" |

Not in the kernel by this proposal: translation and its suggestion, tokens and the index, matching, queries, citation counts, reading duration, hashtags, mentions, commands, anchors, templates, style, sensitivity, the type vocabulary, derivation from objects. The kernel also exposes the attachment points other domains defined once and text must not copy ([Placing a relation](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation)): thing's `supersedes` (a new text that takes another's place), `template`, the shared status loops, and the generic `condition_is` through which a rule attaches.

A property of TK3 worth stating: because the previous value stays reachable by an address derived from the value itself, two texts that hold equal words need not hold the words twice, and a span anchored to an exact value cannot silently drift. This is the only place the Decided "content-derived" half of the identity statement is used; the Decided "immutable-at-creation" half is the ThingUUID.

#### Pluggable: the capability each would own (agent, Tentative)
Each row: the relationship and its processing together; who may create or remove it; what it triggers. A pluggable carries a capability name, not a domain name ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement)).

| Capability | Relationship | Processing | Who may create/remove | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| **reference-resolution** (Tentative) | `(text)-[:mention]->(user)`, `(text)-[:hashtag]->(term)`, `(text)-[:command]->(service)`, `(text)-[:citation]->(text)`, `(text)-[:anchor]->(uri)`: one relation from a *span* of a text to a target | recognizes the marker grammar (`@`, `#`, `/`, a quotation, a link), resolves it to the target's identity at write time, records the span against the exact value, notifies the target where its owner allows | the author at write time (acquired marker, resolved by the system) or the system by parsing (discovered), recorded as different facts; ends with the value change that drops the span | `user_mentioned`, `term_used`, `command_invoked`, `text_cited` |

| **derivation** (Explored) | `(text)-[:derived_from]->(object or text)` with a method and a confidence | records what produced the text (a model and its version, a human, an OCR run); keeps a human correction as a new fact that cites the machine one | the producer; a corrector | `derived_text_added`; a distinguished kind so that derived text never reads as authored |

Things deliberately *not* proposed as modules because the graph already holds them: **sensitivity** is a label (the registered word [Sensitivity](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/sensitivity/sensitivity.md) "can be attached to any node", "does not hide the node by itself", a rule may read it), so the handoff's question "what is a sensitive text" has a draft answer in another module, and the widget's hide and show are one reader's state; a **reading duration** is computable from a value, a language and a reading speed (discovered), and falls under search or a statistics view; the **type vocabulary** is the label domain's `text-type` ([label handoff](../label/label.handoff.md)); a **fallback chain** is a rule inside localization; **normalization for comparison** is configuration of the canonical form (TK2) and the folding rules (search).

#### The three-way boundary: text, content, object (agent, Tentative)
The content research asked this domain to answer (its Next Step 5 and open questions 1, 2 and 6). The proposal is a table of who owns what, and one rule of thumb: *text owns the words; content owns the act of saying them; object owns the bytes.*

| Concern | Text | Content ([content.research.001](../content/content.research.001.md)) | Object / bytes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | a persistent text identity; a content-derived address for each stored value | the work-level identity of a communicated articulation; each content revision a value with a content-derived address | a media object by its bytes' address (not read) |
| What "edit" means | a new value of a text (TK3), authorized by the referrer | a new revision of the *arrangement*: which texts and objects, in what order, as whom | a new object |
| Revision of a body | the value history of each text | K3: the content revision is a manifest that pins `(text identity, value address)` for every text and the address of every object; its own address is derived from that manifest | not applicable |
| Language | one language (and script) per text; the variant relation is localization's | "one text per language" is a rule on the content side (K2) | none (a recording's spoken language is a derived relation to a text) |
| Who said it | not known; the text has a creator, not a speaker | requester, speaker, credited parties (K3, attribution) | not known |
| Mentions, hashtags | the marker and its span live in the text; the resolved relation is reference-resolution's | "mentioned users" for a limit rule is a discovered union over the content's texts | none |
| Reply, quote, place | none | owns | none |
| Derived words (transcript, alt, OCR) | owns the text; derivation owns the link | decides whether it is shown as part of the body | owns the bytes it was derived from |

The rule of thumb gives a test any session can apply: *would the thing still exist if nobody had said it to anyone?* If yes (the words of a legal definition, a glossary term, a dictionary entry), it is a text that content may or may not use. If no (a reply, a post, a message), it is a content that happens to hold texts.

**How this answers the content research (agent, Tentative; each point cites the content research).**
- *Open question 2, hold the text by a relation.* Agreed, and the text side adds reasons the content research did not have: a text is a slot with versions and a language (so a content with two captions, a revision history or a translation has no *one* text to share an identity with), and a single comment that holds one text and nothing else could share the text's identity only by making "the comment" and "its words" the same node, which forbids the comment's own edits and replies from being distinguished from the words' history (rival M3 of the content research stays a no).
- *Open question 1 and the "content-derived or persistent" contradiction (content contradiction 3).* The text side reconciles the two Decided halves the same way the content research proposed for content: persistent identity at the slot (the `ThingUUID`), content-derived address at the stored value. Both layers of the content domain then compose: a content revision is a Merkle-style manifest whose leaves are text value addresses and object addresses, so "Content Addressability, the one Git property worth keeping" lives at the value and manifest level, and "persistent id" lives at the slot and work level.
- *Open question 6, one content with one text per language.* This default rests on the text domain's "two texts" statement (L1 of finding 3) and on nothing else. It is true only if the text domain keeps one language per text. If the owner chooses variants inside one text identity (L2 or L3), the default becomes "one content, one text, many variants". The two questions (this research's 2 and the content research's 6) must be answered together.
- *Next Step 5 (mention and hashtag).* Proposed: the marker and the span stay with the text and the resolved relation is reference-resolution's, attached to *text*. The content research's `mentions` and `topic` pluggables then become a discovered union over the content's texts (a shortcut), not a second relation with the same meaning ([A Relation Several Domains Need Is Defined Once](../../../docs/modeling.md#a-relation-several-domains-need-is-defined-once)). This changes two Tentative rows in the content research, not any Decided one.
- *Open question 12 and Missing 6 (anchor).* The part of a text a reply, a credit or a commentary targets is a span against an exact value; the text domain should define the span address once (open question 11) so that content, tokens and references do not each invent one.
- *Content contradiction 15 (a content wants one text id).* Reported by the content research; this research adds the reason on the text side (above) and does not resolve it.

**Tokens, search and analytics (agent, Tentative; asked for in the brief).** The domain's `edges/tokens`, `match-exactly`, `match-partly`, `match-indirectly`, `citations_number` (as a count), `duration` and `edges-loop/query`, `lowercase`, `typo` produce numbers, matches and normal forms from acquired text. Under [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement) ("Analytics is never kernel ... cross-domain analytics sit under the category's analytics namespace, named by the knowledge produced"), all of that is a pluggable, and the folder the repository already has for it is `modules-plugable/knowledge/analytics/search-analytic/`, empty but for a `protocol/` directory. Memar's own sentence that a general `Text` abstraction is "the natural place to build indexing and search capability once" is satisfied by attachment: the text domain exposes the value and the language, and the search pluggable is installed against *every* domain that holds text, which is why it is cross-domain analytics and not a text-domain module. The survey's default (Q5) is to leave `tokens_by` in the kernel as an acquired relation and move the match edges and scores out. This research asks one more question about it: `(text)-[:tokens_by]->(module)` records *which analyzer processed this text*, which is a processing record (discovered), while *which analyzer is configured for a language* is configuration (acquired); the first leaves with the rest, the second is a setting of the pluggable. The disagreement with the survey's default is small and recorded as open question 5.

### 3. Rival models

Marks: **Tentative** (leaning), **Explored** (explored, unresolved), **Deferred**. None is Decided.

Three independent choices are separated because the documents run them together: *what a text node is* (the TM models), *how languages relate* (the L models), and *what is inside the value* (the S models).

#### Choice A: what a text node is
- **TM1 — text as a string field in each referrer (the industry status quo; Explored, rejected by a Decided statement).** *For:* nothing to build. *Against:* the Decided "human-readable text may be stored only in the Text domain"; every domain re-implements language, validation and history, which is the duplication the Attribute-or-Edge Test names. Included so the others have a floor.
- **TM2 — text as a slot with in-place versions (Tentative as the reading of the carried code).** One `ThingUUID`; the value changes under it; versions are numbered under the same id; language is a key of the version, so one id can hold many languages. *For:* it is what the Go storage does (`Get(textID, language, versionOffset)`, `ListLanguages(textID)`); the referrer's relation never changes when a language is added; fits the Decided shared change mechanism. *Against:* it contradicts "the same text in two languages is two texts" ([text.md](text.md#model)); the version offset is a counter, not an address, so a span or a translation cannot pin "the value I saw"; sharing a slot between referrers makes one edit change all (scenario 11); and read-only values (uri, scripture) need a special case.
- **TM3 — text as an immutable interned value (Explored).** The identity is the value itself; equal words are one node; a change is a new node that `supersedes` the old one; history lives in the referrer's relation events. *For:* it is exactly the Lexical Token of Memar's Content, the one-text-per-URI rule and the token "stored in one place"; it answers "who held the username before" by the referrers' relation history; sharing is safe because nothing changes. *Against:* it contradicts "two things may hold equal text; what is unique is the identity, not the words" ([text.md](text.md#locale-text)) and the Decided `ThingUUID` identity (an identifier whose behaviour is unstated, [thing handoff](../../identity/thing/thing.handoff.md), so content-derived is not excluded, but it is not what the documents say); a translation has no anchor (equal words in two roles are one node); and it makes every keystroke-level edit a new node unless drafts are excluded.
- **TM4 — layered: a slot with immutable, content-addressed values (Tentative, recommended).** The text is the slot: a `ThingUUID`, exclusive to its referrer by default, with a language. Each stored value is immutable and addressed by a derived address of its normalized characters and language; the slot points at its current value; a change is an event that adds a value and moves the pointer; equal values in two slots are stored once. Interned words (hashtags, URIs, tokens) are slots with one value, set read-only, and unique by a rule of their classification. *For:* every Decided statement is true at once (relation not copy; text only here; persistent identity; content-addressed values; two things may hold equal words; "username change included"); copy on write is automatic (scenario 11); a span, a translation and a content revision can pin an exact value; it is the same shape the content research proposed for content (M2), so the two domains compose. *Against:* it asks the owner to ratify that a value is an addressable entity (the same price content M2 pays) and adds the noun *value* beside *text*; the interned-word rule is a convention that the classification must enforce.
- **TM5 — text identity borrowed from the referrer (Explored).** The thing's own UUID doubles as its title's text identity: "if above is true, we can assume that default text as loop edge is title for each vertex" ([modules.md → Top or thing Levels labels](../../modules.md#top-or-thing-levels-labels), a standing TODO) and [edges-loop/text](edges-loop/text/text.md) `(thing)-[:text]->(---)`. *For:* no second identifier for the most common text; the title is always found. *Against:* one text per thing (a product has a title, a description, many review bodies); contradicts "a text's identity is a `ThingUUID` from the thing domain ... unique across all domains" if the same UUID names two nodes; fits only the title. Kept as a possible *convention* for the default title inside TM4, not as the model.

#### How each model meets the scenarios (agent judgment)
Ok = handled without distortion; strain = handled with a convention or an unratified assumption; fails = needs a fabricated or bent node.

| Scenario | TM1 | TM2 | TM3 | TM4 | TM5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 bilingual title, fallback | fails | ok if variants live inside the id (contradicts text.md) | strain (no grouping node) | ok (with localization) | fails |
| 2 equally authentic versions | fails | strain (cannot say authentic or divergent) | ok (separate values, equivalence relation) | ok | fails |
| 3 Persian forms, usernames | fails | strain (canonical form unstated) | ok if address uses a canonical form | ok (address plus folding in search) | strain |
| 4 unsegmented and mixed scripts | fails | ok | ok | ok | strain |
| 5 username changes hands | strain | ok | ok | ok | strain |
| 6 homonym, bare term | fails | strain | ok (interned token plus sense edges) | ok (interned slot plus senses) | fails |
| 7 channels, forbidden characters | strain | ok | ok | ok | strain |
| 8 scripture, variants, spans | fails | strain (in-place versions invite change) | ok | ok | fails |
| 9 transcript, subtitles, alt | fails | ok | ok | ok | fails |
| 10 template and plurals | strain | strain | strain | strain (needs the template capability) | fails |
| 11 form text reused by many | strain (copies) | fails (shared slot edit changes all) | ok (copy on write) | ok | fails |
| 12 erasure | strain | ok | strain (a shared value cannot be erased for one) | ok (with the shared-value rule) | strain |
| 13 poem, whitespace | strain | ok | ok | ok | fails |
| 14 prompt and answer | strain | ok | ok | ok | fails |

Reading: TM1 survives nothing but the simplest case. TM2 is the most faithful to the carried code and fails exactly where sharing and exact citation are needed. TM3 is the most faithful to Memar's Lexical Token and the uri rule, and fails where a thing needs a place to own its words and an authority to edit them. TM5 serves only the title. TM4 meets every scenario that any model meets, at the price of one unratified reading (a stored value is an addressable entity, and a text has two layers); scenario 10 is met by no model, which is evidence that templating is a capability and not a shape.

#### Choice B: how languages relate (smaller, placement in the next table)
- **L1 — one text per language, linked by translation (Tentative, the Decided statement).** [text.md](text.md#model) ("the same text in two languages is two texts"), [translation](edges/translation/translation.md), [translation_suggestion](edges/translation_suggestion/translation_suggestion.md), [language.md](edges/language/language.md) ("A `thing` relate to text by e.g. `title` and this `text` relate to each `Localization` text by this label"). *For:* it is what the documents say, and it fits scenarios 2 and 8 (separate authentic versions). *Against:* a head-and-satellites reading (the title text is the head) makes the head special, which scenario 2 forbids; which language a reader gets is a walk the referrer must not know about.
- **L2 — many languages under one text identity (Explored).** The carried storage and the Go comment ("TextID MUST be unique in all languages"). *For:* Memar's i18n sentence taken literally ("multilingual support is a concern the Text abstraction alone owns", adding a language touches no referrer); one id per title. *Against:* contradicts L1's Decided sentence; cannot say that two language values are authentic or divergent (scenario 2); the sentence "unique in all languages" is itself ambiguous between "no two languages share an id" and "the id covers all languages" (contradiction 1).
- **L3 — a language-neutral anchor with language variants (Explored).** A neutral text identity (the "message") and one text per language hanging from it. *For:* answers both sides: referrers point at the anchor (L2's benefit), each language is its own text with its own history (L1's benefit), the anchor can carry the rule "all variants are authentic". *Against:* a third node kind that no document mentions; the anchor has no value of its own, which fits poorly with "a text is the value a human reads".
- **Where the linking lives (Tentative: pluggable).** In all three, whether the translation relation is part of what a text is, or an optional capability, is a separate question. By the removal test, a monolingual organization never installs it, the referrers do not change when it is installed, and the capability has its own lifecycle (suggested, reviewed, accepted, stale), its own authorization (translators, reviewers) and its own processing (fallback, machine translation, staleness): that is "independent domain semantics" and a pluggable. `text_language` stays kernel because validation, analyzers and rendering all need to know the language and it has no processing of its own. Recommended: L1 for the kernel (every text has one language) with the linking in a `localization` pluggable; L3 is the one to revisit if the owner wants a language-neutral anchor.

#### Choice C: what is inside the value
- **S1 — markup in the value (the carried text; Tentative as a description).** "Text carries style. HTML and CSS are more expressive than Markdown and are what article text should be written in" ([text.md](text.md#text-with-style)). *For:* what the carried validator and widget assume ("A text is rendered into a page"); tokens already weight a token "in a larger or bolder font" higher, which is only knowable if the style is in the value. *Against:* a markup-bearing value is why the character filter exists at all, and a character blacklist is the weakest defence (scenario 7: `=` rejected, a tutorial impossible); Memar's Content says semantic information must not be embedded in the content stream ("Reference as a Relation, Not a Property", Position B on markers).
- **S2 — plain value plus annotation layers (Explored; the direction Memar's Content points).** The value holds characters; style, emphasis, quotation, language switches and references are relations on spans. *For:* "increases what another system can know about represented content without modifying the content itself"; no markup in the value means no execution surface in storage; a render step escapes by construction. *Against:* heavy, and the span address is the unsolved circular problem of Memar's Addressability; authoring tools must edit two things at once.
- **S3 — a declared format on the value (Tentative, recommended for now).** The text names its format (plain, a restricted markdown, a restricted HTML subset) and the validator checks the value against that format's grammar; the plain-character form is the canonical one for comparison and search. *For:* keeps the carried behaviour while moving the XSS defence from a character blacklist to a grammar; a tutorial text can be `plain` and displayed escaped; reachable from the existing `text_type` edge. *Against:* the format is a classification that the label vocabulary must carry; it does not end the question of whether style belongs in the value.

#### Smaller choice: the three validation errors (Tentative: one home, the validator)
The same three conditions (`text-over-flow`, `text-lack`, `text-illegal-character`, domain string `Validation`, same media types) are declared by this domain's [errors.kh](errors/errors.kh) and by Memar's [`validators/errors.kh`](../../process/validators/errors.kh), and Memar holds the function that raises two of them ([text.kh](../../process/validators/text.kh)). Three options: (a) the validators own all three and this domain declares none (it states the requirement and nothing else, which [text.md](text.md#validation) already says of length); (b) this domain owns all three and Memar's validator imports them; (c) both keep them, and this domain's are specializations that compose Memar's. A media type is an identity ([text.md → Validation](text.md#validation) and the Go `Init` strings), so two capsules with one media type are one error declared twice, which is the defect [One Authoritative Location](../../../docs/modeling.md#one-authoritative-location-per-concerns-data) names. Recommended: (a), because the error is raised where the check is, the check is generic (length, characters of a set the *caller* gives), and the one error that is text-specific, illegal character, is not a property of a text at all but of the role or the render target (scenario 7): it is a constraint from the constraining concern. This is a decision "for the memar side as much as this one" ([text.handoff.md](text.handoff.md#questions-this-domain-could-not-settle)); recorded as open question 7.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions

The domain, once decided, has to answer each of these. Questions are written to favour no answer where unsettled; a settled item says so and cites its decision.

#### Identity
- *Settled:* a text's identity is the thing domain's identity ([text.handoff.md](text.handoff.md)); the domain declares none of its own.
- Does the identity stay the same when the value changes, when a translation is added, when the language is corrected, when the text becomes read-only, when it is used by a second referrer? Which of these is a new identity?
- Is a stored value an identity of its own (addressable, referable by a translation, a span or a content revision) or only a position in a sequence? If it has one, is it derived from the characters (and which normalization precedes the derivation) or a counter (the carried storage offers a counter)?
- Is the same text in two languages one identity with two values, two identities linked, or two identities under a language-neutral anchor? ([text.md](text.md#model) says two; the carried storage says one.)
- When two things hold equal words, are they one text, two texts with equal values, or does it depend on the classification (a hashtag is one, a product title is two)?
- Is a word-level text (a token, a hashtag) a text, a different kind of node, or a text with a rule? ([tokens](edges/tokens/tokens.md): the token has a `Field_TextID`.)
- Who may mint a text: the author of the referrer, an importing process, a parser, a model?
- Does a text identity survive the removal of its last referrer, and who may reuse it?

#### Lifecycle
- What states does a text have, and which are positions in a lifecycle (draft, published, read-only, withdrawn, erased) against moderation (hidden by justice) or access (confidential)? The shared status loops cover some; none covers *read-only* as a protection set at creation or *erased*.
- Is a draft a text? If a user types for ten minutes, are there forty values or one?
- May a read-only text be unlocked, and by whom? ([uri loop-edge](../../identity/uri/edges-loop/uri/uri.md): "no one CAN edit ... User can create new one".)
- What does *delete* mean for a text: remove the referrer's relation, hide the value, erase the value? What survives in each case (the content research's tombstone question, at the value level)?
- When a text is changed, what happens to the translations made from the old value, to the spans anchored to it, to the tokens indexed from it, to the mentions inside it?
- When the last author leaves the organization, what is the text?

#### Authorization
- Who may create a text, change its value, mark it read-only, set its language, erase a value? Is it the text's creator, the referrer's owner, a role, a rule? ([text.md → Idea backlog](text.md#idea-backlog) asks the same of the index: "who enforces visibility for user-level vs global indexes".)
- Is the authority to change a text a fact about the text, or always a fact about the referrer, so that a text referred to by two referrers has two authorities? If the second, is a shared text necessarily read-only?
- Who may read a text; does the answer differ per reader and per language; is it re-checked at each query or fixed at placement? (The backlog's unresolved "user may change her last choice, e.g. make her content private/public many times in her life".)
- Who may translate, who may accept a translation, who may declare a set of language versions equally authentic?
- Who may attach an analyzer to a language, and what does that authority reach (every domain's text)?
- Across organizations: may a text in one organization be referred to, translated or cited from another, under whose authority?

#### Rules
- Which rules does the domain itself need, and which belong to the referrer's role or the consumer: a minimum and maximum length (in which unit), a set of characters, a format grammar, a required language, uniqueness of the folded value within a namespace (usernames), an edit window?
- Where each rule attaches: to the text, to the role relation (`title`, `username`), to the render target, to the channel? The carried text says "A text may declare a minimum and a maximum length" ([text.md → Validation](text.md#validation)); a text that declares its own limit cannot be valid for one role and invalid for another (scenario 7).
- What is the unit of a length: bytes, code points, user-perceived characters, words? (Memar's validator measures bytes; scenario 7.)
- What canonical form is used for equality and for the value address, and where does folding (language- and purpose-specific) begin?
- Is "Each text MUST have a specific type" ([text_type](edges/text_type/text_type.md)) a rule of the kernel, and if so what is the vocabulary?
- Is marker syntax (`@`, `#`, `/`) a rule of the text or of the parser that resolves it? The edge docs say "MUST store with the sign".

#### Events
- Which occurrences are events the text exposes as an EventTarget ([Modularity → Event as a Module Capability](../../../docs/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)): created, changed, erased, set read-only, language corrected, translated, cited, mentioned, indexed? The existing `BS_TextCitationNumber` request and response already show a service shape that needs no event type of its own.
- Who consumes them: search (re-index), localization (stale translation), reference-resolution (notify), content (revision), notification?
- Which of these already exists as a service request or response and needs no event type of its own?

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each is one paragraph, as a sketch: not a design, and none is to be built before the owner decides.

1. **A documented kernel (Tentative).** [text.md](text.md) has a four-bullet Model and no statement of what a text is for or must offer. The first missing thing is the definition paragraph and the five-element kernel with their who and trigger (finding 2). Nothing else in the list can be placed without it.
2. **Value and value address (kernel; Tentative).** An immutable value of a text: the exact characters, the language, the script, the normalization form, a length stated with its unit, an address derived from the normalized characters and the language, the requester and approver that produced it, and the value it corrects. The slot points at its current value. A translation, a span, a content revision and a court's "as published" cite a value. The identifier question is open question 1; the normalization form open question 9.
3. **Script (kernel; Tentative).** `(text)-[:text_script]->(script)`, or a part of `text_language`. The carried language table already lists scripts per language (a language may have several: Latin and Cyrillic for one, a half-space convention for another); the vocabulary is language-human's, which says it has none (its handoff).
4. **Read-only and erasure (status vocabulary; Explored).** The uri loop-edge's "readonly" is a status set at creation; erasure is an authority-gated event that keeps the fact it happened. The shared status home ([status](../status/status.md)) has neither word. Owned there, with a rule here about which statuses a text may take ([Placing a relation](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation)).
5. **localization (pluggable; Tentative).** Sketched in finding 2: the translation relation and its suggestion phase with the equivalence and derivation readings; a fallback chain per reader or per organization; the authentic-version flag as a rule of the owner; staleness when the source value changes (a translation pins the value it was made against). Machine translation is a producer, not a part. It replaces `translation` and `translation_suggestion`, and gives language.md's "Localization" a home.
6. **search (pluggable; Tentative).** The tokenizer per language chosen by configuration, the hit list, the match degrees, the query with its precision, the normal forms (case folding where the script has case, spelling variants, Persian yeh and kaf, half-space), ranking, citation and score numbers, and the per-viewer visibility check at query time that the backlog leaves unresolved. Filed in the existing empty `search-analytic`. Its own boundary question is whether the reading-time estimate and similar statistics are inside it or beside it (Explored).
7. **reference-resolution (pluggable; Tentative).** One capability for the span references: marker grammar, resolution to the target's identity at write time, a span recorded against an exact value, notification to the target where allowed, the acquired marker and the discovered resolution recorded as different facts. Replaces the duplicated hashtag and mention folders and the citation relation; gives the content research's `mentions` and `topic` their text-level source.
8. **Span address (kernel-or-shared; Explored).** `(value address, start, end)` and nothing else; a reply, a credit, a citation, a mention, a hit, an annotation can name it. Defined once ([A Relation Several Domains Need Is Defined Once](../../../docs/modeling.md#a-relation-several-domains-need-is-defined-once)). Memar's Addressability notes that the principle is circular; the cheaper answer here is that a text's natural units (word, sentence, verse) are already offsets, and the only new fact is that offsets are meaningful against one value only.
9. **annotation (pluggable; Explored).** Style, emphasis, quotation, language switch inside a text, as layers on spans. Exists only if the owner chooses S2 (open question 3); until then it is the shape the restricted-format validator approximates.
10. **template (pluggable; Explored).** A text with parameters, plural and gender selection per language, a render that is never stored. Answers the handoff's "dynamic data in a title, text substitution, text piping" with "a capability, not a field of `Text`"; to be designed only after the localization pluggable exists because the selection rules are the translator's.
11. **derivation (pluggable; Explored).** The link from a text to what produced it (an object, another text, a model and its version), with a confidence and a kind that keeps derived text from reading as authored; a human correction is a new text citing the derived one. Overlaps content's `channel-import` (imported email is acquired raw plus discovered parts) and content's machine-written summary; open question 14.
12. **Interned-text rule (a rule family; Tentative).** For a classification (URI, hashtag, mention marker, token, command), "exactly one text for a given value and classification, read-only". A get-or-create service per classification; the uniqueness is the classification's rule, not the text kernel's, so that ordinary titles remain free to be equal. The uri loop-edge states it for one classification; the hashtag and mention docs state it by "MUST store with the sign".
13. **Term hub (Explored).** `(term text)-[:described_by]->(content)` for the Decided "a term opens a hub that describes it", with senses for a bare token left unresolved as Memar's Lexical Token prescribes. Homonym and polysemy (scenario 6) are the test. Spans two domains; the owner of the relation is the content side (it is content that describes), with text supplying the interned term.

### 7. Test scenarios

Given/when/then in prose, so another session can write tests once the owner decides. Each is marked with what it depends on: **kept** if it follows from settled text, **TM4** if it assumes the recommended model, **Q n** if it assumes the default of open question n.

1. **Words live only in text (kept).** Given a product with a title. When the title is stored. Then the product holds a relation to a text and no string of its own; no field named for the title exists on the product.
2. **Equal words are two texts (kept: Locale text).** Given two products titled "Large". When both are stored. Then there are two texts with equal values (Q 10), each referred to by one product; editing one does not change the other.
3. **A text has a thing identity (kept).** Given a new text. When it is created. Then its identity is a `ThingUUID`, no `TextUUID` is declared, and the same identity is not reused for a different text.
4. **An edit keeps the identity and the first wording (TM4, Q 1).** Given a text with one value. When its referrer's owner changes it. Then the identity is unchanged, there are two values with a requester on each, the first is reachable by its own address, and the default read is the current value.
5. **A correction cites what it corrects (kept: state change as events).** Given a wrong value. When corrected. Then a new value is recorded that names the value it corrects; nothing is rewritten.
6. **Equal values are stored once (TM4, Q 1).** Given two texts whose values have equal characters and language. When both are stored. Then they share one stored value by address and remain two identities.
7. **The edit authority is the referrer's (Q 4).** Given a text referred to by a product. When a user who may not edit the product tries to change the text. Then it is refused for the product's rule, and the text kernel holds no list of who may edit.
8. **A shared text is read-only (Q 4).** Given one text referred to by two referrers. When either tries to change it. Then it is refused and a new text can be made for that referrer; the other referrer sees no change.
9. **Copy on write for a template (TM4, Q 4).** Given a thousand tasks made from one complaint text. When one owner edits the clause. Then that task has a new value and the other nine hundred ninety-nine still show the original, with no copy of the original stored.
10. **A read-only text refuses a change (kept: uri loop-edge).** Given a URI text. When anyone tries to change its string. Then it is refused; creating a new URI text is allowed; there is exactly one text for a given URI string (Q 10).
11. **Language is a relation (kept).** Given a text in Persian. When asked its language. Then the answer is the `text_language` relation, and a text written without a language is `undetermined` and never refused for it unless a rule requires one.
12. **Two languages are two texts under L1 (Q 2).** Given a title in English and its Persian form. When both are stored. Then there are two texts linked by translation, and the product still holds one relation.
13. **A reader gets a fallback (Q 2).** Given a title in English and Persian and a reader whose list is Italian, then Persian. When the title is requested. Then the Persian text is returned, and no error is raised for the missing Italian.
14. **Authentic versions have no head (Q 2).** Given three language versions declared equally authentic. When the English one is withdrawn. Then the others remain authentic and none becomes "the original".
15. **A translation goes stale (Q 2).** Given a Persian text translated from an English value. When the English text gets a new value. Then the translation is marked stale against the earlier value and keeps pointing at it.
16. **Equality uses a canonical form, storage keeps the characters (Q 9).** Given two usernames typed with Arabic yeh and Persian yeh. When the system compares them for uniqueness. Then they are treated as equal by folding, while each stored value keeps exactly the characters typed.
17. **A poem is not reformatted (kept in spirit; Q 9).** Given a text whose line breaks are significant. When stored and read back. Then the characters are identical; normalization for comparison changed nothing in the stored value.
18. **A length names its unit (Q 8).** Given a role with a maximum of 50. When a Persian text of 40 letters (80 bytes) is validated under "user-perceived characters". Then it passes; under "bytes" it fails; the requirement states which.
19. **The requirement comes from the role (Q 7).** Given a username role with a set of allowed characters and a tutorial body with none. When `<script>` is stored in each. Then the username is refused with a recorded reason and the tutorial body is stored and rendered escaped.
20. **A text containing `=` is valid unless a role says otherwise (Q 7).** Given the text "2+2=4". When validated for a comment body. Then it is accepted (the carried validator rejects it; this test defines the target behaviour).
21. **The validation errors are declared once (Q 7).** Given the repository after the decision. When the three error identities are listed. Then each media type appears in exactly one declaration.
22. **A mention is resolved to an identity (Q 6).** Given a post that contains `@omid`. When it is saved. Then a mention relation to the user exists, recorded against the exact value and span; after the user renames, the relation still points at the same user; the string is unchanged.
23. **A rename does not move old mentions (Q 6).** Given a mention of the user who held "omid". When another user later takes "omid". Then the old mention still resolves to the first user.
24. **A hashtag keeps its sign (kept: hashtag rule; Q 10).** Given the posts "Victory" and "#Victory". When indexed. Then they refer to two different interned texts; the second is classified as a hashtag.
25. **Tokenization is chosen by language (Q 5).** Given the same sentence in Japanese and in English. When analyzed. Then different analyzers are used, each recorded, and re-analysis with a new analyzer leaves the text unchanged.
26. **Search results are recomputable (Q 5).** Given a stored hit list. When the pluggable is removed. Then every text still reads and validates unchanged.
27. **Citation counts are discovered (Q 5, Q 6).** Given text A cited from three texts. When the count is asked. Then the answer is derived from the citation relations; no stored count exists to disagree.
28. **A derived text carries its method (Q 14).** Given a voice note and a machine transcript. When the transcript is stored. Then it is a text with a derived-from relation to the note, a method naming a model and version, and a confidence; a human correction is a second text citing it.
29. **Erasure keeps the fact (Q 15).** Given a text with three values. When the author's erasure request is accepted. Then the values are no longer readable by anyone not authorized, an event records that and when the erasure happened, and the referrer's thread keeps its place.
30. **A shared interned word is not erased for one (Q 10, Q 15).** Given a hashtag text used by many referrers. When one referrer's author requests erasure. Then that author's relation and spans are removed and the interned text remains.
31. **A sensitive text is a label (Q 17).** Given a text marked with the registered word Sensitivity. When a reader's view is built. Then a rule may hide or warn; the text's value and identity are unchanged.
32. **A template is rendered, not stored (Q 16).** Given a text with a plural parameter. When rendered for a Russian and an Arabic reader at count 3. Then each gets the form their language selects, and no rendered text is stored as a text.

### Contradictions found

Reported, not resolved, as asked. Each cites both sides.

1. **Is a text one language or many?** [text.md → Model](text.md#model): "A text's identity is unique across all domains, and therefore unique across all languages: the same text in two languages is two texts". The carried storage, [widgets/text/protocol/storage.kh](ui/text/protocol/storage.kh), keys a record by `(textID, language)`, counts versions per language, and lists `ListLanguages(textID, ...)`: one id, many languages. The Go comment on the field, preserved in [protocol/dt-uuid.kh](protocol/dt-uuid.kh), says "TextID MUST be unique in all languages" and "Wil be unique in all human languages", which can be read either way. [language.md](edges/language/language.md) and [translation.md](edges/translation/translation.md) say a thing's title text "relate[s] to each `Localization` text", a head with satellites. The "therefore" in text.md does not follow from "unique across all domains": uniqueness across domains does not say whether one identity may hold values in several languages.
2. **Content-derived or persistent or borrowed identity.** [text.handoff.md](text.handoff.md): "content-derived or immutable-at-creation stable reference" (Decided); the conversion decision: a `ThingUUID`, an opaque identifier whose behaviour the thing domain leaves unstated; [text.md → Locale text](text.md#locale-text): "What is unique is the text's identity, not its words"; Memar's Content: a string is "a single, globally unique Lexical Token"; [modules.md](../../modules.md#top-or-thing-levels-labels) (a standing TODO): "`product` UUID is as `text` UUID for a product ... default text as loop edge is title for each vertex". These four cannot all be the identity of the same node; finding 3 proposes the layering. The content research's contradiction 3 is the same defect from the other side.
3. **Equal words allowed, equal words required.** [text.md → Locale text](text.md#locale-text): two things may hold equal text. [uri loop-edge](../../identity/uri/edges-loop/uri/uri.md): "Just one text with desire string MUST exist with `URI` edge". [edges-loop/hashtag](edges-loop/hashtag/hashtag.md) and [mention](edges-loop/mention/mention.md): "It MUST store with # sign to distinguish between `Victory` and `#Victory`". [tokens](edges/tokens/tokens.md): a token is "stored in one place, pointing at every text it was found in". Each rule is local to a classification; no document says that this is the difference.
4. **Text with markup, and text that carries no semantics in the stream.** [text.md → Text with style](text.md#text-with-style): HTML and CSS "are what article text should be written in". Memar's Content: semantic information "increases what another system can know about represented content without modifying the content itself", and the Position-B discussion treats a markup marker in a stream as implicit semantic tagging. The XSS validation in [text.md](text.md#validation) exists only because of the first.
5. **A text "for a human in a locale", and a URI that is a text.** [text.md → Model](text.md#model): the text is "text *for a human in a locale* ... not a byte sequence". [uri.md](../../identity/uri/uri.md): "A URI is a thing whose text is the URI string, so a URI record is a thing and is a text"; [uri loop-edge](../../identity/uri/edges-loop/uri/uri.md): "Locale name in the Computer world". A machine locator is a text by composition and not by the Model's own definition.
6. **Hashtag and mention in two buckets with conflicting Models.** [edges/hashtag](edges/hashtag/hashtag.md) `(text)-[:has_hashtag]->((hashtag)text)` with an inverse `hashtag_in`; [edges-loop/hashtag](edges-loop/hashtag/hashtag.md) `(text)-[:hashtag]->(---)`. [edges/mention](edges/mention/mention.md) `(text)-[:mention]->(user)` and `(user)-[:mention_in]->(text)`; [edges-loop/mention](edges-loop/mention/mention.md) `(text)-[:mention]->(---)`. The survey names this defect 4 and asks Q3; this research adds that all four are one reference relation from a span (scenario 5, 6).
7. **Candidate vocabularies pointing at a review that is not there.** [edges-loop/types](edges-loop/types/types.md) says it "answers the standing review in text.md ("is Localization a text type or a query-time behavior?") as open"; [text.md](text.md)'s Idea backlog holds two items about index authorization and no such question. [edges-loop/text](edges-loop/text/text.md) says its home is "beside the source `edges-loop/text.go` (no such file yet)", a dangling remark. The doc [text_type](edges/text_type/text_type.md) says "Each text MUST have a specific type" while [edges-loop/text](edges-loop/text/text.md) lists the same Poem, Scientific, Skill as "candidate (unconfirmed)" kinds and asks whether they are values of text_type.
8. **A loop whose policy relates two texts.** [lowercase](edges-loop/lowercase/lowercase.md) is `(text)-[:lowercase]->(---)` while its policy says `CAT`, `Cat`, `CaT` "all relate to `cat`"; [typo](edges-loop/typo/typo.md) is a loop while "one of usage is to fixed the typo". By [Edge buckets](../../modules.md#edge-buckets) a loop-edge classifies one node.
9. **"Human language" and a language enum that includes machine, mathematics, musical and sign.** [language-human.md](../language-human/language-human.md): "the language people speak and read, as opposed to the languages computers speak". The carried [dt-language.kh](edges/language/labels/dt-language.kh): `LanguageMachine, LanguageMathematics, LanguageMusical, LanguageSign` beside Persian, English, Russian, Arabic. The handoff recommends deleting the file; [language-human.handoff.md](../language-human/language-human.handoff.md) warns that deletion removes "the only statement of which languages the system knows".
10. **The handoff's waiting list.** [text.handoff.md → Waiting on a name another domain owns](text.handoff.md#waiting-on-a-name-another-domain-owns) lists `Field_LanguageUUID` as not declared; [language-human.handoff.md](../language-human/language-human.handoff.md) records it as declared and Decided, as the survey also notes ("text.handoff lists 5 blockers that now exist"). Only this one was verified in this session; the others are not claimed.
11. **The three validation errors exist twice, and one condition is not a text property.** [errors/errors.kh](errors/errors.kh) against Memar's [`validators/errors.kh`](../../process/validators/errors.kh): the same media types, the same domain string, one implementation of the check in Memar ([text.kh](../../process/validators/text.kh), which measures `len(t)` bytes) and another in this domain ([validator.kh](ui/text-editor/validator.kh), the same plus rejecting `=`). [text.md](text.md#validation) says "the text domain states the requirement, not the check" but also "A text may declare a minimum and a maximum length" and "may forbid characters".
12. **Human-facing strings: the text domain or the Type.** [Khayyam practice rule 5](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md): explanatory text "must be returnable and localizable (text domain) or live in documents". [Type → Human-facing identity](../../../docs/type.md): a Type's localized names and descriptions of its failures "belong to the Type itself", external lookup tables are "rejected", and a translator's wording change is accepted as "a change to the Type's definition". The unconverted `errors/locale.*.kh` of this domain and of `language-human` sit exactly on this line.
13. **Memar says Text owns search; the repository says analytics is never kernel.** [Attribute-or-Edge Test](../../../docs/modeling.md#the-attribute-or-edge-test): the abstraction "becomes the natural place to build indexing and search capability once"; [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement): analytics is never kernel, a query with its own storage and processing is a pluggable. Reconciled in finding 2 by attachment, not resolved.
14. **A count in a folder named for a relation.** [citations_number](edges/citations_number/citations_number.md): the Model is two inverse citation relations; the Policy and the service are about the count; and its first open question asks whether the number is read from the edge or from a token. Folder name, Model and service answer three different things.
15. **The widget and the domain disagree about sensitivity.** [text.handoff.md](text.handoff.md#questions-this-domain-could-not-settle): "nothing in this domain's documents says what a sensitive text is". [thing-labels](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/thing-labels.md) registers Sensitivity as "a label a person may attach to any node". The two documents do not refer to each other.

## Recommendation (unratified)
The owner has not reviewed this, and nothing in it is decided.

1. **Define a text as a slot with immutable, content-addressed values (Tentative, medium confidence).** This keeps every Decided statement true: relation not copy, text only here, persistent `ThingUUID`, content-addressability at the value, equal words allowed, a shared change mechanism. It changes the empty first paragraph of [text.md](text.md) and nothing else visible, and it is the same shape the content research proposed for content, so the two domains compose by pinning.
2. **Keep a five-element kernel (Tentative):** identity, value, change (with read-only and erasure), language, referenceability. Everything else is a pluggable or an existing domain.
3. **One language per text; the link between languages is the `localization` pluggable (Tentative).** `text_language` stays kernel. L3 (a neutral anchor) is the alternative to revisit, and the content research's default on one text per language must be answered together with this.
4. **Move analytics out and fold the span references together (Tentative).** `tokens`, `match-*`, `query`, `lowercase`, `typo`, `duration` and the citation count go to `search-analytic`; `hashtag`, `mention`, `command`, `anchor_text` and the citation relation become one `reference-resolution` pluggable on text, and content's `mentions` and `topic` become views over it.
5. **Make constraints come from the role, the format and the channel, and give the three errors one home (Tentative).** The validators own the three errors; the text states the requirement; the length unit and the canonical form are declared.
6. **Choose a declared format (S3) for now; keep plain-value-plus-annotation (S2) as the direction (Tentative).**
7. **Treat sensitivity as a label and the widget's hide/show as one reader's state (Tentative).**
8. **Do not move, delete or convert anything on this evidence.** The mapping in finding 5 is a reading list for the sessions that will, after the owner answers. Among the survey's tasks, T4 ("convert the unblocked text `.kh`") should wait for open questions 1, 2 and 5, because the conversions would fix the shape of `tokens`, the storage service and the language data model in Khayyam before the model is chosen ([Khayyam practice → rule 7](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md)).
9. **Ask the three questions that change what the work is first:** question 1 (what a text node is), question 2 (one language or many, and who links languages), and question 3 (plain value with annotations or markup in the value).

## 8. Open Questions
This is the research's eighth section. Only the owner can decide these. Ordered by what they change: those that change what the domain is first, then those that change how a part is done ([Ask rather than assume](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. They are also entered in the [handoff](text.handoff.md#research-001-questions-and-next-steps-2026-10-15).

1. **What is a text node: a slot with persistent identity and immutable content-addressed values (TM4), an in-place versioned slot (TM2), or an immutable interned value (TM3)?** Default: TM4; the identity is the `ThingUUID`, the value address is derived from the normalized characters and the language. Blocks: the definition in [text.md](text.md), any requirement on `TextModel` beyond a string, the shape of the storage service, the content research's open question 1.
2. **Is a text one language, with languages linked by a pluggable (L1), several languages under one identity (L2), or a neutral anchor with language variants (L3)?** Default: L1 with the link in `localization`. Blocks: the translation and translation_suggestion folders, `storage.kh` (its `ListLanguages`), the content research's open question 6, scenarios 1 and 2.
3. **Is the style, structure or reference in a text part of its value, or an annotation on a plain value?** Default: a declared format (S3) for now, plain-plus-annotation (S2) as the direction. Blocks: [Text with style](text.md#text-with-style), the character validation, token weighting by font, the `annotation` pluggable.
4. **Who may change a text: the text's creator, or always the referrer's authority; and is a text referred to by two referrers necessarily read-only?** Default: the referrer's authority; a shared text is read-only; copy on write. Blocks: template instantiation ([thing template](../../identity/thing/edges/template/template.md)), username change, the sharing rule, scenario 11.
5. **Do `tokens`, `match-*`, `query`, `lowercase`, `typo`, the citation count and `duration` leave for `search-analytic`; does `tokens_by` leave with them?** Default: yes to all; the choice of analyzer per language stays configuration of the pluggable. Blocks: seven folders, survey task T4's token conversions, the empty `search-analytic`, the idea backlog's index authorization.
6. **Are `mention`, `hashtag`, `command`, `anchor_text` and the citation relation one capability on text, with content's `mentions` and `topic` as discovered unions?** Default: yes. Blocks: the duplicated hashtag and mention folders, survey Q3, the content research's Next Step 5 and two of its Tentative pluggables.
7. **Where do the length, character and format requirements come from, and who declares the three validation errors?** Default: the role, the format and the channel state them; the validators declare all three; this domain declares none. Blocks: [errors/errors.kh](errors/errors.kh), the Validation section, `widgets/text-editor/validator.kh` (the `=` rule), the memar-side decision the handoff names.
8. **What is the unit of a length?** Default: declared per requirement, with user-perceived characters for human limits and bytes for storage and channel limits. Blocks: `ValidateTextLength` in Memar, SMS and notification limits, scenario 7.
9. **What is the canonical form for equality and for the value address, and where does language-specific folding begin?** Default: one canonical form at identity (stored characters untouched), folding in `search` and in the rule that needs it. Blocks: the value address, username uniqueness, Persian matching, scenario 3 and 13.
10. **Is the interned-text pattern (one read-only text per value and classification) a rule family of the classifications (URI, hashtag, token, command), or a kernel kind?** Default: a rule family; ordinary texts stay free to be equal. Blocks: edges-loop docs for hashtag, mention, command, term; the `Field_TextID` on `Token`.
11. **Where is the span address defined once: in the text domain, in a shared annotation module, or as a general locator across text, image and recording?** Default: in the text domain, for text only, now; generalize when object needs it. Blocks: the content research's anchor, tokens' offsets, citations, mentions.
12. **Is "Each text MUST have a specific type" a rule, and are the edges-loop folders values of one vocabulary or separate folders?** Default: type optional until a vocabulary exists; values of the label domain's `text-type` until a type earns its own rules; promote `uri`, `hashtag`, `mention` (they have rules). Blocks: [text_type](edges/text_type/text_type.md), the fifteen loop folders, survey Q3.
13. **Are `lowercase` and `typo` (and other normal forms) relations between two texts, held as discovered data?** Default: yes, in `search`. Blocks: the two folders; scenario 3.
14. **Is a text derived from an object or a model (transcript, subtitles, alt text, OCR, generated summary, AI answer) a text, and how is it kept from reading as authored?** Default: a text with a derived-from relation, a method and a confidence, and a kind marker. Blocks: the `ai/text` mapping, the content research's open question 16, accessibility text, scenario 9 and 14.
15. **What does erasure do to a stored value, and may an interned word be erased for one requester?** Default: erasure is an authority-gated event that removes the value's readability and keeps the fact; an interned word is never erased for one requester; a duty to erase is a rule above "nothing lost". Blocks: the change mechanism, the status vocabulary (read-only, erased), the content research's open question 7.
16. **Is dynamic data in a text a capability (`template`) and when?** Default: a capability, designed after `localization` because plural and gender selection is per language. Blocks: the handoff's open idea.
17. **Is sensitivity only the registered label plus a rule, with hide and show as one reader's state?** Default: yes. Blocks: the widget's three services, the handoff question.
18. **Is the text domain the home of design-time human-facing strings (a Type's localized names and failure descriptions), or only of run-time text of the organization's data?** Default: the Type keeps its own, as Memar's Type document says, and relates to Text only where the string is organizational data. Blocks: both `errors/locale.*.kh` pairs, Khayyam rule 5's "returnable and localizable (text domain)".
19. **Is a formal notation (code, mathematics, music, chess) a language of a text, or a format of its value; and is sign language a language of a text at all?** Default: formats are classifications of the value, not language-human members; sign language is a human language whose written form (if any) is a script. Blocks: deleting [edges/language/labels](edges/language/labels/), the language-human vocabulary, S3.
