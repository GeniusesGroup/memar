## Methodology
**Sources consulted.** The post itself, as transcribed by the owner — a public post whose own claims are about its author's own practice and preference, admitted as the claim being weighed and not as support for any conclusion. The standard's maintainers: the [ASD-STE100](https://www.asd-ste100.org/) home page, for the standard's purpose, ownership and current issue, and the maintainers' own [Frequently Asked Questions](https://www.asd-ste100.org/faq.html), written by the Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group, for what the standard is and what its own authors say about writing it, checking it, and where its reach ends. The general encyclopedia article on [Simplified Technical English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Technical_English), for the standard's history, its present scale, and the failure recorded against its use outside the industries that wrote it. This project's governing documents, consulted for what they already own.

**Procedure.** Each proposal was put to the same three questions in the same order: does a governing document already state this; does a governing document contradict it; is the record silent, and if so is the silence neutrality or an omission. A proposal a governing document already owns produced no change. A proposal a governing document contradicts was not adopted, and the contradiction was recorded with the document that draws it rather than with a new rule restating it. Only a silence that turned out to be an omission produced an edit.

The diagram proposal was examined twice rather than once, from the reader's side and then from the reviewer's side, because a notation can pass the first test and fail the second: whether a reader can recover the structure at all is a different question from whether a reader can notice an error in it. The first examination was already recorded in this repository; the second is what this research added.

Every external claim was then traced to the maintainers' own statements where the maintainers' statements were reachable, and a claim that could not be traced to them was removed from the governing documents rather than left standing on a secondary source. Two findings changed as a result: one was replaced, and one was withdrawn against its own premise.

**Limits and blind spots.**
- The post's own host was not reachable from the environment this inquiry ran in, so the post was weighed as transcribed rather than as retrieved. Its central empirical claims — that visual material is processed faster, that controlled language tires the reader less — carry no measurement in the post and are treated here as qualitative statements, not as results.
- The specification document itself was not retrieved, so the questions its maintainers answer on their site are answered at one remove from the specification's own text. The maintainers' answers were preferred over the secondary account wherever the two could be compared, and where they could not be compared the claim was not used.
- No checker was examined by running it, and no reader study or corpus was consulted. Nothing here measures whether a restricted vocabulary would improve comprehension for this project's readers; the negative finding is an argument about what this project has already decided, not a measurement about readers.
- One inconsistency inside the maintainers' own material was observed and left unresolved: the FAQ page still describes Issue 8 as current and Issue 9 as scheduled, while the home page reports Issue 9 as published on 15 January 2025. The later page is treated as the current one, but the discrepancy is a reminder that a maintainers' site is a convenience and not the standard.

## Findings

### What ASD-STE100 is, and what it establishes
- **Its origin is a comprehension problem, not a style problem.** The project began in 1979, when European airlines were translating maintenance documentation for local mechanics and approaching AECMA, now ASD; AECMA set up a project group in 1983 under Fokker's leadership, and the result was the AECMA Simplified English Guide, renamed ASD Simplified Technical English when AECMA merged in 2004. The maintainers state the purpose plainly: STE addresses difficulties in English comprehension related to complex sentence structures, confusing word forms, and ambiguous vocabulary ([ASD-STE100 FAQ](https://www.asd-ste100.org/faq.html) — `Evidence`).
- **Its shape is a register, not a principle.** Two parts, writing rules and a controlled dictionary; Issue 9 carries fifty-three rules and roughly nine hundred approved words, with a general dictionary in which each word has one approved meaning and one part of speech, synonyms admitted one to the exclusion of the others (ibid.; scale from [Simplified Technical English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Technical_English) — `Evidence`).
- **The dictionary is not closed, and the exception is the domain's own vocabulary.** Technical names and technical verbs are not listed in the dictionary; where an unapproved word is used to complete one, it becomes part of that name or verb and is acceptable, and the maintainers call this the only way to use unapproved words while advising it be limited as far as possible (FAQ — `Evidence`). The instrument therefore reserves its own hardest case for exemption: a project's own terms are exactly what the restriction cannot govern.
- **The maintainers draw the line between the language and its principles themselves.** Asked who needs to write in STE, they answer that it is not a language for general writing and that its basic principles — short sentences, one topic per sentence, active voice — can easily be adopted instead (FAQ — `Evidence`). The distinction this research reached independently is the standard's own.
- **The maintainers deny it eases the writer's work.** Asked whether STE is simple to write, they answer that it was created for the maximum benefit of the reader and that this does not necessarily make it simple to write, then list what writing it correctly requires: technical knowledge of the subject, command of the language, understanding of the rules and dictionary, the ability to tell which words qualify as technical names, and the ability to structure thought and understand the reader's needs (FAQ — `Evidence`).
- **The maintainers deny a checker can stand in for the specification.** Asked whether a checker or software product is necessary, they answer no, state that such products cannot replace the specification, and state that ASD and the STEMG do not endorse or certify any vendor's claim of full compliance (FAQ — `Evidence`). Asked whether correctly used STE changes the meaning of a technical text, they answer that it does not *because* it requires a high standard of professionalism, linguistic and technical, on the writer's part (ibid.).
- **The failure recorded against its use outside the industries that wrote it.** Most organizations outside aerospace and defence do not use STE, in part because it no longer serves its original purpose; within aerospace and defence it is often misapplied as a substitute for a grammar-based style guide, with the result that writers standardize grammatically incorrect applications of approved words to keep a document consistent; and the available checkers are not proof of anything, since a checker can accept text that obeys the rules while breaking ordinary English grammar, and an author who trusts the checker's silence writes badly ([Simplified Technical English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Technical_English), citing Knezevic and Bergstrom — `Evidence`).
- **This project reached the same prohibition from the other end.** A checker that narrows the space of what has not been examined without examining it is what Test → What a Passing Suite Establishes (`modules/process/test/test.md#what-a-passing-suite-establishes`) exists to rule out, and separating the tool that runs a check from the claim it checks is [Rule → Normative Claim and Executable Form](../process/rule/rule.md#normative-claim-and-executable-form). An industry reached the constraint under a cost this project has only chosen by argument; that convergence is the finding with the widest reach, and it is the reason the obligation was adopted while the instrument was not.

### A correction this research's own first pass required
- **The originating reader does not exhaust the claim.** A first pass concluded that the vocabulary restriction answers a non-native reader's problem and that the problem therefore does not obtain here. The maintainers answer that question directly and deny the narrow reading: STE was created to help non-native speakers of English, and it *also* improves communication among mother-tongue speakers (FAQ — `Evidence`). The narrow conclusion was therefore withdrawn. What survives is narrower and rests on the instrument rather than on the reader: this project's readers are competent in the record's language, the maintainers locate the standard's benefit to them in comprehension rather than in a defect of their English, and the project's own readers would be asked to accept a professional judgment load the maintainers describe as demanding rather than as lightening.
- **This is recorded rather than quietly repaired** because it is the ordinary shape of an inquiry: a claim that was load-bearing, and was written into a governing document, turned out to rest on a premise its source does not hold. The paragraph in [Knowledge → Write for the Structure of the Subject](./knowledge.md#write-for-the-structure-of-the-subject-not-for-todays-reader-limits) was rewritten to rest on the maintainers' own statements.

### Whether this project's record should adopt a controlled vocabulary
- **No, and the record had already decided it.** [Terminology → Terminology Authority and Governance](../../docs/terminology.md#terminology-authority-and-governance) confines this project's governance to the terms it has taken responsibility for defining and explicitly disclaims ordinary language, and [The Default Meaning of an Unreferenced Term](../../docs/terminology.md#the-default-meaning-of-an-unreferenced-term) gives every other word its ordinary sense. A dictionary-bound register contradicts both, and correcting a word choice while agreeing on the meaning is already listed among the objections that are not critique ([cognition.practice.md](../../docs/cognition.practice.md)).
- **The standard's own escape hatch is the clearest argument against it here.** The hardest vocabulary to constrain is a domain's own terms, and the maintainers exempt exactly that case. A register here would either exempt this project's defined terms, leaving nothing load-bearing under the restriction, or constrain them, contradicting [Terminology → Terminology Authority and Governance](../../docs/terminology.md#terminology-authority-and-governance).
- **A restricted register would also freeze what this project holds open.** Several concepts are recorded as recognized but not yet named, the missing name being itself the open question — the arrangement of a system's parts is excluded from [System → Structure](../../docs/system.md#structure) on the ground that the concept is real and its name is not settled. A register admitting one approved word per concept has no way to hold a concept whose word does not exist yet, and no way to record that its absence is deliberate.
- **What graduates instead.** [Knowledge → Write for the Structure of the Subject](./knowledge.md#write-for-the-structure-of-the-subject-not-for-todays-reader-limits) now states that what a document owes its reader is scaled by what that reader's misreading costs, that where the subject cannot absorb an error what is owed is performability, and that the diagnosis travels while the instrument does not: a reader who misresolves a word is a defect with an owner, answered by defining the term and stating where the definition diverges from ordinary usage ([Terminology → Correcting a Term Used Against Its Definition](../../docs/terminology.md#correcting-a-term-used-against-its-definition)) and not by narrowing the vocabulary a reader may use.

### Whether a governing document here may carry meaning in a diagram
- **No, and three documents already said so.** [Documentation → Written surface](documentation/documentation.md#written-surface) forbids carrying normative logic in blocks that only make sense when rendered as two-dimensional graphics, [Knowledge → Write for the Structure of the Subject](./knowledge.md#write-for-the-structure-of-the-subject-not-for-todays-reader-limits) names decorative two-dimensional layouts a workaround for a temporary reader failure, and [Content → Concrete Evidence: Tree/Graph Notation in This Very Document](content/content.md#concrete-evidence-treegraph-notation-in-this-very-document) records that such notation cannot be recovered by a screen reader, a Braille display, or a token stream. No new rule was added, because restating a rule is the redundant restatement [Documentation → Content Rule](documentation/documentation.md#content-rule-no-fabricated-or-redundant-provenance) forbids.
- **What none of the three stated is the reason.** Readability and checkability come apart. A sentence that misstates a relationship is checked by reading it against what its writer meant; a diagram that misplaces an edge is checked by looking at the diagram, and the eye supplies the very structure whose correctness is in question. The asymmetry is not a property of one consumer: a sighted reader, a screen reader, and a language model each fail to notice a wrong edge the same way, because each trusts the rendering it can see.
- **A generated diagram carries the asymmetry twice.** It holds the generator's reading of the subject and the generator's rendering of that reading in one artifact, and gives its reader no cue about which of the two to check. What makes a diagram worth producing — a shape grasped faster than a sentence is read — is therefore the same property that makes it unsafe to govern by.
- **Where a diagram does belong was already stated.** The canonical Graph is reached by many independent Renderers, each deriving its own representation without the others' knowledge ([Content → Authoring Syntax vs. Semantic Graph](content/content.md#authoring-syntax-vs-semantic-graph)). A diagram is one such rendering. The distinction this project draws is therefore not diagrams versus prose but carrier versus rendering.
- **A rendering names the reader it was produced for, and that is what makes it well-formed.** Documentation is classified by the reader's relationship to its content ([Documentation → Facet](documentation/documentation.md#facet)), and a rendering admits the same classification: an artifact produced so that a person can see at a glance whether a plan took the right path belongs to a human-oversight class and is well-formed there. It becomes malformed at the moment a later reader is expected to recover the model from it, which is the same promotion stated above seen from the receiving side. This is what settles the case for such renderers: not that they are second-best prose, and not that ninety percent usefulness excuses them, but that they are an artifact class of their own, addressed to a reader that recovers structure from an image, and the only thing required of them is that they stay in that class.

### Whether this project should generate HTML pages or video explainers as documentation
- **No change to the record, on two independent grounds.** Neither an interactive page nor a video is section-addressable, diffable, or citable, and neither can be read by a model consuming a token stream, so each is a second copy of the model rather than a view of it — the dual-source problem [Content → Type Reference](content/content.md#type-reference--where-semantic-meets-a-type-system) names for inline structured data. And generative work belongs on the side of the division this project already draws, where a service performs it and prose holds the lasting contract ([Knowledge → Knowledge and Code](./knowledge.md#knowledge-and-code)).
- **Generated video has one legitimate place here, and it is not the record.** A video or simulation produced during a session to think with, and discarded afterward, occupies the status [Modeling → Graphs Are Not Documentation Artifacts](../../docs/modeling.md#graphs-are-not-documentation-artifacts) already gives a graph: a working tool, not a deliverable.
- **Nothing here decides whether this project wants a Renderer.** That question was already open in Content and this occasion did not settle it; it does not need to, and a research that closed it would be deciding a question its own scope excludes.

### Whether a checking tool over this project's prose would be worth having
- **It would be governance-tier, by this project's own test.** [Rule → What a Rule Is](../process/rule/rule.md#what-a-rule-is) draws the line between the two tiers with a single question: whether turning the check off would admit something the owning specification says cannot exist. Turning off a prose-usage check leaves every document meaning what it meant; only how well it is kept changes — which is the definition of a governance rule there, enforced by a linter rather than by a compiler gate.
- **The tool would own the check and never the claim**, which is the shape [Rule → Normative Claim and Executable Form](../process/rule/rule.md#normative-claim-and-executable-form) fixes, and it is the shape the standard's maintainers independently insist on when they refuse to certify any vendor's product as compliant and state that a checker cannot replace the specification.
- **The evidence says the checker is the part that fails.** A checker that accepts text obeying the rules while breaking ordinary grammar, in a register whose own authors report misuse producing ungrammatical output, is a check whose silence is not evidence. A governance check of that kind would report on the record without being able to establish anything about it, which is the shape of rule Test → What a Passing Suite Establishes (`modules/process/test/test.md#what-a-passing-suite-establishes`) exists to refuse.
- **There is a check worth having, and it is not this one.** What this project's record actually needs checked is that a term used against its definition is corrected, and that a load-bearing term is verified against the document that owns it — both already stated as obligations ([Terminology → Correcting a Term Used Against Its Definition](../../docs/terminology.md#correcting-a-term-used-against-its-definition), [Cognition → Definitions outrank terminology](../../docs/cognition.md#definitions-outrank-terminology)) and neither is a matter of word choice. A tool that reported *those* would be reporting a rule this project has actually stated.

### Whether the shift toward oversight requires a change
- **No. The substance is already held in three places.** The expectation is written before the enactment and inherits its authority from the document above it, so a check confined to an implementation cannot tell a wrong implementation from a wrong specification (Test → The Expectation Is Written Before the Work (`modules/process/test/test.md#the-expectation-is-written-before-the-work`)). A passing run establishes one thing and names the three substitutions that would make it carry more (Test → What a Passing Suite Establishes (`modules/process/test/test.md#what-a-passing-suite-establishes`)). And delegating execution to an agent does not transfer accountability for it ([Agency → Accountability](../../docs/agency.md#accountability)). What the post adds is a name for work already governed, not a gap in it.
- **The post's standing, stated once.** Its content is its author's own practice and preference; under [Documentation → Citations](documentation/documentation.md#citations) a claim about a source's own design philosophy or motives is a self-assessment and is to be treated with more skepticism than a claim about observable behavior, and several of its proposals are marked in the post itself as experiments. It is admissible as the occasion of this inquiry and inadmissible as support for a rule — which is why no finding above rests on it. Two of its empirical claims, that visual material is processed faster and that controlled language tires the reader less, carry no measurement even in the post, and under [Content → Methodology](content/content.md#methodology) a numeric or quantitative-sounding claim without an inspectable method is a qualitative judgment.

## Conclusion
Nothing this occasion proposed overturned a governing document, and the two documents that changed between them did so at a silence rather than a contradiction: the record already forbade diagrams as carriers, already excluded a controlled vocabulary by its own terminology governance, already assigned generated artifacts to the service side of the prose-and-code division, and already held the substance of oversight in three separate protocols. What the occasion added is three things the record did not hold — that what a document owes its reader is scaled by what that reader's misreading costs, in [Knowledge → Write for the Structure of the Subject](./knowledge.md#write-for-the-structure-of-the-subject-not-for-todays-reader-limits); that a notation a reader recovers by eye is a notation whose errors a reader cannot see, and a rendering is a comparator rather than a substitute, in [Content → Concrete Evidence: Tree/Graph Notation in This Very Document](content/content.md#concrete-evidence-treegraph-notation-in-this-very-document); and that a rendering names the reader it was produced for, which is what makes it well-formed in a class of its own and malformed only when it is promoted out of it.

The proposals are therefore **not adopted**, with the diagnosis kept and the instrument declined: the obligation travels, the dictionary does not, a diagram is a rendering rather than a carrier, and an artifact no reader of this record can address or check is not documentation. The strongest support for that outcome is not this project's own argument but the standard's: its maintainers state that a checker cannot replace the specification, that writing in it correctly demands professional judgment rather than saving it, and that the principles transfer to general writing while the language does not.

Two outcomes are recorded because they are the kind that recur. First, one claim written into a governing document was withdrawn after its premise failed against the source, and the paragraph was rewritten rather than defended. Second, the negative result is now on file: the next person who forwards this post, or one like it, meets a record of what was examined and what was decided, instead of an open question.

Where the outcome landed: the weighting and the diagnosis-not-instrument paragraph in [Knowledge → Write for the Structure of the Subject](./knowledge.md#write-for-the-structure-of-the-subject-not-for-todays-reader-limits), and the inspectability consequence, the comparator-not-substitute rule, and the reader-class rule in [Content → Concrete Evidence: Tree/Graph Notation in This Very Document](content/content.md#concrete-evidence-treegraph-notation-in-this-very-document), each carried by a changelog entry citing this research as `Evidence`. No document was created to hold a further record of the rejections: the negative result is this research, which is its only home.

One question this research deliberately did not answer stands where its owner keeps it: whether this project wants a Renderer at all.
