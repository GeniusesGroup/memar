# Placements Handoff
Open work for [`placements.md`](./placements.md). This handoff was Classification's; the module was dissolved on 2026-10-23 and its open work stays here with the placements. An entry about another module is moved to that module's handoff when its owner rules on it. Entries are mutable current state — revised as each item moves, removed when it resolves or is dropped. See the repository's [documentation-handoff.md](../documentation/handoff/handoff.md) for what a handoff is. This handoff also carries what the dissolved `lib` module and the category-layout part of the [modularity handoff](./modularity.handoff.md) left open about where modules are placed.

## Decisions
- **Modules are classified by placement, category and label, and the categories of this repository are not final** — Decided (owner, 2026-10-17). The three placements ([Kernel](./labels/kernel.md), [Pluggable](./labels/pluggable.md), [Bundle](./labels/bundle.md)) and the manifest label, first worked out in the `organization` repository, are Memar's, and that repository refers here for why its modules are placed as they are. Neither repository's category list is treated as settled: the owner's own example is `storage` in `organization`, which has no independent identity and belongs under `computer`. Reason: two answers to one question, a shelf for foreign modules and a placement for optional capabilities, cannot both be right, and Memar is the framework that decides such questions. Rejected: leaving the placements in `organization` and mirroring them here.
- **The `lib` category is dissolved; its modules are pluggable and carry the label `guest`** — Decided (owner, 2026-10-17). The question the old handoff asked — whether foreign protocols are grouped under a Memar category or stand flat — is answered by [Pluggable](./labels/pluggable.md): a guest is filed under the category of the module it attaches to, in `modules-plugable/`, and the claim "this is not Memar's" travels in the manifest's `labels`, not in the path. Every module that stood under `lib/` was moved and its `in` addresses were rewritten. The label's own name, `guest`, is the owner's placeholder and may be improved ([below](#is-guest-the-right-name-for-the-label)). Rejected: a flat shelf of foreign names; keeping `lib/` beside the placements.
- **Categories are one level, and a module may hold its own facets** — Decided (owner, 2026-10-17). Documentation keeps its facets inside it rather than as sibling modules with a `documentation-` prefix: a prefix would make a category of a word, and the facets are parts of one concept ([Category Depth](../label/rules/category-depth/category-depth.md)). `time` is not subdivided: it holds few modules and will keep few.
- **Hardware, runtime, identifier, media types, codecs, cryptography and memory belong to `computer`** — Decided (owner, 2026-10-17). `hardware/` (a CPU is a part of a computer), `runtime/` (merged with `computer/runtime/`), `identifier/` and the media types ("the identity and naming of everything", which makes them the computer's), then `codec/`, `crypto/` and `memory/` (parts of how computers work, and a category that would hold them apart from `computer` has no subject of its own) moved under `computer/`. `computer/` is now a large category; the owner accepts that and treats the size as a reason to look for modules whose subject is another category's, not as a reason to split it. Whether `crypto/` is rather `math`'s is [open](#where-the-remaining-categories-end). Rejected: keeping `memory/` apart because its modules are many.
- **`content` and `documentation` stand under `knowledge`; the concept document of content moved with its module** — Decided (owner, 2026-10-17). `documentation` is a direct child of `knowledge`, not of `content`; the Content document and its companions left `docs/` for `modules/knowledge/content/`. The same ruling puts `rule.md` and its companions beside the rule module, by the principle that a document which needs code is not kept in `docs/` ([Modules → What does not belong here](../../README.md#what-does-not-belong-here)). A second ruling the same day moved `knowledge.md` and `process.md` with their companions into their own categories, so each category's `README.md` is a link to the concept document that stands in it and the ambiguity of a category without a main document is gone; the wider question is [below](#do-the-base-documents-of-docs-move-to-their-modules).
- **The Model Context Protocol is supported as a guest adapter, and never as Memar's model** — Decided (owner, 2026-10-10). The standing is the one HTTP has toward sRPC — "guest interoperability, not this protocol's addressing model" ([sRPC → Service ID](../../net/srpc/srpc.md#service-id)) — so MCP is never Memar's model, its addressing, or a channel Memar's own server is built on. The placement is [the rule](./labels/guest.md#the-rule)'s first item: MCP's contract changes only with its own maintainers' agreement, so it is a guest module. Reason: adoption by large companies is not a technical argument, and Memar states its critique of MCP on its merits ([the MCP comparison](../../../docs/comparisons/mcp.001.md)); an agent application that speaks only MCP is met by an adapter, not by a change to Memar's model. Rejected: building Memar's own server on MCP; refusing MCP outright. What the decision leaves open is [MCP as a guest adapter](#mcp-as-a-guest-adapter) below.
- **A kernel module fixes no choice another organization may make otherwise; a pluggable folder may carry a kernel module's name when it holds that module's optional parts** — Decided (owner, 2026-10-20). The rule is [Kernel Neutrality](./rules/kernel-neutrality/kernel-neutrality.md), adopted from the draft of [Modules Categories Research 001](../../../.agents/tasks/20261019-T191746Z-16420/docs/modules.categories.research.001.md#7-owner-decisions-needed) (C1), with a bullet in [Kernel](./labels/kernel.md#what-follows), entries in the design and implement normative-reference lists, and the direction check of `module_graph.py`, whose one allowance is the command module's back edge until organization's Task replaces Memar's session (C5). [Pluggable](./labels/pluggable.md#operational-rules) and [Placement](./rules/placement/placement.md#the-rule) were amended for the name (D1 of [Modules Research 001](../../../.agents/tasks/20261019-T191746Z-12632/docs/modules.research.001.md#6-owner-decisions-needed)). Reason: nothing a domain's core forces on others may be a choice another organization could make differently, and a domain may be partly kernel and partly pluggable, each part judged case by case. Classification has no changelog ledger; this entry is the record (task 20261020-T060221Z-19268). Rejected: a narrower rule for name lists only; the text as a section of `kernel.md`; `modules-plugable/<category>/<kernel-module-name>/` kept forbidden.

## Open Questions

### Does Kernel Neutrality bind bundles, Memar's own tooling, and Memar's tool modules under `modules/`?
- State: [Kernel Neutrality](./rules/kernel-neutrality/kernel-neutrality.md#the-rule) is stated for a kernel module. A bundle is a composition that may name any module ([Placement](./rules/placement/placement.md#the-rule)), so it is where an organization's choices are bound. Memar's tooling (`.agents/scripts`, `bundles/memar`) composes Memar's own choices, the role names among them. Memar's tool modules that sit under `modules/` (task, content) are kernel by their address. The rule does not say which of the three it reaches, so a reader can apply it to the tooling or exempt the tool modules.
- Options: (1) bundles no, tooling no, tool modules under `modules/` yes, written as one bullet under the rule's "What this rule does not claim" — recommended: it follows from Placement, and the task module already keeps only its own small layout; (2) the tooling is bound too, so every tool that names a role reads the profile instead, which touches tooling organization's Task replaces; (3) the tool modules are exempt, a second soft exception to a rule written to have none.
- Anchor: [Kernel Neutrality → What this rule does not claim](./rules/kernel-neutrality/kernel-neutrality.md#what-this-rule-does-not-claim).
- Blocks: nothing; meanwhile the rule is read as option 1.
- Asked: 2026-10-19, by task 20261019-T191746Z-16420 (question 24 of its [note](../../../.agents/tasks/20261019-T191746Z-16420/docs/modules.categories.research.001.md#6-answers)); written here 2026-10-23 by task 20261023-T091509Z-17584.

### Where each minifier implementation goes
- State: the owner ruled (2026-10-17) that the minify abstraction is clean and is Memar's own: any protocol that can be a `Stringer` can also implement it, so `protocol/minify.kh` moved from the guest shelf to `modules/computer/codec/minify/` and is kernel, with no `guest` label. The module still holds two implementations beside the abstraction, `css.kh` and `html.kh`, and the place of an implementation follows the format it minifies, not the abstraction.
- The question, per format: is the format one whose contract changes only with someone outside this project (CSS, HTML), so that its minifier is a [guest](./labels/guest.md) and [pluggable](./labels/pluggable.md) beside that format's module, or is it Memar's own?
- Path: owner ruling per format, then one move each; the abstraction stays where it is.

### Which of the modules that came from `lib` earn `guest`
- State: three modules were in the old shelf without being named in any argument — `modules-plugable/computer/adaptors/`, `modules-plugable/computer/parser/` and `modules-plugable/computer/uuid-ietf/` — and every module that came carries the label so that none is silently unlabelled. `uuid-ietf` passes the test (the identifier format is the IETF's); `adaptors` and `parser` are not shown to.
- The question, per module: does its contract change only with someone outside this project's agreement?
- Path: owner ruling per module, then the label is kept or removed and a module that fails is moved to the category that owns it.

### Two length shapes, and the `Stringer` decision that settles the pattern
- State: the owner ruled on 2026-10-19 that the two `Stringer` shapes are one abstraction, split by Go's limits rather than by a difference in meaning. `modules/computer/codec/string/protocol/stringer.kh` keeps `Stringer`, `Stringer_To` and `Stringer_From`; the mirrored `modules-plugable/computer/string/protocol/stringer.kh` is removed; and `modules/net/protocol/address.kh` names the kernel declaration. The [qualified names rule](../../../bundles/khayyam/rules/qualified-names/qualified-names.md#when-qualification-cannot-separate-two-declarations) records the case as settled. The length pair is the same case and is not merged: `modules/computer/codec/protocol/length.kh` declares `Codec_Length` and `modules-plugable/computer/json/protocol/length.kh` declares `JSON_Length`, both empty and each carrying in its comment the same Go alias of `container.NumberOfElement`.
- Options: (1) the JSON protocol names the codec's `Codec_Length`, one declaration for both — the owner's ruling favours this by the same argument as `Stringer`; (2) the codec takes the name the JSON protocol uses.
- Anchor: `modules/computer/codec/protocol/length.kh` and `modules-plugable/computer/json/protocol/length.kh`.
- Blocks: nothing; no declaration names either type.
- Asked: 2026-10-19, by task 20261019-T133400Z-4624.

### Where the remaining categories end

### Do the base documents of `docs/` move to their modules
- State: with `content`, `knowledge`, `process` and `rule` moved, the base documents that remain in `docs/` (Type, Modeling, Modularity, Protocol, Cognition and the rest) are a place kept for a reason that grows weaker: the owner's reading (2026-10-17) is that once the BM25 ranking behind `memar index` is complete, where a document sits stops mattering for finding it, and that nearly every base document may belong to its own module, or may itself be a module or a large category of modules, once the categories are settled. Moving the sure ones now and recording the rest was the owner's instruction.
- The question: does `docs/` keep any document, and which documents are certain to move now? A research session on the strongest model is asked it and returns a list with an argument per document.
- Path: the research session's result, then one move per certain document by the same script-and-rewrite pass; the rest stay recorded here until their category is clear.

## Assumptions
- **A path need not carry the claim "not Memar's"; a label can** — Stability: Unexamined. The module's reason to dissolve the old shelf rests on it, and the label is searched for, not seen in a listing. What changes it: a reader who finds the label too hidden.
- **"Authority is outside this project" is a workable test for the label** — Stability: Weak. It decides the clear cases — HTTP, JSON, SOAP — and it is untested on a transformation-shaped abstraction like the minifier. What changes it: the first subject filed under the label that no outside world specifies.
- **The categories used on both sides of a placement are the same** — Stability: Moderate. It keeps a pluggable module findable beside the module it attaches to; it breaks when a capability has no category on the kernel side.
