### Supersession: which home owns the obligation, and is it a norm or a check?
Three questions raised by the two October comparisons, investigated against this project's own documents, and left open. What the investigation established, so the next session need not repeat it:
- **The shape is already stated, in this document's base.** "The revision that holds stands in the governing document, and the reasoning of the one that does not stays readable as Considered and not done" ([Knowledge → Task-Centric Knowledge Evolution](./knowledge.md#task-centric-knowledge-evolution)) — a standing answer, a bidirectional citation pair, and an unedited ledger entry. It is distinct from both rewrite-in-place and raw append.
- **A retiring handoff is a cousin of that shape, not the shape.** Its own practice rewrites affected sections, calls itself "a living state-capture, not an append-only history", and graduates by deletion.
- **No facet owns the obligation.** Its three parts sit in three documents of different kinds — the `Superseded` / `Superseded_by` relation vocabulary in [Documentation → Citations](./documentation.md#citations), the append-only rule in [Documentation — Changelog → Structure](./documentation-changelog.md#structure), and one sentence in [System → Reconciling Conflicting Decisions](./system.md#reconciling-conflicting-decisions). That sentence is the only statement of the obligation in the whole doc set, and a writer producing a changelog entry will not meet it.
- **What is missing is a required field, and its three parts are all optional today.** Surviving evidence goes in `Cited` under the `Evidence` relation; the reason for the drop goes in the optional `Considered and not done`; provenance is the entry's own `Time` and `Contributors`. Recoverability therefore holds only because entries are appended, and *which evidence survived* is reconstructed by the reader, once per case, by reasoning backward from prose written to state an outcome. [Modeling → Modeling State Change as Events](./modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates) states the same shape as a property of any concern's state, and [Rule → What a Rule Is](./rule.md#what-a-rule-is) is one artifact kind where replaceability is named in the definition — the precedent an answer would follow.
- Open: whether the obligation should be a norm Changelog states or a check it performs. Nothing read distinguishes a superseding entry from any other entry mechanically, and [Documentation → Written surface](./documentation.md#written-surface) makes a check that finds nothing silent by design. Deciding between a norm and a check is the owner's.
- Open: whether the `Superseded` relation should be restated at claim granularity, so a claim replaced inside a surviving document is expressible the way a replaced document is. Its wording is document-scoped, and `Status` does not move when the document survives. Adding a relation to [Documentation → Citations](./documentation.md#citations) is a change to a cross-cutting vocabulary, which is why it is a question and not a change.
- Open: whether a supersession record inherits the handoff's confidence vocabulary. Nothing consulted connects the two.

### What an agent is to do when it lacks a third-party library's shape
- State, scoped to the search made: a search of `docs/` by the sessions that raised this entry, for the terms third-party library, dependency, documentation corpus, and freshness, matched no document stating the route an agent takes when it needs the current interface of a library the project depends on and cannot reach it from the project's own sources. That is a fact about that search, not about Memar ([Cognition → Report absence rather than supplying it](./cognition.md#report-absence-rather-than-supplying-it)); the exact terms and documents searched were not recorded, so the next session repeats the search, records its terms, and treats a hit as superseding this sentence.
- State, what the surrounding documents already hold, recorded so this is not re-derived. Three are norms: a claim's origin is context and never a substitute for inspectable support ([Cognition → Evaluate claims on their merits](./cognition.md#evaluate-claims-on-their-merits-treat-source-as-neither-authority-nor-disqualifier)); a shared artifact is cognitive material under the same evaluation as anything else, and a stale one is reported rather than propagated ([Cognition → Treat shared artifacts as claims](./cognition.md#treat-shared-artifacts-as-claims-under-the-same-evaluation-not-as-absolute-authority)); an answer the lookup does not hold is reported rather than supplied ([Cognition → Report absence rather than supplying it](./cognition.md#report-absence-rather-than-supplying-it)). The fourth is a scoping sentence, not a norm: the performed service over a live corpus - orientation, extraction, retrieval - is named as work delivered outside this repository ([README → Where Memar Stops, and Where Work Continues](../README.md#where-memar-stops-and-where-work-continues)).
- State, the part Module dependency → Dependencies are repository references (`docs/modules/computer/module-dependency.md#dependencies-are-repository-references`) already answers: a dependency is declared as a repository reference pinned to a specific revision, the consumer chooses the revision, and "the pin *is* the decision". So which revision of a library the project builds against is the project's declaration, never a corpus's ranking. What it does not answer, and what stays open below: whether it reaches a third-party library that is not a Memar project, how an agent that cannot read the pinned source obtains the library's shape, and what standing a text obtained any other way has.
- Open: whether the sanctioned route is the dependency's own interface or its source read at the pinned revision, a corpus the project builds and serves to its own agents, the delivered service the README names, or no framework statement at all and each project's own decision. The sources searched do not distinguish a third-party library's interface from any other third-party dependency.
- Open: whether such material is admissible to an agent as grounding, admissible as evidence to be checked, or neither. The norm above governs a claim's source in an exchange between participants; a text an agent reads as material is not the same act, and no document consulted says which of the two a documentation corpus is.
- Next: the owner rules both. Neither is a mechanics question, and no rule can be written before the ruling.

### What must a derived artifact state so that a mechanical check can say it is stale, and who runs the check?
- State: [Knowledge](./knowledge.md) allows executable projections (a generator's output, an index, a cache, the pointer an install writes), and the Content Rule of [Documentation](./documentation.md#content-rule-no-fabricated-or-redundant-provenance) forbids a hand-kept copy of what can be derived. Where a derived artifact is allowed to exist, nothing says what it must state (its sources, the revision it was derived from, the time) so that a mechanical check, not a reader, can say it is stale, nor who runs that check. Provenance that nothing checks is decoration, and a stale projection read as current is a claim that has stopped being true ([Cognition → Treat shared artifacts as claims](./cognition.md#treat-shared-artifacts-as-claims-under-the-same-evaluation-not-as-absolute-authority)). A search of docs/ for stale, staleness, derived from, rebuildable and provenance finds nothing that answers it.
- Options: (1) a derived artifact states its sources and the revision it was derived from, and the verb that produces it also offers the check that compares them (recommended; cost is a field per artifact and one check); (2) a derived artifact is never kept: it is regenerated on use; (3) leave staleness to the reader.
- Anchor: docs/knowledge.md, the section on executable projections; docs/documentation.md, Content Rule, No derivative inventories. The answer lands in knowledge.md.
- Blocks: nothing is blocked. Assumption meanwhile: no derived artifact is committed (Tentative).

### Material with no producing decision: does Memar need a category for it?
Five questions a placement survey left open, recorded so they are not re-investigated from the documents. The survey's own result is in [Knowledge Changelog](./knowledge.changelog.md#material-with-no-producing-decision-a-placement-survey-and-what-knowledge-already-holds): Knowledge is the base artifact, the Content Rule cannot host the category without changing its stated scope, `docs/modules/` is not the home, and most of the vocabulary the category would need already exists.
- Is the condition decidable from the medium at all? [Cognition → Report absence rather than supplying it](./cognition.md#report-absence-rather-than-supplying-it) holds that a lookup finding nothing is a fact about the lookup - written about what a participant may report, not about what a category may require. That second use is unexamined.
- What would count as evidence that no decision produced an artifact? Memar's record keeps decisions in changelog entries, research companions, and handoffs, but nothing requires every file in the medium to have one, so absence of a record is weak evidence in the direction the category would need.
- What follows for material found in the category - surface it, delete it, adopt it retroactively by recording the decision, or decline to say? Neither Test Practice → The Cycle (`docs/modules/process/test.practice.md#the-cycle`) nor the empty-result rule states an obligation about material nobody decided to keep.
- Does the category reach projects that install Memar, or only this repository's own record? [README → Where Memar Stops, and Where Work Continues](../README.md#where-memar-stops-and-where-work-continues) was not read for the survey, and the boundary between what Memar states about its own documentation and what it states about a consuming project is unexamined.
- Is the phenomenon Memar's at all - or may a project simply have material nothing decided on? Whether the honest answer is "Memar needs a category" or "Memar needs a statement that it declines to require one" is unasked by any document consulted.
