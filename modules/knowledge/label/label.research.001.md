## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The label domain answers one question for every record in an organization's graph: *by what words can anyone reach, group and talk about a thing without changing what the thing is?* It gives each such word one identity that carries no wording, keeps the dated, attributed fact that someone applied it to something, and owns the few rules that make an applied word safe to read: who may apply and withdraw it, and what its application triggers. Everything else a word is used for — the letters it is spelled with, the order of a list, the columns of a board, a certifier's seal, an approval — is another domain's, or a pluggable's, with a rule for when it may take the label.

Why this size. First, Memar's definition already gives the shape: identity for the label, "describes, does not identify" for its effect, "defined once, the definition takes labels" for its structure. Second, the corpus shows the domain used for **six jobs** that are not one thing, and the fact that they share a stem is the problem finding 3 has to solve:

| # | Job | Where the tree shows it |
|---|---|---|
| J1 | a descriptive word attached to any node | [thing labels](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/thing-labels.md), [law labels](../../../modules-plugable/rights/law/labels/labels.md), [account labels](../../../modules-plugable/economics/account/labels/labels.md), [claim labels](../../../modules-plugable/rights/claims/labels/labels.md): "Attaching a word uses `(thing)-[:label]`" |
| J2 | the type of a record (a vocabulary the record draws its kind from) | `Field_LabelType`, hardware node type, `instance_of/address` type, `text-type`, `taxon` gender and rank; "a label's type is itself a label" ([label.md](label.md#type)) |
| J3 | the reason an edge exists, the third parameter of every stored relation | `Graph[thing, thing, label]` with the comment "Edge indicate relation reason e.g. Complement, Substitute" in `storage/graph/protocol/data_model.kh`; the invoice item's `LabelUUID`; the friendship `Circle` |
| J4 | a user's or organization's curated list | [list](edges/list/list.md): "label (`Commodity` own by User(`Omid`)) `list` Product (abc)"; playlist, bookmark, content list |
| J5 | a column or row of a board | [table](edges/table/table.md), [matrix](edges/matrix/matrix.md): `Scrum Board`, `Eisenhower Matrix` |
| J6 | a scope that limits who or what may act | [label-allowed](edges/label-allowed/label-allowed.md), [label-used_by](edges/label-used_by/label-used_by.md), [reply-limit_to_label](../content/edges/reply-limit_to_label/reply-limit_to_label.md), [execution-limit_to_label](../../computer/rule/edges/execution-limit_to_label/execution-limit_to_label.md) |

Third, **the word is used both for the label and for its use.** In one document set the label is a vertex that can be listed, typed and owned (J4, J5, J6, J2), and in another it is the type of an edge with no far end (J3; the status loops; the `label-commodity` self-classification `(product)-[:commodity]->(---)` in [product label](../../../modules-plugable/economics/product/labels/labels.md)). The [label loop](edges-loop/label/label.md) is written `(thing)-[:label]->(---)`, which names an edge *type called `label`* and names no label at all; its identity appears only in the `.kh` residue as `Field_LabelID`. The three spellings of an attachment (the loop with a blank end, the generic edge to a label vertex as in [campaign](../../management/task/edges/campaign/campaign.md) `(task)-[:label]->(campaign)` and [assigner](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/assigner/assigner.md) `(---)-[:assigner]->(label)`, and the typed loop) are the central modeling question of the cluster and the subject of finding 3.

**The word hides four things (agent, Tentative).** *The wording* — the letters, in a language, which [label.md](label.md#text) already sends to the text domain. *The label* — the identity that stays when the wording is renamed, translated or disputed. *The attachment* — the fact that this label was applied to that thing by someone, then. *The use* — what a reader does with an attachment: filter, group, refuse, route. A label is the second; the domain keeps the second and the third; the fourth is a rule's.

**Ten scenarios (agent; general knowledge, illustrative).**

1. **One word, three meanings; one meaning, three words (Persian, English, Turkish).** The Persian *shir* is milk, a lion and a tap; the English *bank* is a river's edge and a lender; the same sea is named in Persian, Arabic and English by different communities in ways that are a political dispute; Bombay became Mumbai, Constantinople Istanbul. Inside this repository: `Commodity` is a user's list in [list](edges/list/list.md) and a product classification in [product label](../../../modules-plugable/economics/product/labels/labels.md) (the `label-commodity` folder), and Memar's own example is `design` as a topic and as a task kind. *Breaks:* a label as a string; a rule that a label name is unique; a rename as a new label. *Says:* a label has an identity; its words are texts in languages, related to it, each with an author and a period; two labels can share a wording without being the same, and one label can have several wordings without being several; a dispute over a name is a dispute about the text relation, and the system should be able to hold both without picking one.
2. **A book in a classification scheme (Dewey, Library of Congress, the Chinese Library Classification, a tenth-century catalogue such as the Fihrist).** One book carries a class number in each scheme a library uses and subject headings besides; schemes are hierarchical (a class sits under a broader class); editions renumber and retire headings. *Breaks:* a flat set of labels; the idea that "kind" is one value; a label whose meaning does not depend on the scheme it came from. *Says:* a scheme is a vocabulary, a label that other labels belong to; broader-and-narrower is a relation among labels, and the closure it implies (a book on Persian poetry is found under literature) is derived, not stored; an edition change is a succession of labels, not an edit of one.
3. **A clinical record (a hospital anywhere): allergy, "do not resuscitate", triage colour, a diagnosis code.** A label added in an emergency changes what the next clinician does; a wrong or stale one harms; a retracted allergy must still be seen to have been retracted; the triage colour changes within the hour and is read by software before a human looks. *Breaks:* the repository's registries' stance that "registration does not choose who may attach the word" ([thing labels](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/thing-labels.md)); labels as harmless commentary; removal as deletion. *Says:* who may attach and who may withdraw each label is the most important rule of the domain; withdrawal is a new fact with an author, never an erasure; some labels (the triage colour) are read synchronously by services and so are statuses in the sense of [status.md](../status/status.md), and some are read later by people.
4. **Categories of persons (caste certificates in India, the race classification of apartheid South Africa with its reclassification boards, ethnicity on identity cards in Rwanda, self-declared religion, gender and sexuality).** The same word is a self-description when a person applies it and an instrument of harm when an organization applies it to them; the label follows a person across organizations; and merely listing the labels in use, or counting them, can disclose a person. *Breaks:* an attachment as a neutral fact; "anyone can attach"; label lists as public facets. *Says:* the attachment must record whether the attacher is the subject or someone else; the subject's rights to see, contest and have an ascribed label withdrawn are requirements of the domain, not of one registry; some labels may not be attachable by an organization at all; visibility of an attachment and of counts derived from it is a rule ([group scenario 13](../../management/group/group.research.001.md) asks the same for a membership list).
5. **A playlist, a wishlist, a reading list, a Hajj checklist (any country).** A list is ordered, owned by someone, shared with some, edited by several; deleting the list does not delete its items; a list can hold lists (a folder of playlists); and the same item sits in many lists. *Breaks:* a label as an unordered description; a label "owned by" a user when Memar's label is "defined once, in one place" and shared; the list's own open question whether a list in a list is a second edge ([list](edges/list/list.md#open-questions)). *Says:* a list is a collection, a thing with an owner and an ordered `contains`, which may itself be labelled; it is not a description of its members; a user's private tag ("my tags") is a different matter, a label definition whose scope is one person.
6. **A Kanban board and an Eisenhower matrix (Toyota's production lines; a president's urgent-and-important grid).** A card moves from "To do" to "Doing" to "Done" by someone with the right to move it, perhaps limited by a work-in-progress cap; the matrix puts each task in one of four cells computed from two independent yes-or-no judgements. *Breaks:* a column as a description (a move is a transition with rules and a trigger); the table and matrix edges' own `TODO::: base on label or status or both??` ([table data model](edges/table/protocol/data_model.kh)); the idea that the board is part of the label domain at all. *Says:* a column is a status in an ordered set owned by a task kind, the board is a projection; the matrix is two orthogonal labels and a discovered cell; the stage vocabulary belongs to the task domain (the [task research](../../management/task/task.research.001.md) already makes Scrum, XP and Eisenhower words pluggable stage vocabularies).
7. **A certification mark (halal, kosher, organic, a conformity mark, a fair-trade seal, a vegetarian dot).** The mark is a claim by an authority about a product or a batch, with a scope, an audit, a validity period and a revocation; self-applied marks are fraud; two authorities use the same word and do not recognise each other (halal bodies of different countries; two organic regimes with an equivalence arrangement). *Breaks:* a label as a commenter's note; "registration does not choose who may attach"; the idea that one word is one label. *Says:* the attachment must be able to carry who attested, to what scope, until when and on what evidence; the same wording under two authorities is two labels; recognition between authorities is an assertion of equivalence, scoped and dated (the thing domain's `same_as` is the nearest mechanism); revocation and expiry are processing, so a pluggable.
8. **A label that changes behaviour (a cloud resource tagged `environment=production`, a rule limited to nodes labelled `server`, a comment thread limited to members labelled `moderator`).** Whoever may attach `server` to a client device decides where code runs; the person who may attach `moderator` decides who may reply. *Breaks:* "a label does not change anything" and "a label is only a comment" ([thing labels](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/thing-labels.md): "It does not by itself block a reader. A rule may read the label"). *Says:* the authority to attach a label must be at least as strong as the strongest rule that reads it, so the contract of a label has to name its readers' stakes; behaviour binds to the label's identity, never its wording ([Modules](../../modules.md#placement): "Behavior binds to a module's UUID, never to a name"); the kernel states the requirement and the rule engine decides ([Modules](../../modules.md#placement), `condition_is`).
9. **A label put on by a machine (a spam or toxicity detector, an "AI-generated" badge, a "friend of a friend" degree).** The attacher is software; it errs at scale; the labelled party needs an appeal; the label has to vanish when the detector is removed; a human's contrary attachment has to outrank it. The repository already says degree labels "are automatic (users cannot assign them)" ([graph](../../storage/graph/graph.md)) and keeps the detectors as separate pluggables ([detection-toxicity](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/detection-toxicity/edges-loop/detection-toxicity/detection-toxicity.md)). *Breaks:* an attachment as an assertion by a person. *Says:* the attacher kind is part of the attachment; a machine's attachment is discovered data (re-derivable, removable whole) and never the source of truth; the contest of one is a case, not a label.
10. **Vocabulary decay (a word becomes offensive and is replaced; "tax" and "taxes" are both in use; one label must become two; a scheme publishes a new edition; a wiki merges two categories).** A heading revised by a library, a category renamed by a community, a tag synonym merged by a site. Ten thousand attachments carry the old label. *Breaks:* relabelling as editing the word; an attachment as stable. *Says:* merge, split and retire are successions of labels (the thing domain's `supersedes`, here with sets on either side), attachments are not rewritten and stay readable as of their date, and the new wording is a new text relation; a rename that changes nothing but the wording is a text change, not a label change.

**The reading the answers assume (second layer; agent, Tentative).** The underlying problem is not "how to tag a record" but "how can many parties describe the same things in their own words and with their own authority, without the description becoming the identity and without a description silently changing what the system does". The answers below therefore favour: a label as an identity without wording; an attachment as a dated, attributed fact that is withdrawn by a new fact; every consequence of an attachment as a rule's; and grouping, ordering, attestation and approval as capabilities that take labels, not as labels. Where the owner's underlying problem is different (for example, if the domain is chiefly meant as the graph's edge-type registry, as the `storage/graph` data model suggests), choice 1 of finding 3 changes first.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

#### The removal test run on the label (agent, Tentative)
Remove the label domain and try to express the rest. The graph needs an identity for the *type* of each edge (its data model is `Graph[thing, thing, label]`, and the invoice item, the transaction reference and the friendship circle also carry a `label_p.UUID` as the kind of relation; the [label handoff](label.handoff.md#decisions) counts about thirty `label_p.UUID` use sites in all); without it each domain would mint its own relation-type identifier, which is the duplication that the collapse onto `ThingUUID` removed for records and that the [edge handoff](../edge/edge.handoff.md#decisions) records for ninety identifiers. At least five domains need an identity for "a word that types a record" ([text](../text/text.md) `text-type`, [taxon](../../identity/taxon/taxon.md) gender and rank, `hardware`, `instance_of/address`, the friendship circle) and four registries exist because without a shared attachment each document repeated the point. The fabrication would land inside the foundational set (the graph's edge type), so the label passes. The second guard also holds: these are existing requirements. The test is silent on how much the label contains, which is the next step.

The same test run on **status** gives the opposite result. Remove the status domain and express a draft, a lock or a soft delete through a label, an event and a rule: nothing foundational has to be fabricated. What status adds to a label is real but small (a synchronous-read guarantee and a transition event, below), and it is a *kind of label* in Memar's own words ("the values of Status are labels that take the label `Status`"), so by the test it is not foundational on its own account. It may still be a module for being the shared home of a vocabulary, which is a different test ([Placing a relation](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation)).

Run on **edge**, the test passes, with a caution: without an edge there is no relation at all, but the edge domain today declares only an identity ([edge.md](../edge/edge.md#what-the-protocol-requires-of-an-edge)); the requirement that makes it kernel (the ends, the type) is the one nothing yet writes down ([edge handoff](../edge/edge.handoff.md#open-questions), "Rule 6, recorded").

### 3. Rival models

Six choices, each separable. The order is the order in which they change what the cluster is.

#### Choice 1: what a label is in the graph
- **A1 — a word on an edge, held in code (Explored; rejected by a Decided statement).** The Go `const` approach: each vocabulary is a list of constants. *For:* nothing to store. *Against:* the label handoff decided that the declarations are contracts and the organization handoff that a label is data, not code; a user's own word cannot be a constant; the status handoff's R4 question exists because constants (`Status_ReadyForDevelopment` and the like) mix three vocabularies.
- **A2 — a vertex reached by a generic edge (Explored).** `(thing)-[:label]->(label)`: the label is a node, the attachment an ordinary edge. *For:* it is Memar's reading taken literally, the label can be listed, typed, owned and itself labelled with no special case; it is the spelling in [campaign](../../management/task/edges/campaign/campaign.md), [assigner](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/assigner/assigner.md) and [reply-limit_to_label](../content/edges/reply-limit_to_label/reply-limit_to_label.md). *Against:* the status document rejects this very form ("A generic `(record)-[:status]->(draft-node)` would promote the marker into a concept it does not independently carry"), and the same reasoning applies to every marker; it makes every attachment a two-node edge, so the `edges-loop` bucket would hold nothing for labels; a synchronous reading has to traverse to the far end.
- **A3 — one identity, used as the type of a loop-edge (Tentative, recommended).** The label is a thing (a vertex with its own text, kind and standing); an attachment is a loop-edge on the labelled thing *whose type is that label's identity*; relations that treat the label as a vertex (`(label)-[:list]->(thing)`, `(label)-[:column_of]->(label)`) are ordinary edges from it. The same identity serves as the reason of any stored edge. *For:* it is the spelling of the [status document](../status/status.md#what-a-status-is), of `storage/graph` (third parameter, queries by label), of the `label-commodity` loops in [product label](../../../modules-plugable/economics/product/labels/labels.md), and of Memar's "a label has an identifier ... that identifier is what a search finds every record that took the label by"; it keeps the Decided "markers stay loop-edges"; the typed-loop and the vertex are two views of one `ThingUUID`, so Memar's "a kind is itself a label" costs nothing. *Against:* edge types become data created at runtime (an unbounded set, every user's word), so the edge-type space needs the vocabulary pluggable's scope rules; the document of the label loop has to say that the type slot of any loop-edge holds a label identity (today it names a type called `label`).

  How each meets the scenarios (agent judgment): A1 fails 1, 5, 10. A2 meets 1 to 10 in outline and costs the loop-edge bucket. A3 meets all ten in outline, at the price of a type slot that needs governance (scenarios 8, 10).

#### Choice 2: label, status and the line between them
- **X1 — two mechanisms (Explored; the status quo).** Status is its own systematic loop-edge; label is the descriptive one; the difference is "async rules" against "sync services". *For:* the status document says so, and the owner confirmed the shared concept. *Against:* the difference is the reader's, not the record's: the same `locked` flag is read by a service in one transaction and by a report later; two places answer "who may attach"; two sets of Policies repeat "indicate which user set it, when, and the chain of changes".
- **X2 — status is a label kind, plus a transition event and a read guarantee (Tentative, recommended).** ST1 to ST4 above. The status domain keeps its document, its shared vocabulary and the transition contract; it does not keep a second attachment mechanism. *For:* it matches Memar ("the values of Status are labels that take the label `Status`"), keeps "a status is a loop-edge" and "a move is an event", gives the shared vocabulary one home and every domain's vocabulary a kind to take, and removes the duplicate Policies. *Against:* it changes the status document's wording from "systematic labels" to "a kind of label"; it needs the family question (ST4) answered before a state machine can be written.
- **X3 — fold status into label (Explored).** No status module; shared words become labels in a registry. *For:* one fewer module. *Against:* the transition event and the synchronous guarantee are real obligations, and a lifecycle (contract: negotiation, partly signed, signed) needs a home that is not a flat vocabulary.

#### Choice 3: what one label identity stands for
- **N1 — one identity per meaning; wording is a relation to a text (Tentative, recommended).** *For:* scenarios 1 and 10; matches [label.md](label.md#text) (nothing here carries the words) and Memar's "design as a topic and design as a task kind are two labels". *Against:* two parties who mean the same thing will mint two labels; reconciling them is an equivalence assertion (the thing domain's `same_as`, still Explored there), which is real work.
- **N2 — one identity per wording per organization (Explored).** *For:* simple lookup by name. *Against:* fails scenarios 1, 7 and 10; makes a rename a new label; contradicts "names are labels; identity is not".
- **N3 — one identity per (wording, kind) pair (Explored; Memar's example read literally).** The two `design` labels differ because their kinds differ. *Against:* it puts the wording back into the identity.

#### Choice 4: who may attach and withdraw
- **U1 — anyone who can see the thing (Explored).** The commenter model of the registries ("Attaching a label is a commenter's act", [law labels](../../../modules-plugable/rights/law/labels/labels.md)). *For:* it is what the registries say; no rule. *Against:* fails scenarios 3, 4, 7, 8: the label then governs behaviour and harm with no authority behind it, and by [No bare edge](../../modules.md#placement) the `label` edge does not exist.
- **U2 — each label's definition names its rule, with a default (Tentative, recommended).** LB3: the contract is stated per label by reference to a rule through `condition_is`; a label with no named rule takes the default of its vocabulary; the default of the default is "the party with authority over the labelled thing, and the maintainer of the label". *For:* answers 3, 4, 7, 8, 9 without the kernel knowing a rule; the `lock` (by owner) against `read-only` ("even owner of the text can't remove this label") pair in the status documents already differs in exactly this. *Against:* every defined label owes two statements; the first vocabularies will carry defaults only.
- **U3 — only authorities attach (Explored).** *For:* safe. *Against:* no personal labels, no commenter words (scenario 5's private tags); contradicts the registries.

#### Choice 5: a label's kind
- **K1 — a kind is required and is itself a label (Tentative, recommended).** Memar's reading: the definition takes labels, the kind among them; the regress ends in a root kind that takes itself (a loop-edge, a real one). *For:* answers the label handoff's third open question ("whether a label's type may be absent") the way Memar answers it; `Field_LabelType` becomes total. *Against:* a root kind is a fabricated first label; a free word that only a user cares about must still take `Topic` or its like.
- **K2 — the kind is optional (Explored).** *For:* matches the handoff's wording and `text-type` ("type optional until a vocabulary exists", [text research](../text/text.research.001.md) question 12). *Against:* an untyped label cannot be told from a mistake, and "is it a status" cannot be asked.
- **K3 — many kinds with no distinguished one (Explored).** *For:* Memar allows more than one label to attach to a content. *Against:* `Field_LabelType` returns one identity; the kind of kinds is lost.

#### Choice 6: how label, status and edge are packaged
- **G1 — three modules, sharpened (Tentative, recommended; the catalogue's B7 default is that the edge stays a kernel meta-domain).** Edge: identity, ends, type (a label). Label: identity, attachment, contract. Status: the kind and the transition contract. *For:* each has a document, a handoff and consumers today; no folder moves. *Against:* edge and label are mutually dependent in meaning (the edge's type is a label; a label's attachment is an edge); at the file level the import graph stays acyclic (`label/dt-uuid.kh` imports thing; `edge/model.kh` would import `label/dt-uuid.kh`; `label/model.kh` imports `edge/model.kh`) and no cycle is created, but the meaning is a pair.
- **G2 — edge and label as one kernel module, "relation" (Explored).** The instance and its type together. *For:* `EdgeID` has no consumer at all while `label_p.UUID` is used about thirty times as the edge's type, and `edge.md` already says `EdgeID` "says what relation this is"; the Go `label_p.Model` composed `edge_p.Model`. *Against:* it moves a domain whose placement is the audit's CONTESTED item, which needs the owner; it merges two documents the repository decided to keep apart.
- **G3 — edge belongs to storage/graph, label stays (Explored; the edge handoff raises it).** *For:* `storage/graph` is the only place that reads edges today. *Against:* it makes the abstract relation a storage detail.

The sub-question inside G1: **what `LabelModel` names.** Today `GenderModel`, `TextQueryModel` and the table model compose `LabelModel` to say "this record is a label" (a gender is a label; a query is a text type), while the Go `label_p.Model` composed `edge_p.Model`, which says "the record that holds the attachment is an edge". Those are two things (the label; the record carrying an attachment of it) with one name. **Explored**; see open question 6.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions
These are questions the cluster's own documents must answer before implementation; each favours no answer where the repository has not settled it ([Ask rather than assume](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). A line marked *settled* cites its source.

#### Identity
- *Settled:* the label's identifier is `ThingUUID`; no `LabelUUID` ([label handoff](label.handoff.md#decisions)). *Settled:* no `EdgeUUID` ([edge handoff](../edge/edge.handoff.md#decisions)). *Settled:* a label's wording is not here ([label.md](label.md#text)).
- Is a label identified by its meaning, by its wording within an organization, or by its wording and kind? Who decides that two labels mean the same, and is that verdict an assertion that can be wrong?
- Is the identity of a label global (one value anywhere), or organization-scoped with a mapping? May two organizations share a label, or only mint their own and relate them?
- May a label exist with no wording, no kind and no attachment? For how long before it is debris?
- Is the identity of the *type of an edge* the same value as the identity of a *label*, or only related? Is `EdgeID` retained when an edge is a thing already?
- Is a private label (one user's tag) a label in the same sense, or something with a smaller scope that may be promoted?

#### Lifecycle
- When does a label begin: when defined, when first attached, when first read by a rule?
- Does a label end, and how does the end of a word differ from the end of a meaning (a retired heading, a split, a merge)? May a retired label be reinstated?
- May a label's kind change without ending it (a topic that becomes a task kind)? Is that a new label?
- What happens to attachments when their label is merged, split or retired: stay, redirect, flag?
- When does an attachment begin and end; is a withdrawal reversible; is the interval an attachment held recorded as valid time, recorded time, or both?
- Is the status of a record a derived reading of its attachments, or an attachment of its own with an exclusive family?
- Is a status family (lifecycle against independent flags) declared by the domain that owns the vocabulary, or by the kind?

#### Authorization
- Who may define a label, attach it, withdraw it, retire it, change its contract? Is the authority per label, per kind, per vocabulary, per organization?
- May a person withdraw a label that was applied to them by an organization? May an organization refuse a label a person applies to themselves?
- Whose authority decides when two organizations attach conflicting labels to one thing?
- How is the right to attach a label made at least as strong as the strongest rule that reads it, and who checks that?
- Who may see an attachment, a label's existence, and counts derived from attachments? Does hiding a label hide the fact that it was ever applied?
- What does the label's maintainer owe a user who has attached a word that is later retired?

#### Rules
- What must a label's definition state for a label that nothing reads, and for one that a rule reads (the minimum of LB3)?
- Is "attach `X` to `Y`" checked against a scope at the time of definition, of attachment, or at both? What happens to attachments made before the scope narrowed?
- May a rule attach a label (a detector), and if so under what contract and with what priority against a person's attachment?
- Which labels exclude each other, and where is that written (the kind, the vocabulary, the owning domain's rule)?
- May a label carry data (a weight, a probability, a score)? The registry notes ask whether probability is a weight on an attachment or a label ([thing labels](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/thing-labels.md#open-questions)).
- Is a flag a label that rules read, or does the reading of it by a service (the synchronous guarantee) change its contract?

#### Events
- Which events does the cluster publish: label defined, label retired, attachment, withdrawal, status transition, contract changed? Which does a pluggable subscribe to?
- Is the transition event one per status or one for the family, and does it carry the previous value?
- Does an attachment by software publish the same event as one by a person, and how is a contest raised?
- Is the "last modifier / last modification time" of a record an event the log holds, or a status the record carries?
- What does an edge's creation or withdrawal publish, and does a loop-edge publish less than an ordinary one?

### 6. Missing edges and modules
Each is a sketch for another session; none is written. A pluggable carries a capability name, not a kernel module's name.

- **`attachment` contract (a section, not a module).** The label loop's Model line should read `(thing)-[:<label>]->(---)` and say that the type slot of any loop-edge holds a label identity (choice 1, A3). It owes the attacher kind, the request, the time and the contract reference of LB3. Who: the party named by the label's contract. Triggers: the attachment event.
- **`withdrawal` (a fact, not a module).** `(withdrawal)-[:withdraws]->(attachment)`: a new fact that references the attachment it ends, with author and time. Nothing is deleted. The same shape the thing research uses for a tombstone and the content research for a correction.
- **`vocabulary` (pluggable).** The capability in finding 2. It also takes the five registries ([thing labels](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/thing-labels/thing-labels.md), [law labels](../../../modules-plugable/rights/law/labels/labels.md), [account labels](../../../modules-plugable/economics/account/labels/labels.md), [claim labels](../../../modules-plugable/rights/claims/labels/labels.md), [product label](../../../modules-plugable/economics/product/labels/labels.md)) as registered vocabularies, each keeping its words; whether the registries stay five pluggables or become folders of one is open. It owns the scope, adoption, default-allowed and merge, split and retire processing, and it is where `CNF_DefaultAllowed = false` and the open question "module-scoping at registration or per assignment" get an answer.
- **`collection` (pluggable).** Ordered, owned, shareable membership; one capability for the label domain's `list`, the content research's `collection` and the board's ordered `part_of`. Who creates: the owner and named collaborators. Triggers: a new-member event for followers.
- **`attestation` (pluggable, or an extension of [certificate](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/certificate/certificate.md)).** A label with an authority behind it: scope, validity, evidence, revocation, recognition between authorities. Who: the authority; revoked by the authority or an audit as a new fact. Triggers: expiry and revocation events. Whether it is a second capability beside `certificate` ("an issued credential over knowledge or skill") or the same one is a question for whoever owns certificate.
- **`approval` (pluggable).** The ladder suggest, request, approve and approval-needed, with quorum and maker-checker; the four documents now filed as status loops become its relations. Who: the suggester or requester, then an approver with authority. Triggers: request, approval and unblock events. It should not be designed before the task and delegate researches settle whether approval is an event on the task or a capability of its own.
- **`scheme` (pluggable; Explored).** `broader` and `narrower` inside a vocabulary, the closure as a derived shortcut, and an edition as a succession. Only if a vocabulary with a hierarchy is actually adopted (the taxon `taxonomic_rank` is the nearest case); otherwise it is the false-taxonomy failure.
- **`label-visibility` (a rule, not a module).** Who may see an attachment, a label's existence and counts. It belongs with sensitivity and authorization; the label domain states only that visibility is a rule that facets and counts must respect.
- **`transition` contract in status (a section).** The event's name, its payload (previous value, author, request), the rule hook, and the family it belongs to (ST4). Without it a status document cannot say what a "move" is.
- **A purpose paragraph in edge (a section).** What an edge is for, which of Memar's three buckets the domain's concept sits in, and how the ends and the type are required of `EdgeModel` (finding 2, ED1 to ED3). The handoff already asks for it.

## Recommendation (unratified)
The owner has not reviewed this, and nothing in it is decided.

1. **Treat a label as an identity with no wording, defined once, taking labels of its own (Tentative, medium confidence).** Three kernel elements: identity and definition, attachment (a loop-edge typed by the label's identity), and a contract slot naming who may attach and withdraw and what an attachment triggers. Everything else takes a label or is a rule's.
2. **Read the typed loop as the attachment (A3, Tentative).** One identity is the label, the typed loop is its use, and the label may also be a vertex that other relations start from. Rewrite the label loop's Model line to say so. Hold A2 as Explored.
3. **Make "who may attach and withdraw" a stated contract per label, with a default (U2, Tentative).** Otherwise the label edge is bare by the repository's own rule. A kernel states the requirement; the rule engine decides.
4. **Make status a label kind plus a transition contract (X2, Tentative).** Keep the status document, the shared vocabulary and the transition event; remove the second attachment mechanism. Give every domain's lifecycle a kind to take, and decide R4 (where a lifecycle's folder lives) after the family question (ST4) is answered.
5. **Split the shared status vocabulary three ways (Tentative).** Flags (`draft`, `hidden`, `lock`, `read-only`, `block`, `optional`, `deleted`) stay; the derived (`edited`, a stored `loop_edge`, `Expired`, a stored current status) become shortcuts or readings; the relations to users (`request`, `suggest`, `approved`, `approval_needed`) move to an `approval` capability; domain outcomes (`failed`, the estate, request and quiddity vocabularies) go to their domains.
6. **Take the list, the board and the registries out of the kernel (Tentative).** `collection` (shared with the content research), `vocabulary` (scope, adoption, merge, split, retire), and the task domain's stage vocabulary and the GUI's projection replace `list`, `label-allowed`, `label-used_by`, `table` and `matrix`. Hold `attestation`, `approval` and `scheme` as Explored.
7. **Give the edge a purpose and the ends (Tentative).** An edge is a thing that joins two ends (equal for a loop), typed by a label, an acquired fact; an edge may be an end.
