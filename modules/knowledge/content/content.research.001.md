## Findings

### 1. What the domain is for

**The plain statement (agent, Tentative).** The content domain gives an organization one answer to four questions about anything that someone made *so that others, or they themselves later, can take it in*: *what is it* (an identity that stays the same through edits and through being shown in many places), *what is it made of* (words, pictures, sound, references to other contents), *what does it answer, quote or belong to* (its relations to other contents), and *who may do what with it* (see it, answer it, pass it on, change it, withdraw it). Everything the industry sells separately as post, comment, message, email, article, note, page, episode, review or announcement is the same question asked of different material. This restates the Decided material in plain words: content is "an independent unit attachable to one or more Timelines — the Twitter model", a comment is content plus `reply_to`, code and RFCs are content with stable identity, and a term opens a hub that describes it ([content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#decisions)).

**The demand side (what the rest of the repository already expects of content).** The domain is where six other documents send a relation they do not want to own, which is the best evidence of what it is for and also a warning that it has become a place to defer to:

| Who defers to content | What it expects | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Task | The intent is also content; the Change Set is content revisions reachable from a task | [task.research.001.md](../../management/task/task.research.001.md#proposed-kernel-agent-tentative) K2, K5 |
| Product | A discussion audience of a product "through its content identity"; Product ∩ Content on one node | [product comment](../../economics/product/edges/comment/comment.md#placement), [course](edges-loop/course/course.md) |
| Ownership | Credits per content topic (SongWriter, Composer, Director) are "content-type semantics" and the capability "must own the labels together with their processing" | [participant](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/participant/participant.md#placement) |
| Timeline | The discussion threads "are content"; the channel is structural | [chat](../../management/timeline/edges/chat/chat.md#placement), [user](../../management/timeline/edges/user/user.md#open-questions) |
| Thing | "Content rating" and "a filtering capability that acts on the rating (the knowledge/content claim)" | [picture](../../identity/thing/edges/picture/picture.md#placement) |
| Text | Per-content visibility ("a user may change her last choice ... make her content private/public many times") | [text.md](../text/text.md#idea-backlog) |
| Thing (`supersedes`) | "A domain that keeps one identity and records edits as events ... Content's current direction is that shape" | [supersedes](../../identity/thing/edges/supersedes/supersedes.md#policies-rules) |

**The word hides two things (agent, Tentative; the evidence is the Decided text itself).** The handoff says both "content is a persistent ContentUUID surviving edits, with change-events attached" and "Content Addressability is the one Git property worth keeping: identity is content-derived or immutable-at-creation" ([content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#versioning-content-identity); [text.handoff.md](../text/text.handoff.md)). They cannot both name the same thing: an identity that survives an edit is not derived from the content, and a content-derived identity changes with every edit. The Task research needs a third noun: a "content version" that a task `changes`. The proposal that follows, and that the scenarios test, is that **the domain's node is the work-level identity (the stable thing people reply to, quote, cite and pin), and that each revision of its body is a separate, immutable, addressable value that the identity points at** — the library-catalogue distinction between a work and its editions, without needing the catalogue's vocabulary. The persistent id answers "the thing we discuss"; the revision answers "exactly what it said when I approved it". That reading keeps both Decided statements true, and it matches Memar's own addressability principle: a part that needs its own relation becomes its own entity, and a revision does (a task `changes` it, an approval refers to it, a quotation can pin it).

**Scenarios.** Thirteen, from several industries and cultures. Each states what is being done, what breaks the obvious model, and what it says.

1. **A family chat message (any messenger, any country).** A grandmother sends a voice note to a family group; a cousin replies to it with a photo; the note is forwarded to a second group, shown there as "forwarded"; the sender edits a caption within minutes; the cousin deletes his reply "for everyone" and a sibling deletes it "for me" only; ticks show delivered, then seen. *Breaks:* edit as overwrite; delete as destruction (two different deletes); forward as a copy; one read state. *Says:* the unit people point at survives the edit; "delete for me" is a viewer's preference, not an event on the content; "forward" places the same content in another timeline and is not a second content; receipts are per recipient and per stage. The document set already holds the pieces under different names (the [forward](edges/forward/forward.md) TODO, the [delivered](edges/delivered/delivered.md) policy, the [user_status](widgets/user_status/protocol/dt-status.kh) ladder).
2. **A newspaper article with a correction (a daily in Tehran, London or Lagos).** The text is published under a byline; an hour later a factual correction is appended; a wire agency's version is carried by thirty papers under licence with attribution; the article is translated to two languages. *Breaks:* one author; edit as silent replacement (readers who cited the first wording must still be able to find it); one language per content. *Says:* the correction is a visible event with its own authority and reason on the same identity, the earlier wording stays addressable, and the translation is either a second body of the same content or a second content related to the first (open question 6). The syndicated copy is the same content *placed* in other timelines by another publisher, with credit, not a new work.
3. **An official gazette notice and its amendments (a state gazette, a register of laws).** A law is published; months later an amendment replaces an article; a court case must apply the text *as published on the day of the offence*. *Breaks:* "the current value is authoritative" ([content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#versioning-content-identity), Decided) is wrong for a reader who needs the as-of version; edits must be refused, only superseding allowed. *Says:* the as-published revision must be addressable by date, and a content may be *sealed* so that the only way to change the world is a new content that [supersedes](../../identity/thing/edges/supersedes/supersedes.md) it. This is the same node as the [law](../../rights/law/) record seen through the knowledge lens; content owns the text and its revisions, law owns validity (unverified: law's own documents were not read).
4. **A commentary tradition (a page of Talmud with Rashi and Tosafot, a Persian divan with its sharh, a tafsir beside a verse).** A base text; commentaries written centuries apart by different authors, each anchored to a *span* of the base text (a verse, a phrase); commentators disagree with each other; the base text itself has manuscript variants. *Breaks:* `reply_to` as a link between whole contents; reply order as time order (a later commentary sits beside the earlier one, not after it); one canonical body. *Says:* a reply may need to target a *part* of another content, and the part must be addressable ([Memar Content → Addressability](./content.md#addressability)); layered commentary is a graph of answers, not a thread; variants of a base text are revisions that nobody has decided is "the current one".
5. **A collaborative article (a wiki with thousands of editors).** Many authors; edit wars; reverts; a page protected for a week; a talk page separate from the article; an editor's contribution is attributed in the history, not in a byline. *Breaks:* a single author; the idea that "who may edit" is the creator; "last write wins". *Says:* authority to revise is a rule attached to the content (protection is a rule that can be attached and removed), every revision carries who requested and who approved ([content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#versioning-content-identity), Decided), and the conflict unit is the semantic unit, not the file (Decided, [Concurrency](content.handoff.md#concurrency-offline-multi-actor--merge-conflict--semantic-unit)): two editors changing the same section is a conflict, two changing different sections is not.
6. **A song, its album, its music video and its cover version (the music industry).** One recording appears as a track on an album, as a single, in a playlist and in a concert film; the lyrics exist in two languages; a cover is a new performance of the same composition; credits (writer, composer, producer) differ per release; the video is a separate work that "integrates a song" ([music_video](edges-loop/music_video/music_video.md)). *Breaks:* the eighteen genre loops as kinds (a recording is `music`, `album` member and `concert` recording at once); credits as a property of the kind. *Says:* "album" is an ordered collection of contents, "music" and "movie" are what the attached objects *are* (a medium read from the object's mediatype, not a label), and the same composition performed twice is two contents related by something the domain lacks. The work/expression/manifestation split of library science fits exactly what the unresolved identity question needs, and it says that translation (scenario 2) and cover version are the same shape.
7. **An email thread between two companies (SMTP).** A message with From, To, Cc, Bcc, a subject, attachments and a signature; replies chained by header; Bcc recipients invisible to others; a message arriving from outside the organization, whose bytes must not be altered because a signature covers them. *Breaks:* edit; the recipient list as text; "reply" as a single in-system action; users as the only addressable parties. *Says:* the original message is an *acquired* immutable object; From, To and Subject are *discovered* by parsing it; per-recipient visibility (Bcc) is part of delivery, not of the body. The [email policy](edges/email/email.md#policies-rules) already says "relate to real data, not the text version" but the carried data model stores strings (contradiction 6).
8. **A court filing, sealed and redacted (any judiciary).** A party files an expert report; the judge seals it; the other party receives a redacted version; later the seal is lifted for the appeal court; the chain of custody must show who held which version. *Breaks:* one body per content; visibility as a toggle on the whole; the redacted version as a copy. *Says:* the redacted version is *derived* from the original and must remain traceable to it; visibility is authority, set and lifted by identities with that authority, per audience; the same content seen by two audiences is two views, not two contents. It shares its premise with the case domain ([case.research.001.md](../../rights/case/case.research.001.md)).
9. **A brand post made by an agency, flagged as a paid partnership, and removed by moderators (social platforms).** An influencer posts under her name for a brand; the platform requires a disclosure label; an agency employee posts on behalf of the brand account; the platform hides the post for violating a rule and the poster appeals. *Breaks:* sender equals requester equals author; hide as delete; the disclosure as a free-text caption. *Says:* three identities are present and differ: the one who made the request (the employee), the one the content speaks as (the brand), and the credited parties (influencer, sponsor). The widget services already offer `ChangeSenderUser` and the `group-user` model carries a note on "Paid partnership" ([widget.kh](widgets/protocol/widget.kh), [group-user data model](widgets/group-user/protocol/data_model.kh)); the [hidden](../status/edges-loop/hidden/hidden.md) status says "by owner or justice". An appeal is a case on the content, not a state of the content.
10. **A voice note recorded offline in a low-connectivity area (rural radio-and-messenger practice in many regions).** A health worker records a voice note on a phone with no signal; a supervisor edits the same group's pinned note from a laptop; both reconnect hours later; the group's comment order must be agreed. *Breaks:* a server-assigned comment index that "needs lock" ([group data model](widgets/group/protocol/data_model.kh): "TODO: Need index or just order by time?? index need lock!"); device clocks as truth. *Says:* the Decided direction (a unique changer per entity, routed by consistent hashing of the entity identity) answers it if the *group's ordering* is an entity with one changer; the content's own creation is local and needs no lock. Time order is a presentation of the group's decided order, not of device clocks ([content.handoff.md → Concurrency](content.handoff.md#concurrency-offline-multi-actor--merge-conflict--semantic-unit)).
11. **Content that nobody wrote (a system notice and a machine-written summary).** "Omid changed the group photo" appears in a chat; a welcome message is built from a record of the group's domain; an assistant writes a summary of a hundred replies and rewrites it when more arrive. *Breaks:* an author; a stable body; the idea that every item in a timeline is content. *Says:* the first two are *events rendered into a timeline*, which the timeline domain already lists as `(timeline)-[:event_is]->(thing)` "note: LogActivity" ([event](../../management/timeline/edges/event/event.md)), duplicating the content widget [log-activity](widgets/log-activity/protocol/data_model.kh); the summary is *discovered* data (derived from the replies and recomputable) and must not be stored as if it were something someone said. Whether a machine-written text is content at all depends on whether anyone answers for it.
12. **A public consultation on a draft law (participatory legislation).** Citizens may comment on a draft; to prevent "discussion entropy" each person may reply once per article; officials may reply to anyone; counting stance per article matters more than reading every reply. *Breaks:* unlimited replies; reply as a free act of the replier. *Says:* this is the origin of [reply-limit_per_user](edges/reply-limit_per_user/reply-limit_per_user.md) and of timeline's [limit_event_per_user](../../management/timeline/edges/limit_event_per_user/limit_event_per_user.md): a limit set by the *owner of the thing replied to*, evaluated when the relation is created, with a count that is state. The constraint belongs to the constraining concern ([Constraints Belong to the Constraining Concern](../../../docs/modeling.md#constraints-belong-to-the-constraining-concern)), and the same shape appears on forward, quote and timeline events.
13. **This organization's own RFC and code module (Decided).** An RFC lives in its author's timeline and in a project's; while in review it triggers notification of designated people when related content appears; a code module is content whose identity does not depend on its file path; a task `changes` three of its revisions. *Breaks:* file and folder as identity; "RFC" as a kind. *Says:* the review phase is a *state with processing*, and it is Decided ([content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#the-orgs-own-document-model)) yet appears in no module folder (a gap; finding 6).

**What the scenarios say together (agent, Tentative).** A content is not always written by one person (5, 9), not always written by anyone (11), not edited in place (2, 3), not read in one language (2, 6), not one thing but a work with revisions (3, 6), not one body for every viewer (8), not a reply to a whole but to a part (4), not ordered by the clock (10), not a member of exactly one timeline (1, 2, 13), not always a kind of its own (6), and not restricted by its replier but by its owner (12). The kernel therefore cannot assume an author, a language, an edit-in-place, a timeline, a kind, an audience or a clock. Each is either a relation with rules or a pluggable capability.

**The second layer: the underlying problem the answers assume.** The first layer answers what was asked. The reading beneath it is that the owner's actual problem is not "how do we model a post and its comments" but "how do we stop the domain from filling up with the vocabulary of whichever product (a chat app, a blog, a mail client, a streaming service, a wiki) contributed the next kind or the next switch". Evidence: eighteen of the nineteen folders under `edges-loop/` are genres and media with no rules; seven `edges/` folders are restrictions on three interactions; three of the widgets duplicate timeline relations; and the Idea backlog proposes more switches (`forward_off`, `quote_off`, `reply_off`). A reader who holds a different reading of the problem can set the proposals aside without discarding the scenarios.

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

#### The removal test run on the content itself (agent, Tentative)
Without a content domain, what must be fabricated or bent among the remaining foundations?

- **Text** would have to carry a stable identity across edits, a place in other texts' conversations, an author's chosen sender identity and withdrawal state. Text is the value a human reads and is deliberately one thing per language ([text.md](../text/text.md#model)); giving it those would distort it, since the same words in two contexts are one text ([text.md → Locale text](../text/text.md#locale-text)).
- **Object** (and `storage`) holds bytes with a mediatype; it would have to carry replies and revisions of meaning, which are not bytes.
- **Timeline** would have to hold the thing placed in it. It is the placement surface (Decided); making it also the thing placed is the reverse dependency of rival M5 and conflicts with the Decided independent unit.
- **Task** would have to invent its own intent body and Change Set revisions; the Decided "Task is also a Content" is exactly the statement that task does not.

All four land inside the foundational set, so the guard is met: the pressure is to bend foundations, not mere inconvenience. The plausible alternative is that Content is a *lens* on Text, Object and Timeline with no node of its own (rival M3). The test shows the *work* needs a home, not that the home must be a separate type; marked Tentative for that reason.

#### Proposed kernel (agent, Tentative)
Four elements, plus the shared attachment points the rest of the repository already defines. Each is stated with its who-may-create-or-remove and what-it-triggers, because [Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement) forbids a bare edge.

| # | Element | Who may create or remove | What it triggers | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | **Identity** — a content is a thing; its identity is the thing domain's `ThingUUID`; it persists through revisions, and the same node may take other identities (a course that is also a product; a task that is also content) | any identity with authority in the organization, through a request; removal is never a delete (see finding 4) | the content exists; a `content_created` event | Decided ([comment.handoff.md](edges-loop/comment/comment.handoff.md#decisions) for the identifier; [content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#versioning-content-identity) for persistence, a decided direction; multi-typing: [course.md](edges-loop/course/course.md), confirmed) |
| K2 | **Body** — the content's parts, by reference: zero or more texts (at most one per language, always through the text domain) and zero or more objects in order (`attach`) | the creator at creation; later changes are revisions under K3 | `content_revised`; validation is the text and object owners' | Decided that human text lives only in text and is related, not copied ([text.handoff.md](../text/text.handoff.md)); the object side is [attach](edges/attach/attach.md) and the [object widget](widgets/object/protocol/data_model.kh) |
| K3 | **Revision and provenance** — each revision of the body is an immutable value; the identity points at its current revision; every revision carries who requested and who approved; withdrawal and hiding use the shared status loops | the requester with authority over the content; revisions are never removed (a correction is a new revision referencing the one it corrects) | `content_revised`, `content_withdrawn`, `content_restored`; the content exposes these as an EventTarget | Decided direction (shared cross-domain mechanism, "who requested the change, who approved it", [Versioning](content.handoff.md#versioning-content-identity)); [Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates); the status loops [deleted](../status/edges-loop/deleted/deleted.md), [hidden](../status/edges-loop/hidden/hidden.md), [edited](../status/edges-loop/edited/edited.md), [draft](../status/edges-loop/draft/draft.md), [read-only](../status/edges-loop/read-only/read-only.md) |
| K4 | **`reply_to`** `(content)-[:reply_to]->(content)` — a content answers another, or a part of it | the replying content's creator, at creation, and only to an existing content; never retargeted; removal is withdrawal of the reply, whose place in the thread stays as a tombstone | `content_replied`; the replied-to content's owner may be notified (that is a pluggable's processing) | Decided that a comment is content plus `reply_to` ([content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#relations--edge-vocabulary-content-relevant), [comment.handoff.md](edges-loop/comment/comment.handoff.md#decisions)); `reply_of` in [reply.md](edges/reply/reply.md) is the inverse reading of the same edge |

A property of K4 worth stating: because the target must exist when the reply is created and the relation is never retargeted, a cycle is impossible by construction, so unlike `part_of` in the Task proposal no cycle guard is needed. The rules that remain are about *who may reply to what* (finding 4, Rules).

Not in the kernel by this proposal: author or credits, the identity a content speaks as, placement in timelines, quote, forward, interaction limits, receipts, link previews, mentions, tags, pins, collections, genre or medium, review phase, reporting, visibility. Reactions already live outside ([reaction](../../../modules-plugable/knowledge/reaction/reaction.md): "an optional overlay on content/users"). The kernel also exposes the attachment points that other domains defined once and content must not copy ([Placing a relation](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#placing-a-relation)): thing's `supersedes`, `template` and `follow`, the shared status loops, timeline's `event_is` for placement, and the generic `condition_is` through which a rule attaches.

#### Pluggable: the capability each would own (agent, Tentative)
Each row: the relationship and its processing together; who may create or remove it; what it triggers. A pluggable carries a capability name, not a domain name ([Modules → Placement](../../modules.md#placement)). Confidence is marked per row.

| Capability | Relationship | Processing | Who may create/remove | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **quoting** (Tentative) | `(content)-[:quote]->(content or revision)` | shows the quoted body inside the quoting one; decides pinned-revision versus live-latest; shows a tombstone when the quoted content is withdrawn; checks the quoted owner's quote restriction | the quoting content's creator at creation; ends only with the quoting content | `content_quoted` to the quoted owner; "who quoted this" is discovered |
| **redistribution** (Tentative; Explored whether it is timeline's) | placing an existing content in another timeline, with provenance (`forwarded from`) and a `silent` flag | creates the timeline event with reason `Reason_FriendForward`, marks provenance, honours the owner's forward restriction, refuses silent redistribution where the owner forbids it | an identity allowed to place into the target timeline; the placer may remove its own placement | an `event_is` in the target timeline; a notification to the original owner |
| **interaction-limits** (Tentative) | an owner-set restriction attached through `condition_is` to a *kind of interaction* (reply, quote, forward, timeline event) on a content or on every content of a group: audience (anyone, mentioned users, a label, per-user count) | evaluates at the moment the interaction would be created; keeps the per-user count; refuses with a recorded reason | the owner of the content, or the group's administrators for a whole group; removal takes effect for new interactions only | `interaction_refused` with reason; nothing else |
| **receipts** (Tentative) | `(user)-[:delivered\|seen\|read]->(content)` as one ladder `delivered < seen < read`, plus timeline's `read_until` cursor as its compact form | creates the per-content facts from a cursor on demand; computes counts per group; honours a user's privacy preference by not creating the fact | the recipient's client (acquired), never the sender; never removed (a privacy setting stops *creation*) | updated unread counts; sender-side status; a metered view count |
| **link-preview** (Tentative) | `(content)-[:preview_link]->(uri)` plus a stored rendering | fetches the page, renders a safe snapshot, stores it as discovered data, refreshes or drops it; protects the fetcher (the organization's servers fetching arbitrary addresses) | the system when a body contains a reference, or the author supplying one; the author may remove the preview | a fetch job; a recorded failure; removal on withdrawal |
| **mentions** (Tentative) | `(content)-[:mentions]->(user)` — the resolved relation, not the `@name` token | resolves tokens to identities at save; creates the relation; grants the access the owner's restrictions give to "mentioned users" | the content's author at creation or revision; ends with the revision that drops it | `user_mentioned` (notification reason `Reason_UserMentioned`); an input to interaction-limits |
| **attribution** (Tentative) | `(content)-[:credited]->(thing)` with a role: author, translator, editor, composer, director, sponsor | holds the role vocabulary per kind of content as configuration; checks a required-roles rule; shows disclosure where a rule demands it | the content's creator or publisher; removal is an event with a reason (a dispute) | a credit list change; downstream (royalty, disclosure) is other capabilities' |
| **collection** (Explored) | `(collection)-[:contains {position}]->(content)` | ordered, curated membership; position changes; new-item notification to followers (thing's `follow`) | the curator; removal by the curator | `collection_extended`; ordering events |
| **review-phase** (Decided as a need; Tentative as a module) | `(content)-[:in_phase]->(phase)` where the phase is a review phase | while in the phase it runs the automatic behaviours the phase declares, for example notifying designated people when related content appears | the content's owner opens a phase; the reviewers close it | `review_opened`, `review_notification_sent`, `review_closed` |
| **channel-import** (Tentative) | `(content)-[:email]->(object)` and the same shape for other channels: the acquired raw artifact | parses the artifact into discovered fields, creates the in-system parties, threads by subject; keeps the raw bytes immutable | the importing identity; the raw artifact is never removed | `content_imported`; parse failures recorded |
| **topic** (Explored) | `(content)-[:about]->(text term)` — hashtags and tags as a relation to a term | indexes terms; builds the hub the term opens ("a term opens a hub that describes it", Decided) | the author (acquired) or the system by parsing (discovered), recorded as different facts | index updates |
| **reporting** (Explored; owner unverified) | `(user)-[:reported]->(content)` | opens a review with the justice or detection capabilities, may hide, tracks the appeal | any user with access to the content; the reviewing authority closes | `content_reported`; hide or restore; a case on the content when appealed |

Two things are deliberately *not* proposed as modules because the graph already holds them: a **thread** is the set of contents reachable through `reply_to` from a root (discovered data; a shortcut at most), and its auto-archive after inactivity ([thread data model](widgets/thread/protocol/data_model.kh)) is a rule attached to the group; and **pin** and **mute** already exist on timeline ([pin](../../management/timeline/edges/pin/pin.md), [mute](../../management/timeline/edges/mute/mute.md)) and must not be copied into content (contradiction 7).

### 3. Rival models

Marks: **Tentative** (leaning), **Explored** (explored, unresolved), **Deferred**.

#### The models
- **M1 — content as a record (the status quo reading; Explored).** A content is a table row with its own fields: body, author, time, parent, group. This is how the widget data models read: a `DataModel` of `CommentID`, `RequestID` and an audit time, with the comment text in a separate text domain but the structure of the thing fixed by the Go interfaces ([widgets](widgets/)). *For:* matches every carried file; no restructuring. *Against:* it is the [implementation mirror](../../../docs/modeling.practice.md#common-modeling-anti-patterns) (the structure matches a database shape more than the domain); an edit is an overwrite unless a version mechanism is bolted on (the group model does exactly that: "each comment is immutable record and so use version mechanism to chain comments"); every kind of content needs its own record.
- **M2 — identity, body by reference, revisions as events (Tentative, recommended).** The four-element kernel of finding 2: a stable identity, a body made of references to text and objects, immutable revisions with a provenance chain, and `reply_to`. Everything else is a pluggable capability or an existing domain. *For:* every Decided statement holds at once (persistent identity, text only in the text domain, comment is content plus `reply_to`, current value authoritative with a request/approval chain); a revision is an addressable value, so a task can `change` it and a quotation can pin it; kinds, switches and receipts become capabilities with an owner. *Against:* it asks two things the owner has not ratified, that a revision is an addressable entity and that the body is a reference list; it adds one noun (the revision) to the vocabulary; and it makes the "same UUID for text" idea in the backlog a no.
- **M3 — content as a lens over text and objects (shared identity; Explored).** A content has no node of its own: any text or object placed in a timeline *is* content, and the content id is the text's id ("if a comment wants `text`, it MUST use the same UUID", [content.md](content.md#idea-backlog)); multi-typing is the mechanism, as it is for Product ∩ Content ([product.md](../../economics/product/product.md)). *For:* no new identity; matches the backlog and Memar's "unclassified content — a base node". *Against:* a text's identity is unique per language and per text ([text.md](../text/text.md#model)), so a content with two languages, or with a picture and a caption, or with a revision history, has no single text to share an id with; it makes identity depend on the *shape* of the body; and the edit story (scenario 2) needs a stable thing that is not the text. Fits only the simplest case (one text, no object).
- **M4 — content as an immutable utterance (Explored).** Every edit is a new content that `supersedes` the previous one; nothing is ever revised ([group data model](widgets/group/protocol/data_model.kh) points this way). *For:* tamper-evident; reuses thing's `supersedes`; fits the gazette (scenario 3). *Against:* it contradicts the Decided direction (persistent identity with events); every reply, quote, pin, reaction and task link then refers to a superseded content and must follow the chain or be re-pointed; "current value authoritative" becomes a query. M2 absorbs its benefit: a *revision* is immutable, so tamper-evidence is kept without moving the identity.
- **M5 — content as an entry in a timeline (Deferred).** A content exists only as an event in some timeline; no standalone content. *For:* the Twitter model read literally; one placement relation. *Against:* the Decided statement that content is an "independent unit attachable to one or more Timelines"; and it makes Timeline primitive, which the handoff leaves open. Deferred until the timeline question is answered.

#### How each model meets the scenarios (agent judgment)
Ok = handled without distortion; strain = handled with a convention or an unratified assumption; fails = needs a fabricated or bent node.

| Scenario | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 chat: edit, forward, receipts | strain (edit as overwrite) | ok | strain | ok for edit, strain for replies | ok |
| 2 correction, syndication, translation | fails (edit) | ok (revision, placement, translation open) | fails (two languages, one id) | strain (chain) | strain |
| 3 gazette, as-of version | fails | ok (sealed, supersede) | fails | ok | strain |
| 4 anchored commentary | fails | ok with anchors (finding 6) | fails | strain | fails |
| 5 collaborative article | fails | ok | fails | strain | strain |
| 6 song, album, video | fails (kinds) | ok with collection and attribution | fails | strain | strain |
| 7 email thread | strain | ok (raw object + discovered fields) | strain | ok | strain |
| 8 sealed, redacted filing | strain | ok (derived revision, audience rule) | fails | strain | fails |
| 9 brand post, three identities | fails | ok (request kernel, attribution) | fails | ok | strain |
| 10 offline voice note | strain | ok (single changer for group order) | strain | ok | ok |
| 11 machine-written | strain | ok (events and discovered data are not content) | strain | strain | ok |
| 12 consultation limits | strain | ok (interaction-limits) | strain | strain | strain |
| 13 RFC and code | fails (file identity) | ok | strain | ok | strain |

Reading: M1 survives only the cases that look like a feed. M3 fails exactly where the body has more than one part or one language. M4 meets most cases but pays on every reference. M5 depends on an unanswered question. M2 meets every case at the price of two unratified readings (revision as an addressable entity; body as references).

#### Smaller choice 1: quote and forward, one capability or two (Tentative: two)
The Twitter pattern the handoff cites has two acts: a retweet places *the same* content in another timeline, a quote tweet creates *a new content that cites* it. The first adds no content, the second does. They also differ in their rules (the owner's forward restriction versus the owner's quote restriction), in provenance ("forwarded from" is a mark on a placement), and in processing. A forward without text is the answer to the forward TODO "Just one situation as SilentForwardable ... Don't add comment detail" ([forward.md](edges/forward/forward.md)): a silent forward is not a content, it is an event in a timeline. Counter-argument: one "reuse" capability with a mode keeps one place to look; against it, [Modules](../../modules.md#placement) says "a difference in processing semantics may be a distinct module".

#### Smaller choice 2: author, requester, sender and credited, one notion or four (Tentative: split)
The documents use "who" four ways: the *requester* of the creating request (every widget model carries `RequestID`, the Decided "who requested the change, who approved it"), the *sender* the content speaks as (`ChangeSenderUser` in the widget services), the *credited* parties (ownership's content participants), and the *owner* of the home timeline (the Twitter model: a tweet in "its author's timeline"). Scenario 9 needs all four to differ. The proposal: the kernel keeps only the request chain (provenance); the sender is the home placement's owner, which timeline already records; credits are the attribution capability. If the owner prefers an explicit `authored_by`, it is one more kernel edge; the question is open question 5.

#### Smaller choice 3: a limit as many edges or one capability (Tentative: one)
The existing shape is seven folders (the survey counted eight) of the form `(content)-[:x_limit_to_y]->(…)` plus `(x_limit_to_y)-[:preference_for]->(x)`, an edge that points at another edge kind ([reply-limit_to_user](edges/reply-limit_to_user/reply-limit_to_user.md)). Three of those are `to_user` ("just mentioned users"), three `to_label`, one `per_user` count, and the edge kinds differ only by the interaction named. [Modules](../../modules.md#placement): "A difference in parameters is configuration inside one module". The survey proposed the same ("one shared preference edge ... 8 folders → one module", [knowledge report](../../../temp/sessions/20261014T071356Z-7572-0/report.md)). Timeline's two limit edges (`limit_event_per_user`, `limit_max_event`) have the same shape one level up, which suggests the capability attaches to any relation, not only to content's. Counter-argument: the per-user count is state, so "to_user" and "per_user" are not the same processing; they may be two modules.

### 4. What the domain must own, as requirements questions

The domain, once decided, has to answer each of these. Questions are written to favour no answer where unsettled; a settled item says so and cites its decision.

#### Identity
- *Settled:* a content's identity is the thing domain's identity ([comment.handoff.md](edges-loop/comment/comment.handoff.md#decisions), Decided); a comment declares none of its own.
- Does the identity stay the same when the body is edited, when a translation is added, when the content is placed in a second timeline, when it is quoted, when it takes a second identity (becomes a product), when it is sealed? Which of these is a new identity?
- Is a revision an identity of its own (addressable, referable by a task or an approval) or only a position in a sequence? If it has one, is it content-derived (a hash, which the text handoff favours) or a counter?
- Is the same body in two languages one content with two texts, or two contents related by translation? ([text.md](../text/text.md#model) makes the texts two; nothing says what the content is.)
- If one identity is at once a content, a task, a product and a group (scenarios 6, 9 of the Task and Product researches), which operations of each may reach it, and what removes one role without ending the others? The unique-across-all-domains wording of the identifier and the multi-typing rule need reconciling (contradiction 3).
- May an external message (scenario 7) become a content in the organization, and whose identity does it carry?
- Who may mint a content: any member, a role, a rule, an importing process?

#### Lifecycle
- What states does a content have, and which are positions in a lifecycle (draft, published, sealed, withdrawn) as against moderation (hidden by justice), access (read-only, locked) or phase (in review)? The shared loops [draft](../status/edges-loop/draft/draft.md), [edited](../status/edges-loop/edited/edited.md), [hidden](../status/edges-loop/hidden/hidden.md), [deleted](../status/edges-loop/deleted/deleted.md), [lock](../status/edges-loop/lock/lock.md) and [read-only](../status/edges-loop/read-only/read-only.md) cover some; none covers *sealed* or *published*.
- Is `edited` a stored marker (with `LastModificationTime`, as the loop's Methods say) or discovered from the revision list? If the revision list exists, the marker is a shortcut.
- What does *delete* mean: soft delete by the author, hide by a moderator, removal for one viewer only, or erasure of the body? ([deleted](../status/edges-loop/deleted/deleted.md) says "Soft Deleted"; the Decided history principle says "nothing lost"; the message scenario needs a deletion that is actually a deletion.) Which of these leaves a tombstone so replies keep their place?
- Is a published content immutable, or may its author keep editing? Is there a deadline after which edits are refused (the "edit within minutes" practice) and who sets it?
- May a withdrawn content be restored, and by whom?
- When a content is withdrawn, what happens to its replies, its quotes, its placements in other timelines and its reactions? Do they follow the content, stay with a tombstone, or go?
- When the only author leaves the organization, what is the content?

#### Authorization
- Who may create a content in which timeline, for whom (as the organization's account), and as which identity? Does a content created "for" another need the other's acceptance?
- Who may revise (the creator only, the creator's role, any member of the group, a moderator), who approves, and is approval itself an authority different from revision?
- Who may read a content; may the answer differ per viewer (sealed filings, Bcc, "close friends")? Is it per-content access control re-checked at each query, which the [text idea](../text/text.md#idea-backlog) leaves unresolved, or a property of the placement?
- Who may reply, quote, forward, mention, react: the interacting identity, or the content's owner, or the group's administrators? Is a restriction set by the owner of the content, or by the owner of the place (the group)? (The Idea backlog's `[group, reply_off, ---]` forms say the group; the existing limit edges say the content.)
- Who may set the visibility (public, private, restricted), and may it flip many times? What does flipping do to copies already delivered?
- Across organizations: may a content in one organization be placed, quoted or followed from another, under whose authorization and accounting? ([follow](../../identity/thing/edges/follow/follow.md) already answers this for facts; a content is a thing and so may be followed.)
- Where does the authority to hide come from when it is "justice" (a law, a case) and not a person ([delegate](../../rights/delegate/delegate.md) holds the authority-versus-permission question)?

#### Rules
- Which rules does the domain itself need, and which belong to organizations: reply only to content in the same group; reply only to content one can see; one reply per person per target; maximum body length and allowed media (text and object own the checks); required credits per kind; required disclosure; edit window; protection; embargo (a content that must not be visible before a time)?
- Where each rule attaches: through `condition_is` on the content, on the group, on the interaction, or in the module that owns the capability? The group-level `reply_off` forms and the content-level limit edges disagree about the level.
- Who may attach or remove each rule, and what does removal leave of what the rule already produced (a limit lifted after refusals)?
- Is the "mentioned users only" audience a rule input (the limit edges use it), which makes a mention an authorization fact and not only a notification?

#### Events
- Which occurrences are events the content exposes as an EventTarget ([Modularity → Event as a Module Capability](../../../docs/modularity.md#event-as-a-module-capability)): created, revised, replied, quoted, mentioned, placed, withdrawn, restored, sealed, reported, moved to a review phase, delivered, seen, read? The widgets already declare event targets for group, group-user and mentioned ([ss-event_target.kh](widgets/group/protocol/ss-event_target.kh)).
- Who consumes them: notification, the timeline feed, analytics, the review capability, the rules engine, task (Change Set)?
- Which of these is a request or response that already exists and needs no event type of its own?
- A correction (a wrongly worded notice, a mistaken withdrawal) is a new revision or event referencing the one it corrects, never a rewrite ([Modeling State Change as Events](../../../docs/modeling.md#modeling-state-change-as-events-not-destructive-updates)). Does the content domain accept that as stated?
- Group-level ordering: is the sequence of contents in a group an event stream with one changer (the Decided concurrency direction), and is its index acquired or discovered?

### 6. Missing edges and modules

Each is one paragraph, as a sketch: not a design, and none is to be built before the owner decides.

1. **Documented kernel (Tentative).** The domain document has no definition, no Model and no Policies. The first missing thing is not an edge but the paragraph defining the node (finding 1) and the four-element kernel with their who and trigger. Nothing else in the list can be placed without it.
2. **`revision` (kernel value; Tentative).** An immutable value of the body at one moment, with the requester, the approver, a reason, and the revision it corrects. The content points at its current revision; a task's `changes`, an approval, a quotation or a court's "as published" cite a revision. It is not a new entity of the thing domain unless the owner decides a revision needs relations of its own beyond those (which the scenarios say it does); the identifier question is open question 2.
3. **`attribution` (pluggable; Tentative).** `(content)-[:credited]->(thing)` with a role. The role vocabulary is configuration per kind of content (SongWriter, Composer, Director from [participant](../../rights/ownership/edges-loop/participant/participant.md)); a rule may require roles (a film needs a director) or a disclosure (a paid partnership). It replaces the ownership file that says "strip". Created by the creator or publisher; a removal is an event with a reason. Its processing is the checks and the credit list; payments or royalties are another capability's.
4. **`collection` (pluggable; Explored).** Ordered, curated membership of contents (album, serial, course, book, playlist). The label domain's `list` already covers unordered membership by an owner; the missing part is *position* and the notification of followers when a member is added. It may be a property of `list` and not a module; open question 10.
5. **`review-phase` (pluggable; Tentative).** The Decided "RFC is a review phase a content can be in, ... the system runs automatic behaviours" ([content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#the-orgs-own-document-model)) has no folder. A phase relation and the processing that notifies designated people when related content appears and closes the phase on a verdict. Documentation's own Status is the nearest existing mechanism; the relation of the two is open.
6. **`anchor` / annotation (Explored).** A reply (or a quote, or a credit) that targets a *part* of a content: a span of text, a region of an image, a time range of a recording. Memar's addressability principle says such a part becomes an addressable entity; the text domain has an `anchor_text` loop-edge (unverified: not read). The sketch: a part is addressed by `(content or revision, locator)` and a reply may name it; whether the locator is a new entity or a field of `reply_to` is the open point. Scenario 4 needs it; no other scenario does.
7. **`redistribution` (pluggable; Tentative).** Sketched in finding 2. Its missing piece is the provenance mark ("forwarded from") and the owner's rule that forbids it; the placement itself already exists in timeline.
8. **`reporting` (pluggable; Explored, owner unverified).** `(user)-[:reported]->(content)` with a reason, a review, a hide or restore and an appeal. The widget services list a `Report()`, the status `hidden` says "by owner or justice", and detection and justice pluggables exist; the missing thing is who owns the report relation and the appeal. Probably the justice side ([case](../../rights/case/case.md): "a company such as a social network that resolves disputes among members"); unverified.
9. **`visibility` (rule family; Explored).** Whether a content may be seen by whom, with the audiences public, restricted, mentioned, and per-viewer views of one content (scenario 8). It is an authorization rule attached through `condition_is`, evaluated by [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md) or [access-control](../../../modules-plugable/management/access-control/access-control.md), whose documents flag their own overlap. The content domain owes the attachment point and the *derived view* relation (a redacted revision linked to its original).
10. **Withdrawal semantics (a status question, Explored).** A tombstone: a withdrawn content keeps its identity, its place in `reply_to` chains and its provenance, while its body is closed to viewers by rule. Erasure of the body is a separate, authority-gated event that keeps the fact that it happened. This is a question of vocabulary for the shared status home (`deleted` carries `DeleterID`; `hidden` carries "by owner or justice") and of the retention duty; open question 7.

### 7. Test scenarios

Given/when/then in prose, so another session can write tests once the owner decides. Each is marked with what it depends on: **kept** if it follows from settled text, **M2** if it assumes the recommended model, **Q n** if it assumes the default of open question n.

1. **A comment is content plus `reply_to` (kept).** Given a content A. When someone creates content B with `reply_to` A. Then B is a content with a `ThingUUID` of its own and the `reply_to` relation to A, B has no type beyond that relation, and nothing declares a `CommentUUID`.
2. **A reply to a non-existent content is refused (M2).** Given no content with id X. When a reply to X is attempted. Then it is refused and nothing is created.
3. **A reply cannot be retargeted (M2).** Given reply B to A. When someone tries to make B reply to C. Then it is refused; the way to change the target is to withdraw B and create a new reply, which leaves the first as a tombstone.
4. **A cycle is impossible (M2).** Given any set of replies. When all are created in order. Then none can reply to a later one; no cycle guard exists or is needed.
5. **An edit keeps the identity and the first wording (M2, Q 1).** Given a content with one revision. When its creator saves a new body. Then the content has the same id, two revisions, a requester on each, and the first revision is still reachable by its own address; reads default to the current revision (Decided: current value authoritative).
6. **A task cites a revision, not a moving target (M2, Q 1).** Given a task whose `changes` names revision 2 of a content. When the content gets revision 3. Then the task still shows revision 2; "all states related to the task" is a walk over `changes`.
7. **A correction is a new revision referencing the old (kept: Modeling State Change as Events).** Given a wrong revision. When the author corrects it. Then a new revision is created that names the one it corrects; nothing is rewritten.
8. **A sealed content refuses edits and accepts only a successor (M2; the sealed state is a missing vocabulary item, finding 6 item 10).** Given a gazette content that is sealed. When someone tries to edit it. Then it is refused; when an authorized identity publishes an amendment, a new content `supersedes` it and the first stays readable "as published".
9. **Body text is always a relation (kept: text only in the text domain).** Given a content with a caption. When the caption is stored. Then the content holds a reference to a text and no string of its own; two languages give two texts and neither shares the content's identity (Q 2).
10. **An empty content is refused (M2).** Given a creation request with no text and no object. When it is submitted. Then it is refused (a silent share is a placement, not a content).
11. **A forward adds no content (M2, Q 3).** Given content A in group G1. When a member of G2 forwards it. Then A appears as an event in G2's timeline with provenance, no new content exists, and the original owner is notified.
12. **A quote adds a content (M2).** Given content A. When someone writes B with a quote of A. Then B is a new content with A inside it; if A is later withdrawn, B shows a tombstone where A was; if A is edited, B shows what the quoting rule chose (pinned or live) and the choice is stated.
13. **A reply limit is evaluated by the owner's rule (Q 15).** Given content A restricted to "mentioned users only". When an unmentioned user replies. Then the reply is refused with a recorded reason; when A mentions that user in a new revision, the same reply succeeds.
14. **One reply per person (kept in spirit: reply-limit_per_user; Q 15).** Given a consultation article with a "one reply per user" limit. When the same user replies twice. Then the second is refused; an official exempted by the rule may reply any number of times.
15. **A limit lifted does not rewrite history (Q 15).** Given three refusals recorded under a limit. When the owner removes the limit. Then the refusals stay in history and new interactions are accepted.
16. **Receipts form a ladder (M2, Q 8).** Given a message delivered to three recipients. When one sees it and a second reads it. Then each recipient has the highest stage reached, the sender sees counts per stage, and a user with receipts turned off creates no fact beyond delivered.
17. **A cursor implies earlier reads (Q 8).** Given a group where a user's `read_until` points at message 40. When the user's unread count is asked. Then messages up to 40 count as read without per-message facts being required.
18. **Delivered stays client-held (kept: delivered policy).** Given a delivery. When the server's stored facts are listed. Then no per-recipient `delivered` row exists on the server.
19. **An email is stored raw and parsed separately (Q 13).** Given an incoming message. When it is imported. Then the raw bytes are an immutable object, From, To and Subject are derived fields that can be recomputed, Bcc recipients are not visible to other recipients, and the parties are in-system identities where they exist.
20. **A machine-written summary is not stored as something said (M2, Q 16).** Given a summary generated from a hundred replies. When ten more replies arrive and it is regenerated. Then no revision of an authored content exists for the summary; it is a derived view with no author.
21. **A system notice is an event, not a content (Q 16).** Given a group photo change. When the notice appears in the timeline. Then it is a timeline event pointing at the record and its version, and no content with an author was created.
22. **Hiding by justice is not deleting by the author (M2).** Given a content hidden by a moderator. When its author asks to delete it. Then both facts are kept, the content is not visible to viewers, and restoring requires the hider's authority.
23. **Withdrawal keeps the thread (Q 7).** Given a content with eight replies. When it is withdrawn. Then the eight replies keep their `reply_to` and show a tombstone where it was.
24. **Sender is not requester (Q 5).** Given an employee posting as the company account. When the content is created. Then the request chain names the employee, the content speaks as the company, and a reader sees the company; an audit shows both.
25. **A disclosure is required by rule (Q 19).** Given a rule that sponsored content needs a credited sponsor. When a content is submitted with a paid-partnership flag and no sponsor credit. Then it is refused with the rule named.
26. **Group order has one changer (kept: concurrency direction; Q 4).** Given two devices that created contents offline for the same group. When both reconnect. Then the group's order is decided by the group's single changer, not by the devices' clocks, and both contents exist.
27. **An edit conflict is a semantic one (kept).** Given two editors changing the same section of one content concurrently. When both save. Then a conflict is recorded for that section; given they changed different sections, no conflict exists.
28. **An annotation targets a part (Explored, Q 12).** Given a base text and a commentary anchored to one verse. When the base text gets a new revision with that verse unchanged. Then the commentary still points at that verse; if the verse changes, the commentary is shown as pointing at the earlier revision.
29. **A redacted view traces to its original (Q 11).** Given a sealed filing and a redacted version. When the other party asks who produced the redaction and from what. Then the answer is the derived-from link to the original revision and the identity that produced it.
30. **Medium is read, not stored (kept: acquired versus discovered).** Given a content with a video object. When asked whether it is a video. Then the answer comes from the object's mediatype; no `movie` marker was stored.

### Contradictions found

Reported, not resolved, as asked. Each cites both sides.

1. **A reply without a group, and a reply only inside a group.** [reply.md](edges/reply/reply.md): a reply "MUST not be forced to be relate to a `group` too", and one that is not in a group "is standalone `thread`". [widgets/reply data model](widgets/reply/protocol/data_model.kh): "Comment can be reply to other comment just in the same group". The comment handoff leaves "may a comment be a reply across groups" open and the catalogue default (F4) is "no cross-group replies" ([comment.handoff.md](edges-loop/comment/comment.handoff.md#open-questions); [catalogue](../../../temp/sessions/20261014T071404Z-26308-0/report.md)). The group storage can answer "which groups does this comment belong to" with a list (`FIND_GroupIDs_BY_CommentID`, [group storage](widgets/group/protocol/storage.kh)), which makes the same-group rule ambiguous.
2. **Three edit models.** Persistent id with change events (Decided direction, [content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#versioning-content-identity)); each comment an immutable record chained by a version mechanism ([group data model](widgets/group/protocol/data_model.kh)); in-place edit marked by `edited` with `LastModificationTime` and `LastModifierId` ([edited](../status/edges-loop/edited/edited.md)).
3. **Content-derived identity versus persistent identity; one id per domain versus multi-typing.** "Identity is independent of location: content-derived or immutable-at-creation stable reference — Content Addressability is the one Git property worth keeping" ([text.handoff.md](../text/text.handoff.md)) against a persistent ContentUUID that survives edits ([content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#versioning-content-identity)). Also: the thing identifier "MUST be unique ID in all domains" (the converted `dt-uuid.kh`, per [comment.handoff.md](edges-loop/comment/comment.handoff.md#decisions)) against "Each UUID can use in more than one vertex category" ([modules.md](../../modules.md#top-or-thing-levels-labels)) and the Decided multi-typing of Product ∩ Content ([course](edges-loop/course/course.md)). Finding 1 proposes a reconciliation (work-level id plus revision value); it does not apply it.
4. **Forward is a content-to-content edge, and it is a placement in a timeline.** [forward.md](edges/forward/forward.md) Model is `(comment)-[:forward]->(comment)`; its TODO says "forward is just put a comment to other `timeline`!"; the limit documents say "embed this comment in other `timeline`" ([forward-limit_to_label](edges/forward-limit_to_label/forward-limit_to_label.md)); timeline's [event](../../management/timeline/edges/event/event.md) already records `Reason_FriendForward` ("retweet or forward other comment to the user timeline"). The quote limits say "embed this comment in other `comment`s".
5. **A `to_label` edge that points at a user.** [forward-limit_to_label](edges/forward-limit_to_label/forward-limit_to_label.md) and [quote-limit_to_label](edges/quote-limit_to_label/quote-limit_to_label.md) have Model `(comment)-[:…_limit_to_label]->(user)`, while [reply-limit_to_label](edges/reply-limit_to_label/reply-limit_to_label.md) points at `(label)`. Also, `preference_for` targets an *edge kind*, an edge-on-edge shape that [Modules](../../modules.md#placement) has no bucket for.
6. **The email policy and the email data model.** [email.md](edges/email/email.md): "relate in real system data not text version of it e.g. `comment` relate to `user` not `username text`". [email data_model.kh](edges/email/protocol/data_model.kh): `From() string`, `To() string`, `CC() string`, `BCC() string`, `Subject() string` (with "TODO::: email subject force to make new comment and save all emails as reply to it"). Both are residue.
7. **Pin in three places.** [widgets/pin](widgets/pin/protocol/data_model.kh) (`CommentPinedID` in a group, unpin by a hidden status), timeline's [pin](../../management/timeline/edges/pin/pin.md) (`(timeline)-[:pin_is]->(thing)` "any quiddity like comment, product"), and the pin GUI widget. One relation defined twice, which [A Relation Several Domains Need Is Defined Once](../../../docs/modeling.md#a-relation-several-domains-need-is-defined-once) names.
8. **Three representations of one reading fact.** [read](edges/read/read.md), [seen](edges/seen/seen.md) and [delivered](edges/delivered/delivered.md) as three edges with different directions; [user_status](widgets/user_status/protocol/dt-status.kh) as one ladder `Delivered, Seen, Read`; timeline's [read_until](../../management/timeline/edges/read_until/read_until.md) as a cursor. `delivered` says "MUST store just in client not servers due to unneeded index" while the widget has a server storage service ([user_status storage](widgets/user_status/protocol/storage.kh)). `read` and `seen` have no policy to tell them apart.
9. **"Comment is not an independent entity", and sixteen edge folders that name `comment` as the node.** Decided: a comment is content plus `reply_to` ([content.handoff.md](content.handoff.md#relations--edge-vocabulary-content-relevant)). ## Recommendation (unratified)
The owner has not reviewed this, and nothing in it is decided.

1. **Define the node as the work-level identity of a communicated articulation, with revisions as immutable addressable values (Tentative, medium confidence).** This keeps both Decided statements about identity true and gives a task, an approval and a quotation something to point at. It changes the empty first paragraph of [content.md](content.md) and nothing else visible.
2. **Keep a four-element kernel (Tentative):** identity, body by reference, revision and provenance, `reply_to`; plus the generic `condition_is` attachment and the shared status loops. Everything else is a capability, a rule, or an existing domain.
3. **Choose M2; keep M4's immutability inside revisions, treat M3 as a no, and defer M5 (Tentative).** A shared UUID with the text domain is rejected for any content with more than one text or one object.
4. **Split forward from quote; make forward a timeline event with provenance (Tentative).** A silent forward is not a content.
5. **Merge the seven limit folders into one capability that attaches to any relation, and the three read-state folders into one ladder (Tentative).** Name them by what they do (interaction-limits, receipts), not by the interaction.
6. **Stop minting genre loops (Tentative).** Medium is read from objects; album, serial and episode are collection membership; the editorial forms wait for rules; `confidential` is visibility. No folder is to be removed on this evidence.
7. **Treat the widgets as a projection with duplicated relations (Tentative).** `pin`, `log-activity`, `thread` and `group` already live, or are derivable, elsewhere; `mentioned`, `user_status` and `tag` map to capabilities.
8. **Do not move, delete or convert anything on this evidence.** The mapping in finding 5 is a reading list for the sessions that will, after the owner answers. `ContentModel` stays unwritten ([Khayyam rule 6](../../../temp/Khayyam.practice.md#governing-rules): an empty abstraction is a design smell).
9. **Ask the three questions that change what the work is first:** question 1 (what is the node, and how do persistent and content-derived identity meet), question 2 (is the body by reference, or the shared UUID of the backlog), and question 3 (does placement in a group belong to timeline).

## Open Questions
This is the research's eighth section. Only the owner can decide these. Ordered by what they change: those that change what the domain is first, then those that change how a part is done ([Ask rather than assume](../../../docs/cognition.md#discourse-norms-derived-from-this-model)). Each carries a recommended default and what it blocks. They are also entered in the [handoff](content.handoff.md#research-001-questions-and-next-steps-2026-10-15).

1. **Is the content the work-level identity with immutable revisions below it, and how do the persistent id and the "content-derived" identity of the text handoff meet?** Default: the node is the persistent identity; each revision is an immutable value with a content-derived address; the persistent id answers "the thing we discuss", the revision answers "exactly what was said". Blocks: the definition in [content.md](content.md), the Task proposal's `changes` target, the as-published reading of laws, any `ContentModel`.
2. **Does a content share the text's UUID (the Idea backlog) or hold the text by a relation?** Default: a relation; a content with zero texts, several texts, a picture or revisions cannot share one. Blocks: the backlog item, the text-ownership convergence in [text.handoff.md](../text/text.handoff.md), K2.
3. **Is placing a content in a group's ordered list the timeline's `event_is`, and is forward a placement with provenance?** Default: yes to both; `widgets/group`, `widgets/log-activity` and `widgets/pin` retire into timeline. Blocks: Timeline primitivity (handoff open question 1), the forward and pin folders, the group index lock question.
4. **Who orders a group's contents: a single changer for the group, or time?** Default: a single changer per group (the Decided direction); time is a presentation. Blocks: the group data model's TODO, offline creation (scenario 10), test 26.
5. **Who is "the author": the requester, the identity the content speaks as, the credited parties, or the owner of the home timeline?** Default: the kernel keeps only the request chain; the speaking identity is the home placement's owner; credits are attribution. Blocks: the `ChangeSenderUser` service, ownership's participant file, authorship rights, scenario 9.
6. **Is the same body in two languages one content with two texts, or two contents related by translation?** Default: one content, one text per language, because the thing people reply to is the same. Blocks: translation handling, the text domain's translation edges, scenario 2.
7. **What does delete mean, and what survives?** Default: delete is the `deleted` status and a rule closing the body; the content keeps identity, place in `reply_to`, and provenance (a tombstone); erasure of the body is a separate authority-gated event that keeps the fact that it happened; "delete for me" is a viewer preference. Blocks: the `deleted` loop's methods, retention duty, the thread-keeping test, scenario 1.
8. **Do read, seen and delivered merge into one ladder, and what separates seen from read?** Default: one capability, ladder `delivered < seen < read`; seen means shown on the viewer's screen, read means opened or acknowledged; `delivered` stays client-held; the cursor is the compact form. Blocks: the three edge folders, `user_status`, the privacy setting.
9. **Do the genre loops stay, and which?** Default: none promoted now; medium is derived from objects; album, serial and episode go to collection; the editorial forms wait for rules; `game`, `sport_game`, `concert` leave as not-content; `confidential` becomes visibility. Blocks: the 19 folders, the Placement sections the survey asks for (T7).
10. **Does collection live in label's `list` (with a position) or as a content module?** Default: extend `list` with a position and keep content free of a module until the album case needs more. Blocks: album, serial, serial-episode, course, and the playlist wording in [list](../label/edges/list/list.md).
11. **Is a derived view (a redaction, a translation of a revision) a revision of the same content or a separate content with a derived-from link?** Default: a separate view with a derived-from link, so access can differ. Blocks: scenario 8, sealed and redacted filings, test 29.
12. **Does a reply or a credit target a part of a content, and is the part an entity or a field?** Default: Explored; a part is addressed by revision and locator and a reply may name it; keep the text domain's `anchor_text` in view. Blocks: scenario 4, the commentary use case, tests 28.
13. **Is an imported email a raw acquired object with discovered fields?** Default: yes. Blocks: the email data model, the Bcc rule, the "import channels" requirement.
14. **Does `bio` leave the content domain for thing?** Default: yes, if it survives the placing test. Blocks: the bio folder.
15. **Should interaction-limits attach to any relation (timeline events as well) and be set by the owner or the group?** Default: yes to both, owner first; a group sets a default for its contents. Blocks: the merge of seven folders, timeline's two limit edges, the backlog's group-level forms.
16. **Is a machine-written text or a system notice content?** Default: a system notice is a timeline event; a machine-written summary is discovered data; only something someone answers for is content. Blocks: `log-activity`, derived summaries, scenario 11.
17. **Who owns reporting and appeal of a content?** Default: the justice side, with the content exposing the attachment point. Blocks: the `Report()` service, the `hidden` status vocabulary.
18. **Should the review phase be a pluggable module, and how does it relate to the documentation Status?** Default: yes later; first decide the relation to Status. Blocks: the RFC automatic behaviours, the document model.
19. **Does a paid-partnership disclosure live in attribution with a rule, or is it a separate capability?** Default: a role in attribution plus a rule per jurisdiction. Blocks: the `group-user` note, any advertising rule.
20. **Where does the misfiled notification template go?** Default: the notification domain. Blocks: nothing; hygiene.
