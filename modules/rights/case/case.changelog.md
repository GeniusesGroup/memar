#### What changed
The morning settled what a complaint is, that a vote stays on its own case, that parties and amount do not identify a claim, that a past majority is derived from retained votes rather than its own edge, that any user type may be a party or an expert, that kidnap and stolen stay as they are, that there is no cap on creating cases and the fixed system fee is the brake, what adjudication cost is made of, that opinions are independent and blind, that a registry inquiry is a claim rather than a draft-gate, that a third party's attack is a new case and each reason is its own edge, and that unordered claims still need a link. #### Deliberation
A falsifier is a test, not a second decision. Every ratified point was written with the test that would show it wrong, and model v4's convention says so in its own words: "Each ratified point is followed by what would falsify it. A falsifier is a test. It is not a second decision." Critique v1 supplied the attack list this ratification answered; it is evidence, and it does not replace a chosen point.

#### Considered and not done
- **A registry inquiry as a draft-gate on later cases** (model v1 and model v2, `acceptance_required`; rejected this morning): the inquiry is its own claim, and the draft chain may change after it or not exist yet, because the filer may not know the defendant.
- **Three exits after an inquiry reject** (model v2 and the synthesis, proposals answering critique v1's finding that a rejected inquiry left later cases in draft with no recovery path; moot with the gate): refile the inquiry and let the waiting cases leave draft only on a later accept; let the parties abandon the waiting drafts; or open a new inquiry on a new path. Abandon there meant the waiting cases stay in shared draft and are not taken to a filing, and it was not a status, a vote, or a shortcut. With no gate, no case waits, and no exit is needed.
- **The arbitrator as an expert the contract parties named, with the judge ruling from their aggregate** (his morning view, recorded as not concluded): replaced the same afternoon. In the doctrines he looked at, an arbitrator is a judge, and the difference is who names them.

#### Decision

#### What changed
The afternoon settled: an independent opinion and an independent vote, with nothing visible to a party until every required judge has voted; an inquiry returning an information package the requester does not receive the records of; no abstention; a partial amount as a reject and then a new claim; one defendant per claim; an arbitrator as a judge with the difference being who names them; civil versus criminal as a label; no generic related edge; specialty, rank, and fee self-declared with evidence; continuation stopping on the margin rather than on wealth; a final ruling that executes; links between claims staying pluggable; a folder per claim-type label; an inquiry as a claim like any other; and seven functional offices that may exist as pluggable edges without reasons yet. One thing was blocked: where the acceptable amount named in a rejection is stored.

#### Deliberation
Afternoon input governs over the morning where the two conflict. Critique v2 is the evidence for several of these and for one against others: it is the evidence against weighting a vote by a party's score, and it did not answer the forum and enforcement queries, which stay the standing falsifier of the one-judge-edge decision.

#### Considered and not done
- **Conditional accept on the vote edge** (rejected): if accept means the claim as filed, a positive disposition at a changed service is a third outcome the margin cannot classify.
- **A party score as a vote weight** (rejected): a vote as acquired fact has single responsibility, which is the disposition on the case. Weight from a party score mixes evaluation of the judge with aggregation of dispositions. That modeling reason was written in the critique record and is carried here because the changelog entry for the rejection had cited no evidence.
- **A kernel fee formula** (rejected): organizations differ in schedule without rewriting the case.
- **Several defendant edges on one case** (a morning line, as model v4 records it; rejected): a claim has one defendant, and solidary or unequal shares are chained claims, one defendant each.
- **A kernel pool of 1, then 3, then 5, adding two judges at each refiling** (model v1; critique v1 found the arithmetic sound; it replaced the 2026-09-26 doubling, which was never adopted): the reason for an odd pool was that an even pool can tie and the case has no tie vote, and model v1 refused abstention for the same reason, since an abstention lets an odd pool tie. Not taken: continuation stops on the vote margin, with pluggable thresholds, so the kernel holds no pool size and no parity. No abstention stands on his own reason, that a judge must choose and must explain the choice.

#### Decision
The afternoon's reject-only handling of a changed amount was withdrawn the same evening. It stands here as the record of what was decided that day, not as a rule.

### Pilot proposal records the rejected alternatives for a changed execution
- Time: 2026-10-07T05:19:00Z
- Type: Added

- Contributors:

  - Grok (grok-4.7 via Cursor) — copied the model-v6 rejected list into this entry

#### What changed
This entry does not ratify a graph. It records every alternative model v6 marked rejected, under the heading Memar uses for that record, so the list is not left only in the review trail.

The shape model v6 proposes — full reject, and the reject may point at one request, plus a consensus claim the claimant registers — is not an applied change. The vote-to-request edge is not drawn. See [case.handoff.md](case.handoff.md).

#### Considered and not done
- **A. Full reject, and the claimant chains an adjusted claim** (rejected): the next claim is not tied to one request a reject already points at, and that next claim may grow new requests, so the chain does not stop. The alternate also has no registered request for execution to read.
- **C. Conditional accept, which needs a consensus claim** (rejected): a positive disposition at a changed service. The margin can read 100 percent accept with nothing to execute. A later phase is not given this shape to add.
- **Accept-with-attachment** (rejected): the same false finality. Accept means the request as filed, with no attachment.
- **Judges multi-picking attachments** (rejected): consensus is not each judge choosing among several requests on one ballot. The margin cannot classify that ballot. Independent votes survive when the claimant has filed one service and each judge accepts or rejects that service.
- **Justice-domain judge aliases** (rejected): evening of 2026-09-27. Domain-specific secrecy may backfire. The alias "Judge 1" is dropped. A competence claim is not how a party learns who voted. A person hiding their own identity is a user-domain choice.
- **A party score changing a vote's weight** (rejected): a party score is not put on a vote. No scoring formula is designed.
- **A system average of the judges' numbers** (rejected): the average is not a ruling. Storing several positive votes at several amounts is the scenario the reject rule avoids.
- **One collectively signed opinion** (rejected): every judge records an independent vote. One person drafting and the others signing is not a set of votes.
- **A generic "related" edge** (rejected): each reason claims are linked is its own edge.
- **Enriching `vote_accept` so that accept no longer means as-filed** (rejected): that is conditional accept under another name.
- **Reusing conditional accepts as a consensus without fresh votes** (rejected): a vote on one case is never a vote on another case. Reuse collapses independent votes.
- **A conditional accept on the consensus case** (rejected): on that case, conditional accept is not allowed. A new attached request there means the chain does not stop.
- **A third ballot edge** (rejected): the disposition stays accept or reject. The request is a pointer the reject may have, not a third outcome.
- **A policy cap on consensus rounds** (not taken; critique v3 and critique v4 listed it as one way to end the chain): a cap contradicts the decision that creating cases has no cap. The chain ends instead because the consensus case files a request an earlier reject already points at and grows no new one.

#### Decision
Nothing in this entry is ratified. Model v6 proposes candidate B and records the items above as not done. Omid has not ratified the proposal. The vote-to-request edge is not drawn.
