## Findings

### 1. What the three domains are for

**Statement (agent, Tentative).** The three domains answer one question for the rest of the system, from three sides: *which parties may validly do or demand what with what, since when, until when, on whose word, and how can that be undone?* **Contract** owns the memory of **an agreement reached**: who assented to which exact terms, when, and what became of the agreement afterwards (in force, amended, ended, set aside). **Ownership** owns the memory of **a title declared**: who is recognised, by whom, as holding which bundle of rights over which thing, from what origin, and how that changed. **Delegate** owns the memory of **an authority conferred**: who may act for whom, within what scope, from what origin, until what ending, so that the agent's act is attributed to the principal. Each is a *source of standing*; none is the standing's content (the rights, the obligations, the powers), which are rules and claims, and none is the check that an act is permitted, which is a rule reading these records.

This is the reading of the Decided "rights first" ([ownership.md](../ownership/ownership.md)): a right is the unit; title is a standard bundle of rights, authority is a bundle of Hohfeldian powers, a claim is a right with a correlative duty; each bundle needs a record of its origin and its life, and that record is what the three domains hold. "Rights exist first" then means *the scope of a title or authority is a set of rights, not that ownership grants them*.

**What the three are not.** Not the law (an independent normative source, [law.md](../law/law.md)); not the case (a dispute about a standing, [case.md](../case/case.md)); not accounting (which records quantity and "holding is not title"); not permission checking (a rule reading standings, [authorization](../../../modules-plugable/management/authorization/authorization.md)); not responsibility for work (task). A delegation is not a permission: a permission lets the holder act as themselves; authority makes the act the principal's. The legacy delegate record (given user, gotten user, a role-rule, a request, a time) is a role-access transfer, which is nearer permission; the proposal below keeps the record and states the difference.

#### Scenarios
Seventeen, tagged by the domain they test (C contract, T title, A authority, G agreement). Illustrative; each says what breaks, what it says about the domain, what it asks.

**S1 (C, G). An Iranian marriage contract (*aqd*, with the *mahr* and added conditions, registered at a notary; also the temporary *sigheh*, and the Hindu or Christian rites registered by a state officer).** Offer and acceptance are spoken in one sitting; two witnesses; the bride's guardian's consent in some cases; the dowry is an obligation of the groom, deferred or on demand; conditions may be written into the register (the wife's right to divorce); registration is by an official; the contract may have a fixed term. *Breaks:* "a contract has two parties" (witnesses, a guardian, a registrar act); "terms are one text" (the register holds standard and added conditions); "a contract is signed" (the act is spoken). *Says:* the owner's Decided position holds: marriage is a contract with ordinary obligations; the witnesses and registrar are not parties. *Asks:* parties and non-party participants are two relations; the registration is an act by an authority with its own record; a term may be a duration.

**S2 (C, G). A rotating savings circle (Iranian *sandoq* and *gam-gardani*, Kenyan *chama*, Mexican *tanda*, West African *susu*, Korean *gye*).** Ten people each pay a fixed sum monthly and each month one takes the pot in an order fixed by lot or by need; nothing is written; trust and reputation enforce; a member may leave, be replaced, or default after taking the pot. *Breaks:* five hard-coded sides (the contract edges stop at `side_fifth`); a fixed party list; "signature". *Says:* a contract may have many parties joining over time and an agreement that precedes any record; the parties act inside a group. *Asks:* the number of parties is data; joining after the start is a novation (a party added by the others' assent), not an edit; the obligations (a calendar of dues and payouts) are claims the contract creates, not contract text.

**S3 (C, G). Click-wrap terms of service and a licence.** One text, millions of "parties", no negotiation; acceptance by pressing a button or by continued use; the provider may change the terms by notice, effective unless the user leaves. *Breaks:* signature as the act of assent; frozen terms; "each contract is a separate node with its own party list". *Says:* the terms are a template value; an assent is a request with an action; a unilateral change is a new version offered to every party, and silence is the acceptance an organization's rule chooses. *Asks:* thing's `template` and `derived_from` carry the "template or filled" rule of [terms](edges/terms/terms.md); acceptance by silence is a rule, not a kernel fact (task's default is the opposite, [task question 6](../../management/task/task.research.001.md#open-questions)).

**S4 (C, T). A construction contract with change orders, progress billing and an expert (Iranian *surat vaz'iyat*, an AIA-style pay application; a retention percentage; an engineer certifies; an arbitration clause; an addendum raises the price).** Obligations arrive over years; an addendum changes some terms and leaves the rest; the contract is also the subject of invoices ([invoice research S10](../../economics/invoice/invoice.research.001.md)); a dispute goes to arbitration named in advance. *Breaks:* `supersedes` is whole-record replacement and an addendum is not; the contract as one frozen text. *Says:* an amendment is a new agreement that modifies a base, and the effective terms are a reading over base and amendments; the clause naming the arbitrator is a term, and on a dispute it feeds [judge assignment](../../../modules-plugable/rights/judge-assignment/judge-assignment.md), which writes the `judge` edge (case model v6: not re-argued). *Asks:* a relation between a later agreement and an earlier that is neither `supersedes` nor `corrects`; the expert of [user-expert](edges/user-expert/user-expert.md) is a designation, not a case edge.

**S5 (C, G). A smart-contract escrow (a Solidity object; the [terms](edges/terms/terms.md) document names it as an option).** Two keys, no names; the code releases funds when a condition is met; a bug drains the pool; a court later orders reversal. *Breaks:* "a contract has a human text and the machine form is a companion"; "parties are users with a real-world identity". *Says:* the executable form may be the only form; when text and code diverge the system must say which binds (the law domain's answer, "a record whose text and computer form diverge is not eligible for signature", [law research 001](../law/law.research.001.md), is for laws). *Asks:* [contract handoff question 2](contract.handoff.md#open-questions) on terms and human code; which form an assent binds to.

**S6 (C, G). A *hawala* remittance and a village council settlement (Somali *xeer*, Pashtun *jirga*, Iranian *reesh-sefidi* mediation).** Money moves between two brokers on a word; the brokers net their debts months later; a settlement is concluded by elders with a guarantor and sealed with a gift. No document; the agreement exists before anyone writes it. *Breaks:* a record is the contract; written terms. *Says:* the system records evidence of an agreement and the assents of those who say they agreed; a record made later by a witness is a statement by a declarer, not the agreement itself. *Asks:* an assent with a declarer other than the assenter (a witness reporting) is a different grade of evidence; a guarantor is a party with a conditional obligation.

**S7 (C, A, G). A minor or an unauthorised agent signs; the principal ratifies later (a nine-year-old's online purchase; an employee signs beyond a limit; a *fuzuli* sale in Iranian law, a *negotiorum gestio* in civil law).** The contract is formed by someone who lacked capacity or authority; it is void, voidable, or valid on ratification. *Breaks:* "an assent is by the party"; "validity is decided at signing". *Says:* an assent records who actually acted and **for whom**; its validity depends on a delegation or a guardianship valid at that instant, or on a later ratifying assent. *Asks:* the contract's status cannot be a flag set at signing; a ratification is an assent by the principal that refers to the earlier one; the check "was the signer authorised" is a rule reading a delegate record (X3).

**S8 (C, T, G). A collective agreement extended by law to non-signatories (German *Allgemeinverbindlicherklärung*, French extension orders), and an insurance policy with a beneficiary who is not a party (a *takaful* or life policy).** A contract binds people who never assented, or gives a right to a person who never assented. *Breaks:* privity, which the statute [contract within law](../../../modules-plugable/rights/laws/contract-within-law/contract-within-law.md) states ("does not bind anyone who did not make it"). *Says:* a contract may *concern* a non-party; a law may adopt a contract's terms for others. *Asks:* the proposal's `concerns` edge (beneficiary) and the question whether extension is [law supersedes contract](../../../modules-plugable/rights/laws/law-supersedes-contract/law-supersedes-contract.md) or a different statute; not decided here.

**S9 (T). An inherited house held undivided by five heirs (Iranian *irth* shares by fixed fractions; a French *indivision*; a common-law joint tenancy with survivorship against a tenancy in common).** Each holds a fraction, or the whole jointly with a right of survivorship; selling needs all or a majority; one heir lives there. *Breaks:* `(user)-[:owner_of]->(thing)` as a bare edge; "one owner". *Says:* title has holders with kinds and fractions; a share is itself transferable; decisions need a rule. *Asks:* fraction and kind on the holder relation; the heir relation ([heir](../ownership/edges/heir/heir.md)) is the origin of these records, from the estate ([estate research](../../identity/estate/estate.research.001.md) H3).

**S10 (T). Land that no individual owns, and land with layers (a *waqf* endowment, Mexican *ejido*, Māori and African customary tenure; a Hong Kong tower, a New York air-rights sale, mineral rights under another's farm, a 99-year leasehold, a Turkish or Iranian apartment with its own deed).** A waqf's founder fixed its purpose and a custodian (*mutawalli*) manages it; nobody may sell it. A clan holds land in trust for the living, the dead and the unborn. Three parties hold three layers of one place. *Breaks:* "an owner is a user who holds the thing"; one title per thing. *Says:* title is a bundle with a scope, several may coexist on one subject, and a custodian's standing is not an owner's; the [estate research](../../identity/estate/estate.research.001.md) E6 and E9 reach the same point from the physical side. *Asks:* scope (the bundle) is part of the record; a holder may be a collective, a purpose, or none with a custodian.

**S11 (T). Registry title against the chain of deeds; a good-faith buyer of stolen goods; a land title won by long possession.** In a Torrens or German register the registration *makes* the title and a later correction does not undo a good-faith buyer's; in a recording system the registry gives priority and the title is the chain of valid transfers; many systems protect a good-faith buyer of movables, others (*nemo dat*) do not; adverse possession turns a wrong into a title by time. *Breaks:* "ownership is a fact"; one declarer. *Says:* title is *declared* by someone with authority (a registry, a court, an organization), the origin is stated, and two declarers may disagree; each organization is authoritative for what it states (X7). *Asks:* the declarer is on the record (the existing [issuer](../ownership/edges/issuer/issuer.md) edge reads this way); a title with a contested status is representable; "who owns X" is "as declared by whom".

**S12 (A). A power of attorney (Iranian *vekalat-nameh* at a notary, with a right to appoint a sub-attorney; an English durable power that survives the principal's incapacity; an agency coupled with an interest that is irrevocable; ended by the principal's death in most systems).** Scope is general or for one act; the agent may sub-delegate; the principal may revoke; a third party who relied on it in good faith is protected; death ends it or does not. *Breaks:* a pair `(given, gotten)` without scope, ending cause or chain. *Says:* a delegation is a record with identity (the Memar escape clause for `agent_for` as an edge applies); a sub-delegation is a delegation whose scope lies inside its parent's and which ends when the parent ends. *Asks:* [delegate handoff question 4](../delegate/delegate.handoff.md#open-questions) (one record or a lifecycle); the ending causes; reliance by third parties (an evidence rule).

**S13 (A). A company that signs through officers (Iran: the gazette lists the signatories and the rule "two of three, with the seal"; Japan: a registered representative director and a seal certificate; Germany: joint representation).** The principal is an organization, which cannot press a button; the authority rests on an external fact (a gazette notice); a threshold applies; the officer changes while open contracts continue. *Breaks:* "a delegate is a person chosen by a person". *Says:* the principal is any user (organization included, Decided); the agent may be a position, not a person ([role research, smaller choice 4](../../management/role/role.research.001.md)); a threshold is a property of the scope. *Asks:* where the external fact is recorded ([organization research](../../identity/organization/organization.research.001.md) puts representation in this domain, finding 6 "representation"); whether authority attaches to the position or the person.

**S14 (A, G). An elected delegate and liquid democracy ([delegate.md](../delegate/delegate.md)'s own definition: "someone elected or chosen to speak, vote, or take decisions for a group").** A citizen appoints a delegate for health votes only; the delegate may appoint another; a cycle is possible; the citizen may override one vote and keep the appointment; the delegate's signature on each proposition is visible ([law.handoff.md](../law/law.handoff.md)). *Breaks:* a delegation that covers every act; no cycles. *Says:* scope may be a topic; delegation chains must be acyclic; an act under a delegation records both the agent and the principal. *Asks:* [law handoff question 2](../law/law.handoff.md#open-questions) (one proposition or the whole appointment); the override is an assent by the principal that takes precedence over the delegation for that proposition.

**S15 (A). An AI or software agent acts with a spending limit (an OAuth token with scopes; a bot buying supplies up to a monthly cap; one of this project's own agents).** It cannot be held liable; it acts at machine speed; its scope is a limit and a list of actions. *Breaks:* "a delegate is a person"; "authority is checked once". *Says:* the synthesis's X3 sentence is right: a software agent is a delegate and accountability stays with the principal ([task Q10](../../management/task/task.research.001.md#open-questions)); the delegation is also an agreement (the consent screen is an assent to the scope). *Asks:* limits are rules at the delegation's points; each act records the delegation it relied on (the request research's `permitted_by`, Q13).

**S16 (A). A guardian, a court-limited guardianship, a regent (Iranian *vali* and *qayyim*; Swedish *god man*; a regent for a minor ruler).** The authority is conferred by law or a court, not by the principal; it is limited by matter (may vote, may not sign a loan); it ends at majority or by court order, not by the principal's request. *Breaks:* a delegation is chosen and revocable by the principal. *Says:* the origin of an authority may be a law, a court or an office; the principal cannot revoke; "attributed to the principal" still holds. *Asks:* the delegate record must carry an origin and a "who may end it" distinct from the principal; the [user research](../../identity/user/user.research.001.md) scenario 4 asks the same.

**S17 (A, G). Representation before a tribunal (the British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal separates a *contact person* of an organization who binds it, a *representative* who needs the tribunal's permission, and a *helper* who may not bind; [case research 003](../case/case.research.003.md)).** Authority needs two keys: the principal's grant and a third party's permission; one role may speak but not bind. *Breaks:* a delegation is valid when the principal grants it. *Says:* scope distinguishes *speak*, *bind*, *assist*; a condition may require another party's assent. *Asks:* [delegate handoff question 5](../delegate/delegate.handoff.md#open-questions) (filing a case for another): the answer is a delegation with scope "file", which the case's plaintiff edge does not need to know (not re-arguing model v6).

**What the seventeen share (agent, Tentative).** Every case needs: an *act* with a time and a declarer (an assent, a registration, a grant); a *stated scope* that is a set of rights or powers; *several parties and layers* (more than two sides; more than one title per thing; chains of authority); an *origin other than the parties' will* in about a third (law, court, registry, inheritance, office); and an *ending with a cause*. None needs a different shape for each kind of relationship, which supports the Decided "no new legal-relation entity per relationship kind".

### 2. Minimal kernel and what must be pluggable

#### The removal test run on each domain (agent, Tentative)
[What Earns Foundational Status](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modularity.md#what-earns-foundational-status): express the part of reality through the remaining abstractions.
- **Contract.** Without it, the agreed terms, the set of assenting parties and the life of the agreement would have to live in invoices, tasks, groups or texts. A group could hold the parties and a text the terms, but nothing would hold *this set of assents to this digest*, nor the status of the agreement, without bending group into an agreement ledger and text into a signed instrument. The pressure to bend is real: it earns a kernel (Tentative).
- **Ownership.** Without it, title would be a field on every domain (an account's holder, a product's owner, a content's owner, an estate's holder) and the many-holders, scope, origin and contested cases of S9 to S11 would be reinvented per domain, or `owner_of` would become a bare edge with no rules. The existing documents already contradict each other here (contradictions 1 and 2). It earns a kernel (Tentative).
- **Delegate.** Without it, acting-for would be a private edge in account, transaction, user, group, organization and role (six homes already proposed, see the table below). It earns a kernel, **provided** it holds a record with identity (Memar's own test for `agent_for` as a node, S12 and S16).
- **Agreement as a node.** Without a separate agreement node, a contract still exists: its parties and terms and the assents of its parties are enough. Agreement is the state in which the required assents are present. **It does not earn a node** (Tentative; contradicts nothing Decided). What does recur is the *assent*, in five domains (below), which earns a shared capability, not a foundational concept.

**Invariants the kernels state (agent, Tentative).**

#### The authority merge candidates (synthesis X3), tested one by one
The synthesis proposes "authority is one capability: rights/delegate plus ownership plus authorization; one home (delegate); no domain keeps a private authority edge" ([base-model](../../../temp/sessions/20261018-T195830Z-17032/base-model.md) section 4, row "authority / instruction"; [decision-graph](../../../temp/sessions/20261018-T195830Z-17032/decision-graph.md) X3). The common shape (principal or holder, scope as rules, interval, origin, ending cause, declarer) is shared; the module is not.

### 3. Rival models

#### 3.1 Contract and agreement
- **A. Bare edges (the status quo, Explored, rejected).** `(contract)-[:side_first_is]->(user)`, terms, status labels. *For:* it exists. *Against:* no who-may-create or what-triggers on the status labels ([status.md](edges/status/status.md)); five ordinal folders; no place for assent, amendment, capacity; fails S2, S4, S7 and the "no bare edges" rule.
- **B. Contract as a node with parties, frozen terms by digest, assents and status events (Tentative, recommended).** The kernel above. *For:* survives S1 to S8; matches the shape every sibling reached; contract.md's Decided "origin of rights, not a right" holds. *Against:* adds a party record (reusing membership may avoid it, small choice 1).
- **C. Agreement-first (Explored).** The primitive is an Agreement node; a contract is an agreement that intends legal consequences; a vote and a group admission are agreements too. *For:* the Memar-ward view that agreement is more general than contract (the task seed's unresolved #8); one node for X6. *Against:* an agreement node holds nothing that the assents plus the contract do not; a vote's consequence differs from a contract's (a threshold binds the dissenters; a contract binds none, S8); agreement is a *state*, so a node would be a stored cache of a derived fact. Run through the [Premature Classification Check](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/modeling.practice.md#challenging-a-proposed-concept) it fails "is this a contextual view of another concept".
- **D. Rights-first: no contract domain, only an `origin_is` edge on rights (Explored).** A contract is a label on the origin of rights. *For:* respects "rights come first". *Against:* the removal test: parties, digest, assents and the status of the agreement have no home without bending another domain; S2, S3 and S5 have rights only through the contract.
- **E. Rule-first (Explored).** A contract is a rule set attached to the parties; the text is a comment. *For:* S5, and the computer-code edge. *Against:* S1, S6, S7 (no machine form exists); and [law research 002](../law/law.research.002.md) keeps the human sentence as a companion the machine form must not outrun.
- **F. Contract as a group, parties as members (Explored; answers the conversion's open question 1).** `group_p.UUID` was an alias. Under group's K2, parties are memberships of a contract that bears members. *For:* reuses membership with provenance and interval; novation is joining and leaving. *Against:* a "member" of a contract is not a member of a group in any other sense; the list of parties would mix parties, guarantors and witnesses unless kinds are enforced; the dependency direction is fine (contract above group) but the words mislead. **Not a group's record** is the proposed answer to the question; the *realization* of the party record as a membership is small choice 1.

**Small choice 1: is the party record a membership (Explored).** (a) A party is a membership of the contract thing: reuse. (b) A party is its own record. Default (a), because the group research's membership has identity, provenance, kind and interval, which is the party record, and admission is the same offer and acceptance. Cost: the contract's rule must read only "party" kinds when applying the aggregation.

**Small choice 2: where terms stop and obligations begin (Tentative).** Terms are the frozen proposition; obligations are claims the contract creates, held in the obligation capability, never edited into the terms. The contract references the terms; the claims reference the contract as origin. This is also how [invoice](../../economics/invoice/invoice.research.001.md) S10 and the `financial` and `settlement_by` edges read the contract.

**Small choice 3: an amendment (Tentative).** A whole replacement uses `supersedes`; a partial change is a new contract that `amends` the earlier and is read with it; the effective terms are computed. The alternative (every amendment is a supersession of the whole) loses the unchanged clauses. Decides S4.

#### 3.2 Ownership
- **O-A. Bare edge `(user)-[:owner_of]->(thing)` (Explored, rejected).** *Against:* no fractions, scope, origin, interval, contested status (S9 to S11); the repository already attaches more edges to an `ownership` node ([ownership handoff](../ownership/ownership.handoff.md#decisions) decision 1).
- **O-B. Title record declared by an authority, with holders, scope, origin and status events (Tentative, recommended).** The kernel above.
- **O-C. Title as a derived reading over the chain of acts (Explored).** No stored title; "who owns X" folds the contracts, inheritances and registrations. *For:* nothing is declared that is not evidenced; matches a recording system. *Against:* a registry constitutes title (S11); contested and uncertain cases need a statement by someone; the fold needs a decider. Survives as the *shortcut* for reading a chain (a derived edge), not as the source.
- **O-D. Ownership as a Right with a correlative duty (the boundaries proposal, Explored).** Title is a bundle of Rights; "who owns" is the holder of the bundle. Compatible with O-B (the scope is that bundle); a separate Right domain waits on [boundaries Q2](../rights-domain-boundaries.handoff.md#open-questions).

#### 3.3 Authority
- **A-A. Authority as a pair edge `(given, gotten, rule)` (the legacy record, Explored, rejected for the kernel).** *Against:* no scope, ending cause, chain or origin (S12, S14, S16).
- **A-B. Delegation as a record with principal, agent, scope, interval, origin, chain (Tentative, recommended).** *For:* every scenario; the Memar escape clause for `agent_for` as an entity applies (S12 and S16 *establish* the requirement).
- **A-C. No delegate domain; agent-for is only an edge (Memar's default, Explored).** [Agency → `agent_for`](https://github.com/GeniusesGroup/memar/blob/main/docs/agency.md#the-agent_for-relationship): an edge unless a requirement shows the relation must be an entity. *Against here:* S12 (a chain whose links end), S16 (an ending by someone other than the principal), S14 (a scope per proposition) are such requirements. Run as an edge it re-grows these on the edge. Marked Explored because the choice is the owner's.
- **A-D. Authority as a rule over standings, with no authority record (Explored, partly adopted).** Who may do X is whatever a rule concludes from title, membership and position. *Adopted* for the stewardship half (the table above); *rejected* for acting-for, because attribution to the principal needs a record the request can cite (`permitted_by`).
- **A-E. Delegate to the position, not the person (Explored; [role research](../../management/role/role.research.001.md) smaller choice 4).** (a) The delegated end is a position; the occupant at an instant exercises it. (b) The delegated end is a person and ending the occupancy must revoke it. Default: both are legal; the agent end may name a user or a position (DK2). The legacy record is (b).

#### 3.4 The shared shape
- **Sh-1. One standing module (Explored, rejected as a module).** A single `standing` kernel with kinds title, authority, claim, permission. *For:* identical fields (party, subject, scope, interval, origin, declarer, status events). *Against:* the removal test does not force it (each domain's reading without the shared module needs no fabrication, only repetition); the three have different invariants (fractions, chains, digests) and different declarers; the base model treats the "admission pattern" the same way ("same shape; share the status vocabulary, not a module").
- **Sh-2. Same shape, no shared module, one shared relation `origin_is` (Tentative, recommended).** The relation "this standing arose from that act" is needed by title, authority, obligation, license and account instruction, holds for any kind of thing, and its who and trigger can be stated without naming one domain. That passes [thing's admission gate](../../identity/thing/thing.research.001.md) (three independent domains, any kind of thing, statable who-and-trigger) as the base model states it. Proposed for definition once on thing (question 12), each domain keeping the rule for who may create it.
- **Sh-3. Assent as the shared atom (Tentative).** Contract, law vote ([law research 001](../law/law.research.001.md): "a vote is a signature over the payload digest, a consent record"), group admission, task acceptance, invoice answer and delegation acceptance share the atom; they differ in the aggregation rule (all parties; the counterparty; a threshold; silence). This answers [normative-bootstrap question 2](../normative-bootstrap.handoff.md#open-questions) (is consent always an agreement, or sometimes weaker): **consent is the atom; agreement is a state of enough atoms; a vote is the same atom under a threshold.** A *unilateral declaration* (an invoice issued, a product declared, a will) is a third shape that is not an assent and is not forced into one.

### 4. What the domains must own, as requirements questions
Phrased as questions; each states what it favours only where a Decided passage settles it.

#### Contract

##### Identity
- What is a contract's identity, and does it survive an amendment? (Favoured: the thing's, one identity; an amendment is a new contract linked to it, small choice 3.) Is a contract a group's record? (Proposed: no.)
- Is a contract unique across organizations, or does each party's organization keep its own node with a mapping? (X7: one identity space; the content of what each states is its own.)
- Can the same terms be adopted by many contracts (a template) and what is the relation? (Thing's `template` and `derived_from`.)

##### Lifecycle
- Which acts are acquired, which readings? (Proposed: proposed, in force, ended with cause are events; partly signed, legal action, overdue are readings.)
- May the terms change after the first assent? (Proposed: no; a new contract.) May a party be replaced? (Novation by the others' assent.) May the parties set be changed after signature, and by whom? (Only before; after, a novation.)
- May a contract take effect with a delay, end by condition, renew itself? (Effective time and recorded time both kept, X4; renewal is a rule that proposes a successor.)
- What ends a contract: performance, agreement, clause, expiry, a court, a supervening law? (Causes, a vocabulary; the law case is a statute.)

##### Authorization
- Who may propose, assent, withdraw, end? (The proposer proposes; a party or its agent assents; withdrawal before the others' assent is the withdrawer's; ending by the parties' assent or a clause or an authority.)
- Who may set non-party participants, before and after signature? (The existing "before signature, or after by a judge" is a rule of the participants capability.)
- Who may read the terms? (A rule through `condition_is`; sensitive terms are a label, not a field.)

##### Rules
- When is an assent valid: the signer's capacity, authority or ratification at that instant? (A rule reading standings; S7.)
- What is the aggregation rule: every party, a threshold, the counterparty, silence? (Chosen by the organization; the kernel knows "all parties on the current digest" only.)
- When text and executable form differ, which binds? (Open; question 7.)
- When may a contract be set aside (void, voidable, unconscionable)? (Law's, by [contract within law](../../../modules-plugable/rights/laws/contract-within-law/contract-within-law.md) and its siblings; not a field here.)

##### Events
- `contract_proposed`, `assent_recorded`, `party_joined`, `party_left`, `contract_in_force`, `terms_amended`, `contract_ended` (with cause), `contract_set_aside`. Which are the contract's own and which belong to others (obligation due, case filed, invoice issued)? (Others'.)

#### Ownership

##### Identity
- Is a title one record per (subject, scope, holders) or one per holder? (Proposed: one record with holder records, so a joint title has one identity.) What is the identity of the *subject*? (A thing; the estate research's rule that a change of extent is a successor estate means a title may need a successor too, [estate research](../../identity/estate/estate.research.001.md) ES5.)
- Does "owner" in this repository name title or record stewardship? (Two things; contradictions 1 and 2.)

##### Lifecycle
- Declared, contested, in force, suspended, ended (cause): which are events? May a title be declared retroactively? (Effective and recorded times both; a back-dated title is a later declaration with an earlier effective time.)
- How does a transfer work: the old title ends and a successor supersedes it, with fractions summing to the transferred share? (Proposed.)
- What happens at the holder's death or dissolution? (Succession proposes successor titles; the old ends with a cause; nothing is deleted.)

##### Authorization
- Who may declare a title? (The subject's declaring organization or an authority it recognises; several may declare, X7.) Who may contest? (A party with standing, by a case.) Who may transfer? (The holders, by the origin act: a contract, a registration.)
- Who may read who owns what? (A rule; ownership of a person's private things is sensitive.)

##### Rules
- Fractions sum to the whole (or to the bundle's measure)? A title with no holder (waqf, S10) needs a custodian? A reserved subject refuses new titles? Conflicting declarations: which does a rule prefer? (Mapping across organizations, not one winner.)

##### Events
- `title_declared`, `holder_added`, `holder_ended`, `title_contested`, `title_ended` (cause), `title_superseded`.

#### Delegate

##### Identity
- Is a delegation one record from grant to end, or a lifecycle of versions? (Handoff Q4. Proposed: one record with an identity; changes of scope are successor records linked by `supersedes`; the legacy `VersionOffset` is the versions of that chain.)
- Is the agent a person, a position, a thing? (A user, or a position; DK2.)

##### Lifecycle
- Granted, accepted, active, suspended, ended with cause. Does it need the agent's acceptance? (Default yes for authority that carries duty; no for a pure power such as a letter of introduction.) May the agent give it back? ("Given back" in the legacy record is the empty rule; proposed: an ending cause.)
- Does a delegation survive the principal's death or incapacity? (A durable grant says so in its scope; the default ends it.)

##### Authorization
- Who may delegate? The legacy comment "delegate user can delegate its role to other; blocked by default" is the policy; proposed: sub-delegation is allowed only where the parent's scope says so. Who may end it? (The principal, or only the conferring authority in S16.) Who may read it? (The principal, the agent, those relying on it, an authority.)

##### Rules
- Scope is within the grantor's own standing; limits and thresholds at the points where acts occur; an act is attributed to the principal only inside scope and interval; reliance by a third party is protected by an evidence rule (the delegation cited by the request is what the third party relied on).
- Cycles in a chain are refused; a topic scope may be overridden by the principal for one act (S14).

##### Events
- `authority_conferred`, `authority_accepted`, `authority_suspended`, `authority_ended` (cause), `authority_exercised` (not an event of this domain: the request records it, citing the delegation).

#### Assent (the shared capability)
- What does an assent bind to, and what is "the same terms"? (The content digest of the proposition; two parties assenting to different digests have not agreed.) What does silence do? (A rule chosen by the organization.) May an assent be withdrawn? (Before the aggregation rule is met, by a new record; after, only by ending the contract.) Who signs when an agent assents? (The agent's request is signed by the agent's key and records the principal; DK5.)

### 6. Missing edges and modules
Each a short sketch, with who creates and removes it and what it triggers.

1. **assent (pluggable, Tentative).** `(user)-[:assent_to {kind, at}]->(proposition)`. Records one party's act over a digest of terms: propose, accept, reject, withdraw, abstain. Created by the assenter or its agent (the request carries the principal); never removed. Processes aggregation rules, expiry of offers and the refusal of a mismatched digest. Triggers `agreement_reached` for the owner of the proposition: a contract becomes in force, a law's vote count changes, a group's membership begins, an invoice is answered. Home: rights, beside contract; the five sibling domains drop their own copies. Needs [request research](../../computer/request/request.research.001.md) RK5 (signature over a digest).
2. **obligation (pluggable or domain, Explored).** A claim with obligee, obligor, subject (an act or a quantity), due, origin. Created by its origin (a contract, a law, a judgment, an invoice); ended by performance, waiver, assignment, set-off. Triggers `obligation_due` and `obligation_breached` (which a case or a rule reads). Cost of not having it: `financial`, `settlement_by`, the invoice rival C and the "Obligation" question of the boundaries handoff all wait. It stays Explored until the owner answers [boundaries Q2](../rights-domain-boundaries.handoff.md#open-questions).
3. **non-party participants (pluggable, Tentative).** `(contract)-[:participant_is {kind}]->(user)`, kind in witness, expert, arbitrator, guarantor. Created by the parties before signature or by a judge after; never removed. Triggers a nomination to judge assignment on a dispute.
4. **amendment (Explored).** `(contract)-[:amends]->(contract)` and the effective-terms reading; created by the parties' assents; triggers `terms_amended` and a recomputation by the invoice and obligation capabilities.
5. **ratification (pluggable, Tentative).** An assent by the principal referring to an earlier assent or act (S7). Created by the principal; triggers `assent_ratified`, which makes a void assent valid from its effective time.
6. **title registry (pluggable, Explored).** The practice of an organization that declares titles: constitutive registration or priority recording; protection of good-faith buyers by rule. Created by the registrar; triggers `title_registered` and the contest window.
7. **succession of title (pluggable, Explored; replaces `heir`).** At a holder's end, proposes successor titles from law, will or contract and kinship; created by law or the will; triggers `title_succession_proposed`.
8. **reservation (pluggable, Explored; replaces `reserved`).** A hold on creating relations to a thing; created and released by an authority.
9. **delegation variants (pluggable, Tentative).** *Sub-delegation* (a delegation `derived_from` a parent); *standing mandate* (a delegation whose origin is a contract, such as a direct debit); *threshold* (co-signature: two of three); *position-bound authority* (the agent end is a position); *guardianship* (origin law or court; the principal cannot end it). Each states who creates and ends it; none is a new kernel.
10. **custodianship and trust (pluggable, Explored).** A title with kinds custodian and beneficiary and a stated purpose (waqf, trust, clan land, S10); created by the founder's act; triggers duties on the custodian read as rules.
11. **security interest (Deferred) and license (Deferred).** Titles or permissions of narrower bundle over another's subject; wait for the boundaries handoff's Permission question.
12. **`origin_is` (a thing-level relation, Explored).** "This standing arose from that act." Proposed for definition once on thing; each domain keeps who may create it.

### 7. Test scenarios
Given, when, then. Numbered; the number of the finding or scenario each tests follows.

1. **Assents bind to a digest (CK2, CK4).** Given a contract whose terms have digest D1 and two parties, one of whom assents to D1. When the other assents to a text with digest D2. Then no agreement is reached, the contract is not in force, and both assents are readable.
2. **Terms are frozen at the first assent (CK2).** Given one party has assented. When the proposer tries to change the terms. Then the change is refused; a new contract is proposed and may `supersede` the first; the first stays.
3. **Partly signed is a reading (CK5).** Given two of three assents. When status is asked. Then the contract is proposed and the reading says two of three; no status event "partly signed" exists.
4. **Ten parties, one contract (S2).** Given ten parties with side ordinals 1 to 10. When the circle begins. Then every party has a party record and the contract is in force when all ten assent; no folder per side is needed.
5. **A late joiner is a novation (S2).** Given a contract in force. When an eleventh person asks to join and the ten assent. Then a party record begins at that instant; the earlier terms are unchanged; an eleventh assent exists.
6. **A witness is not a party (S1).** Given a marriage contract with two witnesses and a registrar. When the aggregation rule runs. Then only the two parties' assents count; the witnesses are participants.
7. **An addendum amends (S4).** Given a contract in force and an addendum raising the price. When the addendum is agreed. Then the addendum is a contract that `amends` the first; the effective price is read from it; the unchanged clauses still bind; the first is not superseded.
8. **A clause names an arbitrator, a dispute seats a judge (S4, case v6).** Given a contract with a participant of kind arbitrator. When a case is filed on it. Then judge assignment may write a `judge` edge for that user; the case has one judge edge type and the contract is not edited.
9. **A template instance (S3).** Given a template contract and a user who accepts it. When the user assents. Then the user's contract is `derived_from` the template, its terms are the template's digest, and the template is not used directly.
10. **A text and a code form disagree (S5).** Given a term whose human text and executable object state different outcomes. When a party assents. Then the system applies the owner's rule from question 7; until it exists, the contract is not opened for assent (by analogy to the law's rule; marked an analogy).
11. **A minor's assent (S7).** Given a user under a guardianship delegation that limits her from signing loans. When she assents to a loan. Then the assent is recorded, the rule reading her standing marks it not binding, and it becomes binding only through her guardian's ratifying assent.
12. **An agent beyond its limit (S7, S15).** Given an employee with a delegation of scope "sign up to 10,000". When he assents to 50,000. Then the act is not attributed to the company, the company may ratify, and the request records the delegation it cited.
13. **Co-owners with fractions (S9).** Given a title with five heirs holding 1/8, 1/8, 1/8, 1/8 and 1/2. When a holder's share is sold. Then the holder's record ends, a successor title with the buyer's holder record begins, and fractions still sum to the whole.
14. **A custodian, no owner (S10).** Given a title with kind custodian and a stated purpose and no owner holder. When the custodian tries to transfer it. Then the rule of the founder's contract refuses and the refusal names the clause.
15. **Layers on one subject (S10).** Given a title for the surface and another for the minerals on one thing. When both are in force. Then neither supersedes the other and a rule decides conflicts by scope.
16. **Two declarers disagree (S11).** Given organization A declares X as holder and organization B declares Y. When "who owns" is asked in A. Then A's declaration is returned with its declarer, and B's is readable by mapping, with no shared node.
17. **A registry correction does not undo a good-faith buyer (S11).** Given a registry that protects good-faith buyers and a title later found forged. When the correction is declared. Then the earlier title ends with cause annulled, the buyer's title stands by the registry's rule, and nothing is deleted.
18. **Stewardship is not title (contradiction 1).** Given a record whose steward is organization O and whose subject is owned by user U. When U asks to edit the record. Then the rule reading stewardship decides, not the title.
19. **A sub-delegation stays inside its parent (S12).** Given a delegation with scope A and permission to sub-delegate. When the agent sub-delegates scope A and B. Then the second is refused; when it sub-delegates A, it ends when the parent ends.
20. **Death ends a delegation unless it says otherwise (S12).** Given a principal who dies. When an agent acts afterwards under a non-durable delegation. Then the act is not attributed to the principal; a durable delegation continues.
21. **A guardianship cannot be revoked by the ward (S16).** Given a court-conferred delegation. When the ward revokes. Then the revocation is refused and the ender named in DK4 (the court) may end it.
22. **An organization signs through two of three signatories (S13).** Given a delegation to three officers with threshold 2. When one officer assents alone. Then the assent is recorded, the company's assent is not complete, and a second officer completes it.
23. **Liquid delegation with override (S14).** Given a delegation for health topics and a vote on a health proposition. When the principal assents directly. Then the principal's assent counts and the delegate's does not; the delegation continues.
24. **A cycle is refused (S14).** Given A delegates to B and B to C. When C tries to delegate to A. Then it is refused.
25. **A tribunal's permission (S17).** Given a delegation with scope "represent" that requires the tribunal's permission. When the permission is absent. Then the representative may speak but the filing is not attributed to the principal; when the tribunal assents, it is.
26. **A software agent's limit (S15).** Given a delegation to an app with a monthly spending limit of 1,000. When a request would raise the total to 1,200. Then the rule at the point before the request refuses it and the principal is not bound.
27. **The delegation is the evidence (request Q13).** Given an exercised act under a delegation. When the act is audited. Then the request cites the delegation and the delegation's scope and interval at that time are readable.
28. **Nothing is deleted (X1).** Given a party who obtains erasure of personal data. When it is executed. Then the identity link on the party, holder and agent records is detached and the facts (an assent happened, a title was in force, an act was authorised) stay.
